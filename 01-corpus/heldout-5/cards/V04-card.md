## Paper identification

The paper studies coordinated flood-control operation of the parallel Guxian (GX) and Luhun (LH) reservoirs in the Yiluo River (YLR) Basin, with downstream protection at the Heishiguan (HSG) section and the Huayuankou system context. Its contribution is a forecast–scheduling–decision chain that models forecast residuals as flow-dependent, time-varying, and non-Gaussian; samples stochastic inflow scenarios; optimizes multi-reservoir releases with NSGA-III; and chooses an operation scheme with FAHP-TOPSIS. The article compares a deterministic multi-reservoir optimization model (DMOM) with an uncertainty-based model (UMOM) for the 18 September 2021 flood (pp. 1, 6, 11–16).

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper begins from the premise that regular scheduling based only on current inflow cannot reserve capacity for future floods, while multi-reservoir optimization must reconcile multiple control points and conflicting reservoir/downstream objectives (pp. 1–2). Forecast residual uncertainty is selected as the practical uncertainty source because its behavior can be derived from historical forecasts, whereas other uncertainties are harder to measure; the authors reject a universal unbiased Gaussian residual assumption because residual skewness, heavy tails, and variance change with flow magnitude (pp. 2–3). They therefore reason that residual mean and standard deviation should depend on forecast flow, residuals should be transformed to a Gaussian-like scale, and temporal dependence should be retained through an AR model. They compare six distribution assumptions and use skewness/kurtosis, standardization boundaries, and PI coverage to identify the time-varying, flow-grouped non-Gaussian case as most reasonable (pp. 7–9). The resulting stochastic scenarios are propagated through a multi-reservoir optimizer; the water-level objective is replaced by a 95th-percentile worst trajectory statistic to hedge extreme inflow cases (pp. 11–12). Finally, fuzzy preference weights and TOPSIS rank Pareto schedules, and a repair operator is reasoned necessary because evolutionary operations may violate final-level feasibility (pp. 5–6). The paper's claims stop at one YLR event and simulation-based comparison.

Reconstructed route: identify multi-reservoir and forecast-uncertainty risk → test residual distribution assumptions by flow groups → select a distribution treatment that better standardizes residuals and covers observations → model residual temporal dependence → generate probabilistic intervals and stochastic inflows → formulate constrained multi-objective reservoir scheduling → replace deterministic water-level objective with stochastic 95th-percentile protection → search Pareto schedules → select a preference-aware compromise and repair infeasible final levels → compare deterministic and uncertainty-aware operation under Monte Carlo scenarios. This is an evidence-based reconstruction, not a claim about private mental processes.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: Historical forecast/observed data from LS Station are grouped into four flow ranges and analyzed under six residual assumptions. For each case, residual moments are estimated, data are standardized, Yeo–Johnson transformation is applied where needed, skewness/kurtosis and standardization are checked, and AR(2) or t-copula dependence is used to obtain predictive intervals and 1000 stochastic inflow scenarios. The multi-reservoir model uses six-hour average releases as decision variables, mass balance and hydraulic constraints, and NSGA-III with 200 individuals, Latin Hypercube initialization, 500 generations, and stated crossover/mutation probabilities. FAHP-TOPSIS ranks nine indicators; final-level violations are repaired. For UMOM, 50 stochastic scenarios are input and the 95th-percentile water-level objective is optimized. DMOM and UMOM are then compared through another 50-scenario Monte Carlo evaluation, using water-level, release, and HSG peak indicators. Forecast-interval evaluation, operation optimization, comparative simulation, and discussion of limitations are distinct branches.

Reconstructed workflow: collect and interpolate event data → prepare deterministic forecasts → group residuals → estimate/standardize/transform residuals → test distribution adequacy → fit temporal dependence → compute PI and generate scenarios → formulate constrained DMOM/UMOM → optimize Pareto schedules with NSGA-III → rank and repair candidate schedules → propagate schedules through scenario simulations → compare indicators and interpret risk redistribution. Residual analysis and scenario generation repeat by case/group; scheduling repeats across candidate populations and scenarios; DMOM/UMOM comparison belongs to evaluation rather than routine deployment.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- Exact deterministic forecast-model architecture, training details, and forecast data partition are not reported in the supplied text.
- The residual fitting sample sizes, transformation parameter estimates, GA settings for moment-function calibration, and AR estimation procedure are not fully specified.
- The paper states AR(2) is selected from partial autocorrelation but does not report the PACF values or formal selection criterion.
- Exact t-copula estimation details, random seeds, scenario reproducibility, and the relationship between 1000 evaluation scenarios and 50 UMOM input scenarios are not fully documented.
- Reservoir storage/level curves, Muskingum parameters, final-level tolerance ε, and all hydraulic parameter values are not reported in the supplied text.
- FAHP pairwise fuzzy judgments, criterion weights, and TOPSIS cost/benefit coding are not numerically given.
- The repair operator's exact allocation algorithm and whether it guarantees all constraints beyond the final level are not quantified.
- The objective minimizes maximum water level rather than directly maximizing remaining flood-control storage; the authors state this is reasonable for these comparable large reservoirs but may not transfer to systems with different capacities (p. 14).
- High-flow PI estimates rely on relatively few samples, and the paper explicitly says reliability above 1000 m³/s is lower (p. 16).
- No field deployment, operator approval, fallback rule, post-event learning, or observed damage validation is reported. The data availability statement gives a Zenodo DOI, but this extraction does not inspect or verify the external data repository.
