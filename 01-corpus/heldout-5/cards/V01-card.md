## Paper identification

The paper studies dynamic control of the flood limited water level (DC-FLWL) at the Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR), using forecast-error domains to decide when and how forecast information at multiple lead times can support floodwater utilization. ## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper starts from a conflict between the statutory/design FLWL and the loss of usable floodwater when reservoirs remain too conservative during the flood season. It argues that dynamic control can exploit forecast information, but that longer lead times have larger uncertainty and can cause premature or insufficient pre-release. The key judgment is therefore whether the uncertainty around a forecast crosses the reservoir's release-rule boundary: if the actual inflow remains within the same rule-relevant domain, the operation is unchanged; if it falls outside, the forecast-based decision may increase risk. The authors use this judgment to select or couple lead times, then evaluate the resulting risk-benefit trade-off. This reconstruction describes the decision logic represented in the method; it does not claim access to private author cognition.

Reconstructed route: establish the floodwater-utilization versus safety problem; represent forecast uncertainty by lead time; define the physically feasible dynamic-control range; specify stage-dependent DC-FLWL decisions; define rule-relative error domains; convert those domains into probabilities of correct decisions; reject or shorten lead time when the probability is below a preference threshold; simulate resulting schedules and risks; compare alternatives and inspect threshold sensitivity.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The workflow begins with historical forecast/observed-flow pairs and a TGR operating specification. It estimates lead-time-specific forecast-error distributions, checks distributional fit, and samples stochastic forecast inflows. Separately, the pre-release capacity equations produce the DC-FLWL upper boundary. For each rolling scheduling scenario, the model computes rule-relative error domains, estimates correct-decision probabilities, filters/couples lead times, and runs the stage-dependent DC-FLWL model. It then computes reservoir and downstream risk and power-generation outputs, compares five options, and varies the probability threshold. The stochastic scenarios and option comparisons are evaluation branches around the main schedule-generation workflow, not evidence of deployment.

Reconstructed workflow: acquire and align daily TGR forecasts and observed inflows; estimate and test error distributions by lead time; generate 1,000 Monte Carlo scenarios; calculate the effective lead time and dynamic upper FLWL; at each rolling time step calculate error domains and correct-decision probabilities; recursively retain usable lead times; run pre-release, flood-control, and pre-refill scheduling; calculate RP and RL and generation/final level; compare Options 1-5; sweep thresholds and report case results.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The supplied text does not provide raw forecast/observed data, the local PDF, or all fitted distribution parameters and historical calibration-window details; data are stated to be available from the corresponding author by request (p. 22).
- The full equations and implementation details of the original flood-control scheduling rules, power-output constraint, shipping constraint, and some reservoir routing details are not reported in the supplied text.
- The exact numerical definition and update procedure for the risk tolerance R_d, beyond the algorithmic description, is unclear/not reported.
- Dependence among errors across lead times and time steps, and whether sampled scenarios preserve temporal or cross-horizon dependence, is not reported.
- The exact procedure for integrating the error distribution to obtain P(t+n), and the handling of threshold equality, is not fully specified.
- The study uses the pre-release rule for error-domain calculation and excludes other flood-control decision rules; applicability outside that rule is therefore unresolved.
- Independent out-of-sample validation, real-time deployment evidence, observed flood consequences, and transfer tests for reservoir groups are not reported.
