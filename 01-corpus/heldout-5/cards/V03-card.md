## Paper identification

The paper studies real-time flood-control operation of Lishimen (LSM) Reservoir in China when short-term inflow forecasts are uncertain. It combines three machine-learning forecasts (BP, LSTM, and ELM), a stacking ensemble, stochastic forecast scenarios, a multi-objective operation model, and a scenario-to-point robust optimization procedure. The paper's case study uses 22 flood events (16 historical events for initial training and 6 for testing; the numerical setup later partitions the 16 into 12 training and 4 validation events), and selects the 48-hour 2004 typhoon flood for operation analysis (pp. 9–11). The extraction distinguishes authors' explicit method descriptions and results from the extractor's route reconstruction.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper frames real-time reservoir scheduling as a conflict among upstream safety, downstream safety, and use of flood-control capacity. Forecast error can cause delayed release and excessive reservoir level when actual inflow exceeds the forecast, or wasteful pre-release and underuse of water when the forecast exceeds actual inflow (p. 2). Existing peak-reduction formulations are judged insufficient because they do not account for future inflow and capacity use across the whole scheduling period (pp. 2–3). The authors therefore reason that a forecast ensemble should feed scenario-based operation, that RRCA should measure whether remaining capacity matches future inflow over the complete horizon, and that robust optimization should select schedules whose worst scenario remains acceptable. STP is introduced because one individual decision vector produces different feasible schedules and several risk values across forecast scenarios; the paper converts each scenario's objective values into regret and carries the largest regret as the representative value during iterative optimization (pp. 8–9). The claim stops at comparative simulation of one large flood at LSM; the authors explicitly identify other uncertainty types, small sample size, parameter sensitivity, other cases, and alternative optimizers as unresolved (p. 18).

Reconstructed route: (1) identify forecast uncertainty and the upstream/downstream/capacity-utilization trade-off; (2) choose data-driven base forecasts and an ensemble while guarding against overfitting; (3) define RRCA to compare future inflow with dynamically remaining capacity; (4) formulate constrained release decisions with deterministic and scenario-based objectives; (5) represent forecast uncertainty by parameter-perturbed scenarios; (6) use STP minimax regret to make the multi-scenario objective robust; (7) compare robust, deterministic-SEM, and observed-inflow controls, then filter compromise schedules; and (8) test the RRCA contribution by removing its risk objective. This ordering is the extractor's logical reconstruction from the reported dependencies, not a claim about private mental processes.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study first collects six-station rainfall and LSM runoff data, selects 22 floods, and constructs BP/LSTM/ELM forecasts from current and up to 18-hour lagged precipitation. Inputs are Min–Max normalized, event-level four-fold validation is used, and hyperparameters are traversed using NSE as the loss and Adam as optimizer. Base-model predictions are stacked into a linear-regression SEM. For the 2004 48-hour flood, model parameters are artificially transformed to create forecast-error scenarios; low-NSE scenarios are screened and 300 scenarios (100 from each model) are retained. These scenarios feed MOROU, in which NSGA-III optimizes hourly release schedules under the five scenario-risk objectives and constraints. STP converts scenario-specific schedules and objective values into regret and iteratively carries the largest regret. Evaluation branches compare MOROU, MOSEM, and MOOB; a compromise solution is selected with combined AHP/entropy weights, and MOROU-noF5 removes the RRCA risk objective to assess its effect. These are study procedures and simulations, not a demonstrated live control loop.

Reconstructed workflow: data and event partition → lag/input construction → model normalization/training/validation/test → SEM meta-training and forecast production → parameter-perturbed scenario generation and quality screening → deterministic/stochastic operation-model construction → NSGA-III constrained search → STP regret aggregation and Pareto sets → compromise filtering and MOROU/MOSEM/MOOB comparison → RRCA ablation and interpretation. Forecast-model fitting and optimization are repeated across folds, scenarios, and evolutionary iterations; comparison and ablation belong to evaluation rather than recurring operations.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The forecast-scenario screening threshold is inconsistent: p. 11 says scenarios with NSE below 0.75 were eliminated, while p. 13 says below 0.85 were eliminated.
- The paper reports 16 initial training floods and 6 test floods on p. 9, then 12 training, 4 validation, and 6 test floods on p. 10; the event-level relationship is not fully explained.
- Exact BP, LSTM, and ELM architectures, hyperparameter candidate ranges, selected values, random seeds, and stopping/convergence criteria are not reported.
- The exact final forecast feature set is unclear because p. 9 lists antecedent streamflow among possible factors while Eq. (19) and p. 10 emphasize six-station precipitation lags; evaporation is explicitly omitted.
- The normalization function used in RRCA and the numerical integration/discretization implementation are not specified beyond the equations and hourly dispatch.
- The deterministic Objective 1 prose says “minimize the maximum discharge,” whereas the supplied rendering of Eq. (5) shows a sum of squared q(t); the intended mathematical objective is unclear.
- The exact NSGA-III reference-point settings, initialization, randomization, stopping/convergence check, parallel implementation, and reproducibility artifacts are not reported.
- Forecast uncertainty is represented by artificial parameter transformations rather than a calibrated probabilistic error model; the transformation distributions and rationale are not reported.
- Other stated real-time uncertainties (measurement error, flood propagation, decision delay), operator intervention, authorization, fallback, and post-event learning are not modeled/reported.
- The study has one reservoir and one selected large flood for operation analysis; independent deployment or cross-case validation is absent. The data availability statement says the data used are confidential (p. 19).
