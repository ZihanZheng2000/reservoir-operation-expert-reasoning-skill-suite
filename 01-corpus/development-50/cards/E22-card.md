## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper starts from the difficulty of hourly flood operation when inflow forecasts are uncertain. NWP provides meteorological information and longer lead time, while LSTM offers a fast data-driven rainfall–runoff mapping that can propagate the uncertainty in ensemble precipitation into inflow scenarios. The operating decision is multiobjective: lower peak releases reduce downstream damage, but excessive drawdown reduces water-supply/hydropower value, so storage should also be retained. The authors translate these objectives into weighted functions, then constrain the feasible solution with water balance, release/storage bounds, and release-change limits intended to protect downstream channels. Since objective weights are decision-sensitive, they use grid search to evaluate weight combinations and a genetic algorithm to optimize each ensemble scenario. The ensemble-based policy is compared with a deterministic/no-forecast policy and observed operation, while storage ceilings and forecast-model metrics provide checks. The authors interpret ensemble mean improvements as evidence of value but acknowledge that the one-day lead time, idealized/case-specific setup, computational burden, omitted downstream warning system, and unavailable institutional data prevent a universal or fully validated claim.

Reconstructed route: identify inflow-forecast uncertainty and short-term flood-control need; obtain NWP precipitation ensembles and supporting hydro-meteorological data; train an LSTM to forecast ensemble inflows; formulate release/storage objectives and hard constraints; search objective weights and use GA to optimize each ensemble member; compare deterministic and ensemble policies on event-level release/storage outcomes; check forecast skill, storage ceiling, and release fluctuations; aggregate/interpret ensemble results; and state transfer and real-time limitations.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study builds an ensemble-based operation pipeline for Dez Dam. It collects reservoir area-volume-elevation and hourly release/storage data together with hourly precipitation, temperature, potential evapotranspiration, discharge, and bias-corrected ensemble precipitation forecasts from NCEP, KMA, and UKMO. A sequential LSTM with four units and a dense layer is trained using Adam and mean-square error for 100 epochs with batch size 1; parameters are calibrated by trial and error. The model produces one-day-ahead deterministic and 20-member ensemble inflow hydrographs. The optimization model uses a 6-hour water-balance interval, minimizes peak release and a storage-deficit objective, and enforces release, storage, and consecutive-release-change constraints. Grid search selects objective weights; GA then searches policies for each of 20 inflow members and each weight combination, resulting in 400 runs per event and about 260 minutes per event. The ensemble strategy, deterministic/no-forecast strategy, observed operation, and ensemble mean are compared for the 13 April 2016 and 1 April 2019 floods. Forecast skill is assessed with correlation/Taylor-diagram statistics; operation is assessed with release peaks, fluctuations, maximum storage, and the 2698.5 MCM/352 m upper limit. The main result is lower release peaks and higher storage for the ensemble mean, but the data are institutionally restricted and the study does not include a downstream flood-warning system.

Reconstructed workflow: define Dez case and hourly datasets; obtain and bias-correct NWP precipitation; train/tune LSTM and generate deterministic/20-member inflows; formulate objectives and constraints; grid-search weights; run GA per member/weight combination; derive ensemble member and mean policies; compare deterministic, ensemble, observed outcomes for two floods; check forecast and storage/release performance; and record computational, data, warning-system, and transfer limitations.
