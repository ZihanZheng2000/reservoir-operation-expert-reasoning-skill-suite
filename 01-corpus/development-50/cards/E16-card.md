## Paper identification

The paper develops a robustness-testing framework for forecast-informed reservoir operations (FIRO) under forecast uncertainty and out-of-sample extreme floods. It compares EFO with an optimized risk curve, expected-value MPC, and a five-day cumulative guide-curve method, using baseline climatology and perfect 14-day forecast MPC benchmarks. Oroville Reservoir simulations use HEFS hindcasts, 100 synthetic 41-member ensembles, four historical floods, and 100/200/300-year scaled hydrographs to estimate required flood pool and water-supply implications.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors reason that FIRO policies can reduce flood pool requirements but may overfit short hindcasts or fail on scaled/out-of-sample extremes. Robustness therefore must be tested across both alternative forecast sequences and altered event magnitudes. They use a common target-storage objective that penalizes flood-pool exceedance much more than conservation-pool drawdown, then vary this weight to expose the water-supply/flood-safety trade-off. EFO, MPC, and the cumulative method embody different uses of forecast information: EFO learns a risk curve over the record, MPC re-optimizes each day, and the cumulative rule uses a short fixed quantile. The claim stops at modeled Oroville behavior; no deployment or actual future-event validation is claimed.

Reconstructed route: (R1) identify insufficient hindcast coverage and event uncertainty; (R2) define required flood pool relative to TOCS and a weighted exceedance/drawdown objective; (R3) compare policy formulations and benchmarks under common release/ramping constraints; (R4) train EFO under alternate forecast/data splits; (R5) test with HEFS, independent synthetic ensembles, and scaled events; (R6) evaluate refill/water-supply implications and forecast-skill diagnostics; and (R7) interpret differences as policy sensitivity plus forecast-sequence effects, bounded by Oroville-specific assumptions.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study models relative storage with a daily mass balance and minimizes a weighted sum of flood-pool exceedance and conservation-pool drawdown. EFO optimizes a 14-value monotone risk curve by differential evolution; MPC solves a daily expected-value stochastic linear program over 14 release decisions and implements only the first; the cumulative method applies a five-day 70th-percentile forecast rule. Policies are trained and tested under four EFO configurations, then simulated against four historical/scaled events using HEFS and 100 synthetic 41-member ensembles. Outputs include required flood pool, storage/release traces, refill checks, forecast bias/eCRPS, and release variability.

Reconstructed workflow: Case data and scaled events feed policy training/testing. EFO training is the main offline optimization branch; MPC optimization repeats daily online within each simulation; the cumulative rule repeats its five-day calculation without training. Evaluation branches include policy/forecast combinations, 1997 holdout, weight sensitivity, water-year refill, forecast-skill diagnostics, and discussion of release/storage assumptions. No live operational deployment is performed.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The exact Oroville release maximum/ramping values and full TOCS/flood-pool conversion are referenced to external plans but not fully enumerated in the supplied text.
- EFO differential-evolution bounds, mutation/population settings, random seeds, and convergence diagnostics beyond approximately 2,500 evaluations and 10 seeds are not reported.
- MPC horizon details such as terminal treatment, solver tolerances, numerical scaling, and infeasibility handling are not reported.
- The precise annual-max log-normal fitting method and return-period scaling factors are not tabulated in the supplied text.
- Synthetic forecast generation details are delegated to Brodeur et al. (2025); only its stated 100×41-member structure and intended statistical properties are available here.
- Required flood-pool estimates depend on the assumption that maximum release is feasible at all storage levels, although actual Oroville release capacity is elevation dependent.
- The refill test through June 1 omits downstream delivery/environmental requirements and is less applicable outside snowmelt-dominated, high-inflow-to-capacity systems.
- The ability to draft into the conservation pool is physically modeled but may lack institutional authority; the paper does not resolve this deployment condition.
- No downstream flow constraints, interactions with other reservoirs, damage/cost functions, or environmental flows are modeled.
- The paper does not establish a universally optimal drawdown weight, policy, risk definition, or flood-pool factor of safety.
