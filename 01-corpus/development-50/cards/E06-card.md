## Paper identification

The paper studies real-time flood-control operation of the Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR) under multiple sources of uncertainty. It proposes a stochastic simulation and entropy-based risk-analysis framework that represents flood-forecast errors, flood-hydrograph shape, discharge capacity, water-level–storage relations, and wind-wave effects, then routes the resulting inflows through the TGR rule-based operation model. The extraction covers the complete supplied article text through the conclusions; the source is a page-separated text transcription, and some table symbols and row labels are unclear because of OCR loss.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper frames flood-control operation as a failure-risk problem in which uncertain inflows and hydraulic relations randomize operational targets. It treats normal, independent-variable analytical approaches as inadequate for skewed, dependent, multiple uncertainties, and therefore assigns different simulation mechanisms according to the structure of each uncertainty: copulas for dependence/skewness and LHS for variables treated as independent. It then uses reservoir routing to propagate those uncertainties to the highest water level and downstream discharge, first measures threshold exceedance by frequency and then uses Shannon entropy to quantify the amount and composition of risk. The interpretation stops at the TGR simulation evidence: hydrograph shape is reported as the main contributor, uncertainty effects can partially offset one another, and the tested system conforms to the stated design criteria at the evaluated levels.

Reconstructed route: (1) identify multiple uncertainty sources and operational failure indicators; (2) match a dependence-aware copula simulation to forecast errors and daily hydrograph generation, while using LHS for the remaining variables treated as independent; (3) fit or select distributions using the reported criteria; (4) propagate sampled uncertainties through the existing TGR pre-release and rule-based routing; (5) compare threshold exceedance and entropy results; and (6) interpret contribution and design-criterion results within the TGR case.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study begins with TGR geometry/operating context and daily flows from 1878–2015. It estimates distributions for three-day forecast errors, simulates conditional daily hydrographs, and samples four additional uncertainty variables with LHS. Each synthetic forecast/inflow realization is routed with the TGR pre-release and rule-based operation model using the discrete continuity/storage relation. The resulting highest water levels (and, in the general formulation, downstream peak discharges) feed two evaluation branches: threshold exceedance probabilities and entropy-based risk by component and in combination. Statistical comparisons of observed and simulated characteristics, fitted-distribution plots, and uncertainty histograms serve as checks before interpreting the risk tables and curves.

Reconstructed workflow: (1) configure the TGR case and operating rules; (2) fit forecast-error distributions and the three-dimensional Student-t copula; (3) generate forecast-error vectors; (4) generate synthetic daily hydrographs recursively; (5) fit/sample discharge, storage, backwater, and wave uncertainties; (6) apply LHS; (7) route each flood with the pre-release/rule model; (8) calculate traditional exceedance risks; (9) bin outcomes and calculate component/system entropy; and (10) compare uncertainty contributions and design criteria. Steps 2–5 are model setup/simulation, step 7 is the repeated operational simulation, and steps 8–10 are evaluation branches rather than recurring field actions.
