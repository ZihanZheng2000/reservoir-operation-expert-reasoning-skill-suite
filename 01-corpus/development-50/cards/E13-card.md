## Paper identification

The paper studies whether statistically generated synthetic hydrologic ensemble forecasts can provide a broader and more demanding test of forecast-informed reservoir operations (FIRO). The case is the Russian River–Lake Mendocino system, where the Ensemble Forecast Operations (EFO) policy uses 1–14-day, 61-member HEFS forecasts to set flood-control releases. The supplied text identifies the authors, venue, year, DOI, and article pagination above. The text is readable throughout the substantive sections; no page or character-loss issue was detected.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper frames FIRO as valuable but potentially overfit to short hindcasts and their single forecast realization. Because extreme-event decisions are sensitive to both forecast central tendency and uncertainty, the authors reason that synthetic ensembles should preserve the multivariate error structure, including heteroscedasticity, non-Gaussian tails, correlations across lead times/sites/members, and conditional bias. They compare direct hydrologic synthesis with synthesis in the meteorological forcing space, then judge adequacy through ensemble verification and operational behavior. Their claim stops at case-bounded robustness analysis: synthetic forecasts are useful for exposing possible EFO failure modes, but they do not fully reproduce every HEFS extreme-event pattern and are not deployment validation.

Reconstructed route: The logical route is (R1) identify hindcast scarcity and overfitting/extrapolation risk; (R2) require synthetic samples to emulate the forecast ensemble properties that affect EFO; (R3) choose two generation spaces with different computational and process-model trade-offs; (R4) modify the prior single-trace multivariate procedure for conditional bias, heteroscedasticity, and member dependence; (R5) test parity and event behavior before using samples operationally; (R6) interpret EFO spill and drawdown differences as a mixture of generator limitations and policy sensitivity; and (R7) use pre-hindcast observations only for exploratory extension under the stated stationarity assumptions.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The workflow first represents forecast errors over multiple lead times, sites, variables, and ensemble members, then transforms and resamples those errors before reconstructing synthetic forecasts. In syn-HEFS, the transformed error process is applied directly to HEFS ensemble output. In syn-GEFS, synthetic meteorological mean forecasts are produced and passed through HEFS. The resulting M ensembles are checked against HEFS statistically and around major events, then fed through the EFO water-balance and release rules to assess storage, downstream flow, drawdown, and spills. A separate pre-hindcast branch uses observations from 1948–1984 with syn-HEFS and compares the resulting operations with PFO.

Reconstructed workflow: Data assembly and EFO setup feed two generator branches. Model fitting consists of error construction, normalization, dependence removal, distribution fitting, and copula/kNN resampling. Forecast reconstruction reverses the transforms. Evaluation branches include aggregate verification, event-specific forecast comparison, hindcast operational validation, and pre-hindcast forecast extension. Within EFO, daily storage and releases repeat through time; synthetic samples create repeated operational traces. The paper reports no live operational deployment.

| E3. Construct multivariate forecast-error records | Form error series for each forecast dimension before stochastic modeling. | **Trigger:** Fit a synthetic model. **Inputs:** Observations O and forecasts F across lead times, sites, variables, and members. **Procedure:** For single-trace components use e = O − F; for ensemble members use the conditional-expectation error in Eq. (4), (e_{t,k,e}=E[F_{t,k,e}|O_t]-F_{t,k,e}). **Output:** Error vectors indexed by time, dimension, and member. | pp. 5–6 and 8–9, Eq. (4). The sign convention in the ensemble expression is as printed; implementation details for conditional expectation are described in E4. |
| E4. Model conditional bias and heteroscedasticity | Stabilize extreme-event representation by estimating conditional expectation and globally modeling error scale. | **Trigger:** Ensemble forecasts show Type-II conditional bias and member noise. **Inputs:** HEFS forecasts/medians, observations, absolute member errors across E members. **Procedure:** Fit a separate LOESS conditional expectation for each of K lead/site/variable dimensions using the HEFS ensemble median; aggregate absolute raw errors across members to an ensemble mean; fit (sigma_{t,k}=gamma_{0,k}+gamma_{1,k}E[F_{t,k,e}|O_t]) by constrained OLS with intercept and slope > 0 and an intercept floor based on the lowest data decile; standardize errors. **Output:** Conditional mean, scale model, and standardized errors. | pp. 6–9, Eqs. | E5. Remove dependence and fit residual distributions | Transform standardized errors into approximately independent draws while retaining a distribution for resampling. | **Trigger:** Standardized errors retain serial/cross-dimensional dependence. **Inputs:** Standardized K-dimensional errors. **Procedure:** Fit a LASSO-penalized VAR with maximum lag 3 using BigVAR to remove auto- and cross-correlation; fit the residuals to four-parameter skew generalized error distributions (SGED) for mean, standard deviation, skew, and kurtosis. **Output:** VAR parameters and per-dimension SGED distributions. | pp. 6–8. Model diagnostics and selection beyond the stated maximum lag are not reported. |
| E6. Reimpose dependence by Schaake shuffle and kNN | Generate synthetic residual vectors conditional on an observed state. | **Trigger:** Create a forecast at simulation time (t_s) with observations available. **Inputs:** Fitted SGEDs, hindcast residual ranks, hindcast observations, simulation observations or criterion (Z_{t_s}). **Procedure:** Independently sample SGED deviates for the hindcast-length record; reorder them with a Schaake Shuffle to restore cross-dimension and observation correlations; find k nearest hindcast observations to the simulation observation, randomly select one, and attach its shuffled residual vector. For ensembles, use the same kNN-sampled observation across all members; use cumulative 14-day LAMC inflow as (Z_t) in syn-HEFS. **Output:** Synthetic standardized residuals associated with each simulation time and member. | pp. 8–9. The value of k and distance metric are not reported in the supplied text; (Z_t) can be replaced by another plausible criterion in the general method. |
| E7. Reconstruct syn-HEFS forecasts | Reverse the transformations to make direct synthetic HEFS ensembles. | **Trigger:** Synthetic residuals are assigned to a simulation time. **Inputs:** Assigned residuals, fitted VAR, normalization/conditional-expectation models, observations. **Procedure:** Simulate through the fitted VAR to recorrelate deviates, destandardize with the normalization model, subtract the generated raw error from the conditional expectation to recover forecast values, and repeat to create M ensembles of E members (case settings M = 100, E = 61). **Output:** Direct synthetic hydrologic forecast ensembles for periods with hydrologic observations. | pp. 8–10. Computational case result: 100 63-year samples within 24 h on the stated cluster; stability and extreme-event deficiencies are evaluated later. |
| E8. Reconstruct syn-GEFS and force HEFS | Make synthetic meteorological forecasts, then use the existing HEFS model to obtain hydrologic ensembles. | **Trigger:** Use forcing-space synthesis or test forecast-skill manipulations. **Inputs:** GEFS ensemble-mean TMAX/TMIN/PRECIP, gauge MAT/MAP, precipitation observations at three sites for t and t−1. **Procedure:** Apply monthly bias correction to TMAX/TMIN; use conditional mean treatment for precipitation; form a 24-value six-hour precipitation observation vector and reduce it to three principal components for kNN; generate M GEFS mean traces and run each through HEFS. **Output:** M hydrologic ensembles with E members. | pp. 9–10. Single-sample generation took 2–3 days in this implementation; exact PCA fitting and HEFS operational-mode settings are not reported. |
| E9. Verify synthetic forecast ensembles | Compare synthetic forecast properties with the actual HEFS hindcast. | **Trigger:** Assess whether generated ensembles mimic the target system. **Inputs:** HEFS and synthetic ensembles, selected leads (1, 3, 5, 10 days), wet-season observations, flow strata. **Procedure:** Compare BSE spread/error relationships, cumulative rank histograms, and eCRPS distributions; run DTS two-sample tests at p = 0.1 for rank histograms and pooled eCRPS as specified. Stratification can obscure absolute skill, which the authors accept because comparison is the aim; DTS non-rejection means statistical indistinguishability under the test, not proof of identity. |
| E10. Validate rare-event forecast attributes | Test whether synthetic samples cover HEFS behavior for major inflows. | **Trigger:** Aggregate parity is insufficient for flood-risk decisions. **Inputs:** Ten largest hindcast extreme events, 100 syn-HEFS samples (and smaller syn-GEFS sample), cumulative forecasts at selected leads, observed cumulative flow. **Procedure:** Compare distributions/ratios of synthetic versus HEFS cumulative ensemble means and variances; inspect whether HEFS values lie within synthetic sample ranges. **Output:** Event-specific coverage and identified bias, especially variance underprediction at longer leads. | pp. 14–15, Figure 7 and supporting figures. ## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The supplied text does not report the exact kNN value (k), distance metric, tie handling, or implementation details for nearest-neighbor sampling.
- LOESS spans/weights, all regression diagnostics, VAR regularization/selection details beyond maximum lag 3, and SGED fitting diagnostics are not reported here.
- Exact missing-data handling, time alignment details beyond the stated daily aggregation, and reproducibility settings/random seeds are not reported.
- The full EFO parameterization, water-balance terms, and maximum-release details are delegated to Text S1 and external/cited model materials; only the main thresholds and logic are in the supplied text.
- The pre-hindcast extension has no actual HEFS hindcast comparator and depends on transfer of forecast-error relationships; its forecast realism is therefore unresolved outside the reported verification checks.
- Synthetic forecasts do not reliably reproduce HEFS large overforecasts at low observed flows before extremes, and syn-HEFS tends toward lower cumulative variance at longer leads (pp. 18–20).
- The paper does not resolve how much of each synthetic spill is generator bias versus plausible EFO vulnerability; it presents qualified evidence for both explanations.
- Nonstationary climate-change operation is proposed as future work and was not executed.
