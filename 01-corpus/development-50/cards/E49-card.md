## Paper identification

The paper develops and tests a robust multiobjective operation and risk decision-making framework for real-time flood control under uncertain inflow forecasts. It directly supports reservoir operation adjustment by generating stochastic inflow scenarios, optimizing upstream/downstream risk probability, surrogate loss, and inundation duration, and selecting a compromise policy for Xianghongdian reservoir. ## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors treat forecast uncertainty as the primary risk source in real-time operation and argue that deterministic, SP, or CCP formulations may control only part of the risk profile. Because flood damage is difficult to calculate in real time, they use three surrogate dimensions: probability of crossing safety thresholds, CVaR of adverse loss, and maximum sequential duration above thresholds. They select robust multiobjective optimization so that upstream/downstream risk probability, vulnerability, and resilience are controlled simultaneously rather than collapsed into a single objective. The resulting noninferior set is then passed to TOPSIS because no solution simultaneously minimizes all competing criteria and decision makers need a compromise reflecting preferences. The paper further reasons that event magnitude affects which objectives matter most: small events emphasize avoiding risk, medium events require probability–loss trade-offs, and very large events make loss/duration mitigation more important. The claims stop at model-based surrogate performance under the Xianghongdian case and historical forecast-error representation.

Reconstructed route: identify forecast uncertainty as dominant risk → represent uncertainty with temporally dependent scenarios → choose probability, CVaR loss, and duration as complementary risk controls → formulate constrained six-objective RMOO and a CCP comparator → generate noninferior policy solutions → select a compromise using TOPSIS preferences → inspect release/storage trajectories and risk-hedging mechanism → test event-scale behavior and state model/data limits.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study first defines forecast error from reforecast and observed inflows, fits a marginal distribution at each lead time, and uses a t-copula plus Monte Carlo simulation to generate 200 stochastic 45-h inflow scenarios for Xianghongdian. These scenarios are fed into a reservoir operation model with six RMOO objectives and mass-balance, storage, release, fluctuation, initial, and terminal constraints. AR-MOEA searches for noninferior policies; a two-objective CCP with the same constraints is run for comparison. Objective ranges, hypervolume, and compromise solutions are then analyzed. TOPSIS with equal objective preference selects RMOO solution R and CCP solution C, whose trajectories and adverse-scenario CVaR are compared. Additional event-scale experiments and Appendix A distribution checks form evaluation branches, not recurring operational actions.

Reconstructed workflow: collect reforecast/observed errors and Xianghongdian point forecasts → fit marginal distributions and t-copula → simulate error and inflow scenarios → calculate six risk objectives under constrained reservoir operation → solve RMOO with AR-MOEA and CCP comparator → compare objective ranges/hypervolume → use TOPSIS to select equal-weight compromises → inspect trajectories and 10% adverse scenarios → repeat interpretation for 100-year/5-year event scales → document limitations.
