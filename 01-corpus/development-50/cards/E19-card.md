The abstract states that conventional precipitation-streamflow FIRO rarely uses PWV, which can give an earlier precipitation signal. The proposed VPS-FIRO derives potential precipitation (PP) from atmospheric water balance, converts it to a vapor-controlled upper bound of flood limited water levels (VCWL), and uses it for prerelease; the Jiaokou case reports a 24-hour earlier prerelease than PS-FIRO, 5.6% less mean spilled water, and a reduction from 1% to 0.3% in duration exceeding maximum prerelease outflow (p. 1, Abstract).

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper starts from a fixed-FLWL dilemma: a static flood-season level safeguards storage capacity but can spill usable water and reduce later supply. Forecast-informed approaches can temporarily raise the level or adjust releases, but PS-FIRO is limited by the precipitation/streamflow forecast horizon. The authors reason that PWV is a physically prior signal because vapor supplies precipitation, and that vapor convergence often precedes precipitation. They therefore derive PP as a conservative upper bound on precipitation from initial basin PWV and inward vapor flux, rather than trying to predict the exact precipitation amount. Because PP is physically meaningful but lacks an operational threshold, they convert it into VCWL using a runoff transformation and the reservoir's prerelease sequence. The resulting three-line defense uses vapor to guide early level adjustment, precipitation to intensify prerelease when an event is within the precipitation horizon, and streamflow to complete prerelease/regulate the flood. They compare this scheme with a PS-FIRO rule held otherwise identical and with historical operation, while acknowledging that the historical comparator has different initial levels and that the scheme is rule-based, idealized, and dependent on forecast accuracy. The claim stops at demonstrated feasibility and benefit in the Jiaokou case.

Reconstructed route: Identify the fixed-level trade-off; use the physical precedence of vapor as a new early indicator; construct a worst-case PP upper bound from atmospheric water balance; make PP operational by converting it into VCWL and a specified outflow/storage sequence; retain precipitation and streamflow for later defense lines; select a common upper FLWL through capacity-constrained prerelease; test the mechanism in representative floods and then across 50 historical floods; validate key data/model components; and interpret gains with explicit caveats about idealized forecasts, rule parameters, and site dependence.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors use a case-study pipeline at Jiaokou Reservoir. They assemble ERA5 hourly PWV/IVT at 0.25° resolution, observed precipitation and streamflow, and reservoir inflow/outflow/level data for 2011–2023. To avoid interference from forecast error when comparing schemes, observed precipitation and streamflow are treated as perfect 48-hour forecasts, while ERA5 reanalysis vapor is treated as a perfect 72-hour forecast. The atmospheric-water-balance calculation transforms PWV and inward vapor flux into PP; PP is converted into floodwater using a runoff coefficient of 0.75 or, in Appendix A, the Xinanjiang model; and Eq. (9) converts that volume into VCWL under a specified prerelease sequence. The operation branch uses vapor to adjust the level before the precipitation horizon, precipitation to increase prerelease, and streamflow to draw down to FLWL and regulate the flood. The evaluation branch compares VPS-FIRO, PS-FIRO, and historical operation using level/storage, spills, peaks, and duration indexes, first in two typhoon events and then in 50 floods. A component-check branch validates the Xinanjiang model and ERA5 PWV against radiosondes. This is a rule-based simulation study; the article does not present a real-time forecast-error experiment or a permanently deployed control implementation.

Reconstructed workflow: define the Jiaokou operating context and data; set perfect-forecast horizons and common FIRO upper bound; calculate PP over the vapor horizon; convert PP to VCWL and specify the VPS sequence; run VPS-FIRO, PS-FIRO, and historical scenarios; inspect representative events; summarize 50-event distributions and means; validate alternate hydrologic and vapor-data components; and interpret benefit/safety results with limitations.
