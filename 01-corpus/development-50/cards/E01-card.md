## Paper identification

The paper studies dynamic control of a reservoir flood limited water level (FLWL) during the flood season, using inflow forecasts and explicit flood-control risk constraints. It develops and applies the method to China’s Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR). ## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper starts from a conflict between the conservative fixed FLWL and conservation benefits during the flood season. A fixed level at 145 m reserves storage for rare large floods but ignores real-time rainfall and inflow forecasts and can leave water unused when the relevant flood threat is lower or delayed. The authors therefore reason that the FLWL should be a forecast-informed interval, with a current FLWL as the lower bound and a calculated upper bound. Because forecast errors and the future flood hydrograph are uncertain, the upper bound is accepted only when the combined risk of alternative operating decisions does not exceed the risk of the current rule. The paper then uses TGR-specific release and downstream-stage constraints to test whether the resulting bound can improve floodwater use without increasing modeled flood risk.

Reconstructed route: (1) identify the fixed-FLWL trade-off; (2) identify inflow forecast error and hydrograph-shape uncertainty as decision-relevant risks; (3) define pre-release, normal flood-control, and refill alternatives from current and forecast inflows; (4) decompose their risks and constrain total risk against the baseline; (5) represent forecast error probabilistically and estimate the admissible upper water level with Monte Carlo simulation; (6) verify forecast performance and enforce TGR downstream safety constraints; and (7) compare the resulting 1-day and 2-day schemes with the current operation rule.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The paper first specifies the TGR baseline, flood-season constraints, forecast inputs, and a validated inflow-forecasting model. It then creates forecast-error realizations, applies the decision modules to candidate levels, calculates the component risks, and searches for the highest candidate upper bound satisfying the baseline-risk constraint. Finally, it simulates 1-day and 2-day lead-time schemes with and without forecast uncertainty and compares spill volume, floodwater utilization, and annual hydropower generation with the current rule. The main operational logic is a proposed module-level rule; the bound estimation and performance comparison are planning/simulation activities rather than a reported field deployment.

Reconstructed workflow: (1) assemble TGR inflow/rainfall and reservoir/downstream specifications; (2) calibrate and validate the MLSM forecast model; (3) derive the forecast-error distribution and synthetic series; (4) define the dynamic-control modules and TGR thresholds; (5) run risk-constrained Monte Carlo bound estimation; (6) apply the candidate bounds in simulated pre-release, normal-control, and refill sequences; and (7) compare outputs against the current operation rule.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The supplied text does not provide the full underlying observed-data files, the exact construction of the N-year forecast series used in Monte Carlo simulation, or the random-seed/reproducibility procedure.
- The paper states that uncertainty in flood hydrograph shape is represented through (P(Z)) and routed risk (R(Z,P)), but it does not fully report the empirical distribution, routing model implementation, or all parameter values needed to reproduce those calculations.
- The complete time-stepped real-time controller, release scheduling within the effective lead time, and administrative authorization procedure are not reported; the authors identify real-time implementation as future work.
- The paper does not report confidence intervals for the converged dynamic bound, a formal sensitivity analysis for the 200 simulations or the 0.05 m convergence threshold, or independent validation of the dynamic policy itself.
- The normal relative-error assumption is supported by cited guidance and prior analysis but is not independently re-estimated for every forecast regime in the supplied case description. Dependence between successive errors is not discussed.
- The lower bound is taken as the current 145 m FLWL and the downstream thresholds are TGR-specific. The text does not establish how these quantities would be recalibrated for another reservoir or a changing downstream protection standard.
- The source text contains OCR/encoding artifacts in symbols and some units; the extraction normalizes them where the surrounding equations and tables make the meaning clear. No image content was analyzed.
