## Paper identification

The paper proposes a many-objective hierarchical pre-release flood operation rule (HPFOR) for the Xiluodu–Xiangjiaba–Three Gorges cascade in the upper Yangtze. It combines DGDNN multi-step inflow forecasts with HMR probabilistic uncertainty, uses a two-stage rolling decision/forecast model to derive forecast-risk-aware bounds, and optimizes multiple flow thresholds and levels with a constrained region search evolutionary algorithm (RSEA). The topic relation is direct: future forecast scenarios trigger pre-fill, pre-release, or normal flood-control actions while balancing flood safety, power generation, upstream storage use, and navigation.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors argue that conventional operation graphs and fixed flood-control rules do not exploit future inflow forecasts or their uncertainty. They retain a rolling two-stage view: current-period release is chosen now, while a forecast-horizon constraint must keep end storage at the flood limit with an acceptable probability of downstream release safety. The proposed HPFOR uses nested flow levels and thresholds so low forecast inflow permits pre-fill, forecasted higher inflow triggers tiered pre-release, and current inflow above the maximum release invokes normal flood control. Because cascade operation has competing generation, upstream storage, downstream safety, and navigation goals, the rule parameters are treated as a many-objective optimization problem. The claim stops at simulated historical performance and algorithm comparison.

Reconstructed route: (R1) identify forecast ignorance and uncertainty as weaknesses of fixed rules; (R2) obtain multi-step probabilistic inflow distributions; (R3) derive risk-aware current release/storage inequalities from the two-stage model; (R4) encode hierarchical flow levels and thresholds as rule parameters; (R5) choose five objectives and hard/flexible constraints; (R6) optimize parameters with RSEA and compare it with other evolutionary algorithms; (R7) inspect Pareto solutions and representative operating traces against the conventional rule and HFOR; and (R8) interpret generation gains only under the modeled safety constraints, acknowledging limited event diversity and omitted uncertainties.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors first obtain conditional probabilistic inflow forecasts from DGDNN/HMR. At each rolling decision, the two-stage equations use current inflow/storage and forecast-horizon distributions to enforce a flood-limit end-storage condition and release-risk threshold. HPFOR then maps forecast/current flows to tiered pre-fill, pre-release, or normal flood-control actions. The flow levels, thresholds, and upstream retention flows are encoded as a 15-element decision vector and optimized against five objectives with RSEA under cascade mass-balance and operating constraints. Historical daily flood seasons are simulated, algorithms are compared by hypervolume, and representative rule traces are checked against conventional/HFOR behavior.

Reconstructed workflow: Forecast generation feeds the rule’s two-stage constraints and scenario evaluation. Optimization is a parameter-search branch; operational simulation is the repeated daily rollout branch. Algorithm benchmarking, Table 4 Pareto summaries, representative years, and conventional/HFOR comparisons are evaluation branches rather than additional operational rules. The supplied text reports no live rolling-horizon deployment.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- DGDNN architecture/training, HMR state count and fitted parameters, forecast calibration, and probabilistic forecast skill are delegated to prior work or not reported here.
- The risk rate threshold (r_a), its operational selection process, and whether it varies across reservoirs/events are not specified.
- Full HPFOR level/threshold bounds, optimized values for all 85 schemes, and the rule’s exact generalized branch algorithm are not fully tabulated in the supplied text.
- Detailed reservoir routing, turbine/head relationships, navigation-rate function, time delays, and data preprocessing are incompletely specified.
- RSEA region construction, crossover/reproduction details, repair implementation, and flexible-constraint violation scaling are not given beyond Algorithm 1 and parameter table.
- The printed upstream objective in Eq. (20) appears to combine “max F4” with a “min” expression; exact objective direction should be checked in the original typeset source before implementation.
- The study uses observed weather conditions as alternatives rather than operational meteorological forecasts, which may overestimate forecast quality and alter forecast-error distributions (p. 18).
- Only one cascade and a limited 41-year record are used; more return periods, extreme events, correlated multi-reservoir inflow uncertainty, and nonstationary conditions are unresolved.
- Damage/cost functions and uncertainties in initial/final levels and operation parameters are omitted; data are available only on request because of confidentiality.
- “Flood-control safety” is demonstrated through model constraints and reported simulated maxima, not through independent observed consequences or field authorization.
