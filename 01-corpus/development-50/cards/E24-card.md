## Paper identification

The paper develops analytical flood-operation hedging rules for a two-stage reservoir/levee system under uncertain upstream and intermediate flows. It treats expected flood-safety margin (EFSM)—the gap between expected total flood flow and downstream conveyance capacity—as a scarce resource allocated between the current and future stages to minimize expected levee-failure damage. Abstract confirmation: the abstract states three optimal allocation cases (large, medium, and small expected floods), with current-stage concentration for large floods, balanced margins for medium floods, and greater future allocation for small floods. The paper is a conceptual hydroeconomic derivation illustrated with a hypothetical Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR) case, not a deployed controller.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: Flood conveyance and storage capacity are scarce resources. A higher current release consumes current conveyance margin but preserves reservoir capacity for later inflow; retaining water protects the current stage but can increase future risk. The authors collapse a flood event into current and future stages, define EFSM as the available gap between expected flow and conveyance, and model levee-failure damage as the exceedance probability multiplied by catastrophic damage. Under Gaussian errors, additional EFSM has diminishing marginal damage reduction. Therefore, the optimal allocation equalizes marginal utility between stages when neither nonnegativity bound binds, with boundary solutions when total EFSM is scarce. Forecast uncertainty increases with lead time, producing thresholds that classify high-, medium-, and low-risk operations. The claim stops at the simplified two-stage model and hypothetical case.

Reconstructed route: define reservoir and downstream flood balances → combine upstream and intermediate forecast errors into total downstream uncertainty → map conveyance gap to EFSM and expected damage → establish convex/diminishing marginal value → allocate total EFSM using KKT conditions → derive uncertainty-dependent thresholds and three operating cases → convert EFSM to downstream flow targets, reservoir releases, and carried-over storage → impose storage-capacity boundaries → demonstrate with TGR parameters and compare hedging with no hedging.

The KKT derivation and hypothetical calculation are model setup/evaluation branches rather than repeated field deployment.
