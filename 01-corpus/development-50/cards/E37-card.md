## Paper identification

The paper compares conventional operation (CO) with forecast-based operation (FBO) for flood regulation at Ankang Reservoir. It identifies forecasted net rainfall as the FBO decision indicator, fits a normal distribution to forecast-error samples using a chi-square hypothesis test, simulates CO and FBO under multiple return-period floods with 10,000 Monte Carlo runs, and proposes conceptual single-step and multi-step discharge remedies for significant forecast bias. The reported risk metric combines the frequency of a simulated threshold event with the occurrence probability of the tested flood return period.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors begin by separating CO and FBO risk sources. Both modes share uncertainty in initial level, storage/level relation, discharge, operation, and decision-maker behavior; FBO adds forecast-factor error, while CO still has imperfect measured/observed inflow. They identify hydrological/forecast uncertainty as the dominant tractable risk source. Because FBO rules use forecasted net rainfall rather than only current level/inflow, the forecast error must be statistically characterized before risk can be assessed. A chi-square test supports a normal error model for the Ankang sample, enabling Monte Carlo perturbations. The authors then compare simulated exceedances under CO and FBO using the same initial level and permissible thresholds. They reason that FBO can lower risk by pre-releasing or storing based on forecast information, but that a significant negative or positive forecast bias requires discharge adjustment. The proposed response is conditional on error direction: increase discharge for false-negative/underforecast conditions and reduce or otherwise adjust for false-positive conditions, with multi-step correction preferred when an instantaneous jump would be unsafe. Claims are bounded by the case rules, simulated return-period floods, and conceptual response diagram.

Reconstructed route: (1) enumerate common and mode-specific uncertainties; (2) prioritize forecast/net-rainfall uncertainty; (3) test and parameterize its distribution; (4) formulate rising-stage FBO rules and retain CO as comparator; (5) Monte Carlo-route perturbed floods and count threshold events; (6) scale event frequency by flood occurrence probability to obtain risk rate; (7) classify forecast bias and adjust discharge, preferably over multiple steps when needed.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study lists shared CO/FBO uncertainties, then analyzes 149 forecasted-net-rainfall versus actual-runoff-depth samples from more than ten historical flood processes. It fits (X\sim N(0.019,0.188^2)) after a five-bin chi-square test. It derives error quantiles for frequencies 0.01–20%. For CO, it uses an observed-inflow error model (Y\sim N(189.32,775.81^2)) as rendered, perturbs typical flood hydrographs, applies CO rules from initial level 325 m, computes actual storage and level by water balance, and counts level/release exceedances. For FBO, it perturbs the net-rainfall series with the fitted forecast-error distribution, determines releases from Table 2, but computes storage and level from actual inflow; 10,000 simulations are run for 10-, 20-, 100-, 1000-, and 10,000-year floods. Finally, the paper sketches single-step and multi-step correction after forecast bias, using a negative-error example at hour 63.

Reconstructed workflow: The main evaluation loop is estimate error distribution → generate random error series → construct forecast/perturbed input → apply CO or FBO rule → route actual inflow and release → test thresholds → tally frequency and risk. Remedy exploration is a separate conceptual branch after risk comparison.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The exact formula connecting simulation frequency (P), return-period occurrence probability, and risk rate Ψ is not clearly printed, although Table 4/5 values reveal the scaling.
- The CO error distribution is rendered as (Y\sim N(189.32,775.81^2)) in the readable interpretation but its parameter convention is not clarified; the forecast-error distribution is similarly affected by OCR notation.
- The FBO rule contains (Q_c) in Table 2 without a definition in the supplied text.
- The FBO rules are limited to flood rising stages; falling-stage, multi-wave, and non-rising event handling is not reported.
- The 149 forecast-error points come from more than ten historical flood processes, but record selection, station aggregation, and temporal dependence treatment are not reported.
- Monte Carlo random seeds, convergence/error bounds, and uncertainty in the fitted error parameters are not reported.
- The conceptual remedies do not specify quantitative detection thresholds, allowable release changes, corrected-forecast assimilation, or a validated control algorithm.
- No operator authorization, emergency communication, fallback hierarchy, post-event review, or live deployment evidence is reported.
- Hydraulic, storage-curve, discharge, initial-level, and decision-maker uncertainties are identified but not jointly propagated in the quantitative risk model.
