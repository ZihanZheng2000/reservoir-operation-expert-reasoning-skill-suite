## Paper identification

The paper derives hedging rules for conserving flood water after a flood recedes while managing both upstream/downstream flood risk and future water-shortage risk. It divides operation into a pre-refill (current) phase and pre-release (future) phase, models unbiased normal inflow forecast errors, imposes a downstream flood-risk chance constraint, and minimizes weighted upstream flood plus water-shortage risks. Abstract confirmation: the abstract reports quasi-linear analytical hedging rules, marginal upstream-flood/marginal-shortage risk balancing, three expected-availability zones, and Xianghongdian tests that outperform capacity-constrained pre-release rules without increasing modeled flood risk. Analytical KKT results produce a three-zone release rule. The Xianghongdian Reservoir case compares the rule with flood-control rules (FRs) and capacity-constrained pre-release rules (CRs), including rolling-horizon simulations for 2015–2016.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: Flood-season operation can waste water after inflows recede, while conserving too much raises upstream flood risk if the refill-phase inflow forecast is wrong and raises downstream risk if future inflows are underestimated. Existing capacity-constrained rules use forecast quantities but do not explicitly trade these risks. The authors therefore use expected water availability A (initial storage plus forecast pre-refill inflow), a two-phase balance, and forecast-error distributions to make conservation a risk decision. They impose a downstream flood-risk probability limit and minimize weighted upstream flood and water-shortage risks. Conservation storage V1 increases upstream risk but reduces future shortage risk; an interior optimum occurs where weighted marginal risks balance. KKT bounds create three zones: store more when shortage risk dominates, use the interior HR when risks balance, and release more when flood risk dominates. The evidence is a model derivation and Xianghongdian case/simulation; it does not establish universal operational superiority.

Reconstructed route: identify flood-water conservation conflict → represent forecast error propagation across current and future phases → define upstream flood, downstream flood, and shortage risk → impose water-balance, physical, and chance constraints → reduce to V1 optimization → derive marginal-risk/KKT zones and analytical rule → calibrate/instantiate parameters and damage-weight ratio → compare FR/CR/HR in event cases and rolling years → vary forecast precision and inspect rule sensitivity.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: Prepare reservoir characteristics, current and forecast inflows/demand, forecast-error variances, risk weights/damage information, storage/release bounds, and acceptable downstream risk. Compute expected availability and phase water balances; use the normal error model to evaluate upstream flood and future shortage probabilities and the downstream chance constraint. Transform the constrained model to V1 and calculate the KKT/analytical HR. During simulation, apply the rule at a 1-hour time interval within a rolling horizon, allocating current-hour release in proportion to forecast current-hour inflow over the phase forecast. Compare HRs with FRs and CRs using water delivery, shortage, spillage, maximum storage/outflow, and ending-storage indices. The case study and forecast-precision experiments are evaluation branches around the rule.

Reconstructed workflow: prepare real-time observations/forecasts and parameters → compute phase volumes and expected availability → establish feasible V1 and risk functions → solve V1* / thresholds → apply zone-specific R1 and implied R2 → update on rolling horizon → record flood/shortage/conservation indicators → compare rules and vary σɛ1/σɛ2.
