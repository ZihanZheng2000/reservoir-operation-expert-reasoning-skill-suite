## Paper identification

The paper studies anticipatory short-term operation of the multi-purpose Três Marias hydropower reservoir to mitigate downstream flooding at Pirapora, while retaining hydropower and operating constraints. It combines MGB-IPH hydrological forecasts, ECMWF deterministic and ensemble precipitation forecasts, scenario-tree reduction, and multi-stage stochastic model predictive control (MPC). Abstract confirmation: the abstract presents a 15-day forecast/MGB framework, precipitation-derived scenario-tree reduction, and multi-stage stochastic optimization, and reports earlier anticipation, more robust decisions, and chance-constraint capability relative to deterministic methods. The supplied text covers the complete article narrative and appendices; no figures were independently analyzed beyond their textual captions/descriptions.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper starts from a conflict between proactive MPC and uncertain forecasts: deterministic MPC can lower storage for an expected flood that may not occur, compromising robustness. Because meteorological uncertainty dominates the available forecast uncertainty and ensemble forecasts have better skill/lead time in this basin, the authors represent possible inflows as a scenario tree. A common control is imposed while future outcomes are unresolved; controls branch as precipitation and hence uncertainty resolve. The resulting stochastic optimization balances spill, elevation, downstream flow behavior, and constraints across scenarios. The authors’ claim stops at conceptual and case-event advantages; incomplete model uncertainty and the small event sample remain limitations.

Reconstructed route: (1) characterize the operational flood-control constraints and the need for short-term anticipation; (2) assess whether probabilistic forcing provides useful additional lead time and skill; (3) choose a scenario-tree representation whose branching follows resolution of precipitation uncertainty and whose reduction preserves probability/distribution quality; (4) choose a computationally adequate downstream routing model; (5) formulate multi-stage stochastic MPC with shared early controls, scenario-specific later controls, probability-weighted objectives, and hard (or optional chance) constraints; (6) compare resulting trajectories with deterministic and perfect-forecast optimizations and interpret robustness within the case boundary.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The workflow ingests real-time gauge data and ECMWF precipitation forecasts, spatially downscales and temporally disaggregates the 50-member ensemble, and runs MGB-IPH with data assimilation to produce reservoir inflow and downstream lateral forecasts. The ensemble is reduced into a precipitation-aware scenario tree with averaged branches, probabilities, branch smoothing, and a nodal partition controlling when decisions can diverge. An internal integrator-delay routing model predicts Pirapora flow during multi-stage stochastic MPC. The first control action is applied in a receding-horizon manner, although the article’s detailed repeated online implementation is described at framework level. Forecast verification, routing-model comparison, and tree-quality checks are evaluation branches; the representative flood-event optimization is the operational demonstration.

Reconstructed workflow: acquire/assimilate observations and forecast rainfall → run MGB-IPH → obtain ensemble inflow/lateral disturbances → reduce ensemble to scenario tree → route reservoir releases to Pirapora inside MPC → solve expected-cost constrained multi-stage optimization → apply/shift the first control in receding horizon → compare outputs and check constraints. In the reported demonstration, the optimization uses a fixed 32-final-branch, 15-day tree and is compared against deterministic 10/15-day and perfect-forecast trajectories.
