## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors begin from a trade-off: using forecasts can raise the reservoir level and use floodwater more effectively, but uncertain forecasts can increase the chance that flood load exceeds available regulation capacity. They therefore focus on inflow forecast errors as the primary uncertainty, represent relative errors probabilistically, and use forecast accuracy as the observable way to set that distribution. Because temporal persistence makes independent synthetic-error sampling cumbersome and because the routing relation is nonlinear, they choose an SDE representation whose time-varying diffusion coefficient follows the inflow hydrograph. They define risk against design and check levels, then test how accuracy measure and accuracy grade alter risk. Their claim stops at estimated case-study probabilities under assumed error distributions and routing rules.

Reconstructed route: frame forecast-enabled prerelease as a benefit–risk decision → quantify error distribution from qualified rate or NSE → transfer error scale to a time-varying stochastic routing process → define design/check exceedance risk → apply the model to calibrated Geheyan forecasting and operating rules → compare accuracy metrics and use an acceptable-risk threshold to infer a required forecast accuracy.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study calibrates and verifies a Geheyan inflow forecasting model, obtains an error scale from forecast accuracy, sets the reservoir’s prerelease and release rules, routes design and check hydrographs through a stochastic storage balance, converts storage to level, and estimates exceedance probabilities from 2,000 forward-Euler trajectories. It then compares α- and NSE-based error scales, varies forecast grades, and reads the resulting risk curves for a possible accuracy requirement. The sensitivity and threshold analyses are evaluation branches around the main routing workflow.

Reconstructed workflow: forecast-model calibration/verification → derive σ from α or R² → specify Geheyan operating rule, initial level, critical levels and flood probabilities → compute time-varying diffusion coefficient → generate K=2,000 stochastic Wiener/Euler paths → transform storage paths to level paths and summarize distributions → calculate conditional design/check risks → repeat over accuracy grades and compare α versus R² → inspect acceptable-risk threshold.
