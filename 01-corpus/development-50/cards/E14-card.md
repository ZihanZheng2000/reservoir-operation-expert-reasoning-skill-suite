## Paper identification

The paper proposes a forecast-informed real-time flood-control framework for Lishimen Reservoir in China. It combines three AI streamflow forecasts with a Cloud model, generates error-based ensemble scenarios using a copula and Gibbs sampling (SE-Cloud), and optimizes releases with either two-objective stochastic optimization (TSO) or seven-objective multi-objective robust optimization (MRO) over risk, resilience, vulnerability, and level/release criteria. The source text contains the full methodological and experimental narrative used here.

## 1. What the paper does

| Element | Extraction | Evidence |

| Research object and problem | It studies short-horizon reservoir flood-control releases when inflow forecasts are uncertain and upstream water level conflicts with downstream release safety. The decision problem is how to combine deterministic/ensemble forecasts and select robust release trajectories for small- and large-scale floods. | Abstract; pp. 2–5, Section 2.3. |

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors begin from rolling-horizon flood operation, where forecast errors and model uncertainty make a single deterministic inflow inadequate. They reason that forecast combination should use distributional similarity as well as point accuracy, while uncertainty generation should preserve temporal dependence in forecast errors. Because upstream level, downstream release, failure duration, and loss severity conflict, a two-objective formulation is insufficient for large floods; MRO therefore adds risk, resilience, and vulnerability. They assess forecast and optimization quality against observed inflows (“Perfect”), while recognizing that a more accurate deterministic forecast need not produce better operational outcomes.

Reconstructed route: The dependency is (R1) separate flood events by scale to avoid fitting too few heterogeneous examples; (R2) select informative rainfall lags and train diverse AI forecasts; (R3) combine models by Cloud-distribution similarity; (R4) model errors and generate temporally dependent ensembles; (R5) compare forecast quality with operational value using Perfect inflows; (R6) choose TSO for two-objective comparison and MRO for large-flood multi-objective trade-offs; (R7) search non-dominated releases and select a compromise trajectory; and (R8) test post-assessed robustness and confidence-level sensitivity, with explicit limits from the single-reservoir case and observed-weather setup.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The study clusters 22 observed events, selects rainfall lag inputs, trains three AI models per cluster, combines them with Cloud weights, and post-processes their errors into 1,000 SE-Cloud inflow scenarios. It then runs four experiments: forecast generation, deterministic forecast value, deterministic-versus-ensemble operation with TSO, and MRO for risk/resilience/vulnerability. NSGA-III searches constrained release trajectories and AHP-Entropy selects compromise solutions. Candidate operations are assessed using observed inflows, and sensitivity tests vary ensemble confidence/scenario representation.

Reconstructed workflow: Forecast preparation and scenario generation feed optimization branches. Experiment 2 uses single-objective formulations; Experiment 3 compares deterministic models, SE-Cloud, S-BMA, and Perfect with two objectives; Experiment 4 compares TSO and MRO for the large flood and then post-assesses under actual inflows and measurement-error scenarios. Daily release dynamics are embedded in the reservoir mass-balance simulation, while hypervolume, Pareto, correlation, and sensitivity analyses are evaluation branches.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- Exact SOM topology/training settings, event normalization, cluster assignment procedure, and all random train/validation details are not reported in the main supplied text.
- LSTM/GRU/XGBoost architecture, grid-search ranges, initialization, regularization, and full preprocessing are delegated to supplements or unavailable here.
- Cloud segment boundaries (z_m), segment count (M), fitted cloud parameters, and handling of edge cases are not specified.
- Marginal distribution parameter estimates, Archimedean copula parameter estimates, Gibbs burn-in/convergence/seed settings, and BMA/S-BMA construction details are not reported.
- Full reservoir operating characteristics and some discharge constraints are delegated to case materials; the main text gives the principal thresholds in Table 1.
- Objective notation for resilience/CVaR and exact implementation of the end-level revise/screen procedure should be checked against the original equations before reproduction.
- Forecasted meteorological inputs were not used; observed weather alternatives may overestimate forecast quality and alter forecast-error distributions (p. 26).
- Damage/cost functions, cascade-reservoir interactions, correlated multi-reservoir inflows, and uncertainty in initial/final levels or operation parameters are omitted and identified as future work.
- The paper does not establish a deployment-ready rule, actual flood-loss reduction, or general validity across return periods; the large-flood evidence is centered on Flood No.201908 (approximately 20-year return period).
