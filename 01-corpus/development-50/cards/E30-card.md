## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors start from the fact that real-time operation combines uncertain forecasts, hydraulic discharge relations, and storage-capacity observations, so deterministic routing cannot directly express risk. They prefer an analytical SDE route because sampling methods depend on sample size and common reliability approximations cannot readily represent correlations or a non-explicit performance function. They represent forecasted inflow as the mean plus an error, and locally linearize discharge and storage curves so the water-level error can be recursively propagated. Temporal correlation matters because a flood is continuous and successive forecast errors share model and parameter uncertainty. Finally, they define both point exceedance and the union of exceedance events through time, retaining adjacent-time dependence while assuming other moments independent for tractability. The resulting curves support a comparison with acceptable risk or subsequent precipitation, subject to normality, interpolation, and correlation assumptions.

Reconstructed route: identify multiple real-time uncertainty sources → choose SDE analytical propagation to retain temporal dependence → express hydraulic/storage relations locally as linear functions → recursively derive water-level error moments → calculate point and union risks with normal/bivariate-normal tails → vary controlled level and inflow-error correlation → interpret curves for operating-program or emergency-response adjustment.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors specify distributions and temporal correlations for uncertainty terms, obtain forecast inflows, run deterministic flood routing under Dahuofang rules to obtain mean levels, and use local piecewise-linear coefficients to recursively calculate water-level-error moments. They then compute point exceedance and whole-flood union risk for controlled levels and several inflow-error correlations. The case varies controlled level and correlation, plots error PDFs/standard deviations, and interprets the curves through two application examples. The sensitivity sweeps and comparator discussion are evaluation branches around the recursive analytical calculation.

Reconstructed workflow: define stochastic inflow/outflow/storage variables → estimate or assume error parameters → deterministic routing for mean level → piecewise-linearize curves at each time → recursively calculate error mean/variance → recursively calculate adjacent covariance/correlation → integrate point and pair exceedance probabilities → combine them into total process risk → sweep controlled level and correlation → derive example operating implications.
