## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper frames flood-risk management as a water-management decision governed by site-specific WCMs and rule curves. The authors identify a mismatch between aging manuals and improved understanding/forecast products, then ask whether forecast information can safely create additional storage or release flexibility. Because USACE operations must comply with federal law, regulations, and existing rule-curve practice, the reasoning favors FIRO approaches implementable with required USACE decision tools, especially HEC-ResSim, rather than an unconstrained optimization method. Pilot sites are treated as distinct cases because objectives, watershed characteristics, and constraints differ. Their alternatives are compared with current operations on multiple benefit and risk metrics; favorable but case-bounded results motivate a staged screening framework. Screening begins with exclusion criteria, then scores benefit versus implementation effort, and finally uses deeper assessment and dialogue to route sites toward viability, more research, no action, or WCM revision. The final claim stops at a conditional implementation pathway: FIRO is suitable where forecasts, controllability, operating rules, collaboration, and site-specific assessment support it.

Reconstructed route: First identify the operational and institutional gap (old WCMs and unavailable formal use of modern forecasts). Next constrain the solution space to USACE-compatible rule-curve operations and required simulation infrastructure. Then use heterogeneous California pilots to test whether forecast-informed alternatives can meet each site's primary objective without worsening flood outcomes. Compare each alternative to the current baseline using site-specific metrics, retain the alternative that balances benefits, flood protection, and implementability, and feed lessons into portfolio screening. Apply screening in increasing levels of rigor, using explicit elimination, scored prioritization, and in-depth dialogue/assessment. Finally, codify a viable strategy in an updated WCM and retain a forecast-skill gate so future forecast/model improvements can be adopted.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The paper reports a layered execution route rather than one single operational algorithm. USACE first organizes FIRO work through a steering committee and workplan, then performs preliminary, in-depth, and final viability assessments (p. 2). Each pilot defines site-specific alternatives and compares them with current WCM operations using simulations/assessments and multiple performance metrics. Lake Mendocino evaluates four alternatives over 16 metrics and temporarily tests modified operations in water years 2019–2020; Prado evaluates five alternatives crossed with five buffer-pool elevations; Yuba–Feather develops at-site/system alternatives and two combined alternatives for preliminary comparison. A separate portfolio branch screens reservoirs in three stages, with progressively more rigorous evaluation and dialogue. Results, instrumentation/model needs, and implementation constraints feed an eventual WCM update; FIRO 2.0 adds a future forecast-skill/model-improvement assessment loop. The article does not expose the detailed forecast ingestion, rule-curve equations, calibration workflow, or all simulation settings from the underlying viability reports.

Reconstructed workflow: Form a multi-agency steering committee and workplan; assemble site observations, forecast information, WCM/rule-curve constraints, and objective definitions; build site-specific baseline and FIRO alternatives; simulate or assess each scenario; compare risk and benefit metrics to baseline; check data/model adequacy and implementation compatibility; select or route the site; and, for suitable sites, update the WCM. For portfolio use, apply Stage A → Stage B → Stage C, with branches to defer, research, assess fully, or revise the WCM. Temporary Lake Mendocino implementation is a field/operational demonstration branch, while the reported pilot comparisons and screening beta test are assessment/evaluation activities rather than a general deployed control algorithm.
