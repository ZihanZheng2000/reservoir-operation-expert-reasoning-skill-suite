## Paper identification

The paper couples a Bayesian probabilistic forecasting system (BFS) with real-time dynamic control of the flood limiting water level (FLWL) for the Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR). It compares a flood-season-segmented bound (Scheme I) with one bound for the whole flood season (Scheme II), using deterministic and quantile inflow forecasts with 1–3 day lead times. ## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors identify a structural trade-off in the static annual FLWL: keeping the level low throughout the flood season protects safety but can waste usable water when the flood season is delayed or ends early. Improved forecasts make a dynamic upper/lower bound and prerelease/refill strategy plausible, but deterministic forecasts conceal uncertainty. Because quantitative rainfall forecasts were unavailable in China for this application, they model hydrological uncertainty around deterministic inflow forecasts with a BFS. They then test all inflow quantiles rather than prespecifying one, because no common quantile-selection rule exists; the chosen quantile is judged by safety and benefit outputs. The two bound schemes and lead times are compared under the same operational model. Claims are bounded by TGR simulation, the omission of rainfall-input uncertainty, and the reported validation period.

Reconstructed route: identify static-FLWL water-use loss → use forecast information to permit dynamic bounds and prerelease/refill → represent forecast uncertainty probabilistically with BFS → generate candidate quantile inflows → apply the operational rule with Scheme I or II and 1–3 day lead time → screen safety and downstream impacts → compare benefits with the static design rule and select the more useful scheme/quantile under the case constraints.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors fit the BFS using 1998–2002 TGR flood-season data, validate forecast performance on 2003–2009 data, and generate posterior inflow distributions for lead times of 1, 2, and 3 days. They define Scheme I and Scheme II bounds, set `SQin=35,000 m³/s`, and run the real-time FLWL model over the full 2003–2009 flood seasons for deterministic inflow and all 1–100% quantile inflows. The main outputs are downstream Shashi water-level categories, floodwater utilization, end-of-season level, and annual hydropower. A 2009 event is plotted in detail, and quantiles matching deterministic utilization are used as illustrative paired runs. Scheme comparison, lead-time variation, and quantile sweeps are evaluation branches around the same control rule.

Reconstructed workflow: fit BFS prior/likelihood → validate deterministic and 50% probabilistic forecasts → produce posterior hydrographs → define Scheme I/II bounds and lead-time settings → at each step determine forecast maximum and prerelease/refill action → route reservoir levels and releases → calculate downstream and benefit metrics → repeat over quantiles, schemes, and lead times → compare with static design rule and inspect selected paired trajectories.
