## Paper identification

The paper assesses uncertainty and risk in forecast-based flood-control operation at Ankang Reservoir, China. It estimates inflow forecast-error statistics from observed/forecast inflows, superimposes simulated error series on representative flood hydrographs, routes them through forecast-based rules with Monte Carlo simulation, and derives frequency distributions of maximum water level (MWL). Abstract confirmation: the abstract states this three-part sequence—derive error series, route Monte Carlo inflows, and use MWL frequency distributions to evaluate uncertainty and operator-relevant risk. It uses an existing 6-hour runoff indicator/rule set and quantifies modeled dam/railway water-level exceedance risk; it does not optimize a new release rule.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: Forecast-based operation can create useful lead time but decisions depend on imperfect forecasts; measured inflows also have error, though usually smaller. Hydrological inflow uncertainty is treated as a primary risk factor. The authors first characterize errors by comparing rolling forecasts with later observations. Because means are small relative to high flood flows and standard deviations vary only modestly above 12,000 m³/s, they simplify high-flow errors to a stationary Gaussian process with approximately μ=0 and σ≈600 m³/s. They then propagate these errors through the existing forecast-based rule and reservoir routing. The resulting MWL distribution, rather than forecast error alone, is the risk quantity relevant to operators. Risk is assessed against 330.0 m and 337.33 m caution/dam thresholds for specified return-period floods. The paper does not directly compare forecast-based and conventional risk because conventional measurement-error risk is not quantified, and it does not claim a new optimized policy.

Reconstructed route: identify forecast-error and routing-risk problem → choose forecast indicator/operating rule from lead-time, watershed response, peak/volume analyses → estimate error distribution → simplify error process for high-flow simulation → superimpose errors on representative/magnified flood waves → route through rule and storage relation using Monte Carlo → summarize MWL moments/distributions and threshold exceedance probabilities → interpret case-specific acceptability and limitations.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The paper first defines a stepwise reservoir water-balance/routing model and fixes the forecast-based 6-hour runoff rule. It uses ten Ankang floods to obtain lead-time-specific forecast errors, checks their distributions, and adopts μ≈0, σ≈600 m³/s stationary Gaussian errors for high-flow simulation. For each of three representative observed flood waves (197409, 196809, 198307), 2,000 random error series are generated and superimposed; each resulting inflow is routed from the 325 m cautionary level through the Table 3 rule. MWL samples are summarized by moments and distributions. For 100-year and 10,000-year waves, a representative hydrograph is randomly selected and linearly magnified before error addition and routing. The evaluation branch estimates exceedance probabilities at 330.0, 333.0, and 337.33 m. The work is uncertainty assessment around an existing rule, not optimization or deployment.

Reconstructed workflow: collect rolling forecasts/observations → estimate error statistics → choose high-flow error approximation → select representative flood hydrographs → generate error realizations → add errors to waves → route with forecast rule → record MWL → repeat 2,000 times per wave → fit/inspect distribution and threshold probabilities → optionally magnify to return-period flood and repeat → interpret acceptability.

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- Full forecast-error sample sizes, estimation/test procedures, temporal dependence, and random-number generation details are not reported.
- The stationary Gaussian approximation is only justified for inflows above 12,000 m³/s; lower-flow error behavior remains unresolved.
- Complete reservoir level–storage/outflow functions and implementation details of Tables 2–3 are not supplied.
- Rule derivation is summarized as repeated comparison/example validation; candidate-rule results and optimization criteria are not reported.
- Routing-model calibration/independent validation, initial-condition uncertainty, measurement-error propagation, and operator delay are not quantified.
- The conventional-rule risk comparison is unavailable because measurement-error risk cannot be quantified in the study.
- Monte Carlo sample convergence, normal-fit diagnostics for MWL, and sensitivity to hydrograph selection/magnification are not reported.
- OCR/encoding artifacts affect some table labels, symbols, and names; unreadable portions were not reconstructed.
