## Paper identification

The paper develops a quantitative pre-processing method for selecting criteria before multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) of reservoir flood-control alternatives. It combines BP-neural-network relative importance, one-at-a-time sensitivity, and a comprehensive discrimination index, then tests the method on ten Pubugou Reservoir alternatives.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors treat criterion selection as a distinct decision before weighting and ranking. They reason that a criterion can be redundant either because it has low inherent contribution to the MCDM output or because the output is insensitive to changes in it; therefore both importance and sensitivity must be considered. A BP network approximates the nonlinear mapping from criteria to ideal-point closeness, while a one-at-a-time sensitivity curve quantifies response. Their product is compared with the average order of magnitude, with a threshold chosen to preserve MCDM rankings while simplifying the criterion set.

Reconstructed route: distinguish candidate generation from MCDM criterion selection; generate representative alternatives and ideal/anti-ideal limits; create stochastic training samples; train and verify the BP mapping; estimate importance and sensitivity; combine them into a discrimination index; delete low-index criteria; test whether multiple MCDM methods preserve rankings.

| R3. Use BP weights for inherent importance | A trained network stores the nonlinear mapping in connection weights. The partial derivative shows that weighted input-to-hidden and hidden-to-output connections determine each criterion’s contribution; averaging across training cycles reduces random-initialization variation. | **Trigger:** estimate a criterion’s inherent influence on closeness. **Inputs:** trained weights, hidden activations, repeated training results. **Judgment/procedure:** calculate Rj from absolute weighted paths and average over N training cycles. **Output:** relative importance ranking. | pp. 4–5, Equations (8)–(13). | R4. Add one-at-a-time sensitivity | Importance alone can miss a criterion that has modest average contribution but strongly changes output when perturbed. One-at-a-time perturbations do not capture interactions among criteria. |
| R5. Combine importance and sensitivity into a deletion rule | A criterion should be selected when both its importance and sensitivity are material. Their product Fj is compared with the mean product; Pj = log10(Fj/mean F) indicates order-of-magnitude difference. Pj < -1 is chosen as the deletion threshold. | **Trigger:** importance and sensitivity rankings disagree or criterion reduction is needed. **Inputs:** Rj, Gj, average Fj, acceptable MCDM change. **Judgment/procedure:** select large-F criteria, delete criteria more than one order below average, and lower the threshold stepwise until rankings change in real applications. **Output:** selected/deleted criterion set. | pp. 6–7, Equations (16)–(17). The threshold remains partly subjective and is case-dependent. |

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors first generate ten non-dominated Pubugou flood-control alternatives by varying the upper water-level limit in a maximum-peak-reduction operation model. Each alternative is routed to obtain ten performance criteria covering reservoir safety, downstream flood effects, water use, sediment, and shipping. Ideal/anti-ideal points define 30 stochastic training samples, which are normalized and used to train a BP(10:11:1) network. Five samples verify the fitted closeness mapping. Relative importance and one-at-a-time sensitivity are calculated, the Pj threshold selects five criteria, and three independent MCDM formulations compare rankings before and after selection.

Reconstructed workflow: build alternatives -> calculate criteria -> construct stochastic ideal/anti-ideal samples -> train/verify BP network -> calculate importance and sensitivity -> apply threshold -> rerun multiple MCDMs -> compare rankings.

The sensitivity method ignores joint perturbations and network uncertainty. |

This is a case-level rationality check; no external decision-maker preference or independent benchmark is provided. |

## 6. Unreported or unresolved items

- The exact stepwise trial-and-error implementation and all routed-event details are not reproduced.
- Risk-index calculations for dam/structure failure and downstream flooding are referenced rather than fully specified.
- The number of repeated BP training cycles used to average Rj and random seeds are not reported.
- One-at-a-time sensitivity does not assess interactions, correlated criteria, or alternative perturbation ranges.
- Equal weights and three MCDM methods provide a rationality check but not domain-owner validation.
