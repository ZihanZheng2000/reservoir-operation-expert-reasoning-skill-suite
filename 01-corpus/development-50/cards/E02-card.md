## Paper identification

The paper studies real-time flood-control decisions for a reservoir and a downstream control point when forecast, optimization, and preference information are uncertain. It applies a stochastic Forecast–Optimization–Decision Making (FODM) framework to the Pubugou Reservoir and Leshan City system in the Daduhe River basin, China. The text defines a synthetic forecast experiment, stochastic reservoir optimization with recourse, risk assessment, and stochastic multicriteria decision making; it does not report deployment with a live ensemble forecast system. Page references below refer to the page numbers printed in the supplied text (PDF pages 1–20).

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper frames reservoir flood control as a linked forecast–optimization–decision problem rather than a deterministic release-rule problem. Deterministic forecasts and fixed operation rules conceal the uncertainty that affects both candidate releases and multicriteria ranking. The authors therefore represent forecast uncertainty as evolving ensembles, optimize competing safety objectives jointly under those scenarios, quantify the chance that reservoir or downstream thresholds are exceeded, and select among the resulting trade-offs with a stochastic ranking method. The immediate release is implemented only for the current period, because later releases should be reconsidered when hydrologic information is updated. The claim stops at a synthetic, model-based demonstration in one reservoir–downstream-control system; the authors explicitly state that a real-time ensemble forecast model and scenario-management procedure would be needed for real-world application.

Reconstructed route: (1) identify uncertainty across the FODM chain; (2) represent evolving forecast uncertainty with MMFE for the demonstration; (3) formulate competing reservoir-level and downstream-discharge objectives with physical and operating constraints; (4) trace an uncertain Pareto front with a two-step constraint method; (5) translate objective distributions into overtopping and downstream-flooding risks; (6) rank alternatives with SMAA-TOPSIS using uncertain criteria and, where available, expert preference weights; (7) implement only the current-period release and repeat after updating the forecast and reservoir state; and (8) use numerical comparisons and parameter experiments to examine uncertainty propagation and ranking reliability.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The workflow starts with the Pubugou–Leshan case and a 36-hour horizon divided into twelve 3-hour periods. At each current period, MMFE generates a 40-member synthetic inflow scenario tree. The stochastic nonlinear program evaluates scenario-dependent releases subject to mass balance and operating limits, and the constraint method generates ten noninferior alternatives. For each alternative, the distributions of peak release and maximum level are used to calculate downstream-flooding and reservoir-overtopping probabilities. These stochastic criteria feed SMAA-TOPSIS, which samples uncertain performance values and weights and returns rank/holistic acceptability and uncertainty indicators. The selected alternative supplies only the immediate release; state and forecast are then updated and the chain repeats. Separate numerical experiments vary forecast uncertainty level and correlation without recourse to diagnose propagation effects.

Reconstructed workflow: case and parameter setup → synthetic forecast/scenario generation → scenario-based stochastic optimization → two-step Pareto construction → distribution and risk calculation → stochastic MCDM/ranking → immediate release selection → state/forecast update and recourse loop → sensitivity and comparator experiments. Distributional tests on (Q_{max}) and (Z_{max}) are used in the case study before the reported normal distributions are used in the decision matrix. The main receding-horizon workflow is distinct from the evaluation branches that compare SMAA-TOPSIS and SMAA-2 or vary (r) and (q_{error}).
