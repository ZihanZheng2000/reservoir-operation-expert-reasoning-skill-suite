## Paper identification

The paper studies risk assessment and selection of joint flood-control operation schemes for a 14-reservoir system in the upper Yangtze River. It treats forecast error, discharge-capacity uncertainty, and water-level/storage uncertainty as stochastic risk sources, evaluates their effects on maximum occupied storage, and ranks candidate schemes with a multi-index decision method. The supplied text covers the article and references, but the detailed optimization constraints and scheme tables are only referenced as supplementary Tables S1–S11; those contents are not present in the assigned text.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The authors frame joint reservoir operation as a decision problem under several hydrologic and hydraulic uncertainties. A probability-of-failure measure alone does not represent tail consequences or the discreteness/dispersion of simulated outcomes, so they choose mean, CVaR, and entropy as complementary indicators. Because the system contains reservoirs with different flood-control capacities and runoff contributions, reservoir-level indicators are weighted using AHP before scheme-level comparison. Because the indicators may disagree, subjective and entropy-derived weights are combined and an improved Mahalanobis-distance TOPSIS procedure converts them into a ranking. The claim stops at simulated/case-study risk evaluation and scheme selection; deployment performance and the omitted supplementary optimization details are not established.

Reconstructed route: (1) derive feasible scheme alternatives for representative flood hydrographs; (2) identify the forecast, discharge, and storage uncertainties that materially affect operation; (3) choose dependence-aware or stratified stochastic simulation for each uncertainty type; (4) propagate each sampled uncertainty set through each candidate operation; (5) characterize the resulting maximum occupied storage with mean, CVaR, and entropy; (6) aggregate reservoir-level indicators using AHP weights; (7) reconcile indicator weights and rank schemes with improved TOPSIS; and (8) repeat over typical floods to assess robustness across flood types.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The workflow first generates candidate operation schemes from seven typical flood hydrographs, a synthesized scheme, and an existing scheme. For every scheme, forecast-error, discharge-capacity, and water-level/storage uncertainties are sampled and passed through repeated operation simulations. Maximum occupied storage is collected by reservoir and summarized by mean, 99% CVaR, and entropy. AHP aggregates reservoir results, and TOPSIS ranks schemes. The process is repeated for typical floods, with the rank table used for a cross-flood comparison. Scheme generation and the detailed operational constraints are only partially visible because the assigned text points to supplementary equations and tables.
