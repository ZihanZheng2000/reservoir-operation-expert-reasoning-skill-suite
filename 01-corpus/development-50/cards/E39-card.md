## Paper identification

The paper develops an analytical integral method (AMIT) for risk probabilities in reservoir flood-control operation with forecast information. It represents risk sources by cumulative net rainfall and its forecast error, defines single-hazard, union-hazard (UHE), and intersection-hazard (IHE) domains, integrates their joint density over those domains, and demonstrates the method at Dahuofang Reservoir. A six-stage forecast-informed rule set is used in the case study. The supplied text includes the appendices that define the forecast-error density, rainfall-density transformation, comparison with the law of total probability method (MLTP), and operating rules.

## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper begins from the observation that forecast-informed operation can improve regulation but forecast error creates risk. A single hazard is insufficient because a reservoir water-level exceedance and a downstream discharge exceedance can occur alone or together; the union threatens safety if either occurs and the intersection represents simultaneous hazards. Numerical Monte Carlo methods are flexible but computationally demanding, while MLTP approximates conditional risk over error intervals. The authors therefore use an integral formulation over a continuous hazard domain, preserving the joint density of rainfall and forecast error and enabling UHE/IHE calculation. The claim is a probability-calculation framework and case demonstration; it does not quantify consequence severity and does not validate deployment outcomes.

Reconstructed route: (1) define rainfall and forecast error as risk sources; (2) express upstream and downstream threshold exceedances and their union/intersection; (3) obtain a hazard boundary by routing a grid of design rainfall processes and error allocations; (4) construct joint source density from marginal densities and a copula; (5) integrate density over the selected domain; (6) compare single-hazard AMIT with MLTP; and (7) vary FLWL to examine UHE/IHE risk and operational capacity.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The case procedure samples design flood frequencies and forecast-error values, reconstructs design net-rainfall processes, allocates error to the main rainfall period, routes each forecast hydrograph using six forecast-informed rules, and stores the resulting maximum reservoir level and downstream flow. The resulting surfaces are intersected with threshold planes to produce hazard curves. A maximum-entropy error density and transformed Pearson type III rainfall density form the source joint density; AMIT integrates it over each hazard region. The method is compared against MLTP for single hazards and then applied to varying FLWL values for UHE/IHE.

Reconstructed workflow: source data/distributions -> error/frequency grid -> hydrograph reconstruction -> forecast-error allocation -> reservoir routing -> hazard surfaces/boundaries -> source joint density -> domain integration -> MLTP comparison -> FLWL risk sweep.
