## Paper identification

The paper studies how uncertainty in dynamically updated streamflow forecasts evolves and how that uncertainty affects real-time flood-season operation of the Three Gorges Reservoir (TGR), China. Its direct operational adjustment is a forecast-informed pre-release module, evaluated through stochastic flood-risk analysis. ## 2. How the authors reason

Reasoning overview: The paper begins from the operational consequence that forecast error can lead to under-preparation or over-preparation, while forecast uncertainty changes as forecasts are updated. It treats the traditional uncertainty-evolution approaches as too restrictive for the observed hydrologic setting because they rely on assumptions such as Gaussianity and stationarity, and because the generalized martingale approach requires normal-quantile transformations. The authors therefore choose a copula model that can use flexible marginals and represent dependence, including dependent extremes. They normalize errors by observed flow, decompose forecast-error evolution into single-period reductions, and calibrate separate distributions for June and July–September after nonstationarity is identified. The resulting uncertainty is propagated into synthetic inflows and the TGR pre-release/routing chain; the claim stops at the modeled, case-specific risk comparison.

Reconstructed route: frame forecast uncertainty as an operational risk; replace restrictive uncertainty-evolution assumptions with a copula formulation; use relative error and additive single-period reductions; split the nonstationary flood season; select marginal distributions using fit diagnostics; model four-period dependence with a Student t copula after an autocorrelation check; generate and validate uncertainty; then compare forecast-informed pre-release risk with the observed-flow/regular-routing reference under TGR flood-control criteria.

## 3. How the authors execute

Execution overview: The authors use TGR forecast and observed inflow data from the 2003–2009 flood seasons, with a daily-updated four-day forecast produced by an MLSM model. They calculate relative forecast errors and their rolling single-period reductions, split the data into June and July–September, fit and check marginal distributions and joint dependence, and generate uncertainty reductions with the fitted CUE model. They reconstruct lead-time uncertainty, generate synthetic daily inflow with a Gumbel copula, and form predicted inflow by multiplying synthetic observed-flow realizations by (1+re). In the evaluation branch, these predicted inflows drive a rolling pre-release rule and reservoir routing; stochastic simulations estimate exceedance risk against the regular-routing/observed-flow reference.

Reconstructed workflow: data and forecast-error construction → rolling reduction variables → seasonal split → marginal fitting and diagnostics → autocorrelation and Student t copula fitting → reduction simulation and moment checks → uncertainty reconstruction → synthetic daily flow and predicted inflow generation → pre-release/routing simulation under TGR rules → 10,000-year risk estimate and baseline comparison. The uncertainty-generation and flow-generation steps are stochastic and repeat over simulated samples; the distribution fitting and baseline comparison are calibration/evaluation activities rather than recurring live actions.
