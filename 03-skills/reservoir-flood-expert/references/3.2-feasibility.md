# Reference for unit 3.2-feasibility: what the papers did

This file covers how papers checked whether candidate reservoir actions, levels, and policies are physically, temporally, storage-wise, system-wide, and institutionally feasible, summarizing 80 steps from 31 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP05-A Hard limits of facilities and operation

**When papers did this**
- When a stochastic, multiobjective, or many-objective solution had to remain physically operable (E02-R4, E15-R6, E22-R4).
- When a candidate rule or policy could exceed safety/design levels, raise storage beyond safe release or ramping limits, or induce a release hazard (E46-R5, E50-R3).
- When ensemble optimization faced hard storage/release constraints (E35-R5), when a large forecast error called for a release jump (E37-R8), or when event and reservoir characteristics restricted admissible operation modes (E48-R3).

**How**
- Hard bounds on mass balance, water level, release, release capacity, release fluctuation, and initial conditions; the end level treated as a generally soft constraint, because storage should be restored for later floods but may not be strictly met (E02-R4).
- Water balance, min/max release and storage, and a consecutive-release fluctuation limit meant to prevent downstream-channel damage; violating policies rejected or constrained (E22-R4).
- Safe release, ramping, capacity, and TOCS constraints kept in both baseline and FIRO models; infeasible releases constrained or penalized (E50-R3).
- Split into rigid constraints, repaired dynamically, and flexible output constraints, ranked by violation (E15-R6).
- PMF simulations to prevent overtopping; rules bounded by channel capacity, monotonicity, outlet limits, and Taiwan regulations; unsafe trajectories penalized or excluded, with barrier functions for non-bound constraints (E46-R5).
- Scenario tree: scenarios share a history up to branch points, because a fully shared average control can be infeasible in a storage-limited reservoir and fully independent per-member controls cannot be implemented (E35-R5).
- Multi-step gradual release correction instead of a single jump when the jump is large (E37-R8).
- Operation modes assigned from upstream to downstream; Houziyan restricted to discharge-capacity operation because of dam-failure risk, giving six admissible cascade alternatives (E48-R3).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Reject/exclude (E22-R4, E46-R5) vs. penalize (E46-R5, E50-R3) vs. repair rigid constraints and rank flexible ones (E15-R6): the cards do not give criteria for choosing.
- Hard vs. soft terminal level: soft in E02-R4.

**Limits and gaps**
- No separate feasibility-repair or fallback procedure (E02-R4).
- RSEA superiority holds only relative to the tested algorithms; no global optimality (E15-R6).
- Full operational procedures and uncertainty margins not reported (E22-R4).
- Max release and ramping were estimated from observed records and may not match actual infrastructure or policy (E50-R3).
- The E37 remedy is preliminary and conceptual (E37-R8). E46 rules are model-calibrated; deployment authorization not studied (E46-R5).
- E48 numerical importance weights not given (E48-R3). The E35 tree-reduction algorithm comes from prior work (E35-R5).

**Example values**
- Dez: α=1665 m³/s release change, Qmax=6844.5 m³/s, Smin=415 MCM, Smax=2698.5 MCM, critical level 352 m, 6-h intervals (E22-R4).

## RP05-B Is there enough time

**When papers did this**
- When a candidate upper level could prevent timely drawdown to the current FLWL before a large inflow (E01-R3, E19-R6).
- When forecast risk exceeded tolerance (E07-R4), when a dry/delayed-flood assumption could fail (E09-R4), or when papers explained performance differences across sites (E17-R4), turned PP into a level bound (E19-R4), defined season FLWL bounds (E29-R4), or chose a forecast horizon (E35-R2).

**How**
- Capacity-constrained mass balance: the candidate upper storage is linked to effective lead time T_c, future inflow, and downstream safety discharge Q_s (Eq. (5) in E01). The level may exceed the current FLWL only if pre-release can restore it in time (E01-R3).
- Same idea in E19: Eq. (7) evaluated over a discrete inflow/max-prerelease-outflow grid (Table 3), then 66 m chosen as the common upper bound because prerelease could finish within 48 h (E19-R6).
- PP converted to floodwater volume (Eq. (8); runoff coefficient 0.75 or the Xinanjiang model), then to VCWL under a given prerelease sequence (Eq. (9)); the VCWL trend shows whether a higher level can be kept (E19-R4).
- Worst case: release at min(qA, g(Z)), compare the required time t with lead time τ; if t>τ, subtract the unreleased volume from ΔWtotal to get ΔWsafe (E09-R4).
- Ensemble prerelease: pick the smallest set of excess members needed to meet tolerance, spread each member's excess volume over the days, cap at release limits and push the excess back to earlier days, take the maximum day-1 release (E07-R4).
- Upper bounds rise with lead time, under seasonal (Scheme I) or whole-season (Scheme II) segmentation (E29-R4).
- Global sampling of R_max, ramping rate RR, and LT, with release and ramping normalized by peak inflow (E17-R4).
- Horizon reference under perfect information: compare peak releases over 6–48 h horizons (E35-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Deterministic lead-time mass balance (E01-R3, E19-R6) vs. worst-case forecast failure (E09-R4) vs. ensemble risk tolerance (E07-R4) vs. precipitation-based VCWL (E19-R4). The cards tie each to its trigger, not to a stated selection rule.

**Limits and gaps**
- Release scheduling within the lead time not fully specified (E01-R3). No optimality proof for the E07 heuristic (E07-R4).
- In E09 all 50 scenarios exceeded lead time (E09-R4).
- E17 ranges do not reflect engineering feasibility at every site (E17-R4).
- Eq. (8) is partly OCR-damaged (E19-R4). The criterion for choosing 66 m is not reported (E19-R6).
- E29 bounds come from an earlier model, not rederived (E29-R4). The "approximately infinite horizon" reading is an assumption (E35-R2).

**Example values**
- E07, 8 Feb 1986: 14/61 members above threshold at day 8, r_8=23.0% vs R_8=9.8%, 8 members selected, 529 cfs day-1 release; all-horizon maximum 1,240 cfs at day 14 (E07-R4).
- E35: 6/12-h horizons delayed release; ≥18 h gave similar safe responses below 200 m³/s (E35-R2). E29 lower bound 145 m (E29-R4).

## RP05-C Is there enough storage space

**When papers did this**
- When a candidate allocation, pre-release, or hedging decision had to fit the remaining capacity and the forecast/design flood (E05-R3, E11-R3, E15-R3, E24-R6, E25-R3).
- When the reservoir had to be able to refill after releasing water (E12-R1, E16-R7), or when an over-FLWL operating scenario was built (E09-R3).

**How**
- Stage-2 balance W_1+d=RFCC splits residual flood-conveyance capacity between conservation storage and safety margin; d_min=rU^{-1}(1−r_a), d_max=R_{2,max}−R_{2,min}, 0≤W_1≤min(W_max, RFCC−d_min), giving feasible zone ABCD (E05-R3).
- Over-FLWL volume = utilization-period ΔWFU + flood-control-period ΔWFC, with qc=min(βqA, g(Z)) (E09-R3).
- Period-specific pre-storm level S0,ts=K+rts−fdes,ts (K capacity, rts maximum safe release volume, fdes,ts design flood volume), converted to a level (E11-R3); the minimum over 1/2/3/5-day windows is taken (E11-R4).
- Pre-release only if future inflow minus minimum downstream demand can restore FLWL when no flood comes; triggered when forecast inflow lies in Qdown–Qup (E12-R1).
- V_{t+1} ≤ V_lim + R_lim·T_f·Δt − Per(P_I, 1−r_a)·T_f·Δt; smaller r_a lowers storage and raises discharge (E15-R3).
- Drawdown compared with remaining water-year inflow before refill (June 1) (E16-R7).
- Carryover storage clipped at 0 or K using bound-specific formulas (Eqs. (28)–(29) in E24) (E24-R6).
- Storms sorted as small / large / overwhelming against qlb/qub; hedging helps only for large storms (E25-R3).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Deterministic design-flood capacity (E11-R3, E09-R3) vs. risk-percentile bounds (E05-R3, E15-R3): the cards do not state when to prefer one.
- E05 and E15 both use r_a, but in different formulations; keep them apart.

**Limits and gaps**
- The normal-error assumption is part of the model (E05-R3). Modeled volumes are not authorization limits (E09-R3).
- Gate behavior not given (E11-R3). Taking the minimum is specific to E11 (E11-R4). Qdown/Qup are calibrated per case (E12-R1).
- The refill test is simplified and specific to Oroville (E16-R7). No authorization or emergency fallback (E24-R6). The categories rest on square-wave assumptions (E25-R3).

## RP05-D Downstream and system conditions limit local action

**When papers did this**
- When a controller needed a downstream safety target and horizon (E43-R4), when a simulated safety level was still exceeded (E43-R5), and when channel segments lay between reservoirs and a control station (E44-R3, E44-R4, E44-R5).

**How**
- Safety level set below the danger level as a buffer; horizon chosen to cover the travel time from the furthest reservoir while staying computable (E43-R4).
- Remaining violations attributed to uncontrolled catchments, full storage, and conduit limits, not only to the controller (E43-R5).
- Each channel segment treated as a channel storage reservoir (CSR) using Muskingum K and x and added to reservoir storage, with conservative estimates where hydraulic data were missing (E44-R3).
- Two-stage risk: draw down reservoir storage, then last-CSR storage; risk taken as probability-weighted end storage, using the allocation with the largest risk (E44-R4).
- Upper WLFS boundaries raised for hydropower only while aggregate risk, refill, and physical limits hold (E44-R5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Settings not calibrated systematically; no field deployment (E43-R4, E43-R5).
- CSR estimates could be improved with hydraulic models (E44-R3). Gaussian unbiased forecast error assumed, actual bias not checked (E44-R4). Optimizer not specified (E44-R5).
- Evidence comes from only 2 papers (provisional).

**Example values**
- Taungoo: 42 m safety level vs 43 m danger level, ~41 h travel time, 30-min simulation step, 3-h control step, 2-day horizon (E43-R4).

## RP05-E Model feasibility is not implementability

**When papers did this**
- When a method was meant for real operation, transfer, or portfolio screening (E17-R1, E18-R2, E18-R8, E20-R10, E22-R9, E25-R6), or when several feasible schemes had institutional owners (E21-R6).

**How**
- Approach kept within USACE rule-curve practice and HEC-ResSim/CWMS tools because of laws and guidance (E18-R2).
- Forecast skill at a sufficient lead time made a prerequisite; poor skill can exclude a site at Stage A (E18-R4).
- Strategy kept only when benefits and flood-risk outcomes are favorable and it fits standard tools (E18-R7).
- Staged portfolio screening: Stage A elimination, Stage B benefit/effort scoring, Stage C detailed assessment (E18-R8). Implementation by WCM update, with adaptive WCP language and a forecast-skill check (E18-R9).
- Cross-site parameter screening with one common control method (E17-R1).
- Final scheme set by inter-departmental consultation led by FCDRH, starting from a model-generated ensemble of schemes (E21-R6).
- Missing prerequisites listed: authority damage curves, initial-level distributions, real-time operation (E20-R10); runtime, lead-time reliability, data permissions (E22-R9); institutional reluctance and lost supply storage (E25-R6).

**Limits and gaps**
- No universal skill threshold (E18-R4). Scoring weights and Stage C thresholds not reported (E18-R8). Selection is qualitative (E18-R7). WCM wording and approval workflow not given (E18-R9). Legal constraints not fully listed (E18-R2).
- Consultation criteria not reported (E21-R6). No deployment trial in E20, E22, or E25 (E20-R10, E22-R9, E25-R6).

**Example values**
- More than 400 USACE WCM-governed reservoirs (E18-R8). About 260 min per-event optimization; one-day-ahead forecast for a basin with ~2-day concentration time (E22-R9).

## EP05-A Calculate feasible level or storage ceilings

**When papers did this**
- When a candidate level above FLWL was proposed before a forecasted inflow (E01-E4, E12-E2), for each scenario/event (E09-E3, E11-E3), or when initializing simulations and seasonal bounds (E19-E7, E29-E3, E41-E6, E34-E4, E44-E2).

**How**
- Lead-time mass balance with safety discharge (Eq. (5) in E01; rising-stage pre-release, normal operation, recession refill) (E01-E4).
- Two-period over-FLWL volume (Eqs. (7)–(10) in E09) plus a worst-case release-time check (Eqs. (11)–(13) in E09) (E09-E3, E09-E4).
- D-PSL from Eq. (7) for 1/2/3/5 days, then the minimum (Eq. (8) in E11) (E11-E3).
- Early pre-release with an FLWL-restoration safeguard (Eqs. (1)–(5) in E12) (E12-E2).
- VCWL via Eq. (9) over vapor-only and precipitation forecast periods (E19-E6); common upper bound from the Eq. (7) table (E19-E7).
- Dynamic bounds by season segment and lead time (E29-E3).
- Capacity-constrained prerelease bounds inside a deterministic benchmark optimization (E34-E4).
- Largest candidate IOWL per stage with credibility area K ≤ Pdesign=0.1% (E41-E6).
- Aggregate storage including maximum CSR storage in the boundaries (E44-E2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Deterministic capacity bounds (E01-E4, E19-E7, E34-E4) vs. worst-case reduction (E09-E4) vs. minimum across windows (E11-E3) vs. risk-grid selection (E41-E6); the cards give no rule for choosing.

**Limits and gaps**
- No controller or authorization procedure (E01-E4). The E19 storage-elevation curve and rounding are not reported (E19-E6). 66 m is a case setting (E19-E7). E29 bounds are adopted from prior work (E29-E3). The E41 grid is conservative (E41-E6). Some CSR estimates are conservative (E44-E2).

**Example values**
- E09: 5-day/6-h → 63.4×10⁶ m³, level 126 m (1 m above FLWL); worst-case safe 23.0×10⁶ m³, 125.4 m (E09-E3, E09-E4); inputs qm=200, qA=2500 m³/s, β=0.5 (E09-E3).
- E19: 30–80 m³/s inflow, 150–200 m³/s prerelease, 48 h → 66 m (E19-E7).
- E29 Scheme I upper bounds at 1 day: 149.70/146.50/150.30 m; Scheme II: 146.10 m (E29-E3).
- E41: 202.5 m (May, 0.0977%), 200.5 m (Jun–Jul, 0.0990%), 204 m (Aug–Sep, 0.0981%) (E41-E6).

## EP05-B Derive bounds from risk or safety requirements and screen

**When papers did this**
- Before solving an allocation or release decision once risk tolerances and forecast distributions were available (E03-E5, E05-E3, E15-E2, E24-E3), for each candidate decision (E03-E6, E41-E5), and in daily ensemble or FIRO risk tests (E07-E4, E50-E4, E45-E6).

**How**
- E03: R_{1,max}=ΔtQ_thresh, R_{2,max}=(T−1)ΔtQ_thresh; chance constraints P(R_2>R_{2,max})≤r_a and P(R_2>R_{2,min})≥P_e, inverted through the error CDF to get d_min, d_max (E03-E5); candidates are rejected when current risk or capacity conditions fail (E03-E6).
- E05: RFCC=R_{2,max}−Ī_2, feasible zone ABCD (E05-E3, E05-E4); r_max branch (r_c0, r_e, r_cr) chosen from Table 2 according to expected inflow and r_a (E05-E7).
- E15: risk r=1−p_R(R_lim) and the percentile condition (Eqs. (14)–(16) in E15) (E15-E2).
- E24: Δ=(T+1)qthres−(s0+q̄1+q̄2), with thresholds K1, K2 (E24-E3).
- Ensemble exceedance fraction r_t compared with a lead-time tolerance curve R_t (E07-E4). E50: risk over 14 days vs a piecewise-linear curve (x1 zero-risk days, x2 slope), TOCS multiplied by x3, target release = max(R_FIRO, baseline) (E50-E4).
- Credibility area K over 14 frequencies, accepted if K ≤ Pdesign (E41-E5).
- CVaR constraint (Eq. (9) in E45) added to the risk limits (E45-E6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Analytical chance-constraint bounds (E03, E05, E15, E24) assume a forecast-error distribution; ensemble-count tests (E07, E50) use member exceedances; E41 uses credibility; E45 adds tail risk when B1's tail loss is above the threshold (E45-E6).
- E03 and E05 both use d_min and R_{2,max} but define them differently (E03-E5, E05-E2); do not merge.

**Limits and gaps**
- The no-intermediate-flow assumption is required (E03-E5); zero Stage-1 risk depends on the triggering assumptions (E03-E6). The 15.9% switch depends on normal errors (E05-E7). Feasibility is analytical only (E05-E4).
- Members treated as equally likely; curve parameterization not in the main text (E07-E4). r_a and update handling not fully specified (E15-E2). Δ>0 required (E24-E3). Integration method not reported (E41-E5). x1/x2/x3 values not reported (E50-E4).

**Example values**
- E07: 111,000 acre-feet threshold, 61 members, 15 days (E07-E4). E50: 40 sampled members (E50-E4).
- E45 B2: FLWLs 145.3/147.3/149.3/151.4 m, generation +4.91%, risk 1.939% (E45-E6).

## EP05-C Impose facility constraints in simulation or optimization

**When papers did this**
- At each decision step or candidate evaluation in simulation, MPC, stochastic programming, or evolutionary search (E01-E6, E02-E3, E16-E2, E17-E3, E22-E6, E35-E5, E36-E4, E49-E5), and when configuring cases (E20-E1, E49-E3).

**How**
- Storage-update constraints in the form S_{t+1}=S_t+Q_t−R_t or V_t^i=V_{t−1}^i+(I_t^i−Q_t^i)Δt (E16-E2, E36-E4, E22-E6, E35-E5, E49-E5, E02-E3).
- Release bounds 0≤R_t≤R_max and ramping |R_t−R_{t−1}|≤Ramp (E16-E2, E17-E3); level-dependent outlet capacity O ≤ O_max(Z) (E36-E4); spillway capacity curve (E35-E5).
- MPC: apply only the first release, update with the observed inflow, re-optimize (E17-E3).
- Violations handled by: rejection/penalty (E22-E6, E49-E5); dynamic repair of rigid constraints and violation ranking for flexible ones (E15-E6); capping and backward redistribution of member schedules (E07-E6); a release floor R_{1,min} applied after mass balance (E03-E9); clipping s1 to 0 or K (E24-E6).
- Threshold-based mode logic with downstream stage limits (E01-E6). The deterministic equivalent solved with LINGO Global Solver (E02-E3). Results checked against limits, e.g. Three Gorges level <171 m (E15-E8).
- Case thresholds kq=MCC, kv=FCL (E20-E1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Terminal level: hard target storage (E49-E5), soft (E02-R4), or possibly softened (E36-E4).
- Constraint handling differs by solver (GA penalty/reject in E22-E6, RSEA repair in E15-E6, heuristic capping in E07-E6); the cards give no comparison.

**Limits and gaps**
- No solver tolerances or infeasibility handling (E02-E3, E35-E5). No ramp or gate check in E03 (E03-E9). Repair algorithm not reported (E15-E6); "flexible" does not mean optional in deployment (E15-E6).
- R_max and RR assumed constant (E17-E3). GA and AR-MOEA constraint handling not detailed (E22-E6, E49-E5). No emergency protocol (E24-E6). Damaged equation glyphs (E49-E3, E49-E5). E05 calibration not reported (E05-E2).

**Example values**
- TGR: Shashi <44.5 m (56,700 m³/s), drawdown to 145 m, maximum discharge 53,900 m³/s (E01-E6).
- E07 worked example: 5,249 acre-feet excess, 331 cfs/day initially → 529 cfs on days 1–5; Hopland 8,000 cfs (E07-E6).
- XHD: release 0–3,300 m³/s, fluctuation 1,500 m³/s, safe release 1,850 m³/s, 3-h interval, 45 periods (E49-E3).
- Spain reservoirs, e.g. Puentes MCC 440 m³/s, Fuensanta 75 m³/s (E20-E1).

## EP05-D Check implementation readiness

**When papers did this**
- When forecast-informed decisions or a viability assessment needed usable information, and when a favorable candidate was screened for operation (E18-E3, E18-E7).

**How**
- Forecast and monitoring capability assessed for the site; Prado precipitation gauges were adequate, but two stream-gage locations needed improvement (E18-E3).
- Pool elevations checked against spillway-crest behavior; a hydrologic engineering management plan used as the comparison framework; Lake Mendocino screened for standard-tool implementability (E18-E7).

**Limits and gaps**
- Forecast products, skill statistics, and gage repair procedure not reported (E18-E3). No formal safety margins or acceptance tests (E18-E7).
- Singleton evidence (only E18).
