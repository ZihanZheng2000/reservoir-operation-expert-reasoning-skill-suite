# Reference for unit 2.2-forecast-reliability-and-timing: what the papers did

This file covers how papers judged forecast reliability by lead time, timed forecast use against drawdown needs, checked forecast quality and value, derived accuracy requirements from risk, handled risk beyond the forecast horizon, and built, prepared, quantified and verified forecasts; it summarizes 91 steps from 37 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; all values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP03-A Reliability varies with lead time; use forecasts by stage

**When papers did this**
- Ensemble storage trajectories might exceed a critical level, and the risk test had to depend on forecast day (E07-R3).
- Choosing an operating horizon and uncertainty model from historical errors by lead time (E10-R1, E27-R3).
- Forecast information updated as a flood approached, or indicators changed across their horizons (E21-R3, E19-R5).
- A dry interval was forecast while the reservoir was near the upper flood-limited water level (FLWL) (E09-R1).
- Explaining case results across lead times and design return periods (E42-R6).

**How**
- Lead-time-dependent risk tolerance: risk = percentage of 61 ensemble members above 111,000 acre-feet; tolerance 0% for forecast days 1–5, rising afterward; release computed only if risk exceeds tolerance (E07-R3).
- Stage division by error: a reliable forecast horizon (RFH) defined where nowcast/short-term relative error is generally <20%, split into current, second and third stages with stage-specific uncertainty coefficients (E10-R1).
- Decision specificity matched to lead time: qualitative 7–14-day forecasts for pre-discharge/reserve capacity; quantitative peak flow/time 3–5 days and 1 day ahead for specific releases and peak staggering (E21-R3).
- Indicator sequence: vapor (VCWL/PP) for early level adjustment, precipitation for stronger prerelease, streamflow for final drawdown to FLWL; post-event forecasts for prefill (E19-R5).
- Two-period release: water-use pre-release while inflow is below utilization discharge in a dry period; switch to flood-control pre-release when a large inflow is forecast (E09-R1).
- Error vs lead time: E27 inspected mean/SD at 1–6 h and used a stationary Gaussian (μ≈0, σ≈600 m³/s) only for flows >12,000 m³/s (E27-R3). E42 compared risk rates and generation losses across four lead times and return periods (E42-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E27 treated error as stationary only in the high-flow regime; E10 kept stage-specific coefficients, arguing a single stationary assumption discards information (E27-R3, E10-R1).

**Limits and gaps**
- E07 tolerance curve was built heuristically; construction details are in supporting information (E07-R3).
- E10's 20% threshold is a paper choice, not authoritative (E10-R1).
- E19 gives no complete pseudocode or all thresholds (E19-R5); E09 triggers are not fully operationalized (E09-R1).
- E21 numerical details cover two floods only (E21-R3); E27 low-flow data too sparse (E27-R3); E42 fits no general scaling law (E42-R6).

**Example values**
- Threshold 111,000 acre-feet, 3 ft below the emergency-spillway crest (E07-R3).

## RP03-B Forecasts must arrive early enough, with the right indicator

**When papers did this**
- A candidate upper level could prevent timely drawdown (E01-R3), or dynamic FLWL scenarios needed lead-time inputs (E09-R2).
- Formulating a forecast-based rule or pre-storm level (E11-R1, E27-R2, E37-R4).
- The existing forecast horizon was too short for prerelease (E19-R2, E19-R3).
- Explaining MPC performance or choosing a horizon (E17-R4, E35-R2).

**How**
- Mass-balance admissibility: upper bound limited by Eq. (5) in E01, linking candidate storage V_u, current-FLWL storage V_0, inflow over lead time T_c, and downstream safety discharge Q_s; level above FLWL allowed only if pre-release can restore it in time (E01-R3).
- Historical lead-time estimation: natural lead time = end of main rain to flood peak, fitted with a normal distribution; dry duration (consecutive ≤3-mm days) fitted with a negative exponential, chosen as the conservative option over asymptotic regression (E09-R2).
- Indicator choice: maximum 1-, 2-, 3-, 5-day flood volume instead of peak only, for reservoirs able to smooth intraday peaks (E11-R1); 6-h forecast runoff volume chosen over 3/12 h to match forecast accuracy, 6–9 h runoff response and ~6 h upstream travel time (E27-R2); 72-h cumulative net rainfall bands with level-dependent releases (E37-R4).
- Precursor indicator: PP = initial PWV + inward vapor flux (evapotranspiration optional), assuming all inflowing vapor converts to precipitation; Eqs. (1)–(6) in E19 (E19-R2, E19-R3).
- Feasibility factors: maximum release, ramping rate and lead time sampled globally, normalized by peak inflow (E17-R4).
- Perfect-information horizon test: compare closed-loop peak releases over horizons 6–48 h and pick where responses stabilize (E35-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Volume targets (E11) were limited to reservoirs adequate to regulate peaks (E11-R1); E27 tied horizon to watershed response (E27-R2); E19 used vapor when precipitation/streamflow horizons were too short (E19-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Release scheduling within the lead time not fully specified (E01-R3); no real-time dry-period forecast model (E09-R2).
- E17 ranges are not engineering-feasible at every site (E17-R4).
- PP is a safety-oriented assumption, not observed precipitation (E19-R3); 72-h/48-h setup is case-specific (E19-R2).
- E27 candidate-rule calculations not all given (E27-R2); E35's "approximately infinite horizon" is an assumption (E35-R2).
- E37 rules cover only the rising stage; Q_c is undefined in the text (E37-R4).

**Example values**
- Effective lead time up to 10 h (E09-R2). Horizons 6/12 h delayed release; ≥18 h gave similar safe responses below 200 m³/s for a 100-year flood (E35-R2). Rainfall bands ≤43, 43–55, 55–62, 62–79, 79–90, >90 mm from initial level 325.0 m (E37-R4).

## RP03-C Verify forecast quality before use

**When papers did this**
- Monte Carlo or scenario generation needed forecast/actual pairs or an error distribution (E01-R6, E28-R2, E36-R2).
- Selecting or combining forecast information for operation or optimization (E14-R3, E23-R2).
- Screening whether forecast-informed operation can be trusted at a site (E18-R4), or moving to a case basin (E28-R5).

**How**
- Metric-to-distribution: zero-mean normal relative error; σ from qualified rate α (Eq. (3)) or from R²/NSE (Eq. (8)); synthetic forecast Q̂_t = Q_t(1+e_t) (E01-R6, E28-R2, E36-R2). Permitted relative error 0.2 (E28-R2, E36-R2).
- Calibration/verification split with α, NSE/R² and volume relative error; Grade A permits official use under the cited Chinese standard (E28-R5).
- Ensemble verification before use: MAE, CRPS, rank histograms, Brier score for 1400 m³/s exceedance, against deterministic and observed-rainfall references (E23-R2).
- Model combination: Gaussian Cloud similarity per segment, averaged and normalized to weights over LSTM/GRU/XGBoost (E14-R3).
- Screening: poor skill at sufficient lead time can exclude a site at Stage A (E18-R4).

**Choosing between approaches**
- α captures peak/volume information (E28-R2) or peak-discharge qualification (E36-R2); R² uses the full hydrograph (E28-R2, E36-R2). Grade ranges can be used when only a qualitative grade is known (E28-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Normality and zero mean are assumptions (E01-R6, E28-R2, E36-R2).
- E23 ensemble is under-dispersed; metric superiority is case- and lead-time-dependent (E23-R2).
- E18 gives no numerical skill or lead-time threshold (E18-R4). E28 reports no operational trial (E28-R5). E14 segment boundaries not reported (E14-R3).

**Example values**
- Lead times evaluated at 1 and 2 days (E01-R6); about five extra days of useful horizon from the ensemble (E23-R2); calibration 1997–2002, verification 2003–2004 (E28-R5).

## RP03-D Forecast accuracy is not operational value

**When papers did this**
- Interpreting forecast-informed benefit or transferability (E07-R8, E17-R6), comparing deterministic/ensemble/operational results (E14-R5, E16-R6), or validating calibrated rules out of sample (E46-R6).

**How**
- Three-reference comparison: baseline (no forecast/climatology), perfect future inflow, and HEFS ensemble under a common MPC; remaining gap attributed to forecast quality and parameter feasibility (E17-R6).
- Post-assessment with observed ("Perfect") inflows, kept separate from forecast metrics and optimization objectives (E14-R5).
- Joint diagnosis of bias, eCRPS in 30-day windows, timing and storage/drawdown (E16-R6).
- Pseudo-optimization with perfect forecasts quantifies the gap of the rule; held-out events test generalization (E46-R6).
- Conditional transferability tied to hindcast reliability and site constraints (E07-R8).

**Limits and gaps**
- "Perfect" is a simulated benchmark, not deployable (E14-R5).
- Synthetic forecasts match bias but not all long-lead eCRPS degradation (E16-R6).
- Results may differ for less skillful events (E17-R6); four-event validation is case evidence only (E46-R6).
- E07 did not simulate the 1964 flood of record; tolerance curve sensitive to training sequence (E07-R8).

**Example values**
- Upper forecast range most reliable at 4–15 day leads; five events with Hopland flows above 15,000 cfs (E07-R8). Four unused typhoons for validation (E46-R6).

## RP03-E Derive the required forecast accuracy from acceptable risk

**When papers did this**
- Assessing whether a forecast with uncertainty r supports both objectives (E05-R6, E05-R7), setting a dynamic design flood (E11-R2), testing robustness to precision (E26-R6), or given an accepted risk (E28-R6, E36-R8).

**How**
- Analytical thresholds (E05): r_c from dW_1*/dr = 0 (mass balance only), r_c0 when W_1* = 0, r_e when W_1* = RFCC − d_min and d = d_min, r_cr when both bind; r_max chosen among r_c, r_c0, r_e by expected inflow and acceptable risk; beyond r_cr even empty storage fails (E05-R6). Preference x and r_max interact (Figs. 10–11 in E05) (E05-R7).
- Skill-to-error bound: σ²_ε = (1 − Cp)·var(V_ts); choose δ_P,ts so Prob(ε_ts ≥ δ_P,ts) = P_des (E11-R2).
- Precision sensitivity: HR surfaces at three historical precision levels (σ_ε1, σ_ε2) (E26-R6).
- Reverse use of risk curves: read the accuracy (or safe level) at which risk falls below tolerance (E28-R6, E36-R8).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E05 and E11 derive thresholds analytically; E28 and E36 read them from simulated risk curves (E05-R6, E11-R2, E28-R6, E36-R8). E36 also offers lowering/raising the safe level instead of improving forecasts (E36-R8).

**Limits and gaps**
- All thresholds are model- and case-specific (E05-R6, E05-R7, E26-R6, E28-R6, E36-R8). Normal error assumed for illustration (E11-R2).
- Direction effects can reverse: higher conservation preference lowers r_max at small expected inflow but may reverse with a binding storage constraint (E05-R7); current- and future-phase precision act in different directions, release can be nonmonotonic (E26-R6).

**Example values**
- Accepted check risk 3×10⁻⁵ implies R² ≈ 0.85 or α ≈ 0.55 (E28-R6). For 6% risk, α > 90.7% or R² > 94.1%; at R² = 85%, Z_s = 846.85 m (E36-R8).

## RP03-F Beyond the forecast horizon and unequal horizons across reservoirs

**When papers did this**
- Forecast information ended before the operation horizon (E33-R2, E40-R2), or adjacent reservoirs had different lead times (E33-R3).

**How**
- Two-stage risk: stage 1 failures counted across forecast scenarios; stage 2 via design-flood routing; combined (Eqs. (2)–(4) in E33) (E33-R2). E40 split lead-time risk R1 and unpredicted-time risk R2, linked through scenario end states (E40-R2).
- Multi-reservoir alignment: downstream inflow by channel routing; design hydrographs chosen with start-time differences matching lead-time differences (E33-R3).

**Limits and gaps**
- E33's causal sequence is a reconstruction (E33-R2); lead-time-difference symbols are corrupted in the text; only two reservoirs shown, more would need rebuilding the end-level/risk relationship (E33-R3).
- E40 assumes the design-flood representation is adequate after the horizon (E40-R2).

## RP03-G How to use a forecast that may be wrong

**When papers did this**
- Point forecasts were insufficient for a safe release bound (E15-R2), a probabilistic hydrograph had to become one operating input (E29-R3), forecast error was detected during operation (E37-R7), or forecasts might fail in severe storms (E46-R2).

**How**
- Probabilistic input: DGDNN multi-step forecasts converted by HMR into a conditional distribution p(x²_t | x¹_t) (E15-R2).
- Quantile scan: run the operation for every 1–100% quantile and keep a quantile/range meeting safety and benefit criteria; deterministic forecast also tested (E29-R3).
- Error-sign correction: underforecast/false negative → enlarge discharge; overforecast/false positive → opposite correction (E37-R7).
- Measurement-based rule as fallback, accepting some loss of ideal attenuation; perfect-forecast optimization only as reference (E46-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E15 and E29 keep forecast uncertainty in the input; E37 corrects after detection; E46 avoids reliance on forecasts when they may degrade (E15-R2, E29-R3, E37-R7, E46-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- E15 skill/calibration not supplied (E15-R2); E29 gives no general quantile rule (E29-R3); E37 trigger thresholds not fully specified (E37-R7); E46 reliability not shown outside validation cases (E46-R2).

## EP02-A Build or train a forecast model

**When papers did this**
- Forecasts were needed to drive pre-release/refill, uncertainty simulation, or a case operation (E01-E2, E03-E11, E28-E1, E40-E1, E43-E2), or a probabilistic/precursor input was needed (E15-E1, E19-E4, E29-E1).

**How**
- Conceptual/linear models: MLSM with Nash impulse responses, optimized by Genetic → Rosenbrock → Simplex (E01-E2, E28-E1); Xinanjiang for intervening basins (E28-E1, E40-E1); Xinanjiang 377 subunits plus 1-D hydrodynamic routing and error correction (E21-E3); distributed SAC-SMA in SOBEK, manually calibrated, NSE (E43-E2); empirical rainfall–runoff diagram and unit hydrograph (E03-E11); storage-function recession model with K, p (E46-E5).
- Data-driven: LSTM/GRU/XGBoost on MIC > 0.3 inputs per cluster (E14-E2) combined by Cloud weights w_k = S_k/ΣS_k (E14-E3); four-unit LSTM, Adam, MSE, 100 epochs, batch 1 (E22-E3).
- Probabilistic: DGDNN + HMR (E15-E1); log-Weibull marginals with normal-quantile dependence, posterior via Eq. (1) in E29 (E29-E1); ensembles from adaptive Metropolis parameter sampling plus precipitation uncertainty (E40-E1).
- Precursor: PP from atmospheric water balance (Eqs. (1)–(6)), converted to floodwater by Eq. (8) with runoff coefficient 0.75, Xinanjiang as refinement (E19-E4, E19-E5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Model details delegated or unreported: E14 architecture/grids (E14-E2), E15 calibration metrics (E15-E1), E22 split and hyperparameter search (E22-E3), E43 NSE values (E43-E2), E21 skill metrics (E21-E3).
- Cloud forecast can be below the best single model and underestimates peaks (E14-E3). OCR corrupts E19 Eq. (5) and Eq. (8) units (E19-E4, E19-E5). E29 excludes rainfall-input uncertainty (E29-E1).

**Example values**
- E01 validation R² = 95.49%, RE = 0.24%, α = 94.52% (E01-E2). E28 verification R² = 92.23%, α = 100%, RE = −1.17% (E28-E1). E40 median 90%-interval R²/RE 0.96/0.02 (calibration), 0.94/−0.01 (validation) (E40-E1). E03 error N(−2.7, 20.88²), 86.5% within ±20% (E03-E11).

## EP02-B Prepare and correct forecast inputs

**When papers did this**
- A long consistent test record was needed (E07-E2), synthetic forecasts were required (E13-E8), or inputs had to be aligned, corrected, or naturalized (E19-E2, E21-E2, E22-E2, E23-E1).

**How**
- Hindcast assembly: 26-year HEFS hindcast, hourly, 61 members, 15-day horizon, not treated as reproducing forecaster refinements (E07-E2).
- Synthetic forcing: monthly bias correction of TMAX/TMIN, conditional mean treatment for precipitation, PCA (three components) + kNN, traces run through HEFS (E13-E8).
- Bias correction of NWP ensemble precipitation (NCEP, KMA, UKMO) before LSTM (E22-E2).
- Downscaling 0.5° QPF with Thiessen polygons, disaggregating 6-h to hourly, hourly state updating from observed-simulated differences (E23-E1).
- Hourly 0.25° ERA5 PWV/IVT and Thiessen mean areal precipitation (E19-E2).
- Naturalized flows accounting for upstream reservoir operations via travel time and water balance (E21-E2).

**Limits and gaps**
- E07 hindcast: underforecast bias Dec–Mar, useful spread and upper-range reliability at 4–15 days (E07-E2).
- Unreported details: E13 PCA/HEFS settings, 2–3 days per sample (E13-E8); E22 bias-correction method (E22-E2); E21 naturalization equations (E21-E2); E19 gauge counts and IVT domain (E19-E2).
- E23 applied no bias correction (E23-E1).

## EP02-C Quantify forecast error and skill by lead time

**When papers did this**
- Before risk simulation, optimization, or scenario generation, whenever historical forecast/observation pairs existed (E01-E3, E06-E2, E12-E1, E13-E3, E36-E1, E37-E2, E42-E1, E49-E1).

**How**
- Error definitions differ; keep them apart:
  - Q̂_t = Q_t(1 + e_t) (relative error, E01-E3).
  - e = O − F for single traces; e = E[F|O] − F for ensemble members, sign as printed (E13-E3).
  - e = (W_forecast − W_actual)/W_forecast × 100% (E41-E2).
  - e = (Q_f − Q_o)/Q_o × 100% per lead time (E42-E1).
  - Observed minus average reforecast inflow (E49-E1).
  - Rolling relative-error reductions between updates (Eqs. (6)–(8), E04-E2).
- Distribution fitting: normal (E01-E3, E10-E1, E11-E1, E28-E2, E37-E2 with χ² test); Wakeby selected by RMSE/AIC among many candidates, with Student-t copula across 1–3 days (E06-E2, E38-E2); maximum entropy with 99.99% interval (E12-E1); fuzzy triangular vs Cauchy membership (E41-E2).
- Lead-time structure: piecewise σ(t) = a(t)Q_in(t) by stage (E10-E1); separate samples per lead time (E27-E2, E42-E1, E49-E1); natural lead time from rain end to peak (E09-E2).
- Quality grading by α and R² (E01-E3, E36-E1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Normal assumption tested by χ² (E37-E2) or assumed without fit check (E28-E2); E06 and E38 compared candidate distributions; E41 chose Cauchy for shape/stability with limited data (E41-E2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Sample sizes or test details missing (E27-E2, E34-E1, E36-E1, E38-E2); E12 fit diagnostics missing (E12-E1); E04 error range is illustrative only (E04-E2).

**Example values**
- α 92.36% (1-day), 91.41% (2-day), Grade A (E01-E3). σ = 0.114 from α = 92%, σ = 0.190 from R² = 92.23% (E28-E2). RFH 24 h, a0 = 0.1, a1 = 0.2, b0 = 0.008 (E10-E1). 1–6 h SDs 525–623 m³/s (E27-E2). α = 89.5%, R² = 91% from 159 floods (E36-E1). N(0.019, 0.188²), χ² = 4.1926 vs 5.9915 (E37-E2). Lead time mean 9.90 h (E09-E2). 129 samples, 12 floods, 45-h horizon (E49-E1).

## EP02-D Verify operational usability of forecasts

**When papers did this**
- Forecasts or synthetic ensembles had to be credible enough for operation or risk propagation (E13-E9, E22-E4, E23-E2, E35-E4, E42-E3, E42-E5), aggregate parity was insufficient for extremes (E13-E10), or site viability needed monitoring checks (E18-E3).

**How**
- Ensemble diagnostics: rank histograms, eCRPS, BSE spread–error, DTS tests at p = 0.1, stratified by flow (E13-E9); MAE, CRPS, rank histograms, Brier score (E23-E2); mean CRPS and band coverage by lead time and event phase (E35-E4); Taylor diagram (NRMSE, correlation, SD) per member and mean (E22-E4); NSE by lead time (E50-E5).
- Extreme-event coverage: synthetic vs HEFS cumulative means/variances for the ten largest events (E13-E10); recession-flood coverage and no-rainfall hit rate (E34-E9).
- Error-model fit: K–S at 0.01, RMSE/MAPE, Q–Q (E42-E3); coverage ratio CR inside the 95% interval (E42-E5).
- Component checks: Xinanjiang NSE and ERA5 PWV vs radiosondes (E19-E12); refill vs remaining inflow plus bias/eCRPS (E16-E9); gauge adequacy (E18-E3); comparator BMA/S-BMA scenarios (E14-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- DTS non-rejection is not proof of identity (E13-E9); E13-E10 validates against a single HEFS realization and finds variance underprediction at longer leads.
- CRPS does not validate operational safety (E35-E4); E23 ensemble under-spread (E23-E2); E22 peaks underestimated, no full tables (E22-E4).
- E19 checks do not validate deployment (E19-E12); E50 saved forecasts are not hindcasts (E50-E5).

**Example values**
- Ensemble beats deterministic after about 48–100 h, useful to ~15 days (E23-E2). No-rainfall hit rate 85.9%, missing-alarm ~14% (E34-E9). IGMD CR 92–95%; K–S 0.012–0.019 below 0.044 (E42-E3, E42-E5). ERA5 R² > 0.97, RMSE < 4.0 mm (E19-E12). 3–5 day forecasts highly accurate (E50-E5).
