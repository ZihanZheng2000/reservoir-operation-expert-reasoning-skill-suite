# Reference for unit 3.5-model-and-method-choice: what the papers did

This file covers how papers chose system representations, analytical routes, solvers, uncertainty-computation methods, forecast-model construction, criterion handling, and optimization or rule-search formulations; it summarizes 104 steps from 41 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP08-A Reservoir system representation and computational trade-offs

**When papers did this**
- When forecast uncertainty had to be represented somewhere in a forecast chain (E13-R3); when thousands of policy evaluations across sites/parameters were needed (E17-R2) or a common policy form had to be optimized across 14 reservoirs (E50-R2).
- When downstream flow had to be predicted inside repeated optimization (E23-R4), or releases had to account for delayed river response and several reservoirs (E43-R2).
- When uncertainty had to be carried through routing step by step (E30-R3, E31-R3), when a tree-based MPC had to be configured (E35-R8), or when a nonlinear routing function blocked a closed-form fuzzy program (E41-R4).
- When reservoir count made direct risk analysis difficult (E44-R2) and channel segments lay between reservoirs and control stations (E44-R3).

**How**
- Compare a cheap direct route with an expensive process route instead of assuming the process route is better: fit HEFS output directly (syn-HEFS) versus synthesize GEFS meteorology and rerun HEFS (syn-GEFS) (E13-R3).
- Use a simplified internal model inside optimization, with a detailed model outside it:
  - E23-R4 screened SOBEK, kinematic-wave, integrator-delay and Muskingum-Cunge models by Bias, MAE, RMSE, NSE and visual gradient propagation, and chose integrator-delay; full dynamic hydraulics stayed as reference (it is too costly and lacks an adjoint).
  - E43-R2 used a linear state-space internal model with SOBEK for detailed behavior and state updates; model losses neglected, free-flow structure assumptions.
- Local linearization of hydraulic curves over short intervals, each paper with its own notation:
  - E30-R3: `q̄(t)=μ_t H̄(t)+ν_t`, `V̄(t)=α_t H̄(t)+β_t` around the current mean level; coefficients enter A(t), B(t), C(t) (Eqs. (8)–(12) in E30).
  - E31-R3: `q̄=a_t Z̄+b_t`, `V̄=c_t Z̄+d_t`, giving an `ω1–ω3` level-error recursion (Eqs. (5)–(16) in E31).
- Scenario-based convex MPC chosen for scalability over SDP (E17-R2).
- Tree size/horizon as a trade-off: compare closed-loop peak release and CPU time over 1–32 branches and 18–48 h (E35-R8).
- Dense frequency coverage plus fuzzy simulation: sample errors from d-level cut sets, route each hydrograph, compute credibility curve and area K (E41-R4).
- Aggregate reservoirs into virtual reservoirs at flood-control stations, recursively for series systems, then decompose (E44-R2); add Muskingum-based channel storage reservoirs (CSR) to aggregate storage, with conservative estimates where hydraulic storage is unknown (E44-R3).
- Compact policy parameterization: lead days of zero allowable risk, a slope, and a TOCS multiplier (three parameters per reservoir) instead of 14 lead-time parameters, to limit overfitting (E50-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- syn-HEFS when HEFS output and observations exist and cost matters; syn-GEFS when a process pathway or manipulated forecast skill is needed (E13-R3).
- Simplified routing/internal models when the model must sit inside repeated optimization (E23-R4, E43-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- syn-HEFS sufficiency is case/model bounded (E13-R3); MPC scalability is not proof of best policy, and trace/tree feedback was left for future work (E17-R2).
- No independent future-event validation of the chosen routing model (E23-R4); transfer limited by case-specific calibration (E43-R2).
- Linearization fitting errors not reported (E30-R3, E31-R3); normality is a model choice (E31-R3).
- No general branch-selection rule; user-defined (E35-R8).
- Parallel-system aggregation is stated, not illustrated (E44-R2); hydraulic models could improve CSR (E44-R3).
- Parameter bounds not reported (E50-R2).

**Example values**
- 16 branches and 48 h selected because 16–32 branch outputs overlap while cost rises (E35-R8).
- 14 frequency levels (E41-R4).

## RP08-B Analytical derivation route

**When papers did this**
- When a constrained allocation between conservation storage and safety margin had to be solved for given forecast/risk conditions (E03-R6, E05-R4), when total EFSM had to be split between stages (E24-R4), when a hedging release Qh had to be chosen in a feasible interval (E25-R4), or when a two-phase stochastic model had to be solved (E26-R4).

**How**
- Check convexity, then use KKT stationarity/complementarity to decide between an interior and a boundary solution:
  - E03-R6: interior requires `f_1(W_1^*)=f_2(d^*)`; binding bounds' shadow prices explain all-to-one-objective or capped-storage cases (Eqs. 31–35 in E03). Branches: lower-storage boundary, interior hedging, upper-storage boundary, minimum-safety-margin boundary.
  - E05-R4: interior equality via Eq. (9); compare marginal utilities at critical points to get `x_D=x_min`, `x_B=x_max`, `x_A` (Eqs. (8)–(12) in E05).
  - E24-R4: Gaussian expected damage is decreasing and convex in EFSM; equal marginal damage reduction unless a nonnegativity bound is active; λ1 = marginal value of total EFSM, λ2/λ3 = bound values (Eqs. (15)–(19) in E24).
  - E25-R4: set the derivative of total expected damage to zero, equating current marginal cost and expected future marginal benefit, using `dQsi/dQh=−tf/tsi` (Eqs. (9)–(15) in E25).
  - E26-R4: reduce to one variable V1 over [V1min,V1max] using water balance and V2=0; express Pu/Ps as normal tail integrals; branches V1max (shortage-risk reduction dominates) or V1min (flood-risk increase dominates) (Eqs. (19)–(29) in E26).

**Limits and gaps**
- Multiplier interpretation is a model interpretation, not observed operator judgment (E03-R6); equality does not mean decisions are unconstrained (E05-R4).
- Sufficiency relies on the stated convex model (E24-R4); E25-R4 also needs convexity in Qh, differentiability and independence.
- E26 Eq. (29) holds for V1*≤Vu and normal assumptions; the formula OCR is partly corrupted, so it is not reproduced (E26-R4).

## RP08-C Choice of solution and search algorithm

**When papers did this**
- When a risk-constrained upper bound was needed before operation and outcomes combined several uncertainties (E01-R7); when the problem was non-linear, constrained and many-objective (E15-R6); when a multiobjective policy was needed per inflow scenario (E22-R6); when a high-dimensional nonlinear scenario model had to be solved (E36-R5).

**How**
- Monte Carlo search: assume Z, compute four risks, accept if Eq. (13) holds, else reduce Z; repeat until the mean bound changes by less than 0.05 m (E01-R7).
- RSEA evolutionary search with region-associated subproblems, weights/neighborhoods, ideal/nadir normalization; repair rigid constraints dynamically, rank flexible ones by violation (E15-R6).
- GA chosen for simplicity, robustness and flexibility, combined with grid search over parameters; one run per ensemble member and weight combination (E22-R6).
- Convert the scenario model to a nonlinear deterministic equivalent and solve with LINGO 17.0 Global Solver (bounds/reduction, branch-and-bound) (E36-R5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- The evidence links each choice to its problem: simulation search for combined uncertainties (E01-R7), many-objective constrained search (E15-R6), per-scenario multiobjective GA (E22-R6), and a global solver for a deterministic equivalent (E36-R5). No paper compares these choices with each other.

**Limits and gaps**
- No confidence interval for the bound (E01-R7); RSEA superiority holds only against the tested algorithms on this problem, and no global optimality is shown (E15-R6).
- GA population, rates, tolerance, encoding and constraint handling not reported (E22-R6); global optimality claimed by the solver, not checked independently (E36-R5).

**Example values**
- 200 repetitions (E01-R7).

## RP08-D Methods for computing uncertainty and risk

**When papers did this**
- When a joint distribution had to be represented without one high-dimensional PDF (E08-R3), possibly through time (E32-R2); when uncertainty had to be carried through nonlinear routing (E30-R2); when a smooth density was needed from limited scenario maxima (E36-R7); when risk had to keep source dependence and complex hazard geometry (E39-R3); when a mixture error model had to be configured (E42-R2).

**How**
- Bayesian networks with expert-specified structure:
  - E08: DAG from routing equations Eqs. (8)–(9) (E08-R3); training data from LHS inflow samples solved by an optimal flood-control model (minimize maximum outflow), equal-distance discretization with K chosen by precision, samples and cost (E08-R4); CPTs by MLE; prior, prediction (given V0/Qmax/IUmax) or diagnosis (given Hmax/QCmax) by Bayes/joint-tree propagation (E08-R5).
  - E32: dynamic BN factorized into static BNs plus links between adjacent time slices (Eqs. (1)–(4)) (E32-R2); LHS/physical routing samples plus expert causal order (E32-R3); equal-width bins `Wxi=(xi,max−xi,min)/Mxi`, MLE parameters (E32-R4).
- Stochastic differential equation of the storage balance, keeping covariance between adjacent error terms, with local linearization; other time points treated as independent in the union-risk step (E30-R2).
- Principle of maximum entropy (POME) exponential-polynomial density, Lagrange multipliers by Newton–Raphson, checked against a normal curve and P–P plot (E36-R7).
- Continuous integration `P(H) = ∬Ω fE,Y(e,y) de dy` over the hazard domain, preferred to MLTP's interval approximation, which stays as a comparator for single hazards (E39-R3).
- Gaussian mixture with K chosen by minimum AIC/BIC, k-means++ initialization, EM fitting, per lead time (E42-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E30-R2 chose SDE because numerical simulation depends on sample size and FOSM/AFOSM/JC cannot readily keep correlation or handle a non-explicit performance function.
- E39-R3 chose integration over MLTP for precision and for combined hazards (UHE/IHE).

**Limits and gaps**
- BN/DBN structure is expert-specified, not learned; no independent causal validation; elicitation process not reported (E08-R3, E32-R2, E32-R3).
- No complete or principled bin-count protocol or discretization-error bound (E08-R4, E32-R4); posterior inference does not authorize a release (E08-R5).
- SDE approximations are assumptions (E30-R2); POME order 3 or 4 said to be appropriate, exact order not stated (E36-R7); the "analytical" E39 framework still uses gridded routing/interpolation (E39-R3).

**Example values**
- 1,000 samples (E08-R4); K = 3 by both AIC and BIC (E42-R2).

## RP08-E Construction choices for data-driven forecast models

**When papers did this**
- When forecasting heterogeneous flood events from few records (E14-R1), defining AI model inputs (E14-R2), producing one forecast from several models (E14-R3), or needing fast real-time ensemble inflows (E22-R2).

**How**
- Cluster events into C1 small-scale and C2 large-scale before fitting (E14-R1).
- Keep precipitation lags with MIC above 0.3, per cluster (E14-R2).
- Combine LSTM, GRU and XGBoost with Gaussian Cloud weights: fit expectation, entropy, super-entropy per segment, compute similarity, average, normalize to weights (Eqs. (1)–(8) in E14) (E14-R3).
- Feed bias-corrected NWP ensemble precipitation into an LSTM to produce ensemble inflow members; deterministic precipitation gives deterministic inflow (E22-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Two clusters are case-specific; more return periods need more data (E14-R1). The 0.3 threshold and omission of streamflow/evaporation are study settings (E14-R2).
- Segment boundaries and some hyperparameters not in the main text (E14-R3). LSTM choice justified by prior studies and speed, with limited architecture/calibration comparison (E22-R2).

**Example values**
- 22 events (E14-R1); one-day-ahead forecasts (E22-R2).

## RP08-F Methods for handling evaluation criteria

**When papers did this**
- When estimating a criterion's influence on MCDM closeness (E47-R3) or its responsiveness (E47-R4); when Pearson coefficients showed widespread high correlation (E48-R4); when principal components needed weights (E48-R5).

**How**
- BP network weight importance Rj from absolute weighted input-hidden-output paths, averaged over N training cycles (Eqs. (8)–(13) in E47) (E47-R3).
- One-at-a-time perturbation by ±5, 10, 15, 20%, normalizing the +20% change into Gj (Eqs. (14)–(15) in E47) (E47-R4).
- PCA instead of deleting correlated criteria; keep the fewest components with cumulative variance above 90% (E48-R4).
- Improved entropy-to-weight conversion, because the conventional one fluctuates when entropy is near one; benefit/cost normalization differs; ln(f)=0 for zero proportions (Eqs. (7)–(11) in E48) (E48-R5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Weight importance is model-dependent, not causal (E47-R3); one-at-a-time perturbation misses interactions (E47-R4).
- No separately elicited preference weight (E48-R5).

**Example values**
- Two components retain 96.7% (E48-R4); weights E=(0.528, 0.472) (E48-R5).

## EP06-A Derive analytical or piecewise rules

**When papers did this**
- After feasibility checks, to compute optimal carryover storage and safety margin (E03-E7, E05-E2, E05-E3); to pick a rule branch from risk regime and expected inflow (E03-E8, E05-E5, E05-E7, E24-E4, E26-E4); to decide whether forecast uncertainty remains useful (E05-E6); for case demonstrations (E03-E12, E05-E9); for ensemble risky-member scheduling (E07-E6, E07-E7); for nested allocations (E10-E2, E10-E3); for pre-release when forecast inflow lies between Qdown and Qup (E12-E2).

**How**
- Two-stage storage–safety allocation (E03, E05):
  - E05-E2 defines `d=R_{2,max}-R̄_2` and `d_min=rU^{-1}(1-r_a)` under unbiased normal error, and minimizes weighted loss (Eq. (6) in E05); E05-E3 reduces stage 2 to `W_1+d=RFCC=R_{2,max}-Ī_2` with `0≤W_1≤min(W_max,RFCC-d_min)`.
  - E03-E7 minimizes L1+L2 over (W1,d) and uses KKT (Eq. 28 in E03); E03-E8 picks Case 1–3 rules from `d_min`, `d_0`, `d_e`, `W_1h` (Eqs. 44–46, Table 1 in E03).
  - E05-E5 solves Eqs. (10)–(12) for `x_min`, `x_max`, `x_A` and classifies Type 1–3 rules; `W_1^*=0` for `x<x_min`.
  - E05-E6 sets `dW_1^*/dr=0` to find `r_c` (Eq. (B1)); E05-E7 picks `r_c0`, `r_e` or `r_cr` (Eqs. (B4)–(B6)) by expected inflow and `r_a` per Table 2.
- E24-E3 computes `Δ=(T+1)qthres−(s0+q̄1+q̄2)` and K1, K2; E24-E4 assigns all Δ to Stage 1 for high risk, else equalizes marginal utility (Eqs. (22)–(24)).
- E26-E3: `V1min=max(V0+I1−R1max, R2min−I2)`, `V1max=min(V0+I1−R1min, R2max−I2)`, then KKT; E26-E4 applies a three-zone rule (release D1 / R1* / Ru1) by expected availability A.
- E10-E2/E10-E3: outer allocation s12 vs s3 by minimizing dependent risk; inner allocation minimizes `R1+(1−R1)R2`, branching detention (s12≥0) vs pre-release (s12<0).
- E07-E6/E07-E7 (heuristic): spread excess volume equally across forecast days, cap at release/downstream/ramping limits, redistribute backward; take the maximum day-1 release over selected risky members and horizons.
- E12-E2: Eqs. (1)–(5) in E12 select Qout(t), with a check that future inflow minus minimum demand restores VFLWL.

**Limits and gaps**
- Convexity/KKT sufficiency asserted under model forms (E03-E7, E10-E2); thresholds depend on assumed loss/error functions (E03-E8); (x,m) and uncertainty scenarios not externally calibrated (E03-E12, E05-E2).
- The 15.9% value follows from the normal error assumption (E05-E7); `r_c` as "maximum allowable" is a model criterion (E05-E6); solver details not reported (E05-E5, E24-E4, E26-E3); E26 rule not given as executable code (E26-E4).
- E07 gives no formal optimization criterion beyond tolerance and constraints; limit equations in supporting information (E07-E6, E07-E7).
- Thresholds case-specific (E12-E2); E24 cases need Δ>0 (E24-E3); E10 second-stage storage averaged conservatively (E10-E3).
- E05-E9 is illustrative, without out-of-sample verification or deployment.

**Example values**
- x=0.6, m=3, r_a=0.01, r=10, 20.88, 50 (E03-E12).
- Nierji: `x_A=0.78`, range 0<x<0.9; `r_max=11.63×10^6 m³` and `73.71×10^6 m³` in two settings (E05-E9).
- 5,249 acre-feet excess → 331 cfs/day initial, 529 cfs days 1–5 adjusted (E07-E6); 1,240 cfs current release (E07-E7).

## EP06-B Formulate and solve deterministic or multiobjective optimization

**When papers did this**
- When objectives competed (E02-E4, E12-E3, E14-E10, E21-E4, E34-E6, E49-E6); when operation samples were needed for BN training (E08-E3); when forecasts had to become trajectories or benchmarks (E14-E7, E22-E8, E34-E4); for per-event parameter evaluation (E20-E4); for generation consequences (E42-E7).

**How**
- Constraint/epsilon methods: E02-E4 two-step constraint method with K=10; E34-E6 epsilon bounds, then select SI/SII against benchmark D.
- Evolutionary search: NSGA-III with AHP-Entropy compromise (E14-E10); NSGA-II (E21-E4); AR-MOEA in PlatEMO vs multiobjective CCP under identical conditions and hypervolume (E49-E6); GA with grid-searched weights `FO=W1F1+W2F2` (E22-E5, E22-E7).
- MILP with piecewise-linear penalties `P = wq×release penalty + wv×storage penalty`, `wq+wv=1` (E20-E4).
- GRG in LINGO with multiple starts (E34-E7); SDP maximizing `E=Σ(KqtHtΔt)` (E42-E7); min-max outflow per sample (E08-E3); penalty α=10³ (E14-E7).
- Common constraints: mass balance, storage/release bounds, fluctuation limits, initial/end conditions (E14-E7, E22-E6, E49-E5); E22-E6 uses `S(t)=S(t−1)+(I−Q)Δt` every 6 h.
- Benchmarks: deterministic LSTM inflow (E22-E8); single-forecast optimization with aggregation/decomposition (E34-E4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Solver/settings not reported or incomplete: E12-E3, E20-E4, E21-E4, E22-E6, E22-E7, E34-E7, E42-E7, E49-E5, E49-E6 (no seeds/convergence diagnostics).
- Notation/OCR problems: E14-E10, E22-E5 (F2), E49-E5, E34-E6 (weights shown as 0.71/0.32).
- "No-forecast" is a benchmark label, not a controlled counterfactual (E22-E8); E08-E3 is not a real-time controller.

**Example values**
- 20 members × 20 weight runs = 400 runs, ~260 min per event (E22-E7).
- Qmax=6844.5 m³/s, Smax=2698.5 MCM, α=1665 m³/s (E22-E6).
- Hypervolume 2.1093 (RMOO) vs 1.9644 (CCP); population 120, 10,000 iterations (E49-E6).
- D weights about 0.7/0.3 (E34-E6).

## EP06-C Scenario-based stochastic optimization and rolling MPC

**When papers did this**
- When a scenario tree or ensemble was available (E02-E3, E36-E4, E40-E6), at each control step of rolling operation (E16-E4, E17-E2, E23-E5, E33-E9, E35-E8, E43-E4), and when scenario policies had to be summarized for decision-makers (E22-E9).

**How**
- Deterministic equivalents of scenario programs: minimize expected maximum level and peak release in LINGO 16.0 Global Solver, 40 equally likely scenarios (E02-E3); minimize `Σ_i P(ω_i) max_t(Z_t^i)` in LINGO 17.0 (E36-E5) with `V_t^i=V_{t-1}^i+(I_t^i-Q_t^i)Δt` and capacity/change limits (E36-E4); expected risk surrogates with nonanticipative current release (E34-E5).
- One common release sequence across members (no per-member feedback): E16-E4 (cvxpy/Clarabel, 14 steps); E17-E2 (expected storage above TOCS plus terminal term).
- Tree-based stochastic MPC with shared early controls that branch later (E23-E5); TB-MPC applying the root release (E35-E8) with objective J1–J5 squared penalties (E35-E6).
- Other: trust-region-reflective solver in MATLAB with a shrinking horizon (E33-E9); interior-point QP with MAVE weights, 3-hour steps (E43-E4); SSDP with weights 0.3/0.3/0.4 (E40-E6); member ranges and ensemble-mean policy (E22-E9).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Single common sequence: scenario comparison but sensitivity to forecast uncertainty, stated as a limit (E16-E4, E17-E2). Tree forms keep scenario-adaptive later controls (E23-E5, E35-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- Solver tolerances/convergence not reported (E02-E3, E23-E5, E33-E9); E23-E5 demonstrates only one forecast time.
- E35-E6 weights chosen by trial and error, values not listed; E35-E8 is a hindcast; E43-E4 has no field implementation.
- Mean-policy selection rule not reported (E22-E9); SSDP discretization and Pareto criterion not reported (E40-E6); end-level softening unclear (E36-E4).
- Unclear: E40-E6 both assigns fixed weights and generates Pareto solutions; the card does not say how the two relate.

**Example values**
- 3805 variables, 9901 constraints, ~60–61 s CPU (E36-E5); 41×14 forecast matrix (E16-E4); 40/42 HEFS members, 14 steps (E17-E2); Q_set=200 m³/s (E35-E6).

## EP06-D Search or calibrate rule parameters

**When papers did this**
- When a rule form was fixed and its thresholds, coefficients or boundary levels had to be found: dynamic FLWL bounds (E01-E8, E41-E3, E41-E6, E44-E5, E45-E5, E45-E6), cascade rule parameters (E15-E4, E15-E6, E15-E7), risk-curve policies (E16-E3, E50-E7), penalty-model parameters (E20-E5), a hedging release (E25-E5), and stage rules (E46-E6).

**How**
- Monte Carlo search with 0.05 m running-mean convergence (E01-E8).
- Grid enumeration: discrete IOWL grid, then the largest level whose K stays under Pdesign=0.1% per stage (E41-E3, E41-E6); 1 m³/s Qh increments (E25-E5); triplets (wq, PVfcl, b) with M=6 fixed to build a risk hypersurface (E20-E5).
- Evolutionary search: RSEA over 15 variables vs MOEA/D, NSGA-III, θ-DEA with HV (E15-E4, E15-E7), repairing rigid and ranking flexible constraints (E15-E6); NSGA-II with probability constraint (B1), then adding CVaR (B2) (E45-E5, E45-E6); differential evolution with 10 seeds (E16-E3, E50-E7).
- Derivative-free local BOBYQA, sequential by stage, random starts, barrier functions (E46-E6).
- Aggregate-risk-constrained boundary optimization with decomposition (E44-E5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E16-E3 kept the median policy over seeds; E50-E7 kept the best-objective seed (Seed 4).
- E45 added CVaR when B1 met the probability limit but exceeded the stationary CVaR (E45-E5, E45-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- No confidence interval, seed policy or repetition sensitivity (E01-E8); repair algorithm not reported (E15-E6); HV is not flood damage (E15-E7).
- DE settings and bounds not reported (E16-E3, E50-E7); grid spacing partly unreadable (E20-E5); no continuous optimum between grid points (E41-E3); optimizer name not given (E44-E5); BOBYQA local-optimum risk remains (E46-E6).
- E16 defines risk as storage encroachment likelihood (E16-E3).

**Example values**
- Qh*=648 (convex), 408 (linear), 400 (concave) m³/s (E25-E5).
- May 202.5 m, June–July 200.5 m, Aug–Sept 204 m (E41-E6).
- B1 generation +6.29% vs B2 +4.91% (E45-E5, E45-E6).
- ~2,500 (E16-E3) vs ~30,000 (E50-E7) DE evaluations; RSEA HV 1.44494 (E15-E7).

## EP06-E Enumerate structurally different alternatives

**When papers did this**
- When baseline and forecasts were understood and alternatives were needed for comparison (E18-E4), risk analysis (E38-E1) or MCDM (E47-E1, E48-E2).

**How**
- Site-specific FIRO alternatives: four at Lake Mendocino; four FIRO plus one non-FIRO over five buffer-pool elevations (25 scenarios) at Prado; at-site, system and two combined at Yuba–Feather (E18-E4).
- Routine rules plus PSO simulation–optimization giving Schemes 1–9 (E38-E1).
- Ten alternatives from different upper level limits in a peak-reduction model solved by stepwise trial-and-error (E47-E1).
- Six combinations of discharge-capacity (outflow=min(capacity, inflow)), regular, and peak-reduction models (squared sum of outflow plus interval inflow) (E48-E2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Exact triggers and parameterization not given (E18-E4); PSO settings in absent supplementary material (E38-E1); peak-reduction objective is a proxy with no damage model (E47-E1); limits and stopping criteria not given (E48-E2).
