# Reference for unit 4.2-adoption: what the papers did

This file covers how papers organized the institutional adoption of a new operating approach: cross-agency review and staged screening, controlled pilots, and the final disposition or rule revision. It summarizes 7 steps from 2 papers (E18, E21); use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach, and treat all values as examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## EP11-A Organize cross-agency review and staged screening

**When papers did this**
- A reservoir was selected for FIRO investigation (E18-E1).
- A portfolio held more reservoirs than could receive full viability studies (E18-E9).
- Candidate operating schemes affected multiple mandates or required authorization (E21-E5).

**How**
- Pilot organization (E18-E1): form a steering committee, define a workplan, assign multiple work teams, and move through a preliminary assessment, a more in-depth and final viability assessment, and eventually a Water Control Manual (WCM) update. Inputs: participating agencies, site objectives, WCM, study needs.
- Staged portfolio screening, the FIRO Screening Process (E18-E9):
  - Stage A: simple elimination criteria (inputs include controlled-outlet status, existence of a WCM/WCP, forecast skill/lead time).
  - Stage B: score candidate suitability over collaboration, hydraulics, operations, environment, and precipitation forecast skill, balancing benefit against implementation effort.
  - Stage C: more thorough assessment and dialogue with prioritized sites, including local-office discussion.
  - Rigor and engagement increase from A to C; output is a suitability score/prioritization and a recommended next route.
- Multi-department consultation during an event (E21-E5): CROC submits a preliminary scheme ensemble; FCDRH-led state/local authorities, CWRC, CWB, hydropower stations, and grid companies consult over hydro-meteorological information, joint operation, and temporary generation schedules, in rounds, then select a final scheme. Inputs include risk/benefit analysis, current forecasts, power/shipping/ecology requirements, and regulations.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E18 used committee-led staged assessment for planning-level adoption at one site (E18-E1) or across a portfolio (E18-E9).
- E21 used rounds of consultation for authorizing operation during floods (E21-E5). The evidence does not compare the two.

**Limits and gaps**
- Committee membership, governance criteria, and workplan contents are not reported (E18-E1).
- The exact Stage B scoring formula and selection threshold are not reported; beta counts come from OCR text (E18-E9).
- Agenda-level decisions, dissent handling, and approval thresholds of the consultations are not reported (E21-E5).
- Agency acronyms (CROC, FCDRH, CWRC, CWB) are not expanded in the cards (E21-E5).

**Example values**
- Beta test: Stage A applied across the portfolio; the OCR text says "more than 80%" of 85 reservoirs were eligible; 71 reservoirs were eligible for Stage B and "only 13 of 71" were selected for beta testing of later stages (E18-E9).
- Ten consultations during the fourth/fifth floods (E21-E5).

## EP11-B Controlled pilot

**When papers did this**
- A pilot strategy had enough support for temporary operational experimentation (E18-E8).

**How**
- USACE temporarily modified Lake Mendocino WCM operations under FIRO in water years 2019 and 2020, operated under the modification with forecast-informed decisions, and compared observed water-year outcomes (E18-E8).
- Output: demonstration evidence about storage, water supply, and downstream flooding (E18-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- Singleton evidence: one pilot in one paper (E18-E8).
- No full operational log, counterfactual design, statistical attribution, or authorization details are reported (E18-E8).
- The comparison baseline for the storage increase is only "the described comparison"; it is unclear what it is (E18-E8).
- The card marks this as a temporary demonstration, not evidence of universal deployment (E18-E8).

**Example values**
- 2020 storage increased by nearly 20% relative to the described comparison; the paper reports increased water-supply benefits and mitigated downstream flooding (E18-E8).

## EP11-C Disposition and rule revision

**When papers did this**
- Stage C information and dialogue were complete (E18-E10).
- Viability and institutional processes supported formal implementation (E18-E11).
- A flood ended, or an automation/equipment emergency occurred (E21-E9).

**How**
- Disposition branches after Stage C (E18-E10): conclude that benefits are too small, that forecast skill needs further improvement (deferred research), that a full viability assessment is warranted, or that no further assessment is needed and the WCM can be updated. Routing follows site evidence and readiness, using Stage B prioritization, deeper site assessment, forecast/model adequacy, and expected benefits and effort.
- Formal rule revision (E18-E11): update the WCM in coordination between FIRO teams and USACE division/district water-management teams. FIRO 2.0 proposes flexible WCP language paired with a forecast-skill assessment that confirms improvements before new forecast/model tools are used in decisions.
- Post-event reanalysis (E21-E9): the ICDSS reanalyzes operation/forecast records and outcome indicators, including contributions of reservoirs and of natural/man-made factors, summarizes cooperation experience, and supports revising the scheme.
- Crisis branches (E21-E9): the ICDSS holds 22 crisis-management plans for automatic-control failures, equipment/power failures, accidents, and emergencies, invoked when needed.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E18 describes pre-implementation disposition and manual revision for a planned program (E18-E10, E18-E11); E21 describes after-event learning and emergency fallback in an operating system (E21-E9). The evidence does not say when to prefer one.

**Limits and gaps**
- No formal branch thresholds, decision authority, or appeal/reassessment rules (E18-E10).
- Actual WCP wording, approval timeline, operational monitoring, fallback rules, and post-update learning cycle are not reported (E18-E11).
- Individual crisis-plan actions, post-event statistical attribution, and a formal learning metric are not described (E21-E9).
- ICDSS is not expanded in the card (E21-E9).
