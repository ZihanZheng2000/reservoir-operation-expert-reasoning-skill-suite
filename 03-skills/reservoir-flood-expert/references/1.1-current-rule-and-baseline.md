# Reference for unit 1.1-current-rule-and-baseline: what the papers did

This file covers how papers diagnosed the limits of a current flood-control rule and how they built the baseline case (data, physical system, executable rule, initial state and experiment boundaries); it summarizes 57 steps from 37 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check your own approach; all values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP01-A Fixed flood-control allocation causes a safety-benefit imbalance

**When papers did this**
- A fixed flood-season level, guide curve, or flood pool was in use while forecasts, seasonal timing, or hydrology varied (E01-R1, E03-R1, E07-R1, E19-R1, E29-R1, E44-R1, E50-R1).
- A reservoir served both flood control and conservation and fixed allocation looked inefficient (E05-R1, E41-R1).
- A receding flood coincided with water-supply shortage risk (E34-R1).

**How**
- Stated the two-sided failure: fixed space is unused or spills water in small floods / normal years, and may be insufficient or too high in large floods (E05-R1, E41-R1, E19-R1, E44-R1, E34-R1). E07 and E50 framed it as flood space reserved from climatological or historical averages, released before dry periods (E07-R1, E50-R1); E07 used the 2012–2013 Lake Mendocino-type example of releases before a dry period (E07-R1).
- Reframed the fixed level as an operating interval or bounded decision:
  - interval below a safety-preserving upper bound (E01-R1);
  - dynamic decision within lower and upper FLWL bounds, with the static regulation as baseline beyond the forecast horizon (E03-R1);
  - original FLWL kept as the lower limit, with stage-specific upper bounds after dividing the season (E41-R1);
  - controlled departures only inside explicit risk and refill limits (E44-R1);
  - fixed level as a baseline constraint, raised/retained in low-risk conditions and drawn down when a flood is indicated (E19-R1);
  - dynamic upper/lower bounds with prerelease/refill, intended for medium/small inflows below a downstream-risk threshold (E29-R1).
- Framed as a hedging problem between current conservation and future flood control, giving a two-stage problem with explicit trade-off variables (E05-R1).
- Framed as a multi-risk problem with conflicting objectives (E34-R1); used forecast-timed releases leading to a formal FIRO policy (E50-R1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E05 notes that if uncertainty or preference is outside a feasible range, one objective dominates and the problem reduces to single-objective (E05-R1).
- E34 distinguishes deterministic screening/soft-risk approaches from direct stochastic optimization (E34-R1). E50 states forecast quality and reservoir constraints determine the benefit (E50-R1).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are design or motivation rationales, not evidence that every reservoir should use a dynamic level (E01-R1, E03-R1, E29-R1, E44-R1); E29's benefit argument is not an optimization proof (E29-R1).
- No real-time authorization protocol (E05-R1); no redefinition of the legal safety standard or general risk tolerance (E19-R1).
- E07 does not quantify a counterfactual decision for the 2013 example (E07-R1).
- "Risk" in E34 is later a surrogate loss ratio, not observed damage (E34-R1).
- E50's climate motivation is contextual; its experiment uses 2013–2023 historical forecasts (E50-R1).

## RP01-B The rule cannot use available forecasts or their uncertainty

**When papers did this**
- Real-time flood-season or short-term operation where future inflow information existed (E15-R1, E23-R1).
- Existing forecast horizon too short for retention/prerelease (E19-R2).
- Pre-refill/pre-release after a flood with surplus inflow and later shortage (E26-R1).

**How**
- Diagnosed that conventional rules respond mainly to current state and do not use forecasts or forecast uncertainty; made forecast and uncertainty part of the rule, leading to a two-stage HPFOR route (E15-R1).
- Diagnosed that deterministic single-trajectory MPC can give fragile pre-releases when the event fails to occur; compared deterministic versus ensemble/scenario formulations, requiring uncertainty-aware MPC (E23-R1).
- Diagnosed that capacity-constrained pre-release rules do not model forecast-error risk; distinguished FRs (prioritize flood control), CRs (use capacity), and HRs (minimize weighted risk), leading to a stochastic analytical HR (E26-R1).
- Diagnosed a horizon gap: used vapor (PWV, vapor convergence) as an earlier indicator, with precipitation and streamflow forecasts taking over when available (E19-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Uncertainty gap (E15-R1, E23-R1, E26-R1) versus horizon gap (E19-R2) led to different routes: uncertainty-aware rules/MPC/hedging versus an extended indicator sequence.

**Limits and gaps**
- No claim that all conventional rules share these limits (E15-R1).
- E23 treats meteorological uncertainty as the largest component as a working hypothesis; hydrological model uncertainty is incompletely sampled (E23-R1).
- E26's two-phase abstraction and relative storage below FLWL are deliberate simplifications (E26-R1).
- E19's lead times are case-specific, not a universal rule (E19-R2).

**Example values**
- 72-hour vapor and 48-hour precipitation/streamflow setup (E19-R2).

## RP01-C The rule boundary omits system objects

**When papers did this**
- A severe, spatially distributed flood affected a reservoir group (E21-R1).
- Downstream flood risk was material during high inflow but absent from local rules (E43-R1).

**How**
- Treated impounding/release actions as coupled through routing and flood-peak encounters of tributary and mainstream floods; set the whole basin/cascade as the operation target, with different cooperation schemes for mainstream and tributary reservoirs depending on flood type and location (E21-R1).
- Distinguished local storage objectives (present vs desired levels for conservation and hydropower) from system flood safety at a downstream control point (Taungoo), keeping current operation as a comparator and moving to coordinated predictive control (E43-R1).

**Limits and gaps**
- E21 does not report routing uncertainty or exact decision equations (E21-R1).
- E43's diagnosis is case-specific and does not show all local rules are inadequate (E43-R1).
- Only two papers; pattern strength is marked provisional in the evidence.

## RP01-D The basis of the rule is outdated or too narrow

**When papers did this**
- A water control manual may not reflect current watershed knowledge or forecast capability (E18-R1).
- A parametric flood-control model must work across heavy/extreme floods but was calibrated on few events or user-tuned (E20-R2).
- Annual maximum streamflow is nonstationary, or consequence magnitude matters (E45-R1).

**How**
- Checked WCM age/content, watershed changes, and forecast science to decide whether a site-specific FIRO viability investigation is needed (E18-R1).
- Replaced event-specific tuning with automatic calibration over a representative stochastic ensemble; used multiobjective aggregation or Pareto analysis depending on commensurability (E20-R2).
- Separated flood hazard probability from damage-loss severity, with stationary and nonstationary probability formulas (Eqs. (1)–(2) in E45), motivating a risk measure that carries tail magnitude (E45-R1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E20 keeps deterministic calibration possible but less general (E20-R2). E45 uses stationary or nonstationary formulas depending on the streamflow series (E45-R1).

**Limits and gaps**
- No threshold for "old" or "improved" manuals (E18-R1).
- E20 does not quantify bias from each alternative calibration strategy (E20-R2).
- E45's motivation does not prove a climate mechanism in the case (E45-R1).

## EP01-A Assemble hydrologic, forecast, and operating data

**When papers did this**
- Setting up a case study, a forecast-informed test, a forecast model fit, or a common simulation across reservoirs (E04-E1, E07-E2, E13-E1, E14-E1, E19-E2, E43-E1, E50-E1); joint operation needing a shared current state (E21-E1).

**How**
- Paired observed and forecast inflow records for error analysis: TGR daily rolling 4-day forecast from a multiple-input single-output linear systematic model, with inflow components, station flows, and areal rainfall from 40 stations (E04-E1).
- Used long ensemble hindcasts to compensate for a short operational forecast record: 26-year HEFS hindcast, hourly, 61 members, 15-day horizon, plus unimpaired flows, transfers, losses, and release constraints (E07-E2); 61-member HEFS 1–14-day daily aggregates with gauge/full-natural-flow observations and GEFS v2 fields; downstream observations adjusted for local flow and negative full-natural-flow values set to zero (E13-E1).
- Classified events: SOM on three weather indicators split 22 events into C1 (10) and C2 (12) (E14-E1).
- Combined reanalysis and gauges: ERA5 total column water as PWV, IVT from six flux components, Thiessen-polygon areal precipitation, reservoir records (E19-E2); TRMM 3-hour rainfall, SRTM DEM, surveyed cross-sections (E43-E1).
- Automated collection of hydro-meteorological, operation, grid-load, and rule data in a decision support system (E21-E1).
- Built a calibrated daily baseline simulator for 14 reservoirs with mass balance S_t=S_{t−1}+Q_t−R_t, daily median seasonal release patterns and annual hydrology scaling (E50-E1).

**Limits and gaps**
- E07 treats the automated hindcast as a realistic test sample, not a reproduction of real-time forecaster refinements; it found December–March underforecast bias (E07-E2).
- Preprocessing, calibration, missing-data handling, or QC are not fully reported in E04-E1, E13-E1, E19-E2, E21-E1, E43-E1; E14 reports no full SOM architecture or randomization (E14-E1).
- E43 acknowledges daily observed data and limited spatial coverage (E43-E1).
- E50 does not represent environmental/regulatory changes or informal operator forecast use (E50-E1).

**Example values**
- TGR 2003–2009, flood season June 1–September 30 (E04-E1); HEFS 1985–2010 (E07-E2, E13-E1); Lishimen 1987–2020 hourly (E14-E1); ERA5 hourly 0.25° (E19-E2); 1005 hydrological stations, 12,129 precipitation stations, 37 radars, 1.7% station failure rate (E21-E1); baseline storage R²=0.865 averaged over 2009–2023 (E50-E1).

## EP01-B Describe the physical system and safety thresholds

**When papers did this**
- Configuring a case study, a simulation environment, or a station-level/multi-reservoir control problem (E02-E1, E06-E1, E19-E1, E20-E1, E22-E1, E32-E1, E39-E1, E44-E1, E48-E1, E49-E3, E50-E3).

**How**
- Fitted characteristic curves: quartic polynomials for storage-capacity and discharge curves (E02-E1); quadratic continuous differentiable fits (E49-E3).
- Listed characteristic levels and design standards: FLWL, 100-year, 1000-year, and upper levels (E06-E1); FLWL and flood-forecast FLWL (E19-E1); FCL, TCP, DFL, CDL, crest levels (E20-E1); crest and maximum operating elevation (E22-E1); design/check frequencies with six-stage rules (E39-E1).
- Defined downstream safety thresholds: MCC with kq set to MCC and kv to FCL, plus release and storage damage curves (E20-E1); safe flows at control points (E32-E1, E39-E1); release safety and loss-identified thresholds, with Qs set to 20% of Qsa (E49-E3).
- Represented multi-reservoir topology: node variables Q, q, V, H, IC, QC per time slice (E32-E1); aggregation reservoirs by summing physical and CSR storage and inflows, using the last station flow as outflow (E44-E1); cascade with a protected downstream city (E48-E1).
- Assigned empirical constraints consistently across sites: average seasonal TOCS, ramping from the highest observed daily release difference, max release from published values or observed maximum if larger (E50-E3).

**Limits and gaps**
- Curve-fitting diagnostics not reported (E02-E1, E49-E3); full source data not provided (E20-E1, E22-E1, E48-E1).
- OCR/garbled tables: E06 Table 1 labels unclear and should not be reconstructed as exact rules (E06-E1); E32 table labels partly garbled (E32-E1); E49 lost inequality glyphs (E49-E3).
- E44 tested series aggregation only; parallel is described but untested (E44-E1).
- E50's empirical ramping may not reflect actual policy and max release may be exceeded in unavoidable spill (E50-E3).
- E39 does not document authorization or operator override (E39-E1).

**Example values**
- Pubugou: 36 h horizon of 12×3 h; storage 5.063×10^9 m³; flood-control level 841.00 m; crest 856.00 m (E02-E1).
- TGR: FLWL 145.0 m, 100-year 171.0 m, 1000-year 175.0 m, upper 180.4 m (E06-E1).
- Jiaokou: 259 km², 120 million m³, flood season 16 July–15 October, FLWL 62.18 m, flood-forecast FLWL 60.18 m (E19-E1).
- Puentes: 1388 km², 1510 m³/s, 71 million m³, 440 m³/s (E20-E1).
- Dez: total storage 3215.6 MCM, crest 354 m, max operating 352 m; floods of 8564 and 7079 m³/s (E22-E1).
- Safe flows 3,580/8,400/8,000/10,000 m³/s; 1 h step, 168 h (E32-E1).
- XHD: FLWL 125 m, release bounds 0–3,300 m³/s, release safety 1,850 m³/s, loss-identified release 370 m³/s, level safety 129.30 m, 45 periods of 3 h (E49-E3).

## EP01-C Encode current or tested rules in executable form

**When papers did this**
- A new rule had to be compared with existing operation (E01-E1, E18-E2, E37-E4, E45-E3, E50-E2), or a forecast rule under test had to be run (E27-E3, E28-E3), or benchmark rules initialized for event/rolling simulation (E26-E1).

**How**
- Simulated the current rule on observed inflows to quantify baseline spill and floodwater utilization (E01-E1).
- Represented the current WCM/rule curve, outlets, downstream capacity, and authorized purposes as baseline constraints (E18-E2).
- Converted phase averages to volumes, Ii=qiTiΔt, Di=dTiΔt, Rui=rmaxTiΔt, with CR conservation bounded by surplus, Vu, shortage, and safe pre-release capacity (E26-E1).
- Piecewise forecast rule: 6-h forecast runoff R6 and level select discharge limits; low R6 follows inflow, intermediate ranges hold 12,000 or 17,000 m³/s if level permits, high ranges pass inflow, R6>40.5 mm allows free discharge (E27-E3).
- Prerelease of the maximum average inflow over the lead time, with release caps by level, and spillway capacity above 203 m (E28-E3).
- Conventional operation driven by current inflow and level bands, releasing 12,000–17,000 m³/s or passing inflow/free overflow (E37-E4).
- Selected the conventional FLWL from a table of generation over FLWL 140–155 m and computed its CVaR benchmark (E45-E3).
- Daily baseline from median historical release, exponential hedging when storage is below median, increased release if predicted storage exceeds TOCS, then safe-release and ramping limits (E50-E2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Without forecasts the papers used state-driven rules (E37-E4, E50-E2, E01-E1); for a forecast rule under test, the rule takes a forecast quantity (E27-E3, E28-E3). E37's CO uses no forecast but does use an observed-inflow error distribution (E37-E4).

**Limits and gaps**
- E27's rule derivation and authorization are not reported (E27-E3); E28's implementation beyond stated rules is not reported (E28-E3).
- CRs omit forecast-error risk; parameters are case-specific (E26-E1).
- E50 does not report hedging parameters or constraint ordering (E50-E2); E18 does not report model configuration (E18-E2).
- E45 treats unit cost c as constant; loss-function details are not in the supplied text (E45-E3).

**Example values**
- TGR baseline: 486.64×10^8 m³ mean annual spill, 82.04% floodwater utilization (E01-E1).
- R6 thresholds 21, 26, 29, 36.5, 40.5 mm; level thresholds 326/328 m (E27-E3); CO start 325 m (E37-E4).
- Geheyan: Z0=194.5 m, 10 h lead time, caps 11,000 m³/s below 200 m and 13,000 m³/s below 203 m (E28-E3).
- Scheme A: FLWL 145 m, stationary cumulative risk 1.98% at 98% confidence over 2020–2039 (E45-E3).

## EP01-D Set initial state, events, and experiment boundaries

**When papers did this**
- Configuring each rolling or two-stage decision (E03-E1, E05-E1, E24-E1), a pre-storm optimization (E25-E1), FIRO event simulation (E16-E1, E17-E1), ensemble runs (E20-E3), beyond-horizon risk (E33-E4), a reproducible experiment (E35-E1), or seasonal bounds (E41-E1); isolating information timing from forecast error (E19-E3).

**How**
- Two-stage storage representation, with different terminal conditions:
  - E03: stage 1 one Δt, stage 2 the remaining T−1; S_1=S_0+I_1−R_1, S_2=S_1+I_2−R_2, W_1=S_1−S_min, and S_2=S_min (E03-E1).
  - E05: stage 1 deterministic, stage 2 forecast-uncertain; W_1=S_1−S_min; evaporation and seepage neglected (E05-E1).
  - E24: flows as forecasts plus errors, square-wave stages, ending storage FSL=0 (Eqs. (1)–(8) in E24) (E24-E1).
- Initial state by convention: relative storage S=0 at TOCS 15 days before peak, negative storage allowed, run to 15 days after peak (E16-E1); all events start at TCP (E20-E3); initial high forebay level (E35-E1).
- Event selection: January 1997 primary, February 2017 supplement, Rmax and ramping normalized by event peak inflow (E17-E1); 100-year design hydrograph with 48 h lead time (E35-E1); homogeneous frequency enlargement of representative floods per season stage (E41-E1); design hydrographs shifted to match lead-time differences between reservoirs, initialized with stage-1 end levels (E33-E4).
- Perfect forecasts: observed P/streamflow as 48 h forecasts, ERA5 as 72 h vapor forecasts (E19-E3).
- Discrete forecast storm scenarios with volumes, durations, and probabilities (E25-E1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Terminal storage differs: S_min (E03-E1) versus FSL=0 (E24-E1); stage-1 inflow is treated as deterministic in E05 (E05-E1). Keep each paper's definition.

**Limits and gaps**
- Terminal settings are modelling assumptions (E03-E1, E24-E1); fixed TCP is a simplification, stochastic initial levels untested (E20-E3).
- Maximum release assumed available at all storages (E16-E1); simplified physical constraints (E17-E1).
- Perfect forecasts leave real-time error untested (E19-E3).
- E33 assumes stage-1 end level is uncorrelated with future floods (E33-E4).
- Illustrative, not calibrated, inputs (E25-E1); synthetic event without all ordinates (E35-E1); stage timing and update frequency not reported (E05-E1).

**Example values**
- HEFS 41 members, 1990–2019, 100 synthetic ensembles (E16-E1); peak inflow 37%–112% of design flood pool (E17-E1).
- Storms 0.8–2 km³ with probabilities 0.3–0.05; k=1 km³, S0=0.3 km³ (E25-E1).
- Yuvacik: peak 597 m³/s, 17.1 hm³, 24 h; 96 h window (E35-E1).
- FENGTAN: 14 frequencies 0.01%–1%; original FLWL 198.5 m; design level 209.56 m at P=0.1% (E41-E1).
