# Reference for unit 3.1-risk-and-acceptable-safety: what the papers did

This file covers how papers defined reservoir-side and downstream failure events, added loss/tail/duration measures, set acceptable-risk benchmarks, derived operating bounds from them, combined risk across objects and time, and calculated exceedance, loss and benefit indicators; it summarizes 147 steps from 46 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP04-A Define reservoir-side failure events

**When papers did this**
- Expected objective values alone did not express exceedance risk (E02-R6); a flood-control decision had to be assessed under uncertain inflow and system response (E06-R1, E30-R1, E27-R4); forecast-based prerelease or a raised flood-limited level was considered (E28-R1, E04-R7).
- Scenario or Monte Carlo operation produced a distribution of maximum levels (E36-R6, E27-R5, E08-R6).
- Policies were compared on flood-space requirement (E16-R2, E17-R3).

**How**
- Load–resistance exceedance of maximum level and/or peak discharge: P_Z = P(Z_max > Z_s), P_Q = P(Q_max > Q_s), with separate pairs for reservoir and downstream (E02-R6); highest level or downstream peak as load, safe value as resistance (E06-R1, Eq. (10) in E06); uncertain level as load against a selected safe/controlled level (E30-R1); flood load exceeding regulating resistance (E28-R1).
- Probability-only definition: Risk = P(Z_max > Z_s) = ∫_{Z_s}^{∞} f(Z_max) dZ_max, because consequences are hard to pre-evaluate (E36-R6, Eq. (17)).
- Risk as summed probability mass above a threshold in a Bayesian/dynamic Bayesian network (E08-R6, E32-R5).
- Monte Carlo maximum water level (MWL) distribution, assumed approximately normal, with tail probabilities at several levels (E27-R4, E27-R5).
- Exceedance of a design-flood level (180.4 m tied to a 10,000-year design flood) under pre-release vs regular routing (E04-R7).
- Required flood pool = maximum accumulated storage above TOCS, max(0, X); percent reduction vs the actual pool, set to 0% when required pool exceeds design pool (E17-R3, Eq. (7)); with a drawdown penalty of weight w on deviations (E16-R2, Eqs. (1)–(2)).
- Event maxima QOmax and Vmax as descriptors linked to downstream damage and dam safety (E20-R4).
- Optimization target: minimize probability-weighted maximum level over scenarios (E36-R4).
- Level hierarchy IL/SL/NL/DL (preparation, safety, conservation, PMF capacity), with space above SL kept as backup (E46-R3).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Probability-of-exceedance definitions were used when a distribution of outcomes was available (E02-R6, E36-R6, E27-R5); storage-based flood-pool metrics when comparing policies on required flood space (E16-R2, E17-R3).
- E06's framework allows level or discharge exceedance, but its case evaluates only highest level (E06-R1).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are model-based exceedance proxies, not observed damage probabilities (E02-R6, E06-R1, E36-R6, E32-R5); flood pool is a storage/factor-of-safety proxy (E17-R3).
- Normal MWL assumption and no observed MWL validation (E27-R5); evaporation/leakage ignored (E27-R4).
- Thresholds are study settings, not authorization procedures (E04-R7); targets are assumed inputs (E46-R3).
- Case N, T, time-step and part of the rule table are unclear in E06 (E06-R6); conversion of scenario-indexed releases to one implemented release is not detailed (E36-R4); temporal trajectories omitted (E20-R4).

**Example values**
- Z_s = 848.00 m, Q_s = 4,200 m³/s (E02-R6); FCWL 145 m, Shashi 44.5 m, release caps 53,900 and 79,000 m³/s (E04-R7); thresholds 330.0, 333.0, 337.33 m with 2,000 error series per flood (E27-R5); w ∈ {0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5} (E16-R2).

## RP04-B Define downstream and engineering consequences

**When papers did this**
- Allocating flood-control storage or choosing releases where downstream levee failure or water shortage matters (E10-R2, E24-R3, E25-R2, E26-R3); characterization variables needed a common consequence scale (E20-R5); forecast uncertainty could cause both flooding and curtailment (E42-R5).

**How**
- Levee-failure probability as the objective, with fixed catastrophic damage so probability substitutes for loss; three-stage dependent form R1 + (1−R1)R2 + (1−R1)(1−R2)R3 (E10-R2).
- Expected flood safety margin (EFSM): δ1 = q_thres − r̄1, δ2 = T·q_thres − r̄2; integrate error density above each margin for expected damage D1(δ1), D2(δ2) (E24-R3, Eqs. (10)–(14)).
- Fragility curve F(Q) nondecreasing between safe capacity q_lb and overtopping capacity q_ub, in convex/linear/concave forms for good/fair/poor levees; pre-storm and future failures assumed independent (E25-R2). Convex F guarantees convex expected cost; more convex curves support more hedging, water-supply losses reduce it (E25-R5).
- Thresholded damage: release damage zero below kq; storage damage Dv = p(break|Vmax) × Cost_break above kv (FCL), p estimated by routing very rare synthetic floods (E20-R5).
- Separate risks Pu = Prob(V1 ≥ Vu), Pd = Prob(R2 ≥ Ru2), Ps = Prob(R2 ≤ D2); chance constraint Pd ≤ γa and minimize Lu + Ls (E26-R3).
- Penalty objectives: squared spill above 200 m³/s plus water-supply deviation and release-change terms (E35-R6); −1.0 per release above maximum safe release added to normalized mean storage (E50-R5).
- Flood risk as count of ensemble events above design flood Qrp; curtailment as generation deviation (E42-R5).
- Safety level 42 m kept below 43 m danger level as buffer (E43-R4).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E20 used monetary damage functions and says to use exceedance-risk functions and a Pareto approach if damage curves are unavailable (E20-R5).
- E25: convex curve → optimum more assured; linear → depends on storm distribution; concave → usually no hedging unless antecedent flow is below q_lb (E25-R5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Fixed/catastrophic damage and Gaussian error are assumptions (E10-R2, E24-R3); fragility curves are conceptual, not calibrated (E25-R2); E25-R5 relationships are numerical-example results.
- Damage functions represented through weights (E26-R3); weights chosen by trial and error (E35-R6); curtailment is not economic loss (E42-R5).
- Horizon not calibrated (E43-R4); differential-evolution settings not reported (E50-R5).

**Example values**
- ~30,000 evaluations, 10 seeds (E50-R5); 30-min simulation step, 3-h control step, 2-day horizon, ~41 h travel time (E43-R4).

## RP04-C One exceedance probability is not enough; add other measures

**When papers did this**
- A single probability was judged insufficient for whole-system risk or uncertainty contributions (E06-R7); damage depends on magnitude and duration (E12-R3); results were random/discrete and asymmetric (E38-R2); nonstationarity or consequence magnitude mattered (E45-R1); control needed frequency, severity and duration (E49-R3); large floods (E14-R6).

**How**
- Shannon entropy over binned highest-level probabilities, summed over uncertainty and risk components; HR = 0 means no modeled risk (E06-R7, Eqs. (9)–(13)).
- Resilience: piecewise system performance integrated over the event, as a third objective alongside maximum level and downstream peak (E12-R3).
- Mean (typical), CVaR (tail) and entropy (dispersion) kept together; no single indicator is final because rankings may disagree (E38-R2).
- CVaR chosen over VaR as expected tail loss (E45-R2, E49-R3); n-year CVaR combines annual CVaR weighted by yearly exceedance probability, with α + cumulative risk = 1 (Eq. (6) in E45, E45-R2).
- CVaR added as a second constraint when probability risk passes but CVaR exceeds the stationary benchmark β_nα; otherwise FLWL reduced (E45-R5); authors prefer CVaR-constrained adaptation in this case (E45-R6).
- Six objectives: exceedance probabilities, loss ratios with CVaR, maximum sequential exceedance duration, upstream and downstream (E49-R3).
- Storage plus maximum-release frequency and trajectories as joint checks (E50-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E14: small floods — level/release objectives (TSO) may suffice, risk/resilience near zero; large floods — five risk/resilience/vulnerability objectives (MRO) (E14-R6).
- E45: stationary vs nonstationary probability formulas (E45-R1, E45-R2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Entropy-as-risk and resilience are modeling choices, not validated (E06-R7, E38-R2, E12-R3); E14's scale rule shown with two events only (E14-R6).
- Independent annual events, Bernoulli occurrence, constant unit cost (E45-R2, E45-R5); no damage validation (E45-R6); loss measures are surrogates (E49-R3); max-release frequency is a proxy (E50-R6).

## RP04-D Set an acceptable-risk benchmark

**When papers did this**
- A candidate level, policy or bound had to be screened for safety or compared with safety requirements (E01-R5, E33-R5, E40-R4, E41-R6, E22-R8, E29-R6); ensemble trajectories might exceed a critical storage (E07-R3); adaptation might raise risk or loss (E45-R3).

**How**
- Current-rule benchmark: R1 + R2 + R3 + R4 ≤ R0, R0 = risk of the design flood regulated from the current FLWL (E01-R5, Eqs. (8)–(15)).
- Stationary benchmark: conventional FLWL 145.0 m gives baseline generation, cumulative risk and β_nα at 98% confidence for 2020–2039 (E45-R3).
- Protection/design standard as accepted risk: R ≤ R_accepted (E33-R5, Eq. (7)); yearly risk vs design return period (E40-R4); extreme-risk frequency Pe vs K and 0.1% (E41-R6).
- Lead-time tolerance: risk = % of 61 members above 111,000 acre-feet (3 ft below emergency-spillway crest); tolerance 0% for first five forecast days, rising after (E07-R3).
- Limit compliance: all ensemble members below 2698.5 MCM (352 m) (E22-R8); TGR upper bounds and Shashi categories vs static design rule (E29-R6); PMF simulations and legal/outlet limits, with barrier functions (E46-R5).
- Hard forebay constraints for every branch; chance-constraint variant proposed only conceptually (E23-R5).
- Authors' case judgment that simulated risks are "small and acceptable" (E27-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Relative benchmarks (current rule, stationary scheme) were used when comparing a new policy to existing operation (E01-R5, E45-R3); prescribed standards when a feasibility constraint was needed (E33-R5, E40-R4, E41-R6); E07's curve reflects forecast reliability and response time (E07-R3).

**Limits and gaps**
- Modeled constraints, not loss guarantees (E01-R5); E07's curve was heuristic and stakeholder-informed, construction not in the main text (E07-R3).
- "Accepted risk" is prescribed (E33-R5); economic justification left for future work (E40-R4); verification not observed-event (E41-R6).
- Conventional operation not comparable in E27 because measurement-error risks were not quantified (E27-R6); weights not reported (E23-R5); no deployment study (E29-R6, E46-R5, E22-R8).

## RP04-E Derive operating bounds from acceptable risk

**When papers did this**
- A prefill/release decision with uncertain future inflow (E03-R4, E05-R3, E15-R3); setting a dynamic design flood (E11-R2); choosing an upper dynamic-control bound or FLWL (E41-R3, E41-R5, E39-R6); a risk estimate exceeded, or a tolerance was set (E30-R6, E31-R6, E36-R8, E28-R6); estimates beyond the precipitation horizon (E19-R3).

**How**
- Invert the forecast-error CDF: d_min = H^{-1}(1 − r_a); under Gaussian error d_min = r·U^{-1}(1 − r_a); d_max from a minimum-release condition (E03-R4); feasible zone ABCD with 0 ≤ W1 ≤ min(W_max, RFCC − d_min), d_max = R2,max − R2,min (E05-R3).
- Percentile bound: V_{t+1} ≤ V_lim + R_lim·T_f·Δt − Per(P_I, 1 − r_a)·T_f·Δt (E15-R3).
- σ²_ε = (1 − Cp)·var(V_ts); choose δ_P,ts with Prob(ε_ts ≥ δ_P,ts) = P_des (E11-R2).
- Credibility constraint: maximize IOWL s.t. Cr{Vmax ≥ Vdesign} ≤ α or Cr{Zmax ≥ Zdesign} ≤ α (E41-R3); pick the highest grid IOWL with K ≤ 0.1% (E41-R5).
- Sweep FLWL 127.2–127.8 m and keep the highest within design risk (E39-R6).
- Read risk curves backward: required forecast accuracy or safe level for a given acceptable risk (E28-R6, E36-R8); solve for controlled level HR, reserve storage, acceptable precipitation (E30-R6); revise program or take emergency action if risk too high (E30-R6, E31-R6).
- Physical upper bound: potential precipitation (PP) = initial PWV + inward vapor flux, converted to VCWL via runoff coefficient or hydrological model (Eqs. (1)–(9) in E19; E19-R3, E19-R4).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Analytic inversion when error is assumed Gaussian (E03-R4, E05-R3, E11-R2, E15-R3); grid search over candidate levels when routing is nonlinear (E41-R5, E39-R6); physical worst-case bound when forecasts are beyond horizon (E19-R3).
- Larger uncertainty or lower r_a raises d_min and narrows the feasible space (E03-R4, E05-R3); smaller r_a lowers storage and raises discharge (E15-R3).

**Limits and gaps**
- Normal-error assumptions (E05-R3, E11-R2); short-reach, negligible intermediate inflow assumption (E03-R4).
- Thresholds are case outputs, not universal rules (E36-R8, E28-R6); emergency actions not executed (E30-R6, E31-R6); grid-only selection (E41-R5); no formal optimization or cost analysis (E39-R6).
- E19 Eq. (8) partly OCR-damaged (E19-R4).

**Example values**
- 6% acceptable risk → α > 90.7% or R² > 94.1%; Z_s = 846.85 m at R² = 85% (E36-R8); accepted check risk 3×10⁻⁵ → R² ≈ 0.85 (E28-R6); 202.5 m gives K = 0.0977%, 203 m gives 0.1040% (E41-R5); FLWL 127.6 m proposed (E39-R6); runoff coefficient 0.75 (E19-R4).

## RP04-F Several safety objects or thresholds at once

**When papers did this**
- Releases had to balance dam/upstream and downstream safety (E02-R3, E20-R1, E40-R3); multiple thresholds could be crossed (E39-R2); multi-reservoir schemes needed comparison or aggregation (E21-R5, E38-R1, E38-R4, E48-R2).

**How**
- Keep both objectives (expected highest level, expected peak discharge) and generate a Pareto set without a prior compromise (E02-R3); two risk objectives for calibration (E20-R1).
- Hazard types: H1 Zmax > Zmax⁰, H2 Qmax > Qmax⁰, H3 = H1 or H2 (UHE), H4 = H1 and H2 (IHE) (E39-R2); P(H) = ∬_Ω f_E,Y(e,y) de dy, preferred to MLTP interval approximation (E39-R3); domains built from routed boundaries (E39-R4).
- Failed scenario if any step crosses Qc or Zc; release-control vs level-control formulations (E40-R3).
- Common risk variable = maximum occupied storage per reservoir and system (E38-R1); AHP reservoir weights from capacity and runoff, CR ≤ 0.1 (E38-R4).
- Hierarchical criteria per reservoir (highest level, terminal level, capacity-use ratio) and per protected region (peak, duration above safety discharge, excess volume) (E48-R2).
- Upstream surplus capacity holds part of the flood so downstream stays higher (E21-R5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Under extreme floods, dam safety may take precedence (E20-R1, qualitative). Compensation requires accurate forecasts and non-coinciding flood types (E21-R5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Probability only, no consequences (E39-R2, E40-R3); E39's "analytical" method still uses gridded routing (E39-R3); no general compensation boundary (E21-R5); no criterion validation (E48-R2).

**Example values**
- CR 0.049 and 0.054, attribute weights {0.5, 0.5} (E38-R4); TGR 153.03 m vs FLWL 145 m (E21-R5).

## RP04-G Aggregate risk over time

**When papers did this**
- Evaluating design vs check hydrographs (E28-R4); exceedance can occur at any time (E30-R5, E31-R2); forecast information ends before the operation horizon (E33-R2, E40-R2); communicating risk across return periods (E37-R6, E41-R4); pre-discharge storage set from forecast (E44-R4).

**How**
- R = P(flood event) × P(max Z > Zc | flood event); design uses DFWL and 0.10%, check uses CFWL and 0.02% (E28-R4).
- Point risk P[Z(t) > Z_R] and process risk P(∪ events) with adjacent bivariate dependence and nonadjacent independence (E30-R5, E31-R2).
- Two stages: lead-time risk from scenarios, beyond-lead-time from design-flood routing conditional on end states (E33-R2, E33-R4, E40-R2); scenario counted once if any exceedance (E33-R4).
- Risk rate Ψ relating exceedance frequency to occurrence probability (E37-R6).
- 14 frequency levels with fuzzy simulation and credibility area K (E41-R4).
- Probability-weighted end storage–risk relation, using the allocation with the largest risk (E44-R4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Nonadjacent independence assumed (E30-R5, E31-R2); post-horizon design-flood representation assumed adequate (E40-R2); E33's causal sequence is reconstructed and gives no rule for choosing release vs level threshold (E33-R2, E33-R4); E37's general formula not clearly printed (E37-R6); Gaussian unbiased forecast assumed (E44-R4).

**Example values**
- Five-year flood: frequency 0.54% → Ψ = 0.108% with 20% occurrence (E37-R6).

## EP07-A Calculate threshold-exceedance risk

**When papers did this**
- After routed, simulated, ensemble or inferred outcome distributions became available (E02-E5, E06-E8, E07-E4, E08-E5, E36-E6, E37-E5, E42-E6).

**How**
- Count fraction above threshold: M1/N for Z_M > Z_s, M2/N for Q_m > Q_s (E06-E8); Monte Carlo counts of max level/discharge above permissible values, 10,000 runs, CO vs FBO (E37-E5, E37-E6); Count(Qen > Qrp)/N_MCMC (E42-E6); any-crossing indicator per scenario divided by m (E40-E3, E33-E3).
- Ensemble share above 111,000 acre-feet per forecast day under no-release forecast, compared with tolerance R_t; release scheduled only where r_t > R_t (E07-E4, E07-E5).
- Integrate a fitted distribution: P_Z, P_Q (E02-E5); POME density fitted from moments with Newton–Raphson, checked against normal with P–P plots, then integrated above Z_s (E36-E6, E36-E7, E36-E8); normal tail from MWL mean/SD (E27-E7).
- BN/DBN: sum state probabilities above thresholds, prior or posterior, prediction or diagnosis (E08-E5, E08-E6, E32-E5, E32-E6).
- Required flood pool from maximum accumulated storage above TOCS (E16-E8, E17-E4); event maxima QOmax, Vmax (E20-E6).
- Level paths from elevation–storage curve with box-plot summaries (E28-E5); Shashi day counts in categories A–D (E29-E5); compare selected-level risk to accepted risk (E31-E7).
- Probability of max level > 180.4 m under pre-release vs regular routing (E04-E10).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Counting was used with sampled ensembles (E06-E8, E37-E5, E42-E6); density fitting when moments were used (E36-E7) or normality assumed (E27-E7); network inference when bidirectional updating was wanted (E08-E6, E32-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- No replication count, estimator uncertainty or convergence check for E04's zero estimate (E04-E10); members treated as equally likely, curve parameterization not given (E07-E4); POME moment order and tolerances unreported (E36-E7).
- Normality assumed (E27-E7); no observed validation (E32-E5, E36-E8, E29-E5); rule-row details OCR-unclear (E06-E8); BN matched MC only for case 1 (E08-E5).

**Example values**
- 14 of 61 members above threshold on day 8 (E07-E5); risks 6.29% (α) and 8.42% (R²) at 846 m (E36-E8); 2-year design at 24 h risk 46.99%, 5-year 0.08% (E42-E6); 100-year MWL mean 329.42 m, SD 0.233, ≈0.0065 above 330 m (E27-E7); 1997 flood pool 662 TAF vs 750 TAF plan (E16-E8).

## EP07-B Calculate losses, tail, and composite risk

**When papers did this**
- Event maxima or simulated outcomes had to become expected loss, tail or composite values (E20-E7, E24-E2, E25-E3, E38-E6, E49-E4); quantifying risk over a design life (E45-E1, E45-E2).

**How**
- Gringorten plotting positions and expected-damage integration: Iq over QOmax with Dq = 0 below kq; Iv over Vmax with failure probability from synthetic routing (E20-E7, Eqs. (10)–(16)).
- Gaussian tail above each EFSM for expected levee-failure damage, including upstream–intermediate correlation in σ_t² (E24-E2).
- Pf(Qh) = F(Qh + Qa), Pf(Qsi) = F(Qsi), conditional (1 − Pf(Qh))·Pf(Qsi), weighted by p_i, times cf; optional spill cost (E25-E3); storms labelled small/large/overwhelming via q_lb, q_ub (E25-E4).
- Normal errors with σ²_ε = σ²_ε1 + σ²_ε2 for Pu, Pd, Ps (E26-E2).
- Expected ratio objectives (scarcity, upstream storage loss, downstream peak/safety discharge) in a stochastic program (E34-E5).
- Mean, 99% CVaR and entropy of maximum occupied storage (E38-E6); two-bin entropy per component and total (E06-E9).
- VaR/CVaR with α + R = 1; stationary risk 1 − P(no exceedance), nonstationary as sum of first-exceedance probabilities; n-year CVaR via Eq. (6) (E45-E1, E45-E2).
- Six objectives incl. CVaR of loss ratios with α = 0.1 and sequential exceedance duration (E49-E4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Overtopping-equals-failure assumed, damage-curve uncertainty not quantified (E20-E7); no error fitting or damage calibration (E24-E2, E45-E1); stage independence and constant cost (E25-E3); category bounds model-specific (E25-E4).
- Variance calibration procedure not documented (E26-E2); entropy discretization not given (E38-E6), bin boundaries not stated (E06-E9); loss ratios are surrogates (E49-E4).

**Example values**
- HR = 0.1921 at 171 m and 0.03087 at 175 m (E06-E9); synthetic floods Tr ≈ 10–200,000 years (E20-E7).

## EP07-C Calculate benefits and non-safety indicators

**When papers did this**
- After a risk-constrained bound or policy was simulated, to compare against current rules or baselines (E01-E9, E03-E14, E07-E10, E22-E10, E29-E6, E50-E8, E12-E5, E15-E5, E19-E11, E35-E10, E42-E8).

**How**
- Spilled discharge, floodwater utilization, hydropower increment by lead time, with/without uncertainty (E01-E9); utilization = (1 − spilled volume/total inflow) × 100% (E29-E6).
- Refilled years, refill probability, mean annual conservation over 30 years (E03-E14).
- Storage exceedance and interquartile ranges, release lead times, uncontrolled spill, downstream flow frequencies at gauges (E07-E10).
- Maximum level, downstream peak/duration, resilience (E12-E5); five objectives incl. generation, level/release maxima, upstream storage use, navigation (E15-E5).
- Final level, storage, spills, duration above maximum prerelease outflow over 50 events (E19-E11).
- Peak release, maximum storage, release fluctuation, limit compliance (E22-E10); release above 200 m³/s integrated, FSI = effective flood storage / FCL storage (E35-E10).
- Curtailment ΔE = observed generation minus forecast-driven generation (E42-E8); normalized storage, May 1 storage, maximum-release occurrences (E50-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are simulated or hindcast results without deployment validation (E01-E9, E03-E14, E07-E10, E12-E5, E22-E10); no damage model (E50-E8, E22-E10, E35-E10); initial-level confounding and no uncertainty intervals (E19-E11); parameters partly unreported (E15-E5).

**Example values**
- Upper bounds 146.1 m (1 day) and 147.4 m (2 days), hydropower +1.55–2.91% (E01-E9); EFO 10 May median storage +22,829 acre-feet (33%) (E07-E10); +8.1% normalized storage (E50-E8); IFU curtailment 29–33% (E42-E8).

## EP07-D Combine risk across objects and hazards

**When papers did this**
- Multiple uncertainty sources, objects or hazards had to be summarized for one decision (E08-E7, E20-E8, E21-E4, E38-E7, E39-E4, E48-E3).

**How**
- Compare one-factor and joint uncertainty cases to attribute risk (E08-E7).
- Sum commensurable euro indices Ir = Iq + Iv and pick the minimum, equal weights (E20-E8).
- AHP-weighted sums of per-reservoir mean, CVaR, entropy across 14 reservoirs (E38-E7).
- Hazard surfaces from error grid and routing, union/intersection domains, copula joint density (c = 1 after independence assessment) integrated numerically (E39-E4, E39-E5, E39-E6); FLWL sweep (E39-E8).
- 12-criterion alternative-by-criterion matrix (E48-E3); NSGA-II cooperation-scheme ensemble (E21-E4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Equal weights not stakeholder-validated (E20-E8); no weight sensitivity (E38-E7); grid step, independence test and quadrature error unreported (E39-E4, E39-E6); E39 method hard beyond two sources (E39-E5); NSGA-II settings not reported (E21-E4); single case and design flood (E08-E7).

**Example values**
- H3 0.274–0.278%, H4 0.131–0.154% over FLWL 127.2–127.8 m (E39-E8).

## EP07-E Combine risk across time and stages

**When papers did this**
- A candidate level/policy needed full-horizon or full-process risk (E01-E7, E33-E6, E40-E5, E41-E5, E44-E4); design/check trajectories were available (E28-E6); moments for point/process risk were available (E30-E5, E31-E4).

**How**
- Sum misclassification and hydrograph risks R1–R4 vs R0 (E01-E7).
- Conditional exceedance × extreme-flood probability for RDF/RCF (E28-E6).
- Univariate normal point tail, bivariate normal adjacent pairs, inclusion–exclusion (E30-E5, E31-E4).
- Two-stage: precompute stage-2 risk vs end-level combinations, combine with stage-1 failures (E33-E5, E33-E6); early failures count 1, others add conditional design-flood risk, divide by m (E40-E4, E40-E5).
- Scale counts to risk rate Ψ (E37-E7); credibility curve area K over 14 frequencies, accept if K ≤ P_design (E41-E5), verified with extreme-risk rate Pe (E41-E7).
- Weighted sum over forecast events of storage–risk relation (E44-E4); stationary Scheme A baseline (E45-E3).

**Limits and gaps**
