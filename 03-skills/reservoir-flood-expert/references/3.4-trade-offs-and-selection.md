# Reference for unit 3.4-trade-offs-and-selection: what the papers did

This file covers how papers identified conflicting objectives, allocated storage or capacity, made safety a constraint before optimizing benefits, kept noninferior sets, formulated objectives, built criteria and decision matrices, ranked and tested ranking stability, selected compromises, and used staged screening or consultation. It summarizes 109 steps from 40 papers; use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach, and read all values as examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP06-A Identify conflicting objectives

**When papers did this**
- A fixed or static FLWL, guide curve, or flood-control storage was used despite changing forecasts, seasonal variation, or flood-season timing (E01-R1, E03-R1, E05-R1, E07-R1, E29-R1, E44-R1).
- Pre-release, pre-refill, or operating above/around the design FLWL was being considered under forecast uncertainty (E08-R1, E12-R1, E26-R1, E28-R1, E34-R1).
- Releases had to balance dam/upstream safety against downstream safety (E02-R3, E20-R1), or release peak against storage retention (E22-R3, E35-R1).
- Actions affected purposes beyond flood control: power, shipping, ecology, maintenance, transmission (E21-R2), or recharge/water supply at multiple sites (E18-R5).

**How**
- Reframe the fixed level as an operating interval or dynamic decision within lower/upper bounds, with a safety-preserving upper bound (E01-R1, E03-R1, E29-R1, E44-R1); E03-R1 keeps static regulation as baseline beyond the forecast horizon.
- Treat current conservation versus future flood control as a hedging / two-stage problem (E05-R1, E26-R1).
- Keep two safety objectives separate rather than collapse them into a weighted one: E02-R3 minimizes expected highest reservoir level and expected peak discharge over forecast scenarios (Eqs. 6–7 in E02); E20-R1 represents released-flow damage and stored-volume/dam-failure risk as separate objectives.
- E22-R3: minimize maximum release (F1) and a storage-deficit expression (F2) (Eqs. 7–8 in E22).
- E42-R5: separate pathways; overestimated inflow leads to flood-threshold exceedance (count ensemble events above design flood Qrp), underestimation to water deficit and curtailed generation.
- E12-R1: pre-release when forecast inflow is in the range Qdown–Qup, provided future inflow plus minimum-demand balance can restore FLWL if no flood occurs (Eqs. 1–5 in E12).
- E21-R2: balance multi-department requirements as an objective/constraint set rather than optimize one objective.
- Evaluate trade-offs in simulation: FCC exceedance vs generation (E10-R6); level, storage, spill, outflow-peak, and duration statistics over 50 floods (E19-R9).

**Limits and gaps**
- Most framings are design rationales, not evidence that every reservoir should use dynamic control (E01-R1, E03-R1, E28-R1, E29-R1, E44-R1).
- Priority between objectives is qualitative or unspecified (E20-R1, E21-R2); E21-R2 gives no utility function or weighting scheme.
- Scope exclusions: E08-R1 excludes discharge-capacity and storage-curve uncertainty; E33-R1 uses only stochastic inflow uncertainty.
- E02-R3 mentions hydropower/water supply but implements only two flood-control objectives.
- Risk is a surrogate: loss ratios (E34-R1), threshold rate and generation deviation (E42-R5).
- E07-R1's 2013 example is illustrative, no counterfactual; E19-R9 does not report its event-selection rule; E18-R5 rules and thresholds are in cited reports; Qdown/Qup calibration is case-specific (E12-R1); E22-R3's F2 is OCR-reconstructed.

## RP06-B Allocate limited resources over time or across the system

**When papers did this**
- After forecasts and risk-feasible release limits were known, residual capacity had to be split between current conservation and future safety (E03-R3, E05-R2, E26-R5).
- A flood forecast was updated over an event and current vs future periods competed for storage/conveyance (E24-R1, E25-R1, E10-R3, E10-R4).
- A downstream reservoir needed help and upstream capacity was available (E21-R4, E21-R5).

**How**
- Two-stage resource identity: E03-R3 uses W_1 + d = R_2,max − Ī_2 (Stage-1 excess storage W_1, Stage-2 safety margin d); with perfect forecast d = 0.
- Weighted loss with marginal utilities. Notation differs between papers:
  - E03-R5: x is the weight on flood control (x near 0 favors conservation); minimize L1 + L2 with convex conservation loss (m > 1) and probability of excessive release as flood loss.
  - E05-R2: x is the weight on conservation and (1−x) on flood control; minimize x·g_w,1(W_1) + (1−x)·g_f,2(d, r), normal forecast error.
- Classify policy by comparing marginal utilities: E03-R7 compares f_2(d_min), f_1(0), f_1(W_max) to get three cases (flood control / hedging / conservation cap segments); E05-R5 defines Types 1–3 by x relative to x_min, x_A, x_max.
- KKT allocation: E10-R3 (across and outside RFH: lower bound active → more to later stage; upper bound active → all to RFH; else equalize marginal risks); E10-R4 (detention for s12 ≥ 0, prerelease for s12 < 0); E26-R5 turns the KKT solution into zones: Zone I R1 = D1, Zone II R1 = R1*, Zone III R1 = Ru1, by expected availability A vs SWA/EWA.
- E24-R5: compute K1 (f1(K1) = f2(0)) and K2 (f1(K2) = f2(K2)); Δ < K1 high risk, all margin to Stage 1; K1 ≤ Δ ≤ 2K2 equalize with δ1 ≥ δ2; Δ > 2K2 equalize with δ2 > δ1.
- E25-R1: single decision variable Qh, comparing marginal current release risk with expected future risk reduction; optional water-supply penalty.
- System-level: E21-R4 selects which reservoir's reserved capacity to use and when, by flood type, season stage, reservoir class, and encounter timing; E21-R5 impounds upstream so TGR stays higher when basin risk is controlled.

**Choosing between approaches**
- Analytical marginal-utility/KKT rules were used for single-reservoir two-stage models with assumed error distributions (E03-R7, E05-R5, E10-R3, E24-R5, E26-R5); E21 used qualitative coordination rules for a cascade, without a reported algorithm (E21-R4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Identities and classifications hold under stated assumptions: ending storage at lower FLWL (E03-R3), Gaussian uncertainty and σ2 > Tσ1 (E24-R5), assumed error/loss functions (E03-R7), KKT sufficiency from convexity (E10-R3).
- x and m are decision parameters without external calibration (E03-R5, E05-R2).
- Type labels are paper-specific (E05-R5); zone thresholds are model-specific (E26-R5).
- Simplifications: square-wave flows (E24-R1, E25-R1), perfectly known antecedent inflow (E25-R1).
- E21 does not report allocation thresholds, proof, or a general compensation boundary (E21-R4, E21-R5).

**Example values**
- TGR held at 153.03 m vs FLWL 145 m through upstream compensation (E21-R5).

## RP06-C Safety first: define the safe domain, then optimize benefits

**When papers did this**
- Choosing an upper dynamic-control bound or FLWL (E39-R6, E41-R3, E41-R5, E44-R5, E45-R4).
- Real-time operation with updated forecasts once flood risk was constrained (E33-R7).
- Comparing candidate policies, quantiles, or pilot strategies that might trade benefit for structural risk (E18-R7, E22-R8, E23-R6, E29-R3, E29-R6, E50-R5).

**How**
- Maximize a benefit subject to a risk constraint:
  - E41-R3: maximize IOWL s.t. Cr{Vmax ≥ Vdesign} ≤ α or Cr{Zmax ≥ Zdesign} ≤ α, plus downstream discharge constraint (Eqs. 8–11 in E41); E41-R5 picks the highest grid IOWL whose K ≤ design frequency.
  - E33-R7: maximize hydropower under a risk constraint, implement the current segment, re-optimize rolling.
  - E44-R5: raise upper WLFS boundaries for annual hydropower only while aggregate flood-risk, refill, and physical limits hold.
  - E45-R4: maximize average annual generation over five-year FLWL blocks s.t. cumulative risk ≤ stationary benchmark; E45-R5 adds a CVaR constraint when CVaR exceeds benchmark (B2), keeps B1 otherwise.
  - E39-R6: recompute risk domains and probabilities over FLWL 127.2–127.8 m, take highest within design risk.
- Penalty form: E50-R5 maximizes normalized mean storage minus 1.0 per release above maximum safe release, by differential evolution.
- Post-hoc safety screen: retain a policy only if storage stays below critical ceiling (E22-R8), or levels stay within TGR bounds and Shashi categories (E29-R6); E29-R3 runs every 1–100% forecast quantile and keeps those meeting safety and benefit criteria.
- E12-R2: fit a maximum-entropy error distribution (mode δ0, 99.99% interval) rather than optimize for max/min error.
- E23-R6: stochastic control enforcing the elevation limit across all 32 tree branches, vs deterministic most-probable scenario.
- E18-R7: retain a strategy where benefits and flood outcomes are favorable and it fits USACE tools.

**Choosing between approaches**
- Probability constraint alone (E45-R4) vs adding tail-loss CVaR (E45-R5), switched by whether CVaR exceeds benchmark.
- Hard credibility constraint (E41-R3), penalty term (E50-R5), or post-simulation screening (E22-R8, E29-R6); the evidence does not state conditions for choosing among them.

**Limits and gaps**
- Grid-based or tested-range selection, no full optimization or cost analysis (E39-R6, E41-R5).
- Safety inferred from modeled limits, no independent validation (E22-R8); simulation only (E29-R6); no deployment evidence (E23-R6).
- No universal quantile rule (E29-R3); E18-R7 criteria qualitative; no human authorization/fallback (E33-R7).
- Optimizer settings unreported (E44-R5, E50-R5); CVaR depends on a constant unit cost (E45-R5).

**Example values**
- K = 0.0977% at 202.5 m vs 0.1040% at 203 m in May, Pdesign 0.1% (E41-R5).
- Proposed FLWL 127.6 m (E39-R6); storage ceiling 2698.5 MCM at 352 m (E22-R8); ~30,000 evaluations, 10 seeds (E50-R5).

## RP06-D No single optimum: keep a noninferior set

**When papers did this**
- Objectives conflicted under uncertain inflows and decision makers needed alternatives (E02-R5, E34-R5, E49-R4).
- Cascades had many objectives (E15-R5, E48-R1) or a single recommendation was needed from competing risks (E20-R6, E20-R9).

**How**
- Constraint / epsilon methods: E02-R5 two-direction constraint method (expected peak discharge with expected highest level fixed, then reversed), K constraint values; E34-R5 bounds two of three expected surrogate ratios while optimizing the third.
- Evolutionary many-objective search: NSGA-III (E14-R7); five-objective HPFOR with RSEA (E15-R5, E15-R7); RMOO with six risk objectives (E49-R4).
- Enumerate feasible alternatives under event logic: E48-R3 restricts Houziyan to discharge-capacity operation and combines options for Shuangjiangkou and Pubugou into six alternatives, then ranks by MCDM (E48-R1, E48-R7).
- Interpret representative solutions (E15-R7: schemes 1, 25, 57, 85 vs conventional HFOR).
- E20-R6: aggregate only when commensurable (both in euros, Ir = Iq + Iv), but still build a Pareto front to check the aggregate is a reasonable compromise (E20-R9).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E20-R6: equal-weight sum when objectives share units and no preference information exists; Pareto treatment when objectives are non-commensurable or explicit multiobjective treatment is needed.
- E49-R4: RMOO over single-risk SP or CCP because each alone may leave a risk dimension uncontrolled; very large events shift emphasis to vulnerability, small events make probability near zero.

**Limits and gaps**
- No global optimality beyond solver run (E02-R5); equal relevance is an assumption (E20-R6); compromise claims are case-bounded (E20-R9, E48-R7, E49-R4).
- Preference elicitation and approval unreported (E14-R7, E15-R7); E48-R3 importance weights not given.
- E15-R5 prints "max F4 = min(…)"; the card flags this as a possible label inconsistency.
- E34-R5 ratios are unvalidated surrogates.

**Example values**
- K = 10 (E02-R5); E48-R7 ranking (6, 4, 5, 3, 1, 2).

## RP06-E The final choice depends on preferences and ranking rules

**When papers did this**
- A single alternative had to be chosen from a Pareto/noninferior set (E02-R7, E12-R4, E49-R5) or from conflicting indicator rankings (E38-R5).
- A scalar objective needed weights (E22-R5, E40-R5, E43-R3); a basin index needed reservoir weights (E38-R4); a criterion set needed screening (E47-R1, E47-R5, E48-R4, E48-R5).
- Several institutions owned the trade-offs (E21-R6).

**How**
- TOPSIS variants: SMAA-TOPSIS with LHS Monte Carlo, compared with SMAA-2, run without weights, with AHP weights from three experts, and with uncertain weights (E02-R7); TOPSIS nearest weighted ideal / farthest nadir (E49-R5); TOPSIS with combined subjective+entropy weights and Mahalanobis distance (E38-R5); ideal-point Euclidean closeness as a training target (E47-R2).
- Fuzzy optimum selection with binary-contrast weights (E12-R4).
- AHP with 1–9 scale and CR ≤ 0.1 (E38-R4); improved entropy weights (E48-R5); PCA instead of deleting correlated criteria, keeping components until cumulative variance > 90% (E48-R4).
- Criterion deletion: Fj = Rj·Gj, Pj = log10(Fj/mean F), delete Pj < −1 (E47-R5).
- Weights by trial: grid search on FO = W1F1 + W2F2 (E22-R5); fixed weights 0.3/0.3/0.4 (E40-R5); MAVE-set weights and hard vs soft constraints in MPC scenarios (E43-R3).
- E05-R7: read preference x jointly with forecast uncertainty r; higher conservation preference lowers allowable r_max for small expected inflow, can reverse for large inflow with binding constraint.
- E21-R6: generate a scheme ensemble, then multi-round consultation led by FCDRH selects the authorized scheme.

**Choosing between approaches**
- Uncertain criteria and weights → stochastic SMAA sampling (E02-R7); correlated criteria → PCA (E48-R4); conflicting indicator rankings → combined weights with covariance-aware distance (E38-R5); institutional owners → consultation (E21-R6). These are the authors' stated triggers, not a tested selection rule.

**Limits and gaps**
- Expert elicitation protocol unreported (E02-R7); fuzzy formulas partly in supplement (E12-R4); weight grid and criterion not fully reported (E22-R5); E40-R5 choice described as arbitrary/reasonable.
- Equal weights are a demonstration (E49-R5); entropy weights lack elicited preference (E48-R5); deletion threshold partly subjective (E47-R5); closeness is a proxy (E47-R2).
- Consultation criteria and authority rules unreported (E21-R6); E05-R7 relationships are model-conditional.

**Example values**
- CRs 0.049 and 0.054; attribute weights {0.5, 0.5} (E38-R4); combined weights (0.2921, 0.3741, 0.3365), Scheme 7 closeness 0.6284 (E38-R5); E = (0.528, 0.472) (E48-R5).

## RP06-F Formulate objectives to fit the problem

**When papers did this**
- Selecting an optimization formulation for a flood stage, event scale, or site purpose (E12-R3, E14-R6, E18-R3, E46-R3, E46-R4).
- Comparing policies on flood-space requirement (E16-R2, E17-R3), choosing releases at each MPC step (E35-R6), or calibrating a model with its own internal objective (E20-R7, E36-R4).

**How**
- Add duration via resilience: integrate piecewise system performance over the event and maximize it alongside min level and peak (E12-R3, Eqs. 6–10).
- Scale-dependent objectives: level/release objectives (TSO) for small floods; add five risk/resilience/vulnerability objectives (MRO) for large floods (E14-R6).
- Flood-pool metrics: required pool = maximum accumulated storage above TOCS; drawdown penalty weight w (E16-R2); percent FP reduction vs actual pool, 0% when required pool exceeds design pool (E17-R3, Eq. 7).
- Single penalized objective: high penalties for downstream release above 200 m³/s and end-of-event supply deviation, plus spill, guide-curve, and release-change terms (E35-R6).
- Minimize probability-weighted maximum level over scenarios (E36-R4).
- Stage-specific objectives with weights wI, wII and targets IL, SL, NL, DL given by decision makers (E46-R3, E46-R4).
- Site-specific objectives per pilot (E18-R3).
- Keep the per-event penalty P separate from the ensemble expected-damage calibration score Ir (E20-R7).

**Limits and gaps**
- Modeled indices or proxies, not observed damage (E12-R3, E17-R3); TOCS offset and w are study constructs (E16-R2).
- Scale rule shown with two events only (E14-R6); weights case-specific / trial and error (E35-R6, E46-R4); targets assumed (E46-R3).
- E36-R4 does not detail conversion to one implemented release; E20-R7 gives no optimality proof; E18-R3 construction rules are in cited reports.

**Example values**
- w ∈ {0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5} (E16-R2); wI = 0.2, wII = 2, l = 2 (E46-R4).

## EP08-A Build the criteria system and decision matrix

**When papers did this**
- Alternatives and risk metrics were ready for preference-based selection or basin-level comparison (E02-E6, E38-E7, E47-E2, E48-E3).

**How**
- List criteria with directions: five cost criteria Q_max, Z_max, P_Q, P_Z, Z_e, with Q_max, Z_max, Z_e random and P_Q, P_Z constant (E02-E6); ten criteria, one benefit, nine cost (E47-E2); 12 cost criteria (Zmax, Ze, Rv per reservoir; Qmax, T, W at Leshan) (E48-E3).
- Aggregate per-reservoir indicators by AHP weights (multiply and sum over 14 reservoirs) (E38-E7).
- Screen criteria: build BP training samples between ideal/anti-ideal points with closeness targets (E47-E3), train BP(10:11:1) (E47-E4), compute importance Rj and sensitivity Gj (E47-E5), delete Pj < −1 (E47-E6).
- Decorrelate: Pearson → PCA → retain components until > 90% variance, varimax loadings (E48-E4); then improved entropy weights (E48-E5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E47 deletes low-influence criteria; E48 keeps all and transforms correlated ones by PCA because deletion may remove meaningful aspects (E48-R4).

**Limits and gaps**
- Normalization details not reported (E02-E6); no weight sensitivity at aggregation (E38-E7); some risk criteria computed by cited methods (E47-E2).
- BP fit is not generalization (E47-E4); sensitivity ignores joint perturbations (E47-E5); sample adequacy not assessed (E47-E3).
- E48-E3 T/W labels come from table extraction; no missing-data treatment or software reported (E48-E4).

**Example values**
- 30 samples (1–25 train, 26–30 verify) (E47-E3); learning rate 0.8, momentum 0.5, tolerance 0.0005, 30,000 cycles; R² 0.981 / 0.968 (E47-E4).
- Two components retain 96.7% (E48-E4); E = (0.528, 0.472) (E48-E5).

## EP08-B Rank and test ranking stability

**When papers did this**
- To check whether rankings change with weights (E02-E7), flood hydrographs (E38-E9), deleted criteria (E47-E7), or correlation handling and method (E48-E6, E48-E7).

**How**
- E02-E7: 10,000 SMAA-TOPSIS simulations; no weights, AHP weights, uniform/normal weights within ±20%; compare with SMAA-2; report rank acceptability, holistic acceptability, P_f, RUD.
- E38-E9: rank schemes for eight 1% design floods, sum order values.
- E47-E7: TOPSIS, fuzzy matter-element, fuzzy optimization with equal weights on original vs selected matrices.
- E48-E6, E48-E7: same three methods on original vs PCA matrix, compare rank agreement.

**Limits and gaps**
- No external benchmark of true ranking quality (E02-E7, E47-E7, E48-E6); comparative case evaluation, not operational validation (E38-E9, E48-E7).

**Example values**
- Normal-weight SD = one-tenth of means (E02-E7); Schemes 5 and 6 lowest order sum 25 (E38-E9).
- Without PCA, alternatives 1, 3, 5 differ across methods; with PCA all agree (E48-E7).

## EP08-C Select a compromise from the noninferior set

**When papers did this**
- A single plan or parameter set was needed from noninferior candidates (E02-E4, E12-E4, E20-E8, E20-E9, E34-E6, E38-E8, E40-E6, E49-E7).

**How**
- Generate the set: two-step constraint method, K = 10 (E02-E4); epsilon bounds with relaxed first pass (E34-E6); SSDP per scenario with shared decisions and weights 0.3/0.3/0.4 (E40-E6).
- Select: fuzzy optimum (E12-E4); minimum Ir = Iq + Iv (E20-E8); Pareto dominance with balanced solution "closest to the origin" (E20-E9); named solutions minimizing one risk at another solution's levels (E34-E6); weighted TOPSIS with Mahalanobis distance, subjective weights (0.45, 0.45, 0.1) (E38-E8); equal-weight TOPSIS plus hypervolume comparison (E49-E7).

**Limits and gaps**
- Equal weights not stakeholder-validated (E20-E8, E49-E7); dominance/tie details unreported (E20-E9); SSDP discretization and Pareto criterion unreported (E40-E6); supplementary details for E12-E4.
- E34-E6 weights reported around 0.7/0.3 but OCR shows 0.71/0.32.

**Example values**
- E49-E7: Solution R 0.080, 0.325, 0.5729, 1.1710, 2.250 h, 28.230 h; Solution C 0.095, 0.290, 0.5815, 1.2443, 3.150 h, 28.785 h; R has 1.5% higher downstream risk probability.
- E38-E8: 1998 example selects Scheme 7.

## EP08-D Staged screening and multi-agency consultation

**When papers did this**
- A portfolio had more reservoirs than full studies allowed (E18-E9); candidate schemes affected multiple mandates (E21-E5).

**How**
- E18-E9: Stage A simple elimination (controlled outlet, WCM/WCP, forecast skill); Stage B suitability scoring over collaboration, hydraulics, operations, environment, precipitation forecast skill, balancing benefit against effort; Stage C detailed assessment and dialogue.
- E21-E5: CROC submits a scheme ensemble; FCDRH-led authorities, CWRC, CWB, hydropower stations, and grid companies consult in rounds and select a final scheme.

**Limits and gaps**
- Scoring formula and thresholds unreported (E18-E9); agenda decisions, dissent handling, approval thresholds unreported (E21-E5). Only two papers (provisional).

**Example values**
- 71 reservoirs eligible for Stage B, 13 selected for beta testing (E18-E9); ten consultations during the fourth/fifth floods (E21-E5).
