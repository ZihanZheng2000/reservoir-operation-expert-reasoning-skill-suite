# Reference for unit 3.3-adaptive-logic: what the papers did

This file covers how papers made reservoir flood operation adaptive: stage-specific rules, threshold branches, rolling (receding-horizon) execution, shared-then-branching decisions, forecast-error correction, seasonal bounds, and the conversion of plans into the current release. It summarizes 85 steps from 34 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; all values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP07-A Different flood stages need different actions

**When papers did this**
- At each decision time during a flood hydrograph (E01-R4), when flood operation begins or changes phase (E46-R1), or when forecast indicators change across their horizons (E19-R5, E21-R3).
- When a dry interval is expected while the reservoir is above or near the planned upper FLWL (E09-R1).
- When explaining why a selected solution changes risk over time (E49-R6).

**How**
- Mode selection by comparing current inflow and the maximum forecast inflow over the remaining lead time with the downstream safety discharge Q_s: pre-release, normal current-rule control, or refill when the forecast is below Q_s (E01-R4).
- Two-period plan: water-use pre-release when inflow is below utilization discharge during a forecast dry period, then flood-control pre-release once a large inflow is forecast (E09-R1).
- Stage decomposition from observable conditions: before arrival create detention capacity, before the peak detain inflow within safety, after the peak recover conservation storage; post-peak sub-stages; multi-peak events may re-enter stages (E46-R1).
- Stage-specific objectives: stage 1 trades level accuracy against smooth release; stage 2 trades peak-release reduction against surcharge above NL; stage 3 minimizes end-of-operation level error. Weights wI, wII and the number of post-peak steps l were varied and checked against target-level coverage and maxima (E46-R4).
- Sequenced forecast "lines": vapor convergence (VCWL/PP) for early level adjustment, precipitation forecast for stronger prerelease, streamflow forecast for final drawdown to FLWL and regulation, then prefill after the event (E19-R5).
- Lead-time staging: qualitative 7–14-day outlooks drive capacity preparation and pre-discharge; quantitative 3–5-day and 1-day peak forecasts drive specific releases and peak staggering (E21-R3).
- Cascade allocation of reserved capacity by flood type, flood-season stage, reservoir class, encounter timing and role relative to TGR; release capacity gradually as post-flood inflow declines (E21-R4).
- Interpretation of hedging as spreading release over more time before the peak, lowering maximum release (E49-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E01 and E29-style threshold modes use a single forecast-maximum comparison (E01-R4); E46 uses measured hydrologic conditions per stage (E46-R1); E19 and E21 use several forecast types ordered by lead time (E19-R5, E21-R3).

**Limits and gaps**
- Thresholds are case-specific (E01-R4, E46-R1); trigger details are only conceptual in E09 (E09-R1).
- E19 gives no complete pseudocode or thresholds (E19-R5); E21 gives no allocation algorithm, thresholds or risk proof, and forecast skill is reported for two floods only (E21-R3, E21-R4).
- Weight selection is case-based without uncertainty quantification (E46-R4).
- E49's mechanism is an interpretation of simulated trajectories; solution R has higher CVaR in rising periods but lower in peak-release periods (E49-R6).

**Example values**
- wI = 0.2, wII = 2, l = 2 (E46-R4).

## RP07-B Switch actions by thresholds

**When papers did this**
- When forecasted risk on a forecast day exceeds the tolerance, r_t > R_t (E07-R4, E07-R6).
- At each operation time, comparing forecast and current inflow with thresholds (E15-R4, E29-R5) or with outer storage allocation (E10-R4), or comparing expected availability A with trigger values (E26-R5).
- When implementing a class-specific hedging decision (E24-R6); when a risk estimate exceeds an acceptable level (E30-R6, E31-R6); during rising-stage operation (E37-R4).

**How**
- Constrained prerelease: select the smallest set of lowest-ranked threshold-exceeding members needed to meet tolerance, spread each member's volume difference over days up to the forecast day, cap entries at the release limit and redistribute to earlier days, then take the maximum day-1 release (E07-R4).
- Operations delegation: risk above tolerance → flood-control (USACE) mode; within tolerance → water-supply (Sonoma Water) mode (E07-R6).
- KKT branches within the RFH: s12 ≥ 0 → detention allocation, s12 < 0 → prerelease; endpoint branches pick the stage with larger marginal risk, the interior branch equalizes marginal risk (Eqs. (17)–(25) in E10) (E10-R4).
- Hierarchical branches under a release cap R_lim: below threshold 1 pre-fill to level 1; up to level 1 release at level 1 to flood limit; up to threshold 2 pre-fill using the cap; up to maximum release pre-release at cap; current inflow above cap → normal control at cap (E15-R4).
- Hedging targets r̄1 = qthres − δ1, r̄2 = T·qthres − δ2, release r̄u,t = r̄t − q̄d,t, with storage clipped at 0 or K (Eqs. (28)–(29) in E24) (E24-R6).
- Three zones: A below SWA → R1 = D1; within trigger range → R1 = R1*; above EWA → R1 = Ru1 (E26-R5).
- If forecast max ≤ SQin store inflow below Zu; if > SQin prerelease evenly before the peak keeping level above Zl (Eq. (3) in E29) (E29-R5).
- Risk curves: if risk is too high revise the program or use emergency responses; for a fixed acceptable risk solve for HR, reserve storage and acceptable precipitation (E30-R6, E31-R6).
- Stepwise 72-hour cumulative net rainfall bands with level-dependent releases and free overflow in the top band (E37-R4).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Ensemble-risk branches (E07) need member forecasts and a tolerance curve; analytic KKT/zone branches (E10, E24, E26) follow from a model's optimality conditions; deterministic forecast thresholds (E15, E29, E37) use a forecast magnitude or rainfall. The evidence does not compare them directly.

**Limits and gaps**
- No optimality proof for E07's heuristic (E07-R4); delegation is simulated, not an implemented authorization protocol (E07-R6).
- Thresholds are model- or case-specific (E15-R4, E26-R5); E24 reports no real-time authorization or emergency fallback (E24-R6); E29 reports no latency or feasibility safeguards (E29-R5).
- E30 and E31 emergency actions are examples, not executed; the threshold is assumed (E30-R6, E31-R6).
- E37's rules are limited to the rising stage and Q_c is undefined in the card (E37-R4).

**Example values**
- 14/61 members above threshold at day 8, r_8 = 23.0%, R_8 = 9.8%, 8 members selected, 529 cfs day-1 release; all-horizon maximum 1,240 cfs at day 14; Hopland limit 8,000 cfs (E07-R4).
- SQin = 35,000 m³/s (E29-R5). Rainfall bands ≤43, 43–55, 55–62, 62–79, 79–90, >90 mm; initial level 325.0 m (E37-R4).

## RP07-C Rolling update: execute only the next step

**When papers did this**
- At the end of each period when new forecasts and state arrive (E02-R8, E33-R7, E34-R7, E21-R7), at fixed steps: daily (E07-R5), 6-hourly until flood end (E10-R5), hourly (E35-R7).
- When choosing a control architecture for many evaluations (E17-R2) or for delayed river response across reservoirs (E43-R2).

**How**
- Optimize through the horizon, implement only the immediate release, advance state by mass balance, regenerate the forecast, rerun optimization, risk assessment and SMAA-TOPSIS (E02-R8).
- 15-day forecast horizon with a one-day implementation horizon; pick the maximum required first-day release (E07-R5).
- One-step release from the current allocation via routing and FLWL/MWL/spill constraints, recomputed each interval (E10-R5).
- Scenario-based convex MPC re-optimized each timestep using ensemble forecasts, chosen for scalability over SDP (E17-R2).
- Maximize hydropower within a risk constraint, implement the current segment, advance one interval (E33-R7).
- Recourse: implement current decision, update, rerun and compare with a fixed no-recourse expected-value strategy (E34-R7).
- Implement only the root release, discard the rest of the open-loop plan, rebuild the DSF/PSF tree (E35-R7).
- MPC with a linear state-space internal model and SOBEK hydrodynamics outside the loop for state updates (E43-R2).
- Basin practice: operation ensembles for different lead times, revised as forecasts update, within consultations (E21-R7).

**Limits and gaps**
- All except E21 are model/simulation procedures; none reports human authorization or fallback (E02-R8, E07-R5, E33-R7, E34-R7, E35-R7). E10 mentions human correction of release fluctuations (E10-R5).
- E21 reports no update frequency, stopping criteria, or oscillation safeguards (E21-R7).
- E17's choice is methodological, not proof of best policy; tree MPC left for future work (E17-R2). E43 neglects losses and depends on case calibration (E43-R2).

**Example values**
- 96 h closed-loop horizon at 1-hour steps (E35-R7); 6-hour interval (E10-R5).

## RP07-D Share current decisions; let future decisions branch with information

**When papers did this**
- At each real-time decision in the flood season (E03-R2) or as flood forecasts update over an event (E24-R1).
- When solving a two-stage model (E10-R3), converting an ensemble into a tree (E23-R3, E35-R5), or setting up multistage stochastic optimization (E23-R5, E34-R6).

**How**
- Two-stage rolling window: Stage 1 is the next interval (inflow treated as predictable), Stage 2 the remaining forecast horizon; shift by one step (E03-R2).
- Stage 1 = current period, Stage 2 = remaining horizon until the flood pool is empty, with stage-average square-wave flows (E24-R1).
- KKT storage allocation between RFH and outside RFH: lower bound active → more to later stage; upper bound active → all to RFH; else equalize marginal risks (Eqs. (11)–(16) in E10) (E10-R3).
- Scenario trees with one early control, then branching; precipitation-dependent distance metrics for a large lagged basin; fixed tree or tolerance-based reduction (E23-R3).
- Probability-weighted expected stage/terminal cost with a nodal partition matrix; hard forebay constraints on every branch; a chance-constraint variant proposed conceptually (E23-R5).
- Nonanticipativity on the current-period release only (Eq. (38) in E34) (E34-R6).
- Mass-conservative tree reduction, sharing history before branch points; motivated because a fully shared average control can be infeasible and fully independent member controls are not implementable (E35-R5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Two-stage abstractions (E03, E10, E24) versus multi-stage trees (E23, E34, E35). E24 states the two-stage form simplifies the real system (E24-R1); E23 ties branch placement to when observations resolve uncertainty (E23-R3).

**Limits and gaps**
- Field forecast updating not reported (E03-R2); KKT sufficiency rests on convexity (E10-R3).
- General tree design beyond fixed binary settings not established; objective weights not legible/reported (E23-R3, E23-R5).
- Nonanticipativity claimed only for the release (E34-R6); E35's tree algorithm is referenced, not reproduced (E35-R5).

**Example values**
- Time step of 6 h or 1 day as practical examples (E03-R2); 10–15-day horizon (E23-R5).

## RP07-E Correction and fallback when the forecast is wrong

**When papers did this**
- When forecast error is detected during forecast-based operation (FBO) (E37-R7, E37-R8).
- When future inflow is uncertain or forecast/monitoring data degrade (E46-R2).

**How**
- Classify error sign: underforecast → enlarge discharge; overforecast → opposite correction (E37-R7).
- Either change discharge at once and return to conventional operation (CO), or adjust gradually over several steps while keeping FBO; multi-step preferred when the jump is large (E37-R8).
- Measurement-based rules using current level, inflow and rainfall as a simpler fallback or alternative, accepting some loss of ideal peak attenuation; perfect-forecast optimization kept only as reference (E46-R2).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E37 corrects within a forecast-based scheme; E46 avoids dependence on forecasts altogether. Only two papers; no comparison reported.

**Limits and gaps**
- E37's remedy is preliminary and conceptual; no quantitative thresholds; positive-error trajectory not fully specified (E37-R7, E37-R8).
- E46's robustness is a design rationale, not proven outside validation cases (E46-R2).

## RP07-F Seasonal staging and multi-year adjustment

**When papers did this**
- When defining a real-time FLWL policy for the flood season (E29-R4) or balancing safety and conservation over a season (E41-R1).
- When the inflow distribution changes over years (E45-R4).

**How**
- Scheme I with pre-flood/main/post-flood bounds versus Scheme II with one whole-season pair; upper bounds increase with lead time (E29-R4).
- Divide the season and allow dynamic bounds with the original FLWL as lower limit (E41-R1).
- Five-year FLWL blocks as decision variables, maximizing average annual generation subject to cumulative risk at or below the stationary benchmark, without a CVaR constraint (Scheme B1, Eq. (8) in E45) (E45-R4).

**Limits and gaps**
- E29's bounds come from an earlier model and are not rederived (E29-R4).
- E45's scenario is in an appendix not reproduced; B1's loss can exceed the stationary CVaR benchmark (E45-R4).

**Example values**
- Lower bound 145 m in E29's schemes (E29-R4).

## EP09-A Convert plan results into the current release

**When papers did this**
- After an optimal storage target, allocation, or member schedules are available (E03-E9, E07-E7, E10-E4, E24-E5), at each rolling forecast update (E04-E9), or once a D-PSL is chosen (E11-E4).

**How**
- Mass balance R_1* = W_0 + I_1 − W_1*; if below R_1,min use R_1,min, leaving storage below the optimum (E03-E9).
- Pre-release equal to mean predicted inflow over the effective h-day lead time (Eq. (14) in E04), routed with release caps (E04-E9).
- Maximum day-1 release across selected risky members and forecast days, after checking post-release risk is within tolerance (E07-E7).
- Route current s1 through reservoir balance with physical constraints (E10-E4).
- Pre-release or retain to D-PSL and route with maximum safety release (E11-E4).
- Total-flow targets from δ, minus forecast intermediate flow, giving reservoir releases and Stage-1 carryover (Eqs. (25)–(27) in E24) (E24-E5).

**Limits and gaps**
- No actuator, ramp or gate checks in E03 (E03-E9) or E24 (E24-E5); E04's full routing and pre-release implementation are not in the card (E04-E9).
- E07 gives no formal optimization criterion beyond tolerance and constraints (E07-E7); routing simplifications in E10 and E11 (E10-E4, E11-E4).

**Example values**
- TGR: FCWL 145 m; caps 53,900 m³/s for Jingjiang safety and 79,000 m³/s at 175–180.4 m; release capacity above 180.4 m (E04-E9).
- FLWL 85 m, EWL 88 m, MWL 93.6 m, max hydropower discharge 2,800 m³/s (E10-E4).

## EP09-B Execute staged or branching rules

**When papers did this**
- At each decision time or forecast update (E01-E6, E15-E3, E27-E3, E29-E4, E26-E4), daily (E13-E2, E16-E5, E50-E2, E50-E4), in recession (E01-E5, E03-E2), or by flood stage (E37-E3, E46-E2, E46-E3, E46-E4, E39-E1, E44-E3, E28-E3, E19-E8).

**How**
- *Forecast-threshold modes:* TGR pre-release / normal / refill from Table 4 thresholds (E01-E6); refill toward Zu with a minimum firm-capacity release, or hold Zu (E01-E5); store if forecast max ≤ SQin, otherwise even prerelease (E29-E4); HPFOR multi-level pre-fill/pre-release/normal branches (E15-E3); pre-discharge when aggregated lead-time inflow exceeds the flood standard, reservoir first then CSR (Eqs. (6)–(11) in E44) (E44-E3).
- *Prefill permission:* level inside FLWL bounds and decreasing, inflow below threshold, no flood forecast in lead time; else prerelease to the lower bound (E03-E2).
- *Analytical hedging cases:* select Case 1–3 rules from d_min, d_0, d_e, W_1h and RFCC (Eqs. 44–46 in E03) (E03-E8); three-zone R1 rule (E26-E4).
- *Ensemble risk rules:* fraction of members exceeding 111,000 acre-ft vs a lead-time tolerance curve, largest release across leads, then maxima with water-supply and emergency releases (E13-E2); fraction exceeding TOCS over 14 days vs a piecewise-linear curve (x1 zero-risk days, x2 slope, x3 TOCS multiplier), minimum release to lower risk, larger of FIRO and baseline (E50-E4); 70th-percentile member over 1–5-day cumulative inflows, release to keep TOCS (E16-E5).
- *Baseline rule:* median historical release by day, exponential hedging below median storage, TOCS increase, safe-release and ramping limits (E50-E2).
- *Rainfall/runoff bands:* 6-h forecast runoff R6 with level thresholds (E27-E3); 72-h cumulative net rainfall bands (E37-E3); prerelease the maximum average inflow over a 10-h lead time with level-tied caps (E28-E3).
- *Measurement-based stages (E46):* pre-release steps 225/600/1000 m³/s from interpolated rainfall thresholds (E46-E2); peak release = α × recent inflow, α bilinearly interpolated from level and net inflow, non-decreasing with limited increments (E46-E3); post-peak stepwise reduction toward 400 m³/s base (E46-E4).
- *Multi-line sequence:* V, P and S lines then prefill (E19-E8); six-stage Dahuofang rules (E39-E1).
- *Trace inspection* of pre-fill, pre-release and recession behavior (E15-E9, E19-E10).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Forecast-triggered rules (E01, E15, E29, E44) versus ensemble-risk rules (E13, E16, E50) versus measurement-based rules (E46); E50 contrasts a no-forecast baseline with FIRO (E50-E2, E50-E4). Note E04-E9 uses the *mean* lead-time inflow and E28-E3 the *maximum average* inflow for prerelease.

**Limits and gaps**
- Thresholds case-specific (E01-E6, E15-E3, E46-E3); full time-stepping not reported (E01-E5); E03 gives no detailed prerelease or crisis schedules (E03-E2) and its thresholds depend on assumed loss functions (E03-E8).
- No complete rule table or fallback in E19 (E19-E8); E19's Muifa run omitted typhoon forecasts (E19-E10).
- Fixed horizon/percentile in E16 (E16-E5); unreported parameters in E50 and E13 (E50-E2, E50-E4, E13-E2); no authorization or override in E27, E39 (E27-E3, E39-E1); no latency handling in E29 (E29-E4); E37 rising stage only (E37-E3); bank erosion unquantified in E44 (E44-E3); step count trades adaptability against gate operations (E46-E4).

**Example values**
- TGR: current inflow < 35,000 m³/s, forecast max 35,000–45,000 or ≥ 45,000 m³/s, max discharge 53,900 m³/s; Shashi < 44.5 m (56,700 m³/s) (E01-E6); minimum firm-capacity release ≈ 7,000 m³/s (E01-E5).
- R6 thresholds 21/26/29/36.5/40.5 mm, limits 12,000/17,000 m³/s, levels 326/328 m (E27-E3). Z0 = 194.5 m; 11,000 m³/s below 200 m, 13,000 m³/s below 203 m (E28-E3).
- Spill crest 116,500 acre-ft (E13-E2); 40 sampled members (E50-E4); lead times 48 h and 72 h (E44-E3); outflow 2–17 m³/s, max prerelease 200 m³/s (E19-E8); VPS prerelease 24 h earlier than PS (E19-E10).

## EP09-C Rolling update and closed-loop recomputation

**When papers did this**
- After each current-period decision or interval (E02-E8, E03-E10, E07-E8, E33-E9, E34-E8), daily (E16-E4), hourly (E26-E5, E35-E8), or after each optimization action (E17-E3, E23-E5, E43-E5); when forecast bias is detected (E37-E8).

**How**
- Implement only the immediate release, advance by mass balance, update forecast, repeat optimization and MCDM (E02-E8); re-evaluate prefill/prerelease and risk bounds each roll (E03-E10).
- Combine EFO release with spill, emergency, minimum and water-supply releases, route, advance one day (E07-E8).
- Daily MPC: ΔS_l = Σ_{t=1}^{l}(Q_f,t − R_f,t), S_f = S_0 + ΔS; minimize expected J over one release sequence; implement R_{t+1} = R*_f,0 (cvxpy, Clarabel) (E16-E4).
- Constrain 0 ≤ u_f ≤ R_max and |u_{t+1} − u_t| < RR; update storage with actual inflow (E17-E3).
- Multi-stage stochastic MPC with shared early controls (E23-E5); TB-MPC root value as the shared current decision (E35-E8); rolling recourse with MOSP adaptive later decisions (E34-E8).
- Current-hour release as a proportion of phase release from current-hour over total phase forecast inflow (E26-E5).
- Hydropower maximization with trust-region-reflective solver, shrinking horizon (E33-E9).
- Optimized flows passed to SOBEK, simulated states update the controller (E43-E5).
- Underforecast correction: increase discharge before point B, once then return to CO, or gradually while keeping FBO (E37-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are simulations or hindcasts; none reports live authorization, override or fallback (E02-E8, E07-E8, E33-E9, E34-E8, E35-E8).
- Constant R_max and RR assumed (E17-E3); no tree feedback in E16 (E16-E4); E23 demonstrates one forecast time and reports no solver checks (E23-E5).
- No software or runtime reported (E03-E10); no fallback if inflow falls outside scenarios (E34-E8); measurement noise not fully represented (E43-E5); E37's remedy is unvalidated (E37-E8).

**Example values**
- 41×14 forecast matrix (E16-E4); periods 1–12 (E02-E8); flood seasons 2015–2016 hourly (E26-E5); 96 h (E35-E8).

## EP09-D Multi-reservoir and multi-agency coordination and emergency branches

**When papers did this**
- When long-lead forecasts signal a major flood (E21-E6), quantitative forecasts support cooperative releases (E21-E7), actions affect power and shipping (E21-E8), after the event or during equipment emergencies (E21-E9), or when storage bounds are violated or floods exceed design conveyance (E24-E6).

**How**
- Pre-discharge TGR on qualitative 7–14-day information while avoiding spillage (E21-E6).
- Upstream reservoirs release reserved capacity and hold flood volumes to stagger tributary/mainstream peaks (E21-E7).
- Temporary generation adjustment by the State Grid and level coordination for navigation (E21-E8).
- Post-event reanalysis and 22 crisis-management plans for automation/equipment failures, accidents and emergencies (E21-E9).
- Impose s1 = 0 or K and recompute; for extreme or successive floods retain volume and give longer evacuation warning (E24-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- E21 gives no formal threshold, allocation calculation, counterfactual, uncertainty interval, or crisis-plan details (E21-E6, E21-E7, E21-E9); not all station trajectories reported (E21-E8).
- E24 has no operational emergency protocol or damage simulation (E24-E6).

**Example values**
- TGR level 163.36 m (29 July) to 153.03 m (14 August), FLWL 145 m, 17.75×10⁹ m³ reserved, about 80% of capacity (E21-E6).
- 8.2×10⁹ m³ held by 21 upstream reservoirs and 10.8×10⁹ m³ by TGR; 3 m stage reduction (E21-E7).
- Xiangjiaba output 2.2×10⁶ kW; level variations 4.95 m and 3.73 m; more than 4,500 ships (E21-E8).
