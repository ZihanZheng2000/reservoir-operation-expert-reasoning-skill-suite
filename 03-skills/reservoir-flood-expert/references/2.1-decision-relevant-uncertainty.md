# Reference for unit 2.1-decision-relevant-uncertainty: what the papers did

This file covers how papers decided which uncertainties matter for flood-season reservoir decisions, how they described error structure and dependence, and how they generated, propagated and checked uncertainty scenarios. It summarizes 158 steps from 40 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach. Values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

Sign conventions for "forecast error" differ between papers. Each definition is given with its step ID; do not mix them.

## RP02-A Forecast uncertainty must enter the decision

**When papers did this**
- A forecast guides a reservoir action or a real-time release that covers future periods (E04-R1, E33-R1, E36-R1, E49-R1).
- Release decisions depend on uncertain inflows 1–15 days ahead (E07-R2), on hourly flood inflows (E22-R1), or on short-term MPC with forecast inflows (E23-R1, E35-R1).
- Forecast-based control acts before the inflow is known (E27-R1, E49-R1).

**How**
- Framed the consequence as two-sided: under-preparation (damage, lost flood storage, higher downstream releases) versus over-preparation (cost, unnecessary water loss) (E04-R1, E22-R1). E35-R1 framed it as forecast errors delaying releases and causing excess downstream spillage.
- Treated how uncertainty changes across lead times, and how it propagates through operation, as the relevant quantity, rather than one static error measure (E04-R1).
- Replaced the single deterministic forecast with multiple scenarios:
  - Equally likely ensemble members (HEFS/ESP), with release decisions based on forecasted risk (E07-R2).
  - Bias-corrected NWP precipitation ensembles (E22-R1).
  - Ensemble or scenario MPC formulations (E23-R1, E35-R1).
  - Copula-sampled correlated errors added to deterministic forecasts, all scenarios equally likely (E34-R3).
  - Monte Carlo scenario trees from `Q_syn,t = Q_obs,t(1+σe_t)` (E36-R3).
  - Forecast error defined as observed minus forecast inflow, with its historical stochastic behavior reproduced (E49-R1).
- Diagnosed deterministic-forecast weakness by comparing MPC driven by a ±30% perturbed single forecast with a perfect-forecast reference (E35-R3).
- Chose numerical simulation over analytical methods because joint uncertainty across several reservoirs is high-dimensional and nonlinear (E33-R1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E07-R2 used the ensemble-risk route for real decisions and kept perfect-forecast operation only as a hypothetical comparator.
- E22-R1 compared a no-forecast approach (measured precipitation) with an ensemble approach (NWP precipitation).
- E36-R1 notes that either the qualified rate α or R² can serve as the accuracy summary.

**Limits and gaps**
- E04-R1 is the authors' motivation, not an observed operational failure.
- E07-R2 claims operators can interpret the rule more easily, but ran no user study.
- E22-R1 did no full probabilistic calibration or reliability analysis.
- E23-R1 assumes meteorological uncertainty is the largest component and samples hydrological-model uncertainty only partly.
- E27-R1 argues the size of measurement error only qualitatively.
- E33-R1, E36-R1 and E49-R1 model mainly inflow or forecast uncertainty, not every source.
- E34-R3 scenarios cover most but not all actual flows, and miss more often at longer lead times.
- E35-R3 used synthetic perturbation, not a measured forecast product.

**Example values**
- 40 scenarios (E34-R3).
- 100 equally likely scenarios over 12 three-hour periods (E36-R3).

## RP02-B Uncertainty does not come only from forecasts

**When papers did this**
- A dynamic flood-limited water level (FLWL) or a forecast-based program is being assessed with an imperfect forecast (E01-R2, E09-R2, E30-R1).
- Uncertain future inflow and uncertain system response must be assessed together (E06-R1).
- Probabilistic initial conditions are needed (E08-R2, E20-R3).
- Deterministic real-time routing is in use (E31-R1).

**How**
- **Forecast error plus hydrograph shape.** E01-R2 split forecast error into incorrect refill versus incorrect pre-release, and handled hydrograph uncertainty through `R(Z,P)`; Eqs. (1)–(8) in E01.
- **Load–resistance risk.** E06-R1 named highest water level or downstream peak discharge as the load and a safe value as the resistance (Eq. (10) in E06). E30-R1 used uncertain level as the load and a safe or controlled level as the threshold.
- **Hydraulic and wind terms** (E06-R5):
  - Actual discharge = coefficient × set discharge, with the coefficient normal.
  - Actual storage = measured storage + error, with the error normal.
  - Backwater height Gumbel, wave run-up Rayleigh.
  - All sampled with LHS, treated as independent.
- **Initial storage.** E08-R2 used a truncated Gaussian for rainless-day error and a normal relative error for flood inflow, then transformed excess storage W into the initial storage V0.
- **Duration and lead time from history** (E09-R2):
  - Counted consecutive dry days (≤3 mm) and fitted both a negative-exponential and an asymptotic regression.
  - Took natural lead time as the time from the end of main rain to the flood peak, and fitted a normal distribution to it.
- **Random initial storage in principle.** E20-R3 treated inflows and initial storage as random variables in its framework.
- **Six-factor specification.** E31-R1 used IFE, OE and SOE with correlation between adjacent time steps.
- **Rainfall as the risk source.** E39-R1 used forecast cumulative net rainfall `Ȳ = Y + E` (Y actual, E error) as a two-dimensional risk source, standing in for flood volume.

**Limits and gaps**
- E01-R2 gives no separate empirical hydrograph-shape distribution.
- E06-R1 "risk" is an operational exceedance indicator, not social or economic consequence.
- E06-R5 independence and distribution forms are assumptions. Backwater height was about 10⁻⁴ in the TGR case and was ignored.
- E08-R2 assumes weather and flood forecast errors are independent.
- E09-R2 builds no real-time dry-period forecast.
- E20-R3 fixed the initial level at the top of the conservation pool in its cases, so initial-level uncertainty was not tested there.
- E30-R1 does not fully report how each case uncertainty was identified.
- E31-R1 omits correlation between different sources.
- E39-R1 restricts to volume rather than peak, argued to suit high-regulation reservoirs.

**Example values**
- E09-R2: lead times up to an effective 10 h; the negative exponential was chosen as the more conservative fit.

## RP02-C Keep only decision-relevant sources and state exclusions

**When papers did this**
- Operating above or around the design FLWL (E08-R1).
- Only deterministic hydrological forecasts are available (E29-R2).
- A full risk model would contain many different factors (E37-R1, E37-R2).

**How**
- **Explicit scope choice.** E08-R1 modeled initial-level uncertainty and flood-forecast error jointly, and excluded discharge-capacity and storage-curve uncertainty by scope. It examined initial-level only, inflow only, and both together.
- **Limited by available inputs.** E29-R2 used a Bayesian forecasting system (BFS: meta-Gaussian normal-quantile transform, log-Weibull marginals; Eq. (1) in E29) around a deterministic forecast. It covered only hydrological and model uncertainty because quantitative rainfall forecasts were unavailable.
- **Inventory, then reduce.** E37-R1 sorted uncertainties into hydrological, hydraulic and decision-making groups. It identified forecast versus observed inflow as the main difference between conventional operation (CO, no forecast-error term) and forecast-based operation (FBO). E37-R2 then focused quantitative work on forecast net-rainfall error and treated other factors as relatively stable or secondary.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E08-R1 excluded sources by stated scope.
- E29-R2 excluded what the data could not supply.
- E37-R2 excluded what it judged less significant or less tractable.

**Limits and gaps**
- E08-R1's exclusions are explicit; the card does not infer that the excluded factors are negligible.
- E29-R2 does not claim full predictive uncertainty.
- E37-R1 does not quantify every listed factor.
- E37-R2 is a modeling choice, not proof that other errors are negligible elsewhere.

## RP02-D Error structure is not a single fixed distribution

**When papers did this**
- Forecasts are updated and uncertainty changes with lead time (E02-R2, E35-R4).
- Errors may be biased, non-Gaussian, heavy-tailed, nonstationary or seasonal (E04-R2, E04-R4, E06-R2).
- Errors differ between low flow and floods and across lead times (E27-R3, E42-R1).
- Hindcasts are short or sparse (E13-R1).

**How**
- **Evolution models.**
  - E02-R2 used MMFE, assuming zero-mean, Gaussian, temporally independent and stationary update increments with a variance–covariance (VCV) matrix.
  - E04-R2 to R3 replaced such assumptions with a copula model of relative error, `re_s,t = (Q_s,t − q_t)/q_t`. It used reductions `rw_s,t = re_s,t − re_{s−1,t}` and the additive relation in Eq. (9) of E04.
- **Seasonal split.** E04-R4 fitted June and July–September separately because the full-season series was nonstationary.
- **Fitted marginals.**
  - E04-R5 used L-moments with bias, RMSE and a K-S test, and selected the generalized logistic (GL) distribution.
  - E06-R3 fitted ten candidates and selected Wakeby by RMSE and AIC, with a Student-t copula.
  - E37-R3 used a chi-square test and accepted normality.
- **Other forms.**
  - Maximum-entropy distribution with its mode δ0 and a 99.99% interval (E12-R2).
  - Fuzzy variable with Cauchy membership and credibility, argued to capture vagueness that probability misses (E41-R2).
  - Gaussian mixture by lead time, with K chosen by AIC/BIC, k-means++ starting values and an EM fit (E42-R1, E42-R2).
- **Regime-limited simplification.** E27-R3 used a stationary Gaussian only in the high-flow regime.
- **Heuristic spread growth.** E35-R4 increased relative standard error toward the end of the horizon, held it when the forecast falls and raised it when the forecast rises.
- **Robustness against sparse hindcasts.** E13-R1 called for repeated synthetic forecast realizations and out-of-sample tests.

**Choosing between approaches**
- Copulas were used for dependent or skewed series (E06-R2).
- Vine copulas were set aside in favor of meta-elliptical copulas (E04-R2, E06-R3).
- E04-R5 relaxed α to 0.005 for main-season X2 because all p-values were very small.
- E27-R3 used the stationary approximation only where it held (high flow).

**Limits and gaps**
- E02-R2's VCV values were designed subjectively. E35-R4's terminal spread was found by trial and error.
- E04-R4 reports no new stationarity statistic.
- E04-R5 and E06-R3 marginal choices are case-specific. Not all E06 copula parameters are legible.
- E12-R2 gives few fitting details.
- E27-R3 low-flow data were too sparse to model.
- E41-R2's membership choice is partly judgmental.
- E42-R1 is univariate.

**Example values**
- E27-R3: μ≈0, σ≈600 m³/s at flows above 12,000 m³/s.
- E37-R3: χ² = 4.1926 < 5.9915, giving `N(0.019, 0.188²)`.
- E42-R2: K = 3.

## RP02-E Errors are dependent

**When papers did this**
- Multiple lead-time variables, serial flows, sites or ensemble members must be generated jointly (E04-R6, E06-R4, E13-R2, E49-R2).
- Downstream risk combines release and intermediate inflow (E24-R2).
- Errors must be propagated through routing over time (E28-R3, E30-R4, E31-R4).

**How**
- **Copulas across lead times.**
  - 4-D Student-t copula after checking autocorrelation at lags 1–23 (E04-R6).
  - t-copula over lead times (E49-R2) and over time and reservoirs, with insignificant dependencies removed (E34-R2).
  - Copula for correlated forecast errors combined with LHS for discharge and storage errors (E38-R3).
- **Serial flow.** E06-R4 used a Gumbel copula with Pearson III (P-III) marginals, conditioning each day on the previous day.
- **Adjacent errors.** E14-R4 used a bivariate copula of adjacent errors with Gibbs sampling, avoiding higher-dimensional copulas.
- **Ensembles.** E13-R2 and E13-R4 preserved bias, spread, temporal and spatial dependence and member clustering, using shared kNN and global heteroscedasticity stages.
- **Analytical covariance.**
  - E24-R2 used total error `ε_t = ε_u,t + ε_d,t` with a covariance term: positive correlation increases uncertainty and negative correlation offsets it.
  - E30-R4 and E31-R4 recursively propagated level-error mean, variance and adjacent Pearson correlation (Eqs. (13)–(17) in E30; Eqs. (13), (15), (23)–(24) in E31).
- **Stochastic routing.** E28-R3 used the SDE `dV = (μQ − μq)dt + σV dW`, with `σV(t) = σQ(t)√Δt` and `σQ(t) = μQ(t)σ`, solved by forward Euler.
- **Structure from the equations.** E08-R3 built a Bayesian network whose graph was set by experts from the routing equations.
- **Independence after a check.** E39-R5 found E and Y independent in its case and set the copula density to 1.
- **Raw ensembles.** E40-R1 used ensemble hydrographs directly because inflow persistence is hard to describe with simple Monte Carlo.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E34-R2 neglected spatial links for weakly connected subsystems.
- E30-R4 set covariance to zero only under its stated independence simplification.

**Limits and gaps**
- E04-R6 does not validate tail dependence separately.
- E06-R4's choice rests on a cited prior study and case statistics.
- E08-R3 was not learned from data.
- E14-R4 does not show that all higher-order dependence is preserved.
- E24-R2's Gaussian forms and correlations are assumed.
- E28-R3's Wiener increments are independent at each step, even though temporal persistence motivated the method.
- E30-R4 and E31-R4 do not demonstrate cross-source covariance.
- E39-R5's independence result must not be generalized.
- E49-R2's parameters are partly corrupted in the source.

**Example values**
- E04-R6: t-copula df = 3, p = 0.21 (June) and 0.10 (main season).
- E49-R2: correlations generally above 0.75.

## RP02-F Derive a distribution from accuracy metrics or deterministic forecasts

**When papers did this**
- Data are limited, or only a qualified rate, an R² or a grade is available (E01-R6, E28-R2, E36-R2).
- A point forecast must become a distribution (E15-R2, E42-R4).

**How**
- **Qualified rate to σ.** Assume zero-mean normal relative error and solve for σ from the qualified rate (Eq. (3) in E01, E28 and E36). Generate forecasts as `Q̂_t = Q_t(1+e_t)` (E01-R6). E36-R2 uses a permitted relative error εp = 20%. E28-R2 uses εα = 0.2.
- **R²/NSE to σ.** E28-R2 and E36-R2 use the whole-hydrograph R² through Eq. (8) in each paper.
- **Model-based distribution.** E15-R2 used a DGDNN forecast plus HMR to obtain the conditional Gaussian `p(x²_t | x¹_t)`.
- **Sampled errors to ensembles.** E42-R4 used MCMC samples from the fitted error PDF with two different formulas: one for retrospective coverage, one for operational ensembles. It checked 95% CI coverage.

**Choosing between approaches**
- α reflects peak or volume information; R² reflects the full hydrograph (E28-R2, E36-R2).
- Grade ranges can be used when only a qualitative grade is known (E28-R2).
- E01-R6 evaluated 1- and 2-day lead times; accuracy declines with lead time.

**Limits and gaps**
- Normality, zero mean and the metric-to-σ mapping are assumptions in E01-R6, E28-R2 and E36-R2.
- E15-R2 gives no calibration or skill results.
- E42-R4's two formulas must not be conflated.
- Assignment note: E15-R2 and E42-R4 do not derive σ from an accuracy metric. They fit this pattern only loosely.

## RP02-G Trace how uncertainty reaches levels, releases, and risk

**When papers did this**
- A release is chosen from uncertain inflows and competing objectives (E02-R1).
- Simulated inflows must be routed (E06-R6, E27-R4).
- A candidate release must be evaluated (E26-R2).
- An ensemble must become an optimizable tree (E23-R3).
- Several connected reservoirs are involved (E32-R1).

**How**
- **Chain framing.** E02-R1 split the process into Forecast–Optimization–Decision Making (FODM) links and tracked forecast, objective-value and weight uncertainty between them.
- **Routing through rules.**
  - E06-R6 iterated the continuity and storage–elevation equations with sampled hydraulic, storage and wind terms under the TGR pre-release rule (Eqs. (6)–(8) in E06).
  - E27-R4 superimposed error series on representative floods and iterated the water balance with the forecast rule `g(R,Z)`.
- **Ensemble generation.** E22-R2 fed bias-corrected NWP precipitation ensembles into an LSTM to get member inflow forecasts.
- **Branching that follows information.** E23-R3 used one control while forecasts are unresolved and adaptive controls after observation. Branching depends on precipitation because of basin lag.
- **Phase errors.** In E26-R2, current-phase error drives end storage V1 and cumulative errors drive future release R2. It assumes unbiased normal errors that are independent across phases.
- **Analytical routing.** E30-R2 and E31-R3 used a stochastic water-balance SDE with local linearization (`q̄ = a_t Z̄ + b_t`, `V̄ = c_t Z̄ + d_t`), keeping adjacent covariance.
- **Dynamic causal process.** E32-R1 treated uncertainty in a connected system as a dynamic causal process rather than one fixed joint PDF.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E30-R2 chose an SDE over numerical simulation, which depends on sample size, and over FOSM/AFOSM/JC, which cannot easily keep correlation.
- E06-R6 and E27-R4 used simulation.

**Limits and gaps**
- E02-R1 is an analytical framing.
- E06-R6 does not clearly report N, T or the time step.
- E22-R2 gives limited calibration details.
- E23-R3's general tree design is not established.
- E26-R2 does not document how variances were calibrated.
- E27-R4 ignores losses.
- E30-R2 and E31-R3 rely on assumed linearization and normality.
- E32-R1 does not show that static methods always fail.

## RP02-H The size of uncertainty changes the optimal decision

**When papers did this**
- A prefill or hedging decision is made with known error spread, acceptable risk and expected inflow (E03-R4, E03-R7, E05-R6, E24-R5).

**How**
- **Safety margin from the error CDF.** E03-R4 defined risk as P(Stage-2 release > R₂,max) and inverted the forecast-error CDF: `d_min = H⁻¹(1 − r_a)`. Under Gaussian error, `d_min = rU⁻¹(1 − r_a)`. Larger uncertainty or lower acceptable risk raises d_min.
- **Three cases.** E03-R7 compared `f₂(d_min)` with `f₁(0)` and `f₁(W_max)`, giving three cases: small uncertainty/large acceptable risk, medium, and large uncertainty/small acceptable risk. Each case has its own flood-control, hedging and conservation segments.
- **Maximum useful uncertainty.** E05-R6 defined it as r_c, r_c0, r_e or r_cr, depending on which constraint binds. If `r > r_cr`, even empty storage cannot meet the acceptable risk.
- **Margin allocation.** E24-R5 computed K1 and K2 from marginal-value curve crossings and classified the event by total margin Δ:
  - High risk, Δ < K1: put all margin in Stage 1.
  - Medium risk, K1 ≤ Δ ≤ 2K2: equalize marginal values with δ1 ≥ δ2.
  - Low risk, Δ > 2K2: equalize with δ2 > δ1.

**Choosing between approaches**
- E03 and E05 work with acceptable risk r_a and the uncertainty parameter r.
- E24 works with σ1/σ2 and total margin. Its notation differs from E03 and E05; keep them separate.

**Limits and gaps**
- E03-R4 assumes negligible intermediate inflow.
- E03-R7 holds only under its assumed error and loss functions.
- E05-R6's thresholds are model-defined, not forecast verification.
- E24-R5 depends on Gaussian uncertainty and σ2 > Tσ1.

## EP03-A Specify or fit error and variable distributions

**When papers did this**
- A risk simulation or optimization needs a probability model for forecast error or for related variables (E01-E3, E12-E1, E37-E2, E42-E1).

**How**
- **Error definitions differ by paper:**
  - Relative `(Q_s,t − q_t)/q_t` (E04-E2).
  - Percentage `(Qf − Qo)/Qo × 100%` (E42-E1).
  - Relative volume `(Wforecast − Wactual)/Wforecast × 100%` (E41-E2).
  - Observed minus average reforecast (E49-E1).
  - `e = O − F`, and for ensembles `E[F|O] − F` (E13-E3).
  - Relative errors for reservoir and lateral inflows (E34-E1, E38-E2).
- **Normal from accuracy metrics.**
  - σ from the qualified rate (E01-E3, E28-E2, E36-E2; `σ = εp/κ'((1+α)/2)` in E36-E2).
  - σ from R² (E28-E2, E36-E2).
  - σ from "70% within 20%" for B-level accuracy (E08-E2, listed under EP03-F).
- **Normal from tests.**
  - K-S and Shapiro–Wilk (E03-E11).
  - Chi-square (E37-E2).
  - RMSE, AIC and chi-square across four candidates (E49-E2).
- **Candidate screening.**
  - L-moments with bias, RMSE and K-S, selecting GL (E04-E4).
  - Ten candidates, RMSE/AIC, selecting Wakeby (E06-E2, E38-E2).
- **Seasonal split.** Fit June and July–September separately (E04-E3).
- **Maximum entropy.**
  - Mode and 99.99% interval (E12-E1).
  - Moment constraints with four Lagrange multipliers found by a genetic algorithm (E39-E2).
- **Transformations.** Rainfall density from the P-III flood-volume density via `Y = 10³W/A` (E39-E3).
- **Fuzzy.** Triangular versus Cauchy membership (E41-E2).
- **Mixture.** Gaussian mixture (IGMD) with K from 2–5 by AIC/BIC, k-means++ and EM to a tolerance of 10⁻⁴ (E42-E2).
- **Heteroscedastic.** LOESS conditional expectation and `σ_t,k = γ0,k + γ1,k E[F|O]` fitted by constrained OLS (E13-E4).
- **Proportional σ.** Piecewise `σ(t) = a(t)Qin(t)` (E10-E1).
- **Non-forecast variables.**
  - Dry-period and lead-time distributions (E09-E1, E09-E2).
  - Period-volume skill (E11-E1).
  - Assumed unbiased normal errors, with `σ² = σ₁² + σ₂²` (E26-E2).
  - Two-stage forecast-plus-error balances (E24-E1).
  - Qualitative factor inventory (E37-E1).

**Limits and gaps**
- Normality and metric mappings are not validated in E28-E2 and E36-E2. E26-E2's calibration procedure is undocumented.
- Fitted parameters are incomplete in E04-E4, E06-E2, E12-E1 and E14-E4.
- E13-E4 does not report the LOESS span or the intercept floor.
- E41-E2 rests on few observations.
- E34-E1 sample sizes are not given.
- E09-E2 does not detail how the 10-h bound was derived.

**Example values**
- E01-E3: qualified rates 92.36% and 91.41% (Grade A).
- E03-E11: `N(−2.7, 20.88²)`; 86.5% within ±20%.
- E28-E2: σ = 0.114 (α = 92%) and 0.190 (R² = 92.23%).
- E36-E2: σ = 0.122 and 0.146.
- E10-E1: a0 = 0.1, a1 = 0.2, b0 = 0.008.
- E27-E2: SDs 525–623 m³/s at 1–6 h.
- E39-E2: support [−48, 48] mm.
- E49-E1: 129 samples from 12 floods.
- E09-E1: median dry spell 4 days.
- E09-E2: mean lead time 9.90 h.

## EP03-B Preserve dependence when generating

**When papers did this**
- Marginals are fitted and correlated variables, lead times, days or sites must be sampled jointly (E04-E5, E06-E3, E34-E2, E49-E2).

**How**
- **t-copula** fitted by maximum likelihood:
  - Lead-time reductions per season (E04-E5).
  - Errors across time and space (E34-E2).
  - Lead times (E49-E2).
  - TGR's three lead times, with LHS for the other sources (E38-E4).
- **Sequential conditional sampling.**
  - Uniforms passed through conditional copulas, then the inverse Wakeby marginal (E06-E3).
  - Daily flows from a Gumbel copula with P-III marginals, conditioned on the previous day (E06-E4, E04-E8). E04-E8 then forms `Q_s,t = q_t(1 + re_s,t)`.
- **Adjacent copula with Gibbs sampling.** Clayton, Gumbel and Frank copulas compared by AIC/OLS; normal marginals with a Frank copula were selected. 1,000 scenarios generated with `e_t = Q_t^o − Q_t` (E14-E4, E14-E5).
- **VAR + Schaake shuffle + kNN.**
  - A LASSO-penalized VAR (max lag 3) removes auto- and cross-correlation, with residuals fitted to SGED (E13-E5).
  - Sampled deviates are reordered with a Schaake shuffle, and one kNN-selected observation is shared across ensemble members (E13-E6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Copulas were used where dependence was fitted directly.
- E13 used VAR and shuffling for multi-dimensional HEFS ensembles.
- E34-E2 dropped insignificant links.

**Limits and gaps**
- No holdout checks in E04-E5.
- E04-E8 does not report its calibration.
- E13-E5 gives no diagnostics.
- E13-E6 does not report k or the distance metric.
- E14-E5 does not report Gibbs burn-in.
- E38-E4 gives no sample count or convergence check.
- E49-E2 does not report copula parameters.

**Example values**
- E06-E3: median mean errors −0.83%, −1.38% and −2.41% at 1–3 days.
- E06-E4: relative errors 0.04%, 0.26% and 0.93% for mean, Cv and Cs.
- E49-E2: normal RMSE 0.2540, AIC −804.3, p = 0.3932.

## EP03-C Generate forecast scenarios and ensembles

**When papers did this**
- Stochastic optimization, risk estimation or a Monte Carlo bound needs scenario inputs (E01-E8, E02-E2, E33-E2, E34-E3).

**How**
- **Multiplicative perturbation of observed or forecast flow.**
  - Normal ε applied to observed inflow (E33-E2).
  - Copula errors applied to deterministic forecasts, all scenarios equally likely (E34-E3).
  - Independent uniform ε in [−0.3, 0.3] (E35-E2).
  - LHS draws with `N(0, 0.20²)` relative errors, routed with Muskingum (E32-E2).
- **Additive Gaussian series on representative floods** (E27-E4).
- **MMFE scenario trees** (E02-E2).
- **Copula reductions, then accumulated by Eq. (9)** into lead-time errors (E04-E6, E04-E7).
- **Growing-spread probabilistic ensembles** (E35-E3).
- **Quantile paths** from BFS (1%–100%) (E29-E2).
- **Synthetic HEFS by inverse transformation, or synthetic GEFS forcing HEFS** (E13-E7, E13-E8).
- **Model ensembles.** Parameter uncertainty (adaptive Metropolis) plus precipitation uncertainty (E40-E1).
- **MH-MCMC from an IGMD** (E42-E4).
- **Fuzzy simulation.** d-level cuts scale design floods (E41-E4).
- **Discrete storm sets** with given probabilities (E25-E1).
- **LHS** of independent non-inflow variables (E06-E6).
- **CO versus FBO comparison.** CO decides with generated floods but computes storage from the typical process. FBO decides with forecast errors and routes actual inflow (E37-E5, E37-E6).
- **Monte Carlo search for a dynamic bound.** Stop when running means differ by less than 0.05 m (E01-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- MMFE values were designed subjectively (E02-E2). E35-E3's spread is a tuning setting. E25-E1's inputs are illustrative.
- No seed or sensitivity tests are reported in E01-E8 or E06-E6.
- E34-E3 discrepancies grow with lead time.
- E29-E2 has no calibrated quantile rule.
- E33-E2 omits model error.
- E41-E4 does not give N.
- E42-E4 does not give MCMC burn-in.

**Example values**
- E01-E8: 200 repetitions.
- E02-E2: I = 40, r = 250 m³/s, q_error = 0.5.
- E27-E4: 2,000 series per flood.
- E33-E2: variance 0.038 (6 h) and 0.021 (12 h).
- E35-E3: M = 50, σ̂₄₈ = 0.2.
- E13-E7: M = 100, E = 61.
- E37-E5, E37-E6: 10,000 runs.

## EP03-D Generate or scale design floods and stochastic flood events

**When papers did this**
- Coverage beyond historical return periods, or stress tests, are needed (E11-E2, E16-E7, E27-E6).
- Event-based calibration is needed (E20-E2).
- The forecast horizon ends (E33-E4).
- Inflow is nonstationary (E45-E4).

**How**
- **AR1 vector model.** Pearson III period floods, 10,000 simulated (E11-E2).
- **Log-normal scaling.** Scale the four largest events to 100-, 200- and 300-year floods, with their forecasts (E16-E7).
- **Event chain** (E20-E2): event probability p → return period Tr = 1/p → SQRT-ETmax maximum daily precipitation → IDF volume → ARMA temporal pattern → curve-number runoff → SCS unit hydrograph → Muskingum routing.
- **Linear magnification** of representative waves (E27-E6).
- **Multisite magnification.** Magnify the main site by `K_main,j = W^D_j/W^O_j` and choose matching processes at slave sites (E33-E7).
- **Aligned design hydrographs** for stage 2 (E33-E4).
- **Homogeneous frequency enlargement** at 14 frequencies (E41-E1).
- **Nonstationary scenario** for 2020–2039, in five-year blocks (E45-E4).

**Limits and gaps**
- E11-E2's synthetic events are not observations.
- E20-E2 reports no representativeness checks.
- E27-E6's linear magnification is an assumption.
- E33-E4 assumes the end level is uncorrelated with future floods.
- E33-E7's simulated statistics differ from observed in several cases.
- E45-E4's generation details are in its appendix.

**Example values**
- E20-E2: 1,000 events per case.
- E33-E7: N = 10,000.
- E41-E1: frequencies 0.01%–1%.

## EP03-E Reduce ensembles to scenario trees

**When papers did this**
- The full ensemble is too large for multistage optimization (E23-E3, E35-E7, E36-E3).

**How**
- **Backward reduction** (E23-E3): simultaneous backward reduction within a Kantorovich-distance limit, using precipitation-dependent distances, member averaging and 10-step smoothing.
- **Mass-conservative binary splits** (E35-E7): preserve the probability-weighted sum of the ensemble.
- **Monte Carlo tree** (E36-E3): `Q_syn,t = Q_obs,t(1+σe_t)`, with I = 100 paths of P = 0.01 each.

**Limits and gaps**
- E23-E3 does not report its tolerance or branch times.
- E35-E7 refers to prior work for the algorithm.
- E36-E3 reports no branch topology or reduction step.

**Example values**
- E23-E3: 32-branch tree; 118 tolerance-level trees also built.
- E35-E7: 16 branches every 10 h over 48 h.

## EP03-F Write non-forecast uncertainty into the water balance

**When papers did this**
- Hydraulic, storage, initial-state or stage-2 inflow uncertainty must enter routing (E03-E4, E06-E5, E08-E1, E38-E3).

**How**
- **Stage-2 inflow as forecast plus error.** `I₂ = Ī₂ + e`, with Stage 1 treated as deterministic (E03-E4).
- **Coefficient and error forms.** `q = λq_s` and `V = V_m + v` (E06-E5, E38-E3), with Gumbel backwater and Rayleigh run-up (E06-E5).
- **Initial storage.** V0 density from excess storage W (E08-E1).
- **Normal relative inflow and lateral-inflow errors** (E08-E2).
- **Storage SDE.** Forward Euler, K = 2,000 paths, Δt = 1 h (E28-E4).
- **Level-error recursion.** Start from zero moments (E30-E4), with stochastic discharge and storage functions (E30-E1).
- **Source parameterization.** Normal IFE from αQ = 90%, Δt = 1 h, T = 48 (E31-E1).

**Limits and gaps**
- E03-E4's general density is reduced to a Gaussian in its analysis, and the case uses a biased normal.
- E06-E5 and E38-E3 give no calibration details.
- E08-E2 omits discharge and storage uncertainty.
- E28-E4 reports no convergence or bound enforcement.
- E30-E1 does not clearly report OE/SOE values.
- E31-E1 settings are assumptions.

**Example values**
- E06-E5: λ ~ normal(mean 1, variance 0.02); v ~ normal(mean 0, variance 0.2).
- E38-E3: variances 0.2 (discharge coefficient) and 0.02 (storage). The pairing is reversed relative to E06-E5; see the note at the end.
- E08-E2: σ = 0.19.
- E30-E1: peak relative-error SD 0.2377.
- E31-E1: σ'ζQ = 0.12.

## EP03-G Check that generated scenarios are faithful

**When papers did this**
- Before or after generated scenarios are used for risk or operation (E06-E10, E13-E9, E34-E9, E42-E3).

**How**
- **Statistic preservation.** Compare observed and simulated statistics, and each uncertainty alone versus all together (E06-E10).
- **Ensemble verification** (E13-E9): spread–error, rank histograms, eCRPS, and DTS tests at p = 0.1, with the lower 90% and upper 10% of flows examined separately.
- **Extreme events.** Check whether HEFS values for the 10 largest events fall within the synthetic ranges (E13-E10).
- **Correlation, bands and hit rate.** Correlation matrices, scenario bands and no-rain hit rate (E34-E9).
- **Distribution fit.** K-S at 0.01, RMSE/MAPE and Q–Q plots (E42-E3).
- **Coverage.** 95% CI coverage rate (CR) (E42-E5).

**Limits and gaps**
- E06-E10 checks are internal only.
- E13-E9: DTS non-rejection does not prove identity.
- E13-E10 compares against a single HEFS realization and finds variance underprediction at longer leads.
- E34-E9 scenarios underestimate receding floods.
- E42-E5 outliers occur in dry seasons.

**Example values**
- E34-E9: hit rate 85.9%, with about 14% chance of a missed alarm.
- E42-E3: IGMD K-S 0.012–0.019, below 0.044.
- E42-E5: CR 92–95%.
