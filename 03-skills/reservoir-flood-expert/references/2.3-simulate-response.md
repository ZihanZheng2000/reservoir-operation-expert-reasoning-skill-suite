# Reference for unit 2.3-simulate-response: what the papers did

This file covers how papers simulated reservoir, downstream, and uncertain responses to inflows and operating decisions, including surrogate models that replace full simulation. It summarizes 59 steps from 37 papers. Use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach; values are examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## EP04-A Build a reservoir water-balance simulator and compute trajectories

**When papers did this**
- To set up one common system model for comparing alternatives (E07-E1), to turn inflows/forecasts into operations (E13-E2, E14-E7), to score candidate rules or parameter sets (E15-E5, E20-E4, E25-E2), at every rolling decision or time step (E03-E3, E04-E9, E16-E2, E17-E2, E27-E1, E33-E1, E35-E5, E46-E1), and to generate alternatives (E48-E2).

**How**
- Explicit single-reservoir continuity, storage advanced by inflow minus release each step: S_{t+1}=S_t+Q_t−R_t (E16-E2); s_k=s_{k−1}+Δt(Q_I−Q_S−Q_WS) with spillway and water-supply terms and a piecewise-linear level-storage curve (E35-E5); Qin−Qout=dW/dt with W=f(Z) and Qout=g(R,Z) by hourly iteration (E27-E1); hourly level-to-storage conversion, inferring inflow from measured levels when needed (E46-E1).
- Multi-component mass balance: storage minus evaporation/upstream losses plus inflow minus flood-control, emergency, water-supply and spillway releases, with junction balances (Eqs. 1–2 in E07) (E07-E1, E13-E2).
- Cascade balance with delayed upstream releases: V_{i,t}=V_{i,t−1}+I_{i,t}Δt+ΣR_{k,t−τ}Δt−R_{i,t}Δt (E15-E5); water balance plus channel routing for downstream inflow (E33-E1, E48-E2).
- Two-stage rolling balance: R_2=W_1+I_2 and R_1+R_2=W_0+I_1+I_2, with the RFCC relation W_1+d=R_{2,max}−Ī_2 (E03-E3).
- Ensemble/scenario trajectories: per-member no-release storage forecasts over 15 days, flagging members above 111,000 acre-ft (E07-E5, E13-E2); MPC storage matrix x_f(T+i,j)=X_{t0}+Σ(q_f−u_f) for one common release sequence (E17-E2); routing each scenario under release control (compute levels) or level control (compute releases) (E40-E2).
- Optimization-embedded simulation: MILP for release and storage with piecewise-linear penalties, wq+wv=1 (E20-E4); constrained trajectories minimizing highest level or peak release with penalty α=10^3 (E14-E7); stepwise trial-and-error peak reduction (E48-E2).
- Rule-based routing with release caps (E04-E9, E10-E4, E11-E4); square-wave storm release Qsi=[Vsi−(k−S0+Qh tf)]/tsi (E25-E2).
- Constraints enforced: release bounds and ramping |R_t−R_{t−1}|≤Ramp (E16-E2, E14-E7, E48-E2); forebay bounds and spillway capacity (E35-E5).
- Checking against history: a 2000–2010 historical scenario compared with observed storage and flows (E07-E3). Tool-based simulation with HEC-ResSim / USACE tools (E18-E5).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Daily time steps were used in E07-E1 and E13-E2; hourly in E27-E1 and E46-E1. E07-E1 routed downstream changes in the same daily step because travel times were under 24 hr above 8,000 cfs at Hopland.
- E17-E2 used one release sequence across members, which allows scenario comparison but has no per-member feedback (E17-E2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Losses neglected: E03-E3, E27-E1, E33-E1, E46-E1; "often ignored" in E40-E2.
- Routing details not given or simplified: E03-E3, E04-E9, E10-E4, E11-E4, E33-E1. Solver, timestep or curve details unreported: E20-E4, E35-E5, E48-E2. HEC-ResSim setup unreported (E18-E5).
- Square-wave average release may underestimate risk from a changing hydrograph (E25-E2).
- E07-E3 is model testing against history, not validation of deployed operation.

**Example values**
- FCWL 145 m; release caps 53,900 and 79,000 m³/s (E04-E9). FLWL 85 m, MWL 93.6 m, hydropower discharge 2,800 m³/s (E10-E4). Release limits 0–4,157 m³/s, safety level 178.64 m (E14-E7). Spill crest 116,500 acre-ft (E13-E2). Initial level 325 m (E27-E1). N_e=40/42, N_h=14 (E17-E2).

## EP04-B Simulate basin, channel, and downstream processes

**When papers did this**
- When rainfall-type quantities had to become reservoir inflow/storage (E19-E5), when upstream operations altered the hydrologic signal (E21-E2), when downstream gauge flow was needed at optimization speed (E23-E4), to screen downstream safety each period (E29-E5), and to define station-level control problems (E44-E1, E44-E2).

**How**
- Runoff conversion: PP mapped to floodwater with Eq. (8) in E19 and a runoff coefficient, with the Xinanjiang model as a refinement (E19-E5).
- Naturalized flow: compare naturalized and measured station flows using operation data, travel time and water balance; anticipate discharge of reservoirs outside joint operation by mined rules (E21-E2).
- Routing model selection: SOBEK full dynamic, kinematic wave, integrator-delay and Muskingum-Cunge compared by error indicators and cost; integrator-delay embedded in optimization (E23-E4).
- Downstream impact: count days in level categories at Shashi and inspect maximum level (E29-E5).
- Aggregation: sum physical and channel storage (CSR) and interzone inflows, using the last station flow as outflow (E44-E1); CSR storage from Muskingum K and x, Eqs. (1)–(5) in E44 (E44-E2).

**Limits and gaps**
- Eq. (8) units and spatial/temporal redistribution unclear (E19-E5). Naturalization equations and rule mining unreported (E21-E2).
- Direct SOBEK integration ruled out by cost/adjoint limits (E23-E4).
- No damage model or observed consequence validation (E29-E5).
- Parallel aggregation not case-tested; some CSR estimates conservative (E44-E1, E44-E2).

**Example values**
- Runoff coefficient 0.75 (E19-E5). Integrator-delay NSE 0.977, RMSE 72.37 (E23-E4). Shashi categories A <43.0 m to D 44.0–44.5 m; warning level 44.5 m (E29-E5).

## EP04-C Propagate uncertainty to levels and flows

**When papers did this**
- When uncertainty samples, ensembles or error distributions were ready and risk had to be quantified (E06-E7, E27-E5, E37-E5, E37-E6, E38-E5, E39-E4, E41-E4), at each routing step for stochastic or moment methods (E28-E4, E30-E4, E31-E3), and after the forecast horizon or for nonstationary schedules (E40-E4, E45-E4).

**How**
- Monte Carlo routing of many floods/samples, recording maxima: E06-E7 (uncertain release coefficient, storage–elevation relation, wind run-up; Z_M and Q_m), E27-E5, E27-E6 (linear magnification of representative waves), E37-E5, E37-E6, E38-E5 (maximum occupied storage per reservoir), E45-E4.
- Event-maximum extraction: QOmax,n=max_t(QOn,t), Vmax,n=max_t(Vn,t) (E20-E6); Z_max per optimized scenario (E36-E6).
- Stochastic differential equation: σQ(t)=μQ(t)σ, σV(t)=σQ(t)√Δt, forward Euler with normal Wiener increments, then level paths summarized by mean, quartiles, min, max (E28-E4, E28-E5).
- Moment recursion: deterministic mean routing, local piecewise-linear discharge/storage coefficients around the mean level, then recursions for error mean and variance starting from zero (E30-E2, E30-E3, E30-E4 with Eqs. 13 and 16; E31-E2, E31-E3 with Eqs. 13 and 15). E31-E4 adds adjacent-step covariance and union risk with bivariate normal adjacent events.
- Hazard surface over an error grid storing (e,y,Zmax) or (e,y,Qmax) (E39-E4); fuzzy simulation scaling DFH by Wforecast(1−ek/100) (E41-E4).
- Conditional post-horizon routing of design floods from each scenario end level (E40-E4).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Sampling methods (E27, E28, E37, E38) used thousands of runs; moment recursions (E30, E31) give mean and variance directly but assume small level change/short step (E30-E3) and normal level error (E30-E4, E31-E3).
- E37 distinguishes two set-ups: CO used generated hydrographs for decisions but storage from the typical process (E37-E5); FBO computed storage from actual inflow (E37-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- Convergence not reported: E27-E5, E28-E4, E31-E3, E38-E5. Initial level, N or grid step not given: E06-E7, E39-E4, E41-E4.
- Timing, gradients and trajectories omitted from E20's criterion (E20-E6).
- Nonadjacent independence assumed (E31-E4); end level assumed uncorrelated with the next flood (E40-E4); "most unfavorable" error allocation (E39-E4).

**Example values**
- 2,000 routings per flood, 6,000 total (E27-E5); K=2,000, Δt=1 h (E28-E4); Δt=6 h, T=22 (E30-E2); 10,000 runs (E37-E5, E38-E5); 100 scenarios, 12 periods (E36-E6); X~N(0.019,0.188²) (E37-E6).

## EP04-D Replace full simulation with surrogate or probabilistic models

**When papers did this**
- When Bayesian-network outcome data were needed (E08-E3, E32-E2), when repeated risk evaluation would be too expensive (E33-E5), and when repeated optimization had to be computationally feasible (E43-E3).

**How**
- Bayesian networks trained on simulated samples: optimization-generated 1,000 records per case, expert DAG, equal-distance discretization, MLE CPTs, prior risk by summing exceedance probabilities, posterior prediction and diagnosis (E08-E3 to E08-E6).
- Dynamic BN: 1,000 LHS samples with relative errors N(0,0.20²), reservoir routing plus Muskingum downstream, expert-defined time-sliced structure, BayesiaLab learning, prediction and diagnosis inference (E32-E2 to E32-E6).
- Precomputed lookup of stage-2 risk versus end-level combinations (E33-E5).
- Linear state-space internal model (82 reaches, 11 reservoirs, discretized De Saint-Venant) in closed loop with SOBEK as the "real" system (E43-E3, E43-E5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Discretization precision versus computation trade-off; bin sensitivity not fully reported (E08-E4, E32-E4).
- Only E08-E5 reports a check (BN prior matched MC for case 1). E32-E5 reports no observed-outcome validation; diagnosis is association, not causal proof (E32-E6).
- E33-E5 does not report grid resolution or interpolation error. E43-E5 does not fully represent measurement noise or forecast error.
- E08-E3 is training optimization, not a demonstrated real-time controller.

**Example values**
- End-level ranges Ankang 305–330 m, Danjiangkou 155–170 m (E33-E5). 48.7% probability for Hengpaitou flow 1,480–1,610 m³/s in one case (E32-E5).
