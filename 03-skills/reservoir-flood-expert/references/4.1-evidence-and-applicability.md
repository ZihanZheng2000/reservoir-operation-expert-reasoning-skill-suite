# Reference for unit 4.1-evidence-and-applicability: what the papers did

This file covers how papers built evidence for reservoir flood-operation results: which baselines they compared against, how they tested robustness, data and model credibility, holdout and transfer, verification of the method itself, and how they bounded applicability and explained mechanisms. It summarizes 177 steps from 50 papers; use it to find concrete methods and to check an approach, and treat all values as examples from specific papers, not defaults.

## RP09-A Improvement must be compared with the right baseline

**When papers did this**
- A candidate scheme had passed a risk condition and its gain had to be interpreted (E01-R8, E12-R5, E10-R6).
- The question was whether forecasts improve storage without raising flood risk (E07-R7, E17-R6), or whether one added information source (vapor, ensembles) adds value (E19-R7, E22-R7).
- Aggregate benefit could come from several mechanisms at once (E50-R4).

**How**
- Current rule / current operations as comparator: E01-R8 (current TGR rule, Table 5), E10-R6 (COR), E12-R5, E18-R6 (16 metrics at Lake Mendocino; 25 scenarios at Prado; two alternatives at Yuba–Feather), E07-R7 (EO = water-control-manual guide curve).
- Lower and upper bounds on information value: no-forecast/climatology baseline plus perfect future inflow, with the real forecast in between (E07-R7, E16-R3, E17-R6, E50-R4). E17-R6 attributes the HEFS-to-perfect gap to forecast quality and parameter feasibility.
- Controlled variants that differ in one element only: PS-FIRO vs VPS-FIRO (vapor omitted) (E19-R7); Baseline-TOCS separates TOCS raising from forecast effect (E50-R4).
- Separate forecast quality from forecast value: post-assess forecast-optimized releases under observed ("Perfect") inflows (E14-R5).
- Deterministic vs ensemble-member vs ensemble-mean vs observed operation (E22-R7); deterministic benchmark D vs stochastic MOSP (E34-R4).
- Same flood, initial level (325 m), thresholds and 10,000 replications for both modes (CO vs FBO) (E37-R5); E37-R1 notes CO observed inflow is also uncertain.
- Stationary benchmark (Scheme A, FLWL 145.0 m) sets the maximum acceptable risk/loss (E45-R3).
- Joint metrics: storage plus frequency of maximum releases (E50-R6); FCC exceedance plus generation (E10-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Perfect-forecast comparators were used to bound potential benefit; all papers using them label them non-deployable (E07-R7, E14-R5, E16-R3).
- Historical operation was treated as context, not a controlled counterfactual, because starting levels and decisions differ (E19-R7, E22-R7).
- E18-R6 compared each alternative with baseline instead of ranking alternatives built on incompatible assumptions.

**Limits and gaps**
- Comparisons are not independent validation or deployment (E07-R7, E10-R6, E22-R7); real-time implementation is future work (E01-R8).
- E18-R6 does not report metric definitions, weights or statistics; E19-R9 does not report the event-selection rule; E22-R7 has no significance test.
- Maximum-release frequency is only a proxy for flood-control burden (E50-R6).

**Example values**
- E04-R7: FCWL 145 m, Shashi stage 44.5 m, 180.4 m upper threshold for a 10,000-year design flood.
- E19-R9: 50 floods, 2011–2023.

## RP09-B Robustness: sensitivity, stress, and holdout tests

**When papers did this**
- Hindcasts were short (roughly 10–35 years) or few extreme events were available (E13-R1, E16-R1, E50-R7).
- Calibration relied on few events or user-tuned parameters (E20-R2).
- Policy or configuration parameters had to be chosen (E03-R8, E20-R8, E35-R8).

**How**
- Vary forecast uncertainty and error correlation: r and q_error (E02-R9); σɛ1, σɛ2 (E26-R6); uncertainty levels/correlations, isolated vs combined factors (E31-R5, E32-R6, E08-R6).
- Vary policy/model parameters: x and m (E03-R8); θ, Qa, k (E25-R5); wq, PVfcl, b with M=6 fixed (E20-R8).
- Worst-case stress: next flood immediately after forecast failure; release at min(qA, g(Z)); if required time t > τ, deduct unreleased volume to get ΔWsafe (E09-R4).
- Synthetic ensembles and scaled events: scale 1960–2019 annual maxima to 100/200/300-year levels with fixed hydrograph shape (E16-R5); train on 100 synthetic ensembles or withhold 1997 (E16-R4).
- Holdout: LOOCV over water years and random-seed variability (E50-R7); held-out typhoons (E46-R6).
- Scenario count and confidence level, including a 1,000-scenario observation-based process (E14-R8); branch counts 1–32 and horizons 18–48 h vs CPU time (E35-R8).
- Cross-flood consistency: rank schemes across eight typical years and sum orders (E38-R6); compare across lead times and return periods (E42-R6); forecast skill, conveyance and reservoir size (E11-R5).
- Compare methods: SMAA-TOPSIS vs SMAA-2 (E02-R9); BN vs MC (E08-R6); traditional model and MC (E31-R5); static BN and MC (E32-R6).

**Choosing between approaches**
- E13-R1 and E16-R1 used synthetic forecasts when hindcast coverage was the concern; E16-R5 used scaling when events larger than history were the concern.
- E50-R2 used a compact three-parameter risk curve to limit overfitting relative to 14 independent lead-time parameters.

**Limits and gaps**
- Most are numerical sensitivity analyses, not independent validation (E02-R9, E03-R8, E25-R5, E26-R6, E32-R6).
- Scaling keeps hydrograph shape and forecast-error attributes (E16-R5); synthetic realism depends on the generator (E16-R1, E13-R1).
- E14-R8 sensitivity covers only two floods; holding out 1997 had little effect, for hypothesized reasons (E16-R4); LOOCV uses a limited 2013–2023 record (E50-R7).
- No general branch-selection rule (E35-R8); summed order has no uncertainty interval (E38-R6); E20-R8 search grid not fully enumerated.

**Example values**
- E09-R4: all 50 tested scenarios exceeded lead time.
- E35-R8: 16 branches and 48 h chosen.
- E38-R6: Schemes 5 and 6 summed order 25.

## RP09-C Data and models must be credible first

**When papers did this**
- Before using an error model or marginal distribution in simulation (E04-R5, E37-R3, E42-R3).
- Before treating synthetic forecasts as inputs to operation (E13-R5).
- Before relying on a risk estimate as a constraint (E33-R6, E41-R6) or claiming operational applicability (E19-R10, E28-R5).
- No external benchmark ranking existed for MCDM (E47-R6, E48-R6).

**How**
- Distribution fit tests: L-moments with bias, RMSE and K–S p-values, GL selected (E04-R5); chi-square at 0.05 with five intervals (E37-R3); K–S at 0.01, Q–Q, mean/variance/CV, RMSE, MAPE comparing IGMD, GMD, SGD (E42-R3).
- Synthetic-vs-HEFS parity: BSE diagrams, cumulative rank histograms, eCRPS, DTS two-sample test, rare-event mean/variance, p = 0.1 where tested (E13-R5).
- Forecast model calibration/verification split: 1997–2002 / 2003–2004, α, NSE/R², runoff-volume relative error, Grade A under a cited Chinese standard (E28-R5).
- Coverage of observations by ensemble bands: 95% CI and CR (E42-R4).
- Risk-estimator checks: R_accepted = f/N under regular operation, require R_r ≤ R_accepted (E33-R6); extreme-risk frequency Pe compared with K and 0.1% (E41-R6); POME density vs normal curve and P–P plot (E36-R7); normal-tail probabilities from Monte Carlo MWL moments (E27-R5).
- Physical and mechanism checks before aggregate statistics: PP above observed P, representative events (E19-R8); component validation with Xinanjiang and radiosondes (E19-R10).
- Event clustering into two scale classes before fitting (E14-R1).
- MCDM: compare rankings across TOPSIS, fuzzy matter-element, fuzzy optimization, with/without deletion or PCA (E47-R6, E48-R6); separate criterion selection from ranking (E47-R1).

**Choosing between approaches**
- Significance levels differed by case: α=0.05 generally and 0.005 for main-season X2 (E04-R5); 0.05 (E37-R3); 0.01 (E42-R3).
- E42-R4 used one equation for retrospective bands and another for operational ensembles; these should not be conflated.

**Limits and gaps**
- Fit and parity checks are not operation validation (E04-R5, E13-R5, E42-R3); E27-R5 has no observed MWL validation.
- E36-R7 does not state the selected moment order (order 3 or 4 said appropriate).
- E37-R3 source alternates variance/SD typography; reported N(0.019, 0.188²).
- Consistent MCDM rankings are not ground truth (E47-R6, E48-R6).

**Example values**
- E37-R3: χ² 4.1926 < 5.9915, 149 points.
- E27-R5: 2,000 error series per flood; thresholds 330.0, 333.0, 337.33 m.

## RP09-D Applicability depends on site, event, and institutions

**When papers did this**
- When moving from a case result to other reservoirs, events, or operational use (E07-R8, E17-R7, E20-R10, E22-R9).
- When selecting sites across a portfolio (E17-R1, E18-R8).

**How**
- Bound conclusions by forecast skill and site limits: upper forecast range most reliable at 4–15 day leads; risk curve sensitive to training sequence (E07-R8).
- State omitted uncertainty and scope: one cascade, observed weather instead of forecasts (E15-R8); single reservoir (E09-R5); dry short record where most benefit comes from TOCS raising (E50-R8).
- Check physical/institutional feasibility: drawdown vs remaining inflow to June 1 refill, and authority to draw into conservation storage (E16-R7); institutional reluctance and lost supply storage (E25-R6).
- Condition on event scale: 100-year vs 5-year events (E49-R7); small floods can give negative added storage (E09-R5); larger floods give more concentrated MWL distributions (E27-R6).
- Cross-site screening with common MPC and thresholds as preliminary targets (E17-R1, E17-R7).
- Staged portfolio process: Stage A elimination, Stage B scored benefit/effort, Stage C detailed assessment, with routes to no implementation, more research, viability assessment or WCM update (E18-R8); codify in WCM with forecast-skill assessment (E18-R9); site-specific objectives (E18-R3).
- Residual risk attributed to uncontrolled catchments, full storage and conduit limits (E43-R5); mean objectives may hide severe scenarios and horizon truncation (E34-R8).
- Deployment prerequisites: authority damage curves, initial-level distributions, real-time operation (E20-R10); runtime about 260 minutes per event (E22-R9); post-event reanalysis to revise schemes (E21-R8).

**Limits and gaps**
- No deployment trial or authority acceptance reported (E20-R10, E25-R6, E43-R5); "acceptable" in E27-R6 is not regulatory approval; E27-R6 conventional risk was not quantified.
- E18-R8 does not report scoring weights or Stage C thresholds; E18-R9 gives no WCM wording.
- E07-R8 does not simulate the 1964 flood of record; E17-R7 covers six reservoirs, no interactions; E21-R8 has no formal counterfactual.

**Example values**
- E09-R5: four of six historical events positive added storage, two negative.

## RP09-E Explain why the result occurs

**When papers did this**
- When component and combined results were available (E06-R8), operations diverged between forecast sources (E13-R6), or a selected solution needed an operational explanation (E15-R7, E48-R7, E49-R6).

**How**
- Compare single-uncertainty and combined entropy to identify the dominant source (hydrograph shape) and offsetting effects; compare with design-criterion entropy (E06-R8).
- Consider both generator bias and policy sensitivity when synthetic runs spill (E13-R6).
- Diagnose bias, eCRPS and timing alongside storage/drawdown rather than one skill score (E16-R6).
- Representative Pareto solutions (schemes 1, 25, 57, 85) plotted against conventional rule/HFOR (E15-R7).
- Shallow decision tree (70/30 split, max depth 3) to turn response surfaces into thresholds (E17-R5).
- Check whether moving along the Pareto front improves one objective only at considerable cost (E20-R9).
- Trajectory comparison: stochastic control lowers forebay and raises initial outflow across 32 branches (E23-R6); spreading release before peak lowers downstream CVaR (E49-R6); reservoir vs downstream criteria trade-off (E48-R7).
- Prediction vs diagnosis direction in a DBN (E32-R5).

**Limits and gaps**
- Attributions are interpretations of simulations, qualified in the papers ("suggests", "likely") (E13-R6, E49-R6, E23-R6); DT thresholds are not causal laws (E17-R5).
- Conclusions are limited to the cases (E06-R8, E20-R9, E48-R7); E32-R5 has no damage function.

## EP10-A Compare with baselines under identical conditions

**When papers did this**
- After a risk-constrained bound, rule set, or optimized policy was ready for case evaluation (E01-E9, E15-E8, E40-E7, E44-E6).

**How**
- Current/conventional rule vs proposed scheme over the same inflows: E01-E9, E03-E13, E10-E5, E11-E5, E12-E5, E15-E8, E44-E6, E33-E10.
- Manual-based baseline and perfect-forecast branch under common constraints and spillway logic (E07-E9), then medians, IQR, exceedance curves, release lead times, spill and downstream-flow frequency (E07-E10).
- Multi-metric site comparison with baseline as common comparator (E18-E5, E18-E6).
- Controlled and historical comparators with the same event inputs (E19-E9, E19-E11).
- Deterministic/no-forecast optimization with same objectives and constraints (E22-E8), compared on peaks, storage and limit compliance (E22-E10); deterministic benchmark built by aggregation/decomposition and prerelease bounds (E34-E4), compared with stochastic solutions (E34-E10).
- Conventional operation driven by current inflow/level (E37-E4); counts scaled to risk rate Ψ by return period (E37-E7).
- Extreme-risk estimate P(max_t Z(t) > 180.4) vs observed inflow plus regular routing (E04-E10).
- Four policy variants Baseline, Baseline-TOCS, Forecast, Perfect (E50-E8); CVaR vs expected loss across Schemes A, B1, B2 (E45-E7); TOPSIS-selected compromises (E49-E7); curtailment = observed-generation minus forecast-based generation (E42-E8).
- Downstream indicators: integrate release above 200 m³/s; FSI = effective flood storage / FCL storage (E35-E10).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are simulated case results, not deployment (E01-E9, E03-E14, E12-E5, E44-E6); E43-E6 and E33-E10 use a single event or season.
- E04-E10 does not report replication count, estimator uncertainty or convergence; "no risk" in E37-E7 means zero events in 10,000 runs.
- E18-E6 lacks metric definitions and uncertainty; E19-E11 has initial-level confounding; E22-E10 has no damage or significance tests.

**Example values**
- E01-E9: bounds 146.1 m (1 day) and 147.4 m (2 days); generation +1.55–2.91%.
- E10-E5: FCC exceedance 0% (Three-OR) vs 11.11% (COR).
- E37-E7: CO 0.108% vs FBO 0.044% for five-year floods.
- E07-E10: 10 May median storage about 22,829 acre-feet higher than EO.

## EP10-B Test with historical events and trajectories

**When papers did this**
- To check a simulator against history before comparing alternatives (E07-E3), or to show mechanisms beyond aggregates (E15-E9, E19-E10, E23-E6, E49-E8).

**How**
- Run a 2000–2010 historical scenario and compare simulated storage and flows with observations (E07-E3).
- Real-time added storage from actual inflow/outflow, classified positive/negative (E09-E5).
- Plot traces for representative schemes and years (E15-E9); partition events into pre-event, prerelease, regulation, prefill periods, including a successive flood (E19-E10).
- Compare perfect, deterministic and 32-branch stochastic trajectories for one major event (E23-E6).
- Compare trajectories and time-indexed CVaR for the 10% most adverse scenarios (E49-E8).
- Post-event reanalysis and 22 crisis-management plans (E21-E9).

**Limits and gaps**
- E07-E3 is model testing, not EFO validation; E09-E5 has no statistical validation; E21-E9 does not describe crisis-plan actions.
- E19-E10 simulation omitted typhoon forecasts operators had.

**Example values**
- E19-E10: VPS-FIRO prerelease 24 hours earlier than PS-FIRO.
- E49-E8: CVaR +15.60% (6–27 h), −11.60% (30–42 h).

## EP10-C Compare information conditions

**When papers did this**
- To determine whether forecast differences matter to decisions or to bound forecast value (E13-E11, E16-E6, E17-E5, E50-E6).

**How**
- Baseline as climatology/historical median, perfect as observed future inflow, substituted into the same MPC (E16-E6, E17-E5).
- Synthetic vs actual ensembles through EFO with PFO comparator (E13-E11); extension to pre-hindcast years with fitted synthetic model (E13-E12).
- Deterministic forecasts compared directly with Perfect operations (E14-E8); TSO with deterministic as S=1, NSGA-III, hypervolume with reference point (1,1) (E14-E9); comparator BMA/S-BMA scenarios (E14-E6); post-assessment under observed and 1,000 noisy inflows (E14-E11).
- Ensemble-member range plus ensemble-mean policy (E22-E9).
- Four policy variants including Baseline-TOCS (E50-E6).

**Limits and gaps**
- Perfect and baseline are benchmarks, not deployable (E16-E6, E17-E5, E50-E6).
- E14-E8: better forecast quality did not consistently imply better operation; two events only. Hypervolume is not damage evidence (E14-E9).
- Unreported: seeds and initialization (E13-E11), BMA details (E14-E6), measurement-error distribution (E14-E11), mean-policy selection rule (E22-E9). E13-E12 assumes forecast relationships transfer.

**Example values**
- E13-E11: M = 100 syn-HEFS and 10 syn-GEFS traces.
- E22-E9: 20 members.

## EP10-D Sensitivity, stress, and extreme tests

**When papers did this**
- To assess how forecast quality, preferences, configuration or event magnitude change results (E02-E9, E05-E8, E10-E6, E28-E7).

**How**
- Forecast-error level/correlation sweeps: r ∈ [50, 400] m³/s and q_error ∈ [−0.5, 0.5] with recourse omitted (E02-E9); r = 10, 20.88, 50 (E03-E12); x and r jointly (E05-E8); ρ = 0, 0.3, 0.5 (E30-E6); σ and ρζQ grids (E31-E5); zero/one/two-SD (E32-E7); three σɛ1 and σɛ2 levels (E26-E6).
- Isolated vs combined factors and comparators: E06-E10, E08-E7, E31-E6, E32-E7.
- Worst-case release per Eqs. (11)–(13) in E09-E4.
- Horizon, amplified (1.5×) floods and flood patterns (E10-E6); branch/horizon sweep with CPU time (E35-E9).
- Scaled events, synthetic ensembles and holdout training designs (E16-E7, E16-E8); refill check against remaining inflow (E16-E9).
- Global parameter enumeration (105,000 combinations of R_max, RR, LT) (E17-E6).
- Forecast-accuracy sweeps and inversion to a target accuracy (E28-E7, E36-E8); confidence-level sweep 5%–95% (E14-E12).
- Parameter and failure-mode branches (E25-E6, E20-E10); scheme and quantile comparisons (E29-E7); repetition across eight design floods (E38-E9); FLWL sweep 127.2–127.8 m (E39-E8).

**Limits and gaps**
- All are model-based sensitivity, not independent validation (E02-E9, E05-E8, E10-E6, E25-E6, E36-E8).
- E03-E12 parameter calibration not reported; E17-E6 ranges are not engineering-feasibility ranges; E16-E9 refill excludes delivery requirements; E39-E8 limited to tested values, no economics.

**Example values**
- E09-E4: 5-day/6-h safe volume 23.0×10⁶ m³, level 125.4 m.
- E30-E6: 2.9%, 5.4%, 7.0% risk at 136.00 m.
- E35-E9: CPU 151 s (DSF MPC), 867 s (16 branches), 1354 s (32).
- E16-E8: 1997 flood pool 662 TAF vs nominal 750 TAF.

## EP10-E Holdout, transfer, and generalization tests

**When papers did this**
- To test out-of-sample performance or produce cross-site screening rules (E17-E8, E46-E7, E50-E10).

**How**
- Decision-tree classifiers per site and target, then aggregated across six sites with thresholds from the better-class path (E17-E7, E17-E8); a supplemental event (2017) checks event dependence (E17-E9).
- Held-out typhoons compared with historical records and a perfect-inflow pseudo-optimization (E46-E7).
- Additional event scales (100-year and 5-year) (E49-E9).
- Correlate site attributes with benefits and compare ten random seeds for equifinality (E50-E9); LOOCV over 10 water years, then rescale the TOCS bound by the ratio of largest 3-day inflow sums and repeat (E50-E10).

**Limits and gaps**
- E17-E7 accuracy not tabulated for every case; E49-E9 lacks input-generation details; E46-E7 has no live test.
- LOOCV uses limited years and policies can fail in clustered floods; bound scaling is a model mitigation (E50-E10).

**Example values**
- E17-E8: example 20% rule R_max > 23%, RR > 4%, LT > 2 days.
- E50-E9: r = 0.55 and r = −0.79 for two attribute relationships.
- E50-E10: failures over 20 days; bound scaling removed exceedances at a 4% mean-storage reduction.

## EP10-F Verify the method itself

**When papers did this**
- To check whether a risk estimate, data component, or evaluation result is credible (E33-E8, E39-E7, E19-E12, E47-E7).

**How**
- Generate 10,000 multisite inflow samples (Pearson-III, magnified typical hydrographs) (E33-E7); verify R_r = 100 f_r / N ≤ R_accepted = 100 f / N (E33-E8).
- Compare a new calculation with a traditional one (AMIT vs MLTP) (E39-E7).
- Extreme-risk rate Pe = integral from Zdesign to infinity of f(Zmax) compared with K (E41-E7).
- Component validation: Xinanjiang calibration/validation and ERA5 PWV vs radiosondes (E19-E12).
- One-at-a-time criterion perturbation (Rj, Gj) (E47-E5); MCDM ranking consistency before and after selection or PCA (E47-E7, E48-E7).

**Limits and gaps**
- Method-to-method and stochastic checks are not independent empirical validation (E33-E8, E39-E7, E47-E7).
- E33-E7: several simulated statistics differ from observed. E47-E5 ignores joint perturbations. E19-E12 relative-error sign is unreliable in OCR.

**Example values**
- E33-E8: R_accepted 1.00% vs R_r 0.97%.
- E39-E7: maximum difference 6.1 × 10⁻⁴.
- E41-E7: 0.09836%, 0.09972%, 0.09813% by season.
- E19-E12: NSE 0.94; R² > 0.97, RMSE < 4.0 mm.
