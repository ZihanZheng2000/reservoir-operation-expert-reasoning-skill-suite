## EP11-A Organize cross-agency review and staged screening

Steering committee, workplan, staged viability assessment, rounds of consultation. Strength: Provisional. 3 steps, 2 papers.

### E18-E1: Organize a FIRO pilot and workplan

**Description:** The FIRO process begins with a steering committee, workplan, preliminary assessment, more in-depth and final viability assessment, and eventual WCM update; multiple work teams execute the workplan.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** A reservoir is selected for FIRO investigation. **Inputs:** Participating agencies, site objectives, WCM, study needs. **Procedure:** Form the steering committee, define a workplan, assign work teams, and progress through staged viability assessments. **Output:** Coordinated pilot work products and a route toward WCM revision.

**Evidence boundary:** Process sequence is stated in §1, p. 2 and Figure 1 is cited. Membership, governance criteria, and workplan contents are not reported in this article.

### E18-E9: Route candidates through the FIRO Screening Process

**Description:** For portfolio scaling, Stage A uses simple elimination criteria; Stage B scores candidate suitability over collaboration, hydraulics, operations, environment, and precipitation forecast skill while balancing benefit against implementation effort; Stage C conducts more thorough assessment and dialogue with prioritized sites.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Portfolio has more reservoirs than can receive full viability studies. **Inputs:** Controlled-outlet status, WCM/WCP existence, forecast skill/lead time, benefit and challenge dimensions, local-office discussion, available resources. **Procedure:** Apply A → B → C, increasing rigor and engagement. In beta testing, Stage A was applied across the portfolio; 71 reservoirs were eligible for Stage B and 13 were selected for beta testing of later stages. **Output:** Suitability score/prioritization and recommended next route.

**Evidence boundary:** Process and beta counts are explicit in §§3.1–3.1.2, pp. 8–10. The OCR text says “more than 80%” of 85 reservoirs eligible and “only 13 of 71” selected; exact scoring formula and selection threshold are not reported.

### E21-E5: Submit schemes for multi-department consultation

**Description:** CROC submits the preliminary ensemble; FCDRH-led state/local authorities, CWRC, CWB, hydropower stations, and grid companies consult over hydro-meteorological information, joint operation, and temporary generation schedules. Ten consultations occurred during the fourth/fifth floods.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Candidate schemes affect multiple mandates or require authorization. **Inputs:** Scheme ensemble, risk/benefit analysis, current forecasts, power/shipping/ecology requirements, regulations. **Procedure:** Conduct rounds of consultation and select a final scheme. **Output:** Authorized coordinated operation and any temporary schedule adjustments.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in Fig. 3 description, p. 4, and p. 6–7. The number of ten consultations is reported; agenda-level decisions, dissent handling, and approval thresholds are not.

## EP11-B Controlled pilot

Temporarily modify the operating manual and observe the effects. Strength: Singleton. 1 steps, 1 papers.

### E18-E8: Temporarily implement and observe a pilot modification

**Description:** USACE temporarily modified Lake Mendocino WCM operations in water years 2019 and 2020. The paper reports increased water-supply benefits and mitigated downstream flooding; 2020 storage increased by nearly 20% relative to the described comparison.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** A pilot strategy has sufficient support for temporary operational experimentation. **Inputs:** Temporary WCM modification, reservoir conditions, forecast-informed decisions, observed water-year outcomes. **Procedure:** Operate under the temporary FIRO modification during the two reported water years and compare outcomes. **Output:** Demonstration evidence about storage, supply, and downstream flooding.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in §2.1.2, p. 5 and Figure 5 caption. The text does not report the full operational log, counterfactual design, statistical attribution, or authorization details; this is a temporary demonstration, not evidence of universal deployment.

## EP11-C Disposition and rule revision

Defer, research, fully assess, or update the manual; review after the event and revise. Strength: Provisional. 3 steps, 2 papers.

### E18-E10: Branch to defer, research, assess, or update

**Description:** Stage C may conclude that benefits are too small, forecast skill needs further improvement, full viability assessment is warranted, or no further assessment is needed and the WCM can be updated.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Stage C information and dialogue are complete. **Inputs:** Stage B prioritization, deeper site assessment, forecast/model adequacy, expected benefits and effort. **Procedure:** Route according to the evidence and readiness of the site. **Output:** Deferred research, full viability assessment, no-action recommendation, or WCM-update route.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in §3.1.1, p. 9. The paper does not specify formal branch thresholds, decision authority, or appeal/reassessment rules.

### E18-E11: Update WCM/WCP and preserve future adaptability

**Description:** For suitable dams, implementation occurs through WCM updates coordinated among FIRO teams and USACE division/district water-management teams. FIRO 2.0 proposes flexible WCP language paired with a forecast-skill assessment that confirms improvements before incorporating new forecast/model tools into decisions.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Viability and institutional processes support formal implementation. **Inputs:** Pilot/screening evidence, WCM update process, proposed WCP language, forecast-skill/model assessment, laws and regulations. **Procedure:** Codify FIRO flexibility in the WCM and use an improvement-confirmation process for future updates. **Output:** Revised operating guidance with conditional adaptability.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in §§3.2–3.2.2 and Summary, pp. 10–11. Actual wording, approval timeline, operational monitoring, fallback rules, and post-update learning cycle are not reported.

### E21-E9: Reanalyze performance and maintain crisis branches

**Description:** After the event, ICDSS reanalysis summarizes cooperation experience and supports revision. ICDSS also includes 22 crisis-management plans for automatic-control, equipment/power failures, accidents, and emergencies.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Flood ends or an automation/equipment emergency occurs. **Inputs:** Operation/forecast records, outcome indicators, failures/emergency state. **Procedure:** Reanalyze contributions of reservoirs and natural/man-made factors, revise the scheme, and invoke relevant crisis plan when needed. **Output:** Lessons, revised cooperation procedures, and emergency response path.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in pp. 7–8. The paper does not describe individual crisis-plan actions, post-event statistical attribution, or a formal learning metric.
