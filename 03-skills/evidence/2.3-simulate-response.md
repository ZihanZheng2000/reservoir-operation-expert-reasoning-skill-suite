## EP04-A Build a reservoir water-balance simulator and compute trajectories

Advance storage, level, and release per period for single or cascade reservoirs; check against history when possible. Strength: Established. 21 steps, 19 papers.

### E03-E3: Build the rolling two-stage water balance

**Description:** Use the immediate interval and forecast horizon to calculate releases, carryover storage, and expected Stage-2 release. All excess storage above the lower FLWL must be released by the end of Stage 2 under the model.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Prefill conditions pass and a rolling decision is required. **Inputs:** Current storage/inflow, expected Stage-2 inflow ̅I₂, Stage-1 release (R_1), Stage-1 excess storage (W_1). **Procedure:** Use (R_2=W_1+I_2) and (R_1+R_2=W_0+I_1+I_2); under expectation, use ̅R₂ and the RFCC relationship (W_1+d=R_{2,​max}-\bar I_2). **Output:** Candidate current release, carryover storage, expected future release, and RFCC.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 3–5 and 9–10, equations 4–7 and 29–30. Negligible evaporation/seepage is assumed; exact within-stage routing is not given.

### E04-E9: Apply forecast-informed pre-release and routing rules

**Description:** They propagate predicted inflows through TGR flood-season operation. The pre-release is the mean predicted inflow over the effective h-day lead time, and it is updated each period in a rolling-horizon process. Release and level constraints encode Jingjiang and TGR safety criteria.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** A predicted inflow sequence is available at each rolling update. **Inputs:** (h)-day predicted inflow, current reservoir state, FCWL 145 m, Jingjiang stage/release limits, and reservoir release capacity. **Procedure:** Compute ̄Q by Eq. (14), update release for each period, route the reservoir, cap releases at 53,900 m³/s when needed for Jingjiang safety and at 79,000 m³/s in the specified 175–180.4 m range, and use release capacity above 180.4 m. **Output:** Simulated storage/water-level trajectory.

**Evidence boundary:** Section 2.3, pp. 7–8; Section 3.4, pp. 724–725; Eqs. (14)–(15), Table 2. The full routing equation and the detailed pre-release implementation are not provided in the supplied text.

### E07-E1: Build the LMO water-balance simulator

**Description:** The authors simulate Lake Mendocino storage and four downstream junctions at a daily time step from 1985 to 2010. Reservoir storage follows mass balance: prior storage minus evaporation and upstream losses, plus inflow, minus flood-control, emergency, water-supply, and uncontrolled-spillway releases; downstream flow is upstream flow plus unimpaired reach gains minus reach losses.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Establish a common system model for all alternatives. **Inputs:** Storage, evaporation, inflows, diversions/losses, release components, reach gains/losses, travel-time assumptions. **Procedure:** Apply the reservoir mass balance (Equation 1) and junction mass balance (Equation 2); route high-flow changes to downstream junctions in the same daily step because travel times are under 24 hr when Hopland exceeds 8,000 cfs. **Output:** Daily storage, release components, and flows at modeled junctions.

**Evidence boundary:** Equations 1–2 and model description on pp. 6–7, Sections 3.1–3.2. The assigned main text points to supporting information for detailed input data and constraints; exact calibration/implementation code is not reproduced here.

### E07-E3: Test model assumptions against historical operations

**Description:** The authors create a 2000–2010 historical scenario, when operational data were readily accessible, and compare simulated storage and system flows with observations. They use the result to support the adequacy of assumptions about unimpaired flows, reach losses, and release constraints before comparing alternatives.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Verify that the common simulator can reproduce historical conditions. **Inputs:** Historical hydrology and operational data, LMO assumptions and constraints. **Procedure:** Run the historical scenario and compare modeled storage and downstream flows with observed conditions during supply and flood-control operations. **Output:** Evidence supporting use of LMO for comparative analysis.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit on p. 8, Section 3.2.1; further results are referred to supporting information. This is model testing against historical observations, not independent validation of deployed EFO.

### E07-E5: Simulate member trajectories under no release and detect risky horizons

**Description:** At each daily time step, LMO first forecasts storage for each member through the 15-day horizon with no reservoir release in the forecast calculation. It checks each horizon day for threshold exceedance and only constructs a flood-control release schedule for days where (r_t>R_t).

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Current daily decision time (T). **Inputs:** Current state and each member's future inflows. **Procedure:** Run independent member forecasts to (t=1:15); identify horizon days and members above 111,000 acre-feet; pass only excess-risk cases to schedule construction. **Output:** Risky member set for each horizon day and no-release risk baseline.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 9–10, Section 3.3.1 and Fig. 6. In the 8 February 1986 example, 14 of 61 members exceed the threshold on day 8. The paper does not describe tie handling or numerical solver details.

### E13-E2: Represent EFO storage and release decisions

**Description:** Simulate daily storage and controlled releases using the water balance and policy rules.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Convert inflows and forecasts into operations. **Inputs:** Prior storage, inflow, evaporation, diversions, spill, controlled releases, water-supply and emergency rules, HEFS ensemble forecasts. **Procedure:** Simulate no-flood-release ending storage for each lead/member; calculate the fraction exceeding 111,000 acre-ft; compare with the lead-time risk-tolerance curve; calculate and constrain proposed flood releases, choose the largest across leads, then take maxima with water-supply and emergency releases. **Output:** Daily controlled release and storage/downstream-flow traces.

**Evidence boundary:** Equations (1)–(2), pp. 3–4, Figure 1 and Text S1 references. Limits include 116,500 acre-ft spill crest and 8,000 cfs Hopland constraint; full parameters are in cited supporting text, not reproduced here.

### E14-E7: Simulate constrained reservoir operation

**Description:** Evaluate release decisions under each forecast type.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Convert forecasts to flood-control trajectories. **Inputs:** Initial level 176.04 m; storage/level limits 156.04–184.0 m; release limits 0–4,157 m³/s; safety level 178.64 m; safety release 1,000 m³/s; target end level 176.04 m; flood-loss level 176.04 m; protection storage (0.314\times10^8) m³. **Procedure:** Apply mass balance, storage/release limits, release fluctuation constraints, and initial/end conditions; for deterministic comparisons minimize highest level or peak release with penalty (alpha=10^3). **Output:** Water-level and release trajectories.

### E15-E5: Evaluate five objectives under cascade dynamics

**Description:** Simulate each candidate rule over the three-reservoir cascade.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Score a candidate HPFOR. **Inputs:** 1970–2010 daily June–September flood data, initial levels at flood limits, reservoir characteristics, delayed upstream releases, navigation function. **Procedure:** Apply mass balance (V_{i,t}=V_{i,t-1}+I_{i,t}Delta t+\sum R_{k,t-\tau}\Delta t-R_{i,t}\Delta t); calculate generation plus end energy, Three Gorges level/release maxima, upstream flood-storage use, and navigation rate. **Output:** (F_1)–(F_5) objective vector.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 8–12, Eqs. (17)–(25), Table 1. Objective definitions are reported, but detailed turbine/head, navigation, time-delay, and data preprocessing parameters are not all given.

### E16-E2: Define storage objective and shared constraints

**Description:** Simulate storage and bound every policy.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Generate a release at each time step. **Inputs:** (S_t,Q_t,R_t,TOCS,H,w,R_{max},Ramp). **Procedure:** Apply (S_{t+1}=S_t+Q_t-R_t), (0\le R_t\le R_{max}), and (|R_t-R_{t-1}|\le Ramp); evaluate (J=\sum[(1-w)(S_t-TOCS)^+ + w(S_t-TOCS)^-]). **Output:** Objective value and required flood pool.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 2–4, Eqs. (1)–(4). (w) range is reported; exact H and unit conversion details are not fully described.

### E17-E2: Form scenario-based MPC state forecasts

**Description:** Build ensemble-dependent storage trajectories for each candidate release sequence.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** At each time (T), update the rolling optimization. **Inputs:** Current storage (X_T), observed inflow (Q_T), prior release (U_T), (N_e=40/42) HEFS forecasts (q_f(T+i,j)), (N_h=14), TOCS. **Procedure:** Compute (x_f(T+i,j)=X_{t_0}+\sum_{k=1}^i(q_f-u_f)); optimize one release sequence against expected positive storage above TOCS over all leads/members plus expected terminal negative storage. **Output:** (u_f^*), forecast storage matrix (x_f).

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 5–6, Eqs. (1)–(4). The formulation uses one common release sequence across members, allowing scenario comparison but no per-member feedback tree.

### E18-E5: Simulate/evaluate candidate scenarios with required decision tools

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Candidate alternatives are specified. **Inputs:** Baseline and FIRO scenario definitions, hydrologic/forecast information, reservoir and downstream system representation, HEC-ResSim/USACE decision-tool context. **Procedure:** Run the relevant pilot assessment/simulations under the defined scenarios. **Output:** Scenario performance results for risk, supply, recharge, environmental, and operational metrics.

**Evidence boundary:** Tool compatibility is explicit in pp. 2 and 4; detailed HEC-ResSim setup, timestep, ensembles, calibration, and computational settings are not reported.

### E20-E4: Encode and run the per-event MILP RFCM

**Description:** The RFCM minimizes P = wq×release penalty + wv×storage penalty for each flood event. Piecewise-linear penalty curves represent release and storage consequences; constraints represent maximum/minimum reservoir levels and outlet/spillway volume limits.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** A candidate parameter set must produce an operating trajectory for one event. **Inputs:** Inflow hydrograph, initial storage, candidate wq/wv/PVfcl/b, penalty curve segments, timestep, horizon, hydraulic/operational limits. **Procedure:** Solve the MILP for QO and V, using linearized constraints/penalties and complementary weights wq+wv=1. **Output:** Event release/storage trajectories.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in §2.3 and Appendix A, pp. 7, 14; Eq. (18), Eq. (A1), Table A1. MILP global-optimum assurance is described generally for the linearized formulation; solver, timestep, and full constraint values are not reported.

### E25-E2: Compute candidate storm releases

**Description:** For each Qh, increase available future storage by Qh tf and store storm volume up to capacity; release the remainder over tsi.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** evaluate one candidate pre-storm release. **Inputs:** Vsi, k−S0+Qh tf, tsi. **Procedure:** Qsi=[Vsi−(k−S0+Qh tf)]/tsi and Si fills the remaining available storage subject to Eq. (2); no other water loss in the storm balance. **Output:** Si and Qsi for each storm.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 5115–5117, Eqs. (2)–(4). Square-wave average release may underestimate risk from a changing hydrograph/maximum release; authors flag this limitation.

### E27-E1: Define routing and operating-rule inputs

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** each hourly flood-routing step. **Inputs:** inflow, storage/level curve, forecast runoff R, rule thresholds, initial level. **Procedure:** iterate routing and rule application; ignore evaporation/leakage/losses. **Output:** Qout and Z series.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4560–4561, Eq. (1). Initial simulation level is 325 m; exact f/g implementation beyond Tables 2–3 is not fully documented.

### E33-E1: Specify state and routing equations

**Description:** Propagate each reservoir’s storage by inflow minus release and losses, and derive downstream inflow by channel routing plus interval inflow.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** every simulated or optimized time step. **Inputs:** prior storage (V_t^k), inflow (I_t^k), release (Q_t^k), time interval, and downstream routing inputs. **Procedure:** apply the water-balance equation; losses are set negligible in this study; use the channel routing function for downstream inflow. **Output:** next storage and reservoir inflows.

**Evidence boundary:** Section 2.1.1, p. 2, Eq. (1); Section 2.3.2, Eq. (10). Negligible losses and the detailed routing function are case assumptions/details; the extracted text does not give all implementation parameters.

### E35-E5: Build the reservoir dynamic/constraint model

**Description:** Propagate storage and forebay elevation and enforce physical/operational limits.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** each optimization solve. **Inputs:** previous/current storage, inflow, spillway release, water supply, piecewise-linear level-storage curve, spillway capacity curve, forebay bounds. **Procedure:** apply continuity (s_k=s_{k-1}+Δt(Q_I-Q_S-Q_{WS})); compute forebay from storage; enforce zero continuity residual, forebay minimum/maximum, and spillway capacity. **Output:** feasible state/control trajectory.

**Evidence boundary:** Sections 3.3–3.4, pp. 12–13; Eqs. (16)–(21). The detailed curves and numerical solver tolerances are not reported.

### E46-E1: Simulate reservoir continuity

**Description:** They convert water level to storage and update storage from inflow and release each hour, ignoring precipitation, evaporation, seepage, and leakage as small relative to flood volumes.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** advance an operating period. **Inputs:** measured level, inflow, chosen release, storage function. **Procedure:** apply Equation (1), infer current inflow from measured levels when needed, and calculate the next level. **Output:** level/storage trajectory.

### E48-E2: Enumerate and route six alternatives

**Description:** They combine discharge-capacity, regular, and maximum-flood-peak-reduction models subject to reservoir-specific admissibility. The discharge-capacity model sets outflow to min(capacity, inflow); the regular model uses forecasted inflow/current storage; the peak-reduction model minimizes the squared sum of reservoir outflow plus interval inflow under operating constraints.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** generate feasible alternatives. **Inputs:** event inflow, inter-reservoir inflow, model assignment, initial/target states, limits. **Procedure:** route each combination; for peak reduction use stepwise trial-and-error and enforce mass balance, highest water level, terminal level, discharge, and outflow-variation constraints. **Output:** six time series of storage/water level/outflow.

**Evidence boundary:** Evidence pp. 8–9, Table 2, Eqs. (26)–(32). The text does not give the numerical values of all time-varying limits, initial states, or trial-and-error stopping criteria.

## EP04-B Simulate basin, channel, and downstream processes

Channel routing, local inflow, naturalized flow, channel storage, downstream station levels. Strength: Established. 6 steps, 5 papers.

### E19-E5: Transform PP into floodwater volume

**Description:** The authors map PP to a floodwater estimate using Eq. (8). The main implementation uses a conservative runoff coefficient of 0.75; Appendix A also applies the Xinanjiang model to simulate streamflow/floodwater volume.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** PP must become a reservoir inflow/storage quantity. **Inputs:** PP, runoff coefficient or hydrological model, basin/runoff relationship. **Procedure:** Convert atmospheric potential water to estimated floodwater; retain the simplified coefficient as a practical alternative and test the hydrological model as a refinement. **Output:** Estimated floodwater volume for VCWL derivation.

**Evidence boundary:** The coefficient value is explicit on p. 5; Xinanjiang calibration/validation is described in Appendix A, p. 12. Spatial/temporal redistribution and exact Eq. (8) units are not fully presented in the OCR text.

### E21-E2: Simulate upstream reservoir effects and naturalized flows

**Description:** Because upstream cascade operations change natural-flow propagation and forecast accuracy, ICDSS mines impounding/discharge rules for reservoirs outside joint operation and anticipates their discharge. Naturalized and measured flows at key stations are compared using actual operation data, flood travel time, and water balance.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Actual upstream operations alter the hydrologic signal used for downstream decisions. **Inputs:** Upstream operating rules/data, reservoir releases, travel times, water balance, hydrological stations. **Procedure:** Estimate the operation-adjusted naturalized flow and anticipated upstream discharge for routing/forecast use. **Output:** More realistic inflow/routing inputs to cooperation decisions.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in Fig. 4 caption and ICDSS model description, pp. 5, 7. Exact naturalization equations and rule-mining method are not reported.

## EP04-C Propagate uncertainty to levels and flows

Route many scenarios, SDEs, or moment recursions; collect maxima and other outputs. Strength: Established. 20 steps, 13 papers.

### E06-E7: Route each simulated flood with pre-release and sampled uncertainties

**Description:** Apply the discrete reservoir continuity/storage–elevation routing to each synthetic flood and uncertainty sample under the current TGR operating rules.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Inflow/forecast and uncertainty samples are ready, with an initial water level specified. **Inputs:** Q_i,t (synthetic inflow plus generated forecast-error series), q_s,t from the operating model, λ_j, v_j, e_j, h_pj, flood duration T, time interval, and initial level. **Procedure:** Iterate Eq. (8) over t; calculate actual outflow through λ_jq_s,t, update storage, convert storage to level with the uncertain relation, and include wind backwater/run-up. Use pre-release based on real-time forecasts before a large flood. **Output:** N routed operations and maximum water level Z_M (and potential downstream peak Q_m) per realization.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4–7, Section 3, Eqs. (6)–(8), Fig. 3. Repeat N times for subsequent risk analysis. Specific initial level, time step, flood duration, N, and exact rule branch are not fully reported/legible.

### E20-E6: Extract maximum release and storage per event

**Description:** For each event, apply the maximum operator to released flow and stored volume: QOmax,n=max_t(QOn,t) and Vmax,n=max_t(Vn,t). These vectors are the characterization outputs for a candidate parameter set.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** A candidate parameter set has generated all event trajectories. **Inputs:** QO and V time series for each event. **Procedure:** Calculate event maxima and retain their ensemble vectors. **Output:** QOmax and Vmax samples for distribution/risk calculations.

**Evidence boundary:** Explicit in §2.2, p. 6, Eqs. (8)–(9). The article acknowledges that timing, gradients, and full trajectories are omitted from the calibration criterion.

### E27-E5: Route Monte Carlo floods and record MWL

**Description:** Run 2,000 routings for each of three representative floods (6,000 total), beginning at 325 m; record inflow, outflow, level, and maximum water level.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** evaluate output risk under forecast errors. **Inputs:** generated inflow series, Table 3 rule, reservoir routing model, initial Z. **Procedure:** time-step simulation through flood passage; identify MWL (e.g., heaviest 198307 event peaks around hour 23 and returns near 325 m by hour 36). **Output:** MWL sample distributions and routed series.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4565–4568, Section 4.2 and Fig. 6. Only first 300 series are plotted for illustration; code, convergence, and independent routing validation are not reported.

### E27-E6: Magnify return-period floods and estimate exceedance risk

**Description:** For 100-year/10,000-year analyses, randomly select a representative wave, linearly magnify it, derive 6-h forecast runoff at lead times, apply rules, add errors, route, and repeat.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** quantify threshold risk at specified flood return periods. **Inputs:** representative waves, target return-period magnitude, error process, thresholds. **Procedure:** five-step hydrograph magnification and routing method described on p. 4567. **Output:** MWL distributions and mean/SD for Table 4.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4567–4571, Figs. 10–11 and Table 4. Linear magnification and approximate normal MWL distribution are assumptions; return-period hydrology and real dam response are not independently validated.

### E28-E4: Transfer error to stochastic routing

**Description:** They compute `σQ(t)=μQ(t)σ` and `σV(t)=σQ(t)√Δt`, then solve the storage SDE with forward Euler and standard-normal Wiener increments.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** each routing time step. **Inputs:** mean inflow/outflow, storage, `Δt=1 h`, diffusion coefficient, initial storage, random normal increments. **Procedure:** iterate Eq. (13) over time nodes and K=2,000 sample trajectories. **Output:** stochastic storage trajectories `V(j,k)`.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4 and 6–7, Eqs. (11)–(13), Fig. 5. K=2,000 and Δt=1 h are reported. Numerical convergence, truncation, or physical-bound enforcement is not reported.

### E28-E5: Convert and summarize level paths

**Description:** They apply the elevation–storage relationship to every storage trajectory and compute temporal distribution summaries.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** storage paths are available. **Inputs:** `V(j,k)`, elevation–storage curve. **Procedure:** obtain `Z(j,k)`; calculate mean, quartiles, minimum and maximum; display box plots. **Output:** time-varying stochastic water-level distribution and deterministic comparison.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4 and 7–8, Figs. 6–8. The text says the mean agrees with deterministic routing; it does not report calibration or error checks for the elevation–storage curve.

### E30-E2: Obtain deterministic mean routing

**Description:** They run the forecast inflow through the reservoir flood-control operating rules to obtain the mean level process.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** error recursion needs the mean level at each time. **Inputs:** forecast inflows, initial storage/level, Dahuofang rules, discharge and storage relations, `Δt=6 h`, `T=22`. **Procedure:** route deterministically; use the resulting `H̄(t)` as the mean stochastic level. **Output:** mean reservoir level and release process.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 4–6, case rules and Fig. 7. Rule thresholds include downstream combined-flow limits 6,260 and 4,000 m³/s under stated flood probabilities; no independent routing validation is reported.

### E30-E3: Fit local discharge/storage coefficients

**Description:** They use the deterministic level as the center of piecewise-linear fitting at each step.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 3–4, Fig. 2, Eqs. (8)–(12). The approximation is conditioned on small level change/short time step; fitting diagnostics are not reported.

### E30-E4: Recursively calculate level-error moments

**Description:** They initialize zero error mean/variance at time zero and propagate expectation and variance using the SDE-derived recursions.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** each time index through `T`. **Inputs:** prior `E[η]`, `D[η]`, inflow-error moments, outflow/storage terms, local coefficients, adjacent covariances. **Procedure:** iterate Eqs. **Evidence boundary:** pp. 4 and 6, Fig. 3, Eqs. (13)–(16), (30)–(31). Initial moments are explicitly set to zero; distributional normality of level error is assumed rather than empirically validated.

### E31-E2: Obtain mean routing and local curve coefficients

**Description:** They route forecast inflows deterministically under the Foziling rules, then use the mean level path to select piecewise discharge/storage coefficients.

**Evidence boundary:** pp. 3–6, Eqs. (5)–(6), Fig. 4 and case rules. No independent routing validation or curve-fitting error check is reported.

### E31-E3: Recursively calculate level-error moments

### E31-E4: Recursively calculate level-error dependence and risks

## EP04-D Replace full simulation with surrogate or probabilistic models

Bayesian networks, simplified state-space models, precomputed risk relationships. Strength: Established (at threshold). 12 steps, 4 papers.

### E08-E3: Generate feasible operation samples

**Description:** Solve the flood-control model for each sampled input.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Need BN outcome data. **Inputs:** V0, Q, IU samples; level, outflow, capacity, and ramping constraints. **Procedure:** Minimize maximum reservoir outflow subject to Eq. **Evidence boundary:** Three cases use V0 and Q/IU as deterministic or random as specified on p. 5. This is a training/evaluation optimization, not a demonstrated real-time controller.

### E08-E4: Build and fit BN

**Description:** Encode routing dependencies and estimate conditional probabilities.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Training records available. **Inputs:** Nodes V0/V1, H0/H1, Q, q, IU, QC; discretization range and K. **Procedure:** Use expert DAG, equal-distance discretization, then MLE CPT fitting. **Output:** Trained BN for each uncertainty case.

**Evidence boundary:** K trades precision against computation; optional sensitivity to K is suggested, not fully reported (p. 4).

### E08-E5: Perform prior risk inference

**Description:** Propagate the trained BN without posterior evidence.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** Need baseline probability distributions. **Inputs:** Trained BN, safety level Hs, safety flow QCs. **Procedure:** Infer maximum H and QC distributions and sum probabilities exceeding thresholds using Eqs. (13)–(14). **Output:** Reservoir/downstream risk curves by rainless-day horizon and uncertainty case.

**Evidence boundary:** BN prior results match MC for case 1 in the reported example (p. 6, Fig. 7), supporting the implemented inference for that check.

### E08-E6: Perform posterior prediction or diagnosis

**Description:** Update risk or infer possible causes after evidence is supplied.

**Retained details:** **Trigger:** New forecast/state evidence or observed maximum levels/flows. **Inputs:** L1–L3 values for V0, Qmax, IUmax, Hmax, QCmax. **Procedure:** Prediction infers H/QC from causes; diagnosis infers V0/Qmax/IUmax from Hmax/QCmax via joint-tree propagation. **Output:** Posterior distributions, risk increments, and likely uncertainty-source ranges.

**Evidence boundary:** Evidence definitions and values are given in Table 1 and pp. 8–10. Higher evidence levels widen or concentrate distributions depending on case; these are simulated posterior patterns.

### E32-E2: Generate physically consistent training samples

### E32-E5: Run prediction inference and threshold risks
