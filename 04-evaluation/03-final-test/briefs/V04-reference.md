# V04 Reference Route (judges only)

## (a) Key questions
- What shape do forecast residuals actually take? Are they Gaussian or not, biased or unbiased, constant or dependent on flow, constant or varying over time?
- How should inflow uncertainty be carried into the optimization of a multi-reservoir system?
- How much do simple Gaussian assumptions understate the water-level risk?
- How should one plan be chosen from a Pareto set when decision-makers' preferences are fuzzy?

## (b) Methods and steps
1. **Probabilistic forecasting with flow-dependent residuals** (an extension of Jiang et al. 2019).
   - Residuals were split into four flow groups: 0-100, 100-500, 500-1000 and 1000+ m3/s.
   - Within each group, the residual mean and standard deviation were modeled as linear functions of the forecast flow. A genetic algorithm (GA) fitted the parameters.
   - The standardized residuals were made Gaussian with a Yeo-Johnson transform, then checked with skewness (SK) and kurtosis (KU).
   - An AR(2) model, chosen from the partial autocorrelation function (PACF), captured how residuals carry over in time.
   - The 5%/95% prediction intervals were built from this model.
2. **Six controlled cases.** These ranged from unbiased Gaussian with constant parameters (Case 1) to non-Gaussian with flow-group, time-varying parameters (Case 6). Cases 1-4 were sampled with a t-copula. Cases 5-6 were sampled from the AR(2) model followed by the inverse transform.
3. **Monte Carlo inflow scenarios.** 1000 stochastic inflow scenarios were generated by adding sampled residuals to the deterministic forecast.
4. **Deterministic model (DMOM).** An NSGA-III optimization with three objectives: lowest maximum level at Guxian (GX), lowest maximum level at Luhun (LH), and lowest peak at Heishiguan (HSG).
   - Constraints: water balance, maximum levels, release capacity, end-level tolerance and release ramping. Muskingum routing carried releases downstream.
   - Settings: 6 h time step, start and end at the flood-limit level, population 200 initialized by Latin Hypercube sampling, crossover 100%, mutation 90%, 500 generations.
   - Only odd-period releases were optimized. Even periods were interpolated between them to suppress sawtooth release patterns.
   - A search corridor bounded the decision space.
5. **Plan selection.** FAHP-TOPSIS ranked the plans on 9 indicators covering reservoir safety and downstream safety. A repair operator then fixed any plan that broke the end-level constraint.
6. **Uncertainty-based model (UMOM).** The level objective became the 95th percentile of maximum levels across 50 stochastic scenarios.

## (c) Validation
- Prediction-interval coverage was checked on 4 historical floods at Lushi.
- SK/KU thresholds were applied: SK within [-1, 1] and KU within [1, 5].
- The best DMOM plan was driven with the 1000 scenarios from each case to get water-level uncertainty bands.
- DMOM and UMOM were compared by Monte Carlo simulation on 50 extra forecast hydrographs.

## (d) Findings
- Prediction-interval coverage rose from 35-79% in Case 1 to 94-98% in Case 6. Case 6 intervals narrowed as each flood receded.
- Only the Case 6 assumption passed the SK/KU test in all four flow groups.
- The forecast model overpredicted low flows and underpredicted floods above 1000 m3/s.
- At Guxian, the 95th-percentile maximum level was 0.88 m higher under Case 6 than under Case 1. The Gaussian assumptions therefore understate the level risk.
- UMOM compared with DMOM:
  - Average HSG peak fell from 1515.3 to 1260.9 m3/s (-254.4).
  - Luhun's maximum release fell by 155.8 m3/s, while its maximum level rose by 0.7 m.
  - At Guxian, the maximum level fell by 0.3 m and the end level by 0.23 m.
  - The net effect was that risk shifted toward the lower-risk reservoir.

## (e) Limitations
- UMOM costs several times more computation than DMOM, which limits real-time use.
- The high-flow prediction intervals rest on few samples.
- The water-level objective ignores remaining flood storage. That is acceptable only because the two reservoirs have similar capacities.
- At Guxian, the constraint that the level may not fall more than 3 m below the flood limit left little room to optimize.
- The method was tested on a single event.
- The forecast data came from a deep-learning model.
