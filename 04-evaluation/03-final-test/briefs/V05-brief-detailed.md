# Problem Brief V05: Revising Flood Operating Rules at the Stânca–Costești Reservoir

## 1. Background

The Stânca–Costești reservoir is on the Prut River, on the border between Romania and the Republic of Moldova (Danube basin). Built in 1973–1979, it serves flood control, hydropower, water supply, irrigation and fish farming, and is Romania's second-largest reservoir by volume. Its catchment is steeper than the rest of the Prut basin.

National rules require the operating rules to be revised every 10 years. Large floods in 2005, 2008, 2010 and 2020 raised concerns about the design peak flow and especially the design flood volume.

**Outlets.** Two power galleries (16 m² each), a backup intake, two central bottom outlets (9 m² each), two lateral bottom outlets (15.76 m² each), and six weir bays with flap gates (16 × 2.70 m). Each outlet can be opened or closed on its own.

## 3. Current operation and constraints

- **Downstream.** The riverbed carries 550 m³/s, or 700 m³/s between the dikes. Up to a 1% flood the aim is to stay within the riverbed capacity; for rare floods such as the 0.1% flood, dam safety comes first.
- **Reservoir.** Overflow above the raised flap gates is limited to 0.50 m.
- **Pre-release.** Allowed on a flood warning, usually at most 3 days ahead; over that window the bottom outlets release at most about 100 mil. m³.
- **Current practice.** Outlets are brought into use when flow at the upstream gauge reaches 200 m³/s and the 3-day forecast shows at least 600 m³/s. For inflows of 200–600 m³/s, the level is held almost constant.
- **Original design.** The 0.1% design flood was the 1969 flood shape scaled to 4300 m³/s.

## 4. Data available

- Discharge at the Rădăuți–Prut gauge (9296 km²), about 160 km upstream along a winding reach, for 1978–2020; 12 h resolution for routing.
- Reservoir levels, outflows and operators' gate operations in 2008.
- Flows above 500 m³/s are not measured; the rating curve there is extrapolated. Sources disagree on the 2008 peak (4240 m³/s originally, about 3300 m³/s after reassessment), and flood volumes at the gauge and at the dam do not match.
- **2008 flood:** peak about 3300 m³/s, about 1100 mil. m³; the level rose above the flap gates and downstream areas flooded.
- **2010 flood:** three peaks within a few weeks, about 1600–1700 mil. m³.
- National guidance gives a +10–20% climate uplift, recommended only for 1% flows.
- Forecasts of 3-day flow and 10-day areal rainfall. The data are restricted.

## 5. Research question

Review the design floods and set out per-outlet flood operating rules. The work should settle:

- which design floods, with what climate allowance, the rules must handle;
- when each outlet should be opened and closed, in a form operators can apply in real time, when the size of a coming flood is not known at warning time.

The rules must protect the dam and limit downstream flooding.
