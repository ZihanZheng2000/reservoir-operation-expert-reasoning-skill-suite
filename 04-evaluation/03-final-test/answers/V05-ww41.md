# V05 – Stânca–Costești: design flood review and per-outlet flood operating rules

## 0. Framing: what actually governs this reservoir

A quick volume balance from the brief shows that the binding problem is **flood volume and release timing, not peak flow**:

- Flood storage from the conservation level (90.80) to the 1% level (98.20) is 1285 − 735 = **550 mil. m³**. To the 0.1% level (99.50) it is **665 mil. m³**. Between 99.50 and the dam crest there are another 309 mil. m³, which is the last safety margin and not operating storage.
- Releasing 550 m³/s, the riverbed capacity, moves about **47.5 mil. m³/day**. Releasing 700 m³/s, the between-dikes capacity, moves about **60 mil. m³/day**.
- 2008: about 1100 mil. m³. If the reservoir starts at 90.80 and releases a full 550 m³/s for about 10 days (≈ 475 mil. m³), plus pre-releases 100 mil. m³, about 1125 mil. m³ can be absorbed without passing 98.20. So a 2008-type flood was **roughly manageable within the riverbed only if full non-damaging releases started early**. Holding the level steady while inflow is 200–600 m³/s, and waiting for a ≥ 600 m³/s forecast before using the outlets, gives up exactly the release time the reservoir needs. This is a plausible reason for the 2008 overtopping of the flap gates. It is a hypothesis to test against the 2008 operations record, not a conclusion.
- 2010: 1600–1700 mil. m³ over several weeks. No storage strategy holds this volume. What matters is **recovering storage between peaks**, meaning drawing back down at the maximum non-damaging rate after each peak.

So the review has to deliver (a) design floods described by **peak and volume over several durations, with several hydrograph shapes including multi-peak sequences**, and (b) rules that start releasing early, escalate as evidence about the flood's size builds up, and recover storage quickly. Both have to be robust because the flood's size is unknown when the warning is issued.

Assumptions used throughout (to be confirmed):
- A1. "Overflow above the raised flap gates limited to 0.50 m" means the raised gates may be overtopped by at most 0.5 m (up to 98.70). Above that, gates must be lowered, and lowering is how the 0.1% level of 99.50 is reached without overstressing them. The brief does not say this outright, and it affects the dam-safety stage, so it must be confirmed with the dam owner.
- A2. Each outlet's discharge-versus-head curve, gate opening times and minimum/maximum opening constraints can be obtained from the operator. I do not invent them. The order-of-magnitude numbers below are only illustrative.
- A3. The conservation level of 90.80 is the normal starting level for a flood. The brief gives no seasonal flood-control drawdown.
- A4. Downstream capacities of 550 and 700 m³/s apply just below the dam. Tributary inflow downstream is not given and must be checked when converting them into dam release limits.

## 1. Design floods: what the rules must handle

### 1.1 Repair the data first (this sets the uncertainty on everything else)

1. **Rating-curve extrapolation above 500 m³/s.** Every flow above 500 m³/s, which covers every flood of interest, is extrapolated.
   - Rebuild the high-flow rating at Rădăuți–Prut with a 1D/2D hydraulic model of the gauge reach: surveyed cross-sections, roughness calibrated on measured flows ≤ 500 m³/s, and the floodplain included.
   - Report a rating uncertainty band rather than one curve.
2. **Reservoir water balance as an independent inflow estimate.** For 2008, and for any other flood with level and outflow records:
   - Back-calculate inflow as Q_in = ΔS/Δt + Q_out, using the level–storage curve and outlet rating curves together with the recorded gate operations.
   - This gives a **dam-site flood volume that does not depend on the gauge rating**. It is probably the most reliable volume we have.
   - It should settle the 4240 vs ≈ 3300 m³/s question to the extent that volume, attenuation and timing constrain it. A smoothed back-calculated peak carries its own uncertainty from level-measurement noise.
3. **Gauge-to-dam transfer.** The gauge covers 76% of the catchment (9296/12212 km²). The 2916 km² between the gauge and the dam is steep and ungauged, and the gauge is about 160 km upstream on a winding reach.
   - Calibrate a routing model (Muskingum-Cunge or a hydrodynamic model at 12 h steps or finer) plus a local-inflow term against the back-calculated dam inflows for every event with reservoir records.
   - Quantify the gauge-to-dam volume ratio and the travel time for each event. The mismatch between gauge and dam volumes is the key quantity here, not noise to average away.
   - The travel time sets how much warning the gauge alone provides. I expect 1–2 days, but this must be confirmed.
4. **Homogenisation.** Re-derive the 1978–2020 flood series with the revised rating curve. Flag years where the rating or the gauge changed.

### 1.2 Flood frequency analysis

- **Peaks:** annual maxima, or peaks over threshold, 1978–2020, using the revised series. Fit GEV/Pearson III/LP3 with L-moments or maximum likelihood, and report confidence intervals. With 43 years, the 0.1% quantile is a large extrapolation, so show the uncertainty explicitly.
- **Volumes:** annual maximum volumes over 1, 3, 5, 10, 15 and 30 days (flood-duration-frequency / QdF). The 15–30-day volumes matter because of 2010-type sequences.
- **Joint peak–volume:** fit a bivariate model (copula) of peak and volume to get design pairs. Use at least two pairs: "peak-critical" and "volume-critical" pairs on the same 1% and 0.1% joint-probability contours. With storage this small relative to flood volume, the volume-critical case will usually govern.
- **Extend the record:**
  - historical floods before 1978 (1969 and older notes, flood marks);
  - regional frequency analysis using other Prut and Siret gauges, adjusted for the steeper catchment at the dam;
  - optionally, continuous rainfall–runoff simulation driven by long or stochastic rainfall series.
- **Stationarity check:** Mann–Kendall/Pettitt tests on peaks and volumes. 2005, 2008, 2010 and 2020 within 15 years points to clustering or a shift; test it rather than assume it.

### 1.3 Design hydrograph shapes (replacing the single scaled 1969 hydrograph)

Scaling one shape to a peak also fixes its volume, and that is the weak point of the original design. Use a set:
1. 1969 shape, rescaled to the new peak and separately to the new volume.
2. 2008 shape (back-calculated dam inflow), scaled.
3. **2010 multi-peak sequence** (three peaks in a few weeks), scaled to the 15–30-day design volume.
4. Synthetic hydrographs from the joint peak–volume pairs, with a range of times-to-peak and durations, plus a "late steep local-inflow" variant for the ungauged steep sub-catchment.

### 1.4 Climate allowance: provisional judgement

| Design case | Purpose | Climate allowance (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| 1% flood, peak and multi-day volumes, all shapes | Downstream protection target: keep releases ≤ 550 m³/s, with ≤ 700 m³/s as a fallback, and level ≤ 98.20 | **+20% on peak and on volumes** |
| 0.1% flood, peak and volumes, all shapes | Dam safety: level ≤ 99.50, flap-gate constraint respected, freeboard to 102.50 | **No uplift as the design case** (national guidance), **+10/+20% as a mandatory stress test** |
| 2010-type multi-peak sequence at 1% volume | Tests storage recovery between peaks | +20% on total volume |
| 2008 and 2010 replays with their revised hydrographs | Validation against reality | none |

Reasons:
- National guidance gives +10–20% for 1% flows. I take the upper end because the concern is mainly volume, four large floods in 15 years suggest the record may understate current risk, and the rating curve adds unknown bias.
- The uplift must also apply to **volumes**. Guidance speaks of "flows", but a peak-only uplift misses the actual failure mode. This is an interpretation, and the regulator must agree to it.
- For 0.1%, uplift is not recommended by guidance, so I would not make it the formal design case. Since dam safety comes first, the uplifted 0.1% should still be run to show how much margin remains to the dam crest (309 mil. m³ above 99.50) and how far above 99.50 the level goes. If the uplifted 0.1% exceeds the crest or the gate structural limits, that is a dam-safety finding for the owner and regulator, not something the rules can absorb.

What would change this:
- **Lower the 1% uplift toward +10%** if the revised series shows no trend and the revised 2008 volume sits comfortably inside the new 1% volume.
- **Raise the 0.1% case formally, or change the spillway arrangement or design level,** if the revised 0.1% volume, or the 1% volume with uplift, cannot be passed within 99.50 even with releases from the start.
- **Possibly revise the downstream objective** if the 2008/2010 volumes turn out to be below 1% by volume. That would mean the 1% target is unreachable within 550 m³/s for some shapes, and the regulator needs to know that.

## 2. Outlet capacities and the state of the system (inputs to the rules)

For each outlet, build a discharge table by reservoir level and gate opening:
- the 2 power galleries (turbine flow, plus any bypass);
- the backup intake;
- the 2 central bottom outlets (9 m²) and the 2 lateral bottom outlets (15.76 m²);
- the 6 flap-gate bays (16 × 2.70 m), both raised (overflow, ≤ 0.5 m head) and lowered (crest effectively 95.50).

Illustrative only, assuming free weir flow with C ≈ 2:
- **Raised flaps:** 0.5 m of overflow across all 96 m of weir gives only about 70 m³/s.
- **Lowered flaps:** with the level at 98.20 (2.7 m head) the bays pass several hundred to about 850 m³/s; at 99.50, roughly 1500 m³/s.
- **Bottom outlets:** 49.5 m² in total probably passes several hundred m³/s at normal heads.

So the bottom outlets plus turbines are the tools for the 550–700 m³/s range. Lowering the flap gates is the dam-safety tool. These figures must be replaced with the real curves.

Also needed:
- stilling-basin and energy-dissipation limits, such as symmetric opening and minimum openings to avoid vibration and cavitation;
- gate operating times and power supply/backup;
- allowed ramp rates for downstream release changes (bank stability, public safety);
- whether Romanian and Moldovan operators share decisions, and the notification lead times.

## 3. Per-outlet operating rules for real time

### 3.1 Design principle: decide on evidence that builds up, not on a flood-size label

At warning time the flood's size is unknown. So the rule is a **staged escalation driven by observable state**. Every stage is safe if the flood turns out small, and it still leaves room to escalate if the flood turns out large. The state variables are:

1. **H**: reservoir level, and its rate of rise dH/dt over the last 12 h.
2. **Q_R**: observed flow at Rădăuți–Prut, and its rising trend.
3. **V_obs**: cumulative flood volume observed at Rădăuți since flood start, scaled to the dam with the calibrated gauge-to-dam ratio.
4. **Q_F, V_F**: 3-day forecast flow and volume, plus a 10-day areal rainfall flag. These are used with a calibrated error margin, not at face value.
5. **Projected storage need**: S_need = (V_obs still in transit + V_F upper bound) − (planned release over the same horizon), compared with the storage remaining below the stage threshold.

The operator's core test is: **"If I keep releasing at the current stage's rate, will the level exceed the next threshold within the forecast horizon, using the upper forecast band?"** If yes, move up a stage now. This is a simple projected-level check that can run every 12 h, or every 6 h in a flood, on a spreadsheet or nomogram. It handles the unknown size because the projection updates as observed volume builds up.

### 3.2 Stages (thresholds are placeholders to be set by the optimisation in Section 4)

**Stage 0: Normal.** H ≈ 90.80, no warning. Normal turbine operation.

**Stage 1: Alert and pre-release.** Trigger: a flood warning, OR Q_R ≥ ~200 m³/s and rising, OR the 3-day forecast shows the upper band ≥ a threshold Q₁, set by analysis (the current 600 m³/s is a starting point).
- Power galleries at full turbine flow.
- **Open the central bottom outlets**, then the lateral ones if needed, so that total release equals min(550 m³/s, release needed to lower the level by up to ~100 mil. m³ over ≤ 3 days).
- Never let total outflow exceed the current inflow plus the pre-release rate, and never exceed 550 m³/s.
- **Stop pre-release** and return toward 90.80 if the forecast drops below Q₁ for two consecutive updates. The refill risk to water supply and hydropower is quantified in Section 4.
- Change from current practice: do **not** hold the level constant while inflow is 200–600 m³/s if a warning is active. Release at full riverbed capacity instead.

**Stage 2: Riverbed-capacity flood control.** Trigger: inflow > outflow capacity of Stage 1, or H > 90.80 and rising.
- Hold total outflow at **550 m³/s**, the downstream objective for floods up to 1%. Release this from the start of the rise, not after the level reaches a high threshold, so that storage is spent last.
- Outlet order:
  1. turbines;
  2. central bottom outlets, which are smaller and give fine control;
  3. lateral bottom outlets;
  4. raised flap-gate overflow when the level passes 98.20 (unavoidable). Account for it in the total.
- Adjust bottom-outlet openings every 12 h (or 6 h) to keep the total at 550 m³/s as the head rises.

**Stage 3: Between-dikes capacity.** Trigger: the projected level (upper band) exceeds a threshold H₃ before the inflow peak, H₃ to be set below 98.20 by optimisation. This means the 550 m³/s strategy will not hold the flood below the 1% level.
- Raise total outflow to **700 m³/s** with bottom outlets fully open, plus turbines.
- Notify downstream authorities on both banks.

**Stage 4: Dam safety.** Trigger: H ≥ 98.70 (raised flaps overtopped by 0.5 m), OR the projected level exceeds 99.50 within the horizon.
- Lower the flap gates **bay by bay in a symmetric sequence** (for example, centre bays first) until H stops rising.
- Keep all bottom outlets and turbines open, as long as the turbines are safe.
- Outflow may exceed 700 m³/s, but **must never exceed concurrent inflow** (no man-made amplification of the natural flood).
- The aim is to keep H ≤ 99.50 and never approach 102.50.

**Recession and closing (the most important part for multi-peak floods).**
- After the inflow peak has passed (Q_R falling for two consecutive steps and dH/dt ≤ 0), **keep the stage's release rate**: 550 m³/s, or 700 m³/s if Stage 3 was used.
- Close outlets in **reverse order**:
  1. raise the flap gates once H < 98.20 − margin;
  2. close the lateral bottom outlets;
  3. close the central bottom outlets;
  4. return the turbines to normal.
- Respect the ramp-down limits.
- Continue drawdown until H is back to 90.80. The point is to recover storage for a second peak (2010).
- **Exception:** if the forecast and 10-day rainfall show no further event, the drawdown may slow to protect refill targets. For a single flood, maximum-rate drawdown is not required.
- If a new rise starts during the recession, re-enter the stage matching the current H and the projected need. Do not restart at Stage 1.

### 3.3 Operator product

Deliver the rules as:
1. a one-page decision table per stage: trigger → actions per outlet → exit condition;
2. outlet opening tables showing which gate positions give 550/700 m³/s at each reservoir level;
3. a projected-level nomogram or spreadsheet using observed and forecast volume;
4. a log template so future events can be used to re-check the rules.

## 4. Deriving and testing the rule parameters

1. **Simulation model.** A 12 h (or 6 h) reservoir mass balance with:
   - per-outlet rating curves;
   - gauge-to-dam routing plus local inflow;
   - operational constraints (gate time, ramp rates, the 0.5 m flap limit).
2. **Validation on 2008.** Replay the recorded gate operations and reproduce the recorded levels; this checks the model and the back-calculated inflow. Then run the proposed rule on 2008 and show the difference in maximum level and in downstream flow above 550/700 m³/s.
3. **Test ensemble.**
   - All design hydrographs from Section 1.3 at 1% (with uplift) and 0.1% (with and without uplift).
   - Smaller floods (10%, 20%, 50%), because the rule must not over-release or cause refill failure in frequent events.
   - Historical floods 1978–2020.
   - Multi-peak sequences.
   - Forecast errors: synthetic 3-day forecasts with error statistics estimated from the restricted forecast archive, or if access is denied, assumed error levels covering timing and magnitude, plus false alarms.
   - Varying starting levels.
4. **Objectives and metrics.**
   - (i) Maximum level: hard constraints ≤ 98.20 for 1% and ≤ 99.50 for 0.1%, with the 0.5 m flap rule.
   - (ii) Downstream: hours and volume above 550 and above 700 m³/s.
   - (iii) No outflow above inflow.
   - (iv) Level at end of flood and the probability of failing to refill (water supply/hydropower).
   - (v) Number of gate operations, as a proxy for operator burden.
5. **Parameter search.** Tune the thresholds (Q₁, H₃, forecast margin, pre-release volume and timing, drawdown targets) with grid search or multi-objective evolutionary search across the ensemble. Pick a **robust** setting that meets the hard constraints in all design cases and gives the best worst-case downstream outcome. Do not optimise for one hydrograph.
6. **Simplification check.** Turn the optimised rule into the operator table, re-simulate the table version, and confirm the loss of performance is small.
7. **Sensitivity.** Test sensitivity to:
   - the rating-curve band;
   - the gauge-to-dam volume ratio;
   - forecast skill;
   - the outlet being unavailable (one bottom outlet or one flap bay out of service, as a single-failure check).
   - A rule that only works with every outlet available is not acceptable for dam safety.

## 5. Provisional recommendations, with what would confirm or change them

1. **Design floods.**
   - 1% peak and multi-day volumes **+20%**.
   - 0.1% without uplift as the formal case, with a +10/+20% stress test.
   - An explicit **multi-peak, 15–30-day volume case**.
   - All based on the revised rating curve and the reservoir water-balance inflows.
   - *Confirm/change:* the revised frequency analysis and trend tests, and regulator acceptance of applying the uplift to volume.
2. **Release earlier.** Release at 550 m³/s from warning or first rise, not "hold level for 200–600 m³/s". Use pre-release up to about 100 mil. m³ when the upper forecast band crosses Q₁.
   - *Confirm:* simulating 2008 under the new rule keeps the level ≤ 98.20 and reduces downstream flooding.
   - *Change:* if false-alarm pre-releases cause unacceptable refill failures, raise Q₁ or cap the pre-release volume.
3. **Escalation by projected level, not by forecast peak alone.** This is how the rule handles an unknown flood size.
   - *Confirm:* the rule meets the constraints across the full ensemble including forecast errors.
4. **Fixed outlet order:**
   - opening: turbines → central bottom → lateral bottom → flap gates (dam safety only);
   - closing: the reverse order, with fast storage recovery after each peak.
   - *Change:* if hydraulic or stilling-basin limits require another order, for example lateral outlets first for energy dissipation.
5. **Possible structural or policy findings.** If no rule keeps the uplifted 1% volume-critical case within 98.20 at ≤ 700 m³/s, or the 0.1% case within 99.50, the rules cannot fix this alone. The options then are:
   - a seasonal flood-control drawdown below 90.80 (a trade-off against water supply and hydropower that the stakeholders must decide);
   - more spillway capacity;
   - raising downstream capacity;
   - formally accepting higher flood risk downstream.
   - I would report this as a finding and not assume any of these options.

## 6. Main risks and limitations

- **Rating curve:** the extrapolation above 500 m³/s may dominate the uncertainty in all design quantiles.
- **Short record:** 43 years is short for 0.1% estimates.
- **Restricted forecasts:** the forecast data may limit how well forecast error can be described. If so, report the rules with assumed error levels and a commitment to recalibrate.
- **Shared river:** the Prut is a border river. Rule changes, especially pre-release and Stage 3 releases, need agreement with the Moldovan side and coordinated warnings.
- **Assumption A1:** the flap-gate assumption must be confirmed before the Stage 4 rules are finalised.
