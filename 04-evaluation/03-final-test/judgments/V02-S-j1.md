## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V02-5yg5)
- **KQ (9):** Names the three questions that decide the study: what to assume beyond the 6-h forecast, how to define and measure acceptance, and whether three floods can tell a better method from luck. It settles Q1 concretely. It frames the critical period as the gap between peaks and turns that into a quantitative check (f, k, inter-peak gaps) as the first step. It adds a terminal-storage guide with two derivation routes, and a perfect-foresight horizon curve (P_h) to test whether horizon length is the binding limit. It does not ask whether objective weights or level parameters should change with hydrological state. The state-dependent terminal guide comes close to that question but is not the same.
- **TC (9):** I checked the arithmetic. 1/6.5 is about 15 %. The correlated-error variance 6σ²(1+5ρ) = 30σ² and the √5 understatement are correct. A 1 cm level error over 10 km² is 1e5 m³, about 28 m³/s per hour. The Monte Carlo standard error √(p(1−p)/N) is correct. The mass-balance bound and the volume-conserving smoothing of derived inflow are sound. I found no concept errors.
- **ME (9):** The steps run in order. Data and closure check on the simulator; diagnosis of current practice; inflow forecast and lead-time error verification; a correlated error model; continuation scenarios; a hierarchy of hard constraints, then objectives, then form terms. Eight schedulers each have a clear purpose, including D6 to show the cost of myopia and P/P_h as upper bounds. Validation covers leave-one-flood-out tuning, Monte Carlo runs, stress tests and acceptance criteria agreed in advance. Fallback and abort rules are part of the scheduler.
- **PR (9):** Very specific. Release increments, holding times and no rising-limb reversals. Plan churn is a metric. Pre-release is capped by refill and downstream tests. It handles gate-out-of-service, flags the upstream Yongdam releases as known-ahead inflow, and sets out the institutional approval chain, shadow operation, audit logging and downstream warnings.
- **CA (8):** It keeps the brief's numbers separate from symbols and labels the recommendation as provisional. It says plainly that three events support no probabilities. The institutional facts (K-water, the Geum River Flood Control Office) and Yongdam are hedged with "as far as I know" and marked to confirm, which is acceptable. The ρ = 0.8 value is marked as illustrative. N ≥ 2,000 Monte Carlo runs per case is somewhat heavy for the scope but reasoned.
- **RC (6):** Covers the receding-horizon MPC with first-step execution and the previous schedule implicitly in the state; penalties on change size and count; level/safety constraints; operator elicitation of practical objectives; synthetic forecast noise; perfect versus uncertain inflow; horizon variation (P_h); the three historical events; and the trade-off between change penalty and peak release. It misses step-varying (dynamic) weights or SH chosen by a search at each step, and fixed-weight MPC baselines framed as such. It also omits keeping peak outflow below the inflow peak so far and turbine-before-spillway priority. Changes between consecutive schedules appear as a metric (plan churn), not as an objective term.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, strengths:** Strong quantitative reasoning from the brief: pool-use per hour of delay, an inter-peak recovery-time check, a mass-balance upper bound on any gain, noise in derived inflow, correlation of forecast errors, and Monte Carlo resolution. The P_h horizon curve is a good diagnostic for whether the 6-h forecast is the binding limit. It identifies the upstream Yongdam releases as known-ahead inflow. Fallback and abort logic is written into the scheduler. The adoption pathway includes shadow operation and approvals.
**Answer 1, gaps:** No state-dependent or per-step adaptation of objective weights or the tolerated level. Schedule-to-schedule consistency is only measured, not penalised in the objective. No constraint keeping outflow below the observed inflow peak. Turbine-versus-spillway priority is not distinguished, so the decision is treated as a single total release.

**Answer 2, strengths:** Elicitation comes first and the operators sign off the objectives, which matches the reference's view that practical objectives come from operator experience. It penalises schedule stability against the previous issue, adds a notification lead constraint, and handles move blocking with an honest discussion of the integer versus L1 trade-off. Catchment memory as information beyond 6 h is a good point. The "price of acceptability" Pareto analysis and the two-mode emergency scheme are well judged.
**Answer 2, gaps:** The terminal free-storage target is not derived. The forecast error model has no lead-time correlation. Upstream reservoir releases are not separated out. Shadow operation is deferred. There is no per-step adaptation of weights or parameters, and no peak-outflow-below-inflow-peak or turbine-first terms.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered:**
- Weights and the highest tolerated level (SH) chosen dynamically at each time step by a search (GA warm-started from the previous step), rather than fixed or tuned once offline.
- A two-level split: a linearised MPC generates candidates, and a nonlinear evaluator scores them, including complementarity-type objectives (gate-state continuity, turbines before spillway).
- The objective that peak outflow stays below the inflow peak observed so far.
- Turbine-first use before the spillway, with total and spillway outflow as separate control inputs.
- Time-decaying weights on changes within the horizon, and MAVE-based normalisation of the objectives.
- Explicit fixed-weight MPC baselines (one favouring stability, one favouring peak reduction) to show the value of adaptive weighting.
