## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V03-dd22)

- **KQ.** Names the three questions that decide the study: the usable lead time (rain-to-inflow lag against the time a release needs to move volume), the error level above which a forecast stops beating a forecast-free rule (sigma*), and which safety limit binds plus how to combine upstream and downstream risk. It settles each with a planned measurement and a stated decision rule. It also covers initial level, local inflow between dam and control point, and successive floods. It does not treat whole-event use of flood storage ("too risky" against "too conservative") as its own question. It touches it only through the pre-release cap and the end-of-event level. It also never asks how several forecast models would be combined. Score 8.
- **TC.** The worked numbers are all correct. The correlated-sum variance nσ²(1+(n−1)ρ) = 30σ² gives about 0.91σ against 0.41σ under independence, a factor of about 2.2. The other figures also check: pre-release 300 m³/s × 6 h ≈ 6.5×10⁶ m³, flood volume 24×10⁶ m³ per 100 km², about 1 m of level rise for 4.8×10⁶ m³ over 5 km², the rule-of-three bound 3/22 ≈ 14%, Monte Carlo relative SE of about 31% at p = 0.01 and N = 1000, 1−0.95¹⁰ ≈ 0.40, and a wave travel time of 1.4–2.8 h. The concepts are also right: back-calculated inflow noise, closed-loop rolling evaluation, and common random numbers. No errors found. Score 9.
- **ME.** The steps are ordered and can be executed: data assembly, response-time measurement, baseline rule encoding, simulator check, rainfall–runoff model, two separate error types, five-policy comparison, forecast-value curve, Monte Carlo sizing, leave-one-event-out, a held-out Rananim test, and sensitivities. Verification is built in, including checking that scenario generation reproduces the error statistics by lead time and the lag-1 correlation. One gap is that the rule thresholds are "calibrated" without a stated optimizer or objective for the calibration. Score 8.
- **PR.** Very specific. It covers failure of one gate, loss of mains power, ramping limits, measurement error, the gauge-failure fallback, typhoon warnings from the meteorological service, Chinese approval channels (operating regulations, annual flood-season plan, flood-control headquarters), shadow-mode trial, downstream warning, and preventing man-made exceedance. Score 9.
- **CA.** Every number is marked illustrative. Forecast-error effects are stated as hypotheses. Agency data are listed as required inputs. Rananim operation is treated as context, not a counterfactual. One weakness is that the plan is fixed in advance as forecast-free ("forecasts not used during the event"), which somewhat prejudges the study's own second question. Score 8.
- **RC.** Covered: inflow forecasting from six-gauge rainfall, uncertainty represented as scenarios, upstream and downstream exceedance probabilities over scenarios, a deterministic-forecast benchmark and an observed/perfect-inflow benchmark (analogues of MOSEM and MOOB), mass balance with level, release and ramping constraints, and Rananim as the test. Missing: a multi-forecaster ensemble, a whole-event storage-use index, a scenario-to-point (regret or worst-case) fitness, a multi-objective Pareto search, and compromise-solution selection. Its acceptance rule (upper CI not worse than baseline) is a partial substitute for scenario aggregation. About half covered. Score 5.

### Answer 2 (V03-p307)

- **KQ.** Identifies the lag as "the single most important number," the break-even error level, the value of lead time, the asymmetry between under- and over-forecast, and storage conservation against false alarms. It also flags whether Rananim is an extrapolation. It settles the combination of upstream and downstream risk by a lexicographic order. It does not treat whole-event storage use as its own question, and the role of local inflow downstream stays conditional. Score 7.
- **TC.** No errors. Xinanjiang as the conceptual model, block-bootstrap autocorrelated errors, a chance constraint on the level limit, and the warning against white-noise errors are all sound. It gives little quantitative checking. "Never exceeds dam-safety levels in any tested realization" is a strict gate that may be unattainable for scaled check floods, though it is not wrong. Score 8.
- **ME.** Eight clear steps. The empirical hindcast error model by lead time, built in two modes (observed rain only, and perfect future rain), is a strong design. So are the P0–P4 policy ladder, the Monte Carlo factorial over magnitude, bias, timing and lead time, stress tests, the frozen-policy test on Rananim, and a four-step selection procedure. It has no Monte Carlo sample-size reasoning, and it does not say how P2's thresholds are optimized. Score 8.
- **PR.** Covers gate-change limits, the existing regulation as baseline, design-flood hydrographs from design documents, refill and irrigation costs, a fallback to the tuned rule, an emergency override, and the deliverables. There is less on equipment failure, approval pathways, downstream warning and trial operation. Score 7.
- **CA.** Assumptions are listed explicitly and labelled. It names weaker substitutes and states what would confirm or change the recommendation. The provisional recommendation (error-aware MPC with a P2 fallback) leans on a forecast system the brief says is not yet reliable. That is acceptable only because a clear switching condition is attached. Score 8.
- **RC.** Covered: a forecast model from rainfall (with ML offered as a comparison), uncertainty from an empirical error model with scenarios, an error-aware/scenario-based rolling optimization (closer to the reference's robust model), regret against perfect foresight as a robustness metric (close to the STP minimax-regret idea), exceedance probabilities upstream and downstream, deterministic-forecast and perfect-foresight benchmarks, constraints, and a Rananim test. Missing: forecaster ensembling/stacking, a whole-event storage-use index, multi-objective Pareto search, and weight-based compromise selection (it uses a lexicographic order instead, which is justified). Slightly closer to the reference than Answer 1. Score 6.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1: good.** Splits "committed inflow" from rain already fallen from future-rain forecasts and fits a separate error distribution to each. Correctly argues and quantifies that independent hourly errors understate volume error. Includes downstream local-inflow timing, with three timing cases for local and release peaks, and a man-made-exceedance metric. Gives a strong statistical argument for synthetic events (the 0/22 rule of three) and sizes the Monte Carlo runs with paired comparison. The admissibility test against the current rule is defined at the upper confidence bound. The operational plan is detailed: watch, rising limb, dam-safety switch, recession, correction and fallback. It covers the approval and shadow-trial pathway.

**Answer 1: missing or weak.** Fixes the plan's structure as forecast-free before the forecast-value experiment has run. It has no multi-model forecast or ensemble, no event-integrated storage-use measure, and no explicit multi-objective optimization or Pareto trade-off. It does not say how the rule thresholds are optimized. It has no scenario-aggregation rule such as worst-case or regret for ranking candidates, only an admissibility check.

**Answer 2: good.** Builds an empirical hindcast error model by lead time and flow level, separating no-QPF and perfect-rain modes, which bounds the value of rainfall forecasting. Its policy ladder from existing rule through tuned rule, deterministic MPC and error-aware MPC to perfect foresight mirrors the reference's MOSEM/MOOB/MOROU comparison. It uses regret against perfect foresight as a robustness metric. The lexicographic selection (dam safety, then downstream, then conservation, then simplicity) is a defensible alternative to AHP/entropy weights. It handles the extrapolation concern if Rananim is the largest event, and stress-tests with design floods and two-peak sequences.

**Answer 2: missing or weak.** Gives less quantitative feasibility reasoning: no Monte Carlo sizing, no volume or lead-time arithmetic. Downstream local inflow and routing are optional rather than examined. It is thinner on operational failure modes (gate or power loss), approval, and downstream warning. The recommended plan depends on a forecast system that does not yet exist operationally.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered.**
- Several forecast models (BP/LSTM/ELM) combined by a stacking ensemble into one deterministic forecast.
- A whole-event flood-storage use index (RRCA, target 1) to avoid "too risky" and "too conservative" operation, and its ablation.
- An explicit multi-objective formulation with upstream and downstream exceedance probabilities and fractions of hours above limits as separate objectives, solved by a many-objective evolutionary algorithm (NSGA-III).
- A scenario-to-point aggregation (minimax regret) used as fitness inside the optimizer. Answer 2 has regret only as a reported metric.
- Picking a compromise solution from a Pareto set by AHP and entropy weights. Both answers substitute ordered rules, which is acceptable but different.
