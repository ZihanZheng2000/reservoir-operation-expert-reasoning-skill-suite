# Judgment V04-S-j2

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V04-8j4n)

- **KQ.** Identifies the deciding questions well: error size and dependence for large floods (by lead time and flow class, cross-site, autocorrelation, timing), where the binding risk sits (dams vs Heishiguan, interval inflow share), and whether the record can resolve the differences. It asks whether a normal fits within each flow class, but does not frame "how much does a Gaussian assumption understate risk" as an explicit comparison. It also does not ask how to choose one plan from a trade-off set under imprecise preferences ("choose a compromise after the safety screen" only). Most questions are found; some are settled loosely.
- **TC.** Mostly sound: the hydrology, routing, chance constraints, CVaR, union versus joint probabilities, MC standard error and the 1/sqrt(2n) spread SE are correct. There are two slips. (1) Var(sum) = n sigma^2 (1 + (n-1) rho) is the equicorrelated formula, but it is applied to a "lag correlation" rho = 0.8. For AR(1)-type persistence the inflation over 24 h is roughly 7-8x, not 19.4x, so the 4.4x SD factor is overstated. (2) The error is defined as e = (f - o)/o, yet scenarios are built as forecast x (1 + e). That is inconsistent: with this definition o = f/(1+e). Neither slip changes the design, but both would propagate into numbers.
- **ME.** Complete and ordered: baseline simulator checked against 2021, consistent hindcasts (leave-one-event-out retraining), error statistics, copula or trajectory resampling, faithfulness checks on held-out events, response simulation with timing cases, risk definitions, a ΔR decomposition by source (including independent versus dependent errors), strategies A-F in closed loop, error-scale k sweep, sensitivity, mechanism attribution. The optimization formulation is less concrete (scenario MPC with "tens of branches", grid or evolutionary search), with no objectives or settings spelled out for the multi-objective search.
- **PR.** Very specific: retrained-model hindcasts, 2021 start levels and non-modelled constraints (gate availability, basin-authority instructions), Huayuankou main-stem relevance, measurement-based fallback with abort triggers, point-of-no-return logic, a prerelease cap, YRCC/Henan approval route, shadow operation, acceptance criteria, downstream warning. Hydropower and supply are treated as end-level indicators.
- **CA.** Assumptions are explicit; numbers are labelled illustrative; institutional facts are hedged ("as far as I know"). The provisional judgment is stated as a hypothesis. The only overreach is the strong numerical rhetoric resting on the overstated autocorrelation factor.
- **RC.** Covers flow-class-dependent error statistics, a normality test with non-Gaussian alternatives, temporal autocorrelation, t-copula sampling, MC scenarios (forecast plus sampled error), Muskingum routing, a noninferior set over dam and Heishiguan risk, and comparing deterministic versus error-aware operation. It misses linear mean/SD-in-flow modelling with a normalizing transform, SK/KU checks, the explicit Gaussian-vs-non-Gaussian case ladder that quantifies understated level risk, a three-objective setup (GX level, LH level, HSG peak) with percentile objectives, and fuzzy MCDM plan selection. About half or a little more is covered, partly through substitutes.

### Answer 2 (V04-5ahj)

- **KQ.** Finds the same core questions: error versus lead time and magnitude (heteroscedasticity and bias, noting underprediction of large floods), timing versus magnitude, autocorrelation, cross-basin correlation, interval flow, in-sample leakage, and the believed-versus-actual risk gap. It treats tail and form assumptions as a sensitivity rather than a headline question, and like Answer 1 it does not address how to pick a single plan from the Pareto front under decision-maker preferences. Most questions are found; some are settled loosely.
- **TC.** No substantive errors. The transform-space (NQT/Box-Cox) conditional or autoregressive error model, HUP/BJP, copulas and quantile regression are all appropriate. PIT, CRPS and leave-one-event-out validation are correct. The rolling-horizon remark (ignoring updating exaggerates the cost of uncertainty) is right. Using 1000 scenarios for risk and a reduced 20-50 set for optimization, checked against the full set, is sound practice.
- **ME.** Executable and ordered A-E, with validation of the error model, verification of the routing model, explicit scenario designs (deterministic / independent / fitted / comonotonic), risk decomposition, five strategies including a chance-constrained and a multi-objective NSGA-II formulation, held-out and 2021 replay, and a sensitivity list. It is less specific than Answer 1 on the closed-loop baseline simulator, the error-scale sweep and the fallback logic, and it gives no algorithm settings or decision-variable handling.
- **PR.** Addresses in-sample forecasts, model retraining, update frequency, unnecessary releases as a cost, end-of-event storage for supply and hydropower, and a margin rule as an operator-friendly fallback. Gate rules and administrative dispatch are named only as threats to validity. There is no approval route, shadow operation or switching triggers. This is more than generic but less specific than Answer 1.
- **CA.** Well scoped: assumptions section, a hypothesis framing, the statement that absolute probabilities should be claimed more weakly than rankings, and explicit handling of tail-calibration failure. No invented facts.
- **RC.** Covers heteroscedastic, flow-dependent and biased error modelling with a normalizing transform plus an AR error model (a close equivalent of the reference's Yeo-Johnson + AR(2)), coverage-interval validation, ~1000 MC scenarios, a reduced scenario set inside optimization (cf. UMOM with 50 scenarios), a multi-objective evolutionary search with a Pareto front over GX/LH levels versus HSG (close to the NSGA-III three-objective DMOM), Muskingum routing, and a deterministic versus uncertainty-aware comparison on the 2021 event. It misses the explicit Gaussian-vs-non-Gaussian case comparison of level risk (only a sensitivity on "error-model form"), SK/KU checks, percentile-based level objectives (it uses chance constraints and CVaR instead, a reasonable substitute), and FAHP-TOPSIS-style plan selection.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good:** Strong operational realism: a closed-loop executable baseline checked against 2021, measurement-based fallback, abort and point-of-no-return rules, a prerelease cap tied to low-side refill, the Huayuankou main-stem link, and an approval and shadow-operation route. The ΔR decomposition (per source, dependent versus independent) and the error-scale k sweep, used to find where strategies switch, answer "how much does error change risk" directly. The event-count precision argument is a good calibration device.
**Answer 1, missing or wrong:** The equicorrelated variance formula is misapplied to lag correlation, overstating volume-error inflation. The error definition and scenario construction are inconsistent. There is no concrete multi-objective formulation (objectives per reservoir level and HSG peak, decision variables, smoothing of release sequences), and no structured method for choosing a plan from the noninferior set. The Gaussian-vs-non-Gaussian effect on maximum-level risk is not isolated as a result.

**Answer 2, good:** A technically clean and well-chosen error-model menu (a transform-space AR model is essentially the reference approach), proper probabilistic verification (PIT, coverage by flow class, CRPS), explicit dependence designs including a comonotonic bound, an NSGA-II Pareto front over upstream levels versus Heishiguan, scenario reduction for optimization, a 2021 leakage control, and explicit attention to unnecessary releases as the cost of caution.
**Answer 2, missing or wrong:** Lighter on operations and regulation: no approval body, no shadow operation, no switching or abort triggers. No closed-loop baseline simulator validated against the 2021 record. No error-scale sweep. Like Answer 1, it does not isolate the Gaussian-vs-non-Gaussian effect on level risk, and it has no plan-selection method under fuzzy preferences.

**Reference essentials covered by neither:**
- An explicit ladder of residual-assumption cases (unbiased Gaussian constant through non-Gaussian, flow-group, time-varying) whose level-risk bands are compared, to show how much Gaussian assumptions understate the 95th-percentile maximum level.
- Residual mean and SD modelled as linear functions of forecast flow within flow groups, with Yeo-Johnson normalization and SK/KU acceptance thresholds.
- Release-sequence smoothing (odd-period optimization with interpolation, a search corridor) and ramping in the optimizer.
- Pareto-plan selection with FAHP-TOPSIS over reservoir- and downstream-safety indicators, plus a repair operator for end-level violations.
- An explicit statement that a level-based objective is acceptable only because the two capacities are similar (neither discusses normalizing level versus remaining storage), and that UMOM's computational cost limits real-time use.
