# V01 Judgment (S, judge 1)

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V01-hzdc)

- **KQ.** Identifies the deciding questions clearly: whether the reservoir can always return to 145 m in time (lead time x drawdown rate x downstream prerelease capacity), what "safe enough" means (a non-compensable, no-worse-than-current-practice criterion on each failure event, using upper confidence bounds), and whether one season of data can resolve the difference. Correctly separates which lead times may permit a rise (1-3 d) from those used only as early warning (4-7 d). Recognises cross-lead error correlation as a design issue. Misses the reference's point that graded, threshold-based release rules tolerate forecast error within a rule grade (error domain), and does not pose dynamic per-day selection/combination of lead times; lead-time use is fixed a priori rather than chosen from a correctness-probability criterion.
- **TC.** Arithmetic checks are correct (2.0x10^9 m3 / 2.59x10^5 s ~ 7,700 m3/s; variance of a 3-day sum with rho = 0.7 = 7.2 sigma^2, factor 1.55; Monte Carlo standard error ~0.001 at p = 0.01, N = 10^4; 50-year risk 39.5% -> 41.0%). Planning values (22.15x10^9 m3 flood storage, 180.4 m check level, ~30,000 m3/s turbine flow, 0.6 m/day drawdown, head ~75-80 m vs rated head) are plausible and flagged for replacement. The pre-release storage constraint in R1 is correctly formulated, including the "no man-made flood" cap on outflow. Static storage per metre near 145 m may be somewhat low, but it is labelled as an assumption and does not change the design. No design-changing errors.
- **ME.** Complete, ordered, and executable: simulator with checks against 2019, executable baselines (static 145, current practice, historical, perfect forecast), forecast-error analysis by lead and flow class, a copula/MVN error model with checks of scenario statistics, closed-loop candidate rules with explicit stage logic (normal / prerelease / flood control / recession / fallback / abort), Monte Carlo safety evaluation over design and historical floods, benefit evaluation restricted to rules that pass, a 2019 replay explicitly labelled in-sample, and a sensitivity sweep including "forecast accuracy at which the rule stops passing". Parameters are not tuned on the 2019 replay. Verification is well built in.
- **PR.** Very specific to TGR: Jingjiang/Shashi and Chenglingji compensation, Dongting/Qingjiang timing, Gezhouba tailwater, drawdown-rate limits for bank stability, dynamic (backwater) storage, back-calculated inflow error, sediment reason for the low flood-season level, approval chain (MWR/CWRC/headquarters, CTGC), shadow operation, reversion rules, warning of downstream provinces and the grid, and version control of the forecast system.
- **CA.** Assumptions are explicit and numbers are labelled as planning values. States clearly that 2019 cannot show safety and that it is in-sample for the error model. Hedges governance claims ("as far as I know"). The provisional ceiling (~148 m) and 1-3% energy gain are presented as hypotheses with stated conditions for changing them. Some quantitative assertions (seasonal generation 40-50 TWh, 0.2 kWh/m3) are unverified but flagged.
- **RC.** Covers: forecast error per lead time with a fitted distribution and Monte Carlo scenarios; an upper limit set by pre-release capacity over the lead time; a three-stage structure (prerelease on rising, original flood rules, recession/refill), which is close to the reference's three modules; upstream risk (reservoir level exceedance / p_miss) and downstream risk (Jingjiang flow exceedance); comparison with 3-day current practice; error-inflation sensitivity. Missing: the error-domain concept and the probability of a correct decision; dynamic choice of the lead time to trust by dropping lead times until a probability threshold is met; the upper limit expressed explicitly per lead time; a P-threshold-type risk-benefit sweep (partly replaced by the p and ceiling sweep). About half to slightly more, with reasonable substitutes.

### Answer 2 (V01-dorv)

- **KQ.** Frames the problem correctly around pre-release capacity: lead time, spare release under the downstream safe flow, forecast error (with underestimation on rising limbs as the dangerous side), and the level-volume relation. Defines an equal-or-lower-risk test against both the design rule and current practice, and leaves the risk tolerance to the authorities through a risk-benefit frontier. Defines effective lead time with a pre-set criterion. Like Answer 1, does not identify the threshold-grade error tolerance and treats lead time as a searched parameter rather than a daily, data-driven choice or combination of lead times. Drawdown-rate limits are mentioned but not analysed as a binding constraint the way Answer 1 does.
- **TC.** The feasibility check is correct (3x10^9 m3 over 3 days ~ 11,600 m3/s). The pre-release formula (minimum over prefixes of cumulative spare release, with an error quantile on forecast inflow) is sound and in one respect more careful than a simple sum, although the meaning of e_q as a multiplicative quantile of underestimation is slightly loose. 175 m as the 1000-year level and the 100-year compensation level are correct. No errors that change the design.
- **ME.** Executable and ordered: forecast characterisation, error model with bootstrapping and inflation, a well-chosen rule set (R0 design, R1 current, R2 static raised levels to isolate the value of forecasts, R3 proposed, R4 MPC, R5 perfect forecast), three-tier safety testing (2019 hindcast, design-flood Monte Carlo starting from the raised level, stress tests), and a hydropower model calibrated to 2019. Gaps relative to Answer 1: the closed-loop daily procedure and the stage logic for recession/refill, fallback and abort are thinner; there is no explicit Monte Carlo sample size or confidence-interval treatment; parameter selection is not explicitly separated from the 2019 evaluation.
- **PR.** Practical and realistic: data that must be obtained, sub-period ceilings, downstream compensation targets, Dongting/Chenglingji stages, cascade non-coordination, reduced outlet availability, backwater at Chongqing, sediment, navigation and Gezhouba, and a supervised trial. Less specific than Answer 1 on operating governance (who approves, reversion, warning) and on drawdown-rate numbers.
- **CA.** Very well calibrated: explicitly refuses to assume specific values for the safe discharge, levels and turbine capacity; proposes to check how 2019 ranks among flood years; frames the 146-150 m ceiling and the moderate energy gain as hypotheses; does not invent facts.
- **RC.** Covers: forecast error by lead time and a Monte Carlo error model; a pre-release-capacity upper limit over the lead time (close to reference step 2); pre-release triggering on rising limbs and flood rules afterwards; upstream (level exceedance, not back near 145 m) and downstream (control-point flow) risk; comparison with 3-day practice; error-inflation stress tests; searching L in {1, 2, 3, 5, 7}, which resembles the reference's options comparing lead times. Missing: error domain and correct-decision probability; daily adaptive lead-time selection/coupling; an explicit falling-stage pre-refill module; the per-lead-time upper limit as a result. About half, with reasonable substitutes.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1 - good.** Identifies that the binding limit is time (drawdown rate x reliable lead time) rather than storage, with clear arithmetic; correctly explains why correlated errors across leads widen the margin on 3-day volume; a full closed-loop rule with prerelease, flood, recession, fallback and abort logic; non-compensable acceptance on upper confidence bounds; p_miss as the key diagnostic; separates head and stored-volume effects and warns that spill reduction is limited; strong adoption path (shadow operation, reversion, version control of forecasts). **Missing or weak.** No error-domain idea (that a forecast error only matters when it moves the decision across a rule threshold); lead-time use is fixed (1-3 d permit, 4-7 d warn) rather than selected each day by a reliability criterion; no explicit per-lead-time upper-limit table; the static storage per metre and head values are rough planning figures.

**Answer 2 - good.** Clean statement of the pre-release logic and a sound cumulative-capacity formula with an explicit error quantile; a well-designed comparison set including a static-raised baseline (R2) and a perfect-forecast bound (R5) to value forecasts; design-flood Monte Carlo started from the raised level; multi-peak and 1998-type floods; sub-period ceilings; a risk-benefit frontier for the authorities; careful statement of what one season can show and the suggestion to rank 2019 among years. **Missing or weak.** Recession/pre-refill and fallback/abort logic are thin; no Monte Carlo sample-size or confidence-interval reasoning; the drawdown-rate limit is named but not quantified as a possible binding constraint; no adaptive daily lead-time choice; no error-domain concept; governance and operational trial details are thinner.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered.**
- The error domain: graded, threshold-based release rules tolerate forecast error as long as forecast and observed inflow fall in the same grade (bounds [thetaL, thetaU] around the pre-release threshold of 35,000 m3/s).
- The probability of a correct decision per lead time, and the procedure of dropping lead times from the longest until that probability reaches a threshold P_theta (0.9995), falling back to real-time inflow if none passes, i.e. coupled, adaptive lead-time selection in real time.
- A sweep over the decision-correctness threshold P_theta as the risk-benefit trade-off parameter.
- An explicit dynamic-range upper limit computed per lead time (1 to 7 days) under a fixed release cap and typical inflow.
- (Partly) an explicit falling-stage pre-refill module that keeps power output; Answer 1 touches it in its recession logic, Answer 2 barely.
