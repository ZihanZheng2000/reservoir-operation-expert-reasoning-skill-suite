## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1

- **KQ (8).** Answer 1 identifies the deciding questions:
  - how errors behave above 1000 m3/s, where the archive is thin;
  - which constraint binds;
  - routed timing at Heishiguan;
  - whether simple Gaussian, independent error understates level risk;
  - how to carry the ensemble into optimisation;
  - how to choose one plan.

  It settles most of them with a clear route. The non-Gaussian marginal and the flow-dependent bias are named, but bias gets less attention than spread.
- **TC (7).** Most of the arithmetic is correct:
  - the volume-over-window floor on the Heishiguan peak (407 Mm3 / T gives about 1180, 940 and 790 m3/s);
  - the reading of NSE as RMSE of about 0.22 sd(obs);
  - the binomial standard error of the tail percentiles;
  - the level change from a volume error, dZ = dV / A.

  One slip: the variance factor n sigma^2 (1 + (n-1) rho) assumes equal correlation between every pair of steps (exchangeable), but the answer labels rho a "lag correlation". Under AR(1) decay the factor is smaller, so the 2.5x understatement is somewhat overstated. Because the number is presented as illustrative, the slip is minor.
- **ME (8).** The method is complete and ordered:
  - a check that the archive matches the forecast system;
  - an error-model ladder (M0 to M4), validated by reproducing archive statistics and interval coverage;
  - a hourly simulator with Muskingum routing, checked against 2021 observations;
  - Monte Carlo runs with an open-loop and a closed-loop plan;
  - multi-objective optimisation over the ensemble;
  - a screen, merge and TOPSIS selection step;
  - baselines, events held out of tuning, and sensitivity tests.

  Gaps: no specific normality check on the transformed residuals, and no treatment of release smoothness beyond the ramp limits.
- **PR (8).** Concrete and realistic:
  - no Dongwan archive, so the error model is transferred from Lushi or rebuilt by hindcast;
  - the forecast lead time is set against the time Luhun's pre-release needs;
  - the pre-release has a point of no return;
  - rolling re-planning has explicit triggers and a dam-safety fallback;
  - a shadow-mode trial, warnings to downstream services, and approval through the annual dispatch plan.
- **CA (7).** Illustrative values (A = 50 km2, sigma = 0.15, rho = 0.8, 120 Mm3) are flagged as placeholders, and the answer says what evidence would overturn its judgment. Some provisional language is strong: errors "almost certainly" grow with flow, and the understatement is called "material". The remarks on agency roles are hedged.
- **RC (7).** Coverage of the reference:
  - **Covered:** flow-conditioned, heteroscedastic, non-Gaussian errors; t-copula temporal dependence; a ladder of simple-to-realistic error cases; Monte Carlo inflow scenarios; prediction-interval coverage checks; Muskingum routing; an NSGA-type three-objective model (Heishiguan peak and both maximum levels); an ensemble-based upper-percentile objective; and TOPSIS with weight-sensitivity testing (a justified equivalent of FAHP-TOPSIS).
  - **Missing:** SK/KU normality checks; AR(2) selected from the PACF; and steps against sawtooth releases or to bound the search space.

### Answer 2

- **KQ (8).** Answer 2 names the same set of deciding questions:
  - high-flow error extrapolation;
  - heteroscedasticity, bias, temporal dependence and cross-correlation;
  - deterministic versus robust formulation;
  - hard screening before multi-criteria choice.

  It adds a sharp point (A4): if the 2021 forecasts were driven by observed rainfall, they understate real-time error. It frames the problem well as a fixed-volume timing problem.
- **TC (8).** No errors found. The error-model concepts match the brief:
  - a transform to stabilise variance;
  - conditional mean and spread as functions of flow;
  - an AR term in time;
  - chance constraints and CVaR.

  The claim that the binding limits are release capacity, ramps and end level rather than total storage is reasoned correctly from the event volumes. It is less quantified than Answer 1, which leaves less room for error.
- **ME (8).** The steps are ordered and executable:
  - build and verify the system model;
  - characterise the errors;
  - propagate them;
  - isolate one feature at a time, with the same seeds;
  - compare deterministic, robust and rolling optimisation, all evaluated on the same independent ensemble;
  - cross-check the front with an LP or DP solution;
  - handle the end-level condition by penalty or repair.

  Gaps: no explicit check of prediction-interval coverage or normality after the transform, and the ensemble-size reasoning is vaguer ("about 1000, check convergence").
- **PR (7).** It flags real data limits: no Luhun archive, the observed-rainfall issue, interpolation, and the need for safety thresholds and safe flow from the agency. It requires a recorded decision trail. It is weaker than Answer 1 on operational execution: no forecast lead-time analysis, no reversion triggers, and no shadow trial or regulatory adoption path.
- **CA (8).** Assumptions A1 to A6 are explicit, and the answer refuses to give numbers it cannot support. The high-flow understatement is to be reported as a range. Claims are scoped to the single event.
- **RC (7).** Coverage of the reference:
  - **Covered, close to the reference's core:** flow-dependent conditional mean and standard deviation, a normalising transform (Box-Cox/NQT rather than Yeo-Johnson), an AR time term, bias at high flow (it anticipates underprediction of peaks), feature-by-feature comparison of simple versus realistic assumptions, Muskingum routing, NSGA with three objectives, and repair of the end-level constraint.
  - **Also matches the reference:** a deterministic model versus an ensemble-percentile model, compared on an independent ensemble (the reference's DMOM and UMOM), and TOPSIS with stakeholder weights.
  - **Missing:** prediction-interval coverage validation, SK/KU checks, release smoothing and the search corridor.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good.**
- The mass-balance analysis is useful: the event volumes are below 25% of flood storage, and the return-to-flood-limit requirement sets a floor on the Heishiguan peak equal to the event volume divided by the window T.
- The error-model ladder M0 to M4 is a clean analogue of the reference's Cases 1 to 6. It uses a t-copula across lead times, flow-class-conditioned marginals and cross-station dependence.
- Scenarios are checked against archive coverage before use.
- Open-loop and closed-loop execution are separated.
- The staggered coordination sequence is detailed, with a pre-release point of no return.
- Plan selection includes a resolution check that merges plans differing by less than Monte Carlo noise.
- Baselines bound the result: perfect forecast and a no-forecast rule.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong.**
- The correlation-inflation formula assumes exchangeable correlation, which overstates the factor for decaying lag correlation.
- Bias, such as overprediction at low flows and underprediction of floods, is mentioned only in passing.
- There is no normality diagnostic.
- Answer 1 does not anticipate sawtooth release patterns on the 6-hour step.

**Answer 2, good.**
- The error model is closest to the reference: the mean and spread are conditional on flow, a variance-stabilising transform is applied, and an AR term captures persistence.
- It expects systematic underestimation of peaks by a data-driven model trained mostly on low flows, which the reference confirms.
- It removes one simplification at a time to show which one drives the understatement.
- It compares deterministic, robust and rolling formulations on an independent ensemble, which matches the reference's DMOM/UMOM validation.
- The observed-rainfall caveat on the forecast is insightful.
- It cross-checks the evolutionary front with an LP/DP solution.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong.**
- It does not check prediction-interval coverage on historical floods.
- It gives no normality test and no reasoning about ensemble size for tail percentiles.
- It covers less on lead time and on executing the pre-release in real time.
- Its practical adoption path is thinner.

**Reference steps neither answer covered.**
- Skewness and kurtosis thresholds (SK within [-1, 1], KU within [1, 5]) to check that the transformed residuals are Gaussian.
- Choosing the AR order (AR(2)) from the PACF, and fitting the parameters by genetic algorithm.
- Optimising only odd-period releases and interpolating the even periods to suppress sawtooth releases.
- A search corridor to bound the decision space.
- An explicit fuzzy treatment of decision-maker preferences (FAHP) with a defined set of about 9 indicators.
- Explicit acknowledgment that an ensemble-based model costs several times more computation, which limits real-time use.
