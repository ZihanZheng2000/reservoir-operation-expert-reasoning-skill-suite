## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (kcyw)

- **KQ.** It sets out the three questions that decide the study. Q1 asks whether a 1% flood with uplift can be held within 550 m³/s at all, even with perfect operation. Q2 asks how much the rating extrapolation changes the design volume. Q3 asks what operators can observe early enough, and which state variable safely triggers escalation. It settles the climate allowance explicitly: +20% on peak and volume for the 1% flood, none for the 0.1% design case with +10/+20% as a stress test. It flags applying the uplift to volume as an interpretation the authority must confirm. It treats volume and multi-peak sequences as governing and makes the rule independent of flood size through a state-driven release chart. Every deciding question is found and given a defensible provisional answer.
- **TC.** I checked the arithmetic and it is correct:
  - Storage zones: 325 / 225 / 115 / 309 mil. m³.
  - Excess volume of a triangular hydrograph above a constant release, V(1 − q/Qp)²: 2008 gives ≈764 mil. m³ above 550 m³/s.
  - Pre-release of 100 mil. m³ over 3 days ≈ 386 m³/s.
  - Mean 2010 inflow over 21 days ≈ 910 m³/s.
  - 0.1% constant outflow ≈ 1150 m³/s.
  - Weir flow ≈ 850 / 1540 / 70 m³/s.
  - Monte Carlo sample size N ~10⁵ for p = 0.001 at ±10%.
  - Variance inflation for equicorrelated forecast errors: 24σ².

  The outlet head (25 m) and weir coefficient are assumptions, and the answer labels them as such. The triangular-shape approximation is stated. The constant-correlation error model is a simplification, not an error. No slip changes the design.
- **ME.** The method is complete and in order:
  1. Repair the rating with a hydraulic model, using alternative ratings to bracket the uncertainty.
  2. Reconstruct 2008 by water balance as an independent check.
  3. Transfer gauge → dam with a stated sensitivity range.
  4. Run multi-duration frequency analysis of volume and peak, with a copula, historical information and trend tests.
  5. Build design hydrographs from several shapes and find the critical duration.
  6. Generate a synthetic ensemble for risk.
  7. Build a simulator and validate it on 2008.
  8. Encode the current rule as a baseline.
  9. Run a constrained parameter search, keeping a non-dominated set.
  10. Test closed-loop, under three forecast-information conditions, leave-one-event-out, and with outlet-failure stress cases.

  The per-outlet mode table gives entry and exit conditions. Gaps: it does not test frequent floods (10–20%), and it does not check that a simplified operator table still performs.
- **PR.** It is specific and realistic:
  - Local inflow from the 24% steep ungauged area, with a water-balance inflow estimate and its accuracy.
  - A travel-time lead of 15–45 h, compared with the 3-day pre-release window.
  - The cost of a false alarm as refill time (≈23 days), against the benefit as ≈1.2 m of level.
  - Hysteresis against gate hunting and ramp limits.
  - Transboundary approval and warnings, shadow-mode trial, reversion triggers, and after-event review.
- **CA.** Assumptions are explicit and tied to what would change them: the outlet ratings, the meaning of the 0.5 m flap limit, the 2008 shape and starting level, and the rating outcome. It treats "the rule failed in 2008" as unproven. The institutional names are hedged ("as far as I know … please confirm"). It adds a check/safety-evaluation flood "if the dam's class requires one", which goes slightly beyond the brief but is hedged.
- **RC.** It covers:
  - rating re-evaluation with a hydraulic model;
  - the gauge–dam volume mismatch;
  - volume as the governing quantity;
  - peak and volume frequency with an uncertainty band;
  - peak-critical vs volume-critical cases;
  - several shapes with a critical-duration search;
  - optimisation of per-outlet thresholds against penalised objectives (gate manoeuvres, release limits);
  - an adaptive state- and forecast-based rule with the 10-day rainfall input;
  - validation by 2008 water balance;
  - comparison with the current and 2008 operator rules;
  - a 2010 multi-peak test;
  - outlet-failure and climate stress tests.

  Its Q1 feasibility test anticipates the reference's finding that even optimised rules exceed 98.20 m and 550 m³/s for 2008. Missing: the 10%/5% rule sets, explicit shape selection by compactness, and volume sub-classes as decision branches. Its (Z, I) chart is a justified equivalent for the last.

### Answer 2 (ww41)

- **KQ.** It identifies:
  - volume and release timing as governing;
  - rating repair and water-balance inflow;
  - peak-critical vs volume-critical design pairs;
  - multi-peak storage recovery;
  - the climate allowance (same choice as Answer 1, with the volume interpretation flagged);
  - escalation by projected level for an unknown flood size.

  It does not pose the feasibility question (can the 1% target be met at all?) as decisive. Its quick 2008 balance instead answers it loosely and probably in the wrong direction.
- **TC.** The weir and storage numbers are correct. The opening volume balance is flawed: 550 storage + 475 released over 10 days + 100 pre-release ≈ 1125 ≥ 1100, so it concludes "roughly manageable". This assumes inflow stays at or above 550 m³/s for 10 days, so that the full release is available, while ignoring that only the volume above the release cap must be stored. For a 3300 m³/s peak carrying 1100 mil. m³, that excess is ≈700–800 mil. m³, which exceeds 650. The answer labels the conclusion a hypothesis, but it shapes the diagnosis: it blames the current trigger, where the outcome may have been forced by the flood. There are other minor looseness points: "raised flap-gate overflow when level passes 98.20 (unavoidable)" sits inside a 550 m³/s stage, and the S_need formula is informal. Otherwise there are no conceptual errors.
- **ME.** The method is executable and well ordered:
  1. Rating repair with an uncertainty band.
  2. Water-balance inflows.
  3. Routing calibrated against back-calculated dam inflows, plus local inflow.
  4. Homogenisation.
  5. Multi-duration QdF, copula, regional and historical extension, and trend tests.
  6. Several shapes, including a steep local-inflow variant.
  7. A simulator validated on 2008.
  8. A test ensemble that includes frequent floods (10–50%) and forecast errors.
  9. Robust multi-objective search.
  10. A simplification check of the operator table.
  11. Sensitivity and single-failure tests.

  Gaps: there is no explicit critical-shape or critical-duration selection, no encoding of the current rule as a baseline beyond replaying 2008, and less detail on how the projected-level test is computed offline.
- **PR.** It covers stilling-basin and vibration limits, gate times, power backup, ramp rates, bilateral notification, the operator products (decision table, opening tables, nomogram, log), refill risk, and the restricted-forecast fallback. All are realistic, though somewhat less specific than Answer 1 on lead time and local-inflow timing.
- **CA.** Assumptions A1–A4 are explicit. It refuses to invent outlet curves and reports structural or policy options as findings, not decisions. The 2008 "manageable" claim is hedged as a hypothesis, but it rests on a weak balance.
- **RC.** Its coverage is similar to Answer 1:
  - hydraulic rating re-evaluation and the volume mismatch;
  - frequency analysis of peak and volume with uncertainty;
  - peak- and volume-critical pairs;
  - several shapes, including 2010;
  - threshold optimisation per stage with gate-operation and smoothness (ramp) objectives;
  - an adaptive forecast and observed-volume rule (its V_obs is close to the reference's volume sub-classes);
  - 10-day rainfall;
  - 2008 validation and a 2010 multi-peak test;
  - frequent-flood (10%) rules;
  - outlet failure.

  Missing: shape selection by compactness, an explicit comparison against the original design rule and the current rule, and a 2010 water-balance check.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good:**
- The ideal-operation excess-volume test, V(1 − q/Qp)², gives a feasibility test for the 1% riverbed objective before any tuning. Its conclusion, that 2008 may have been forced even with perfect operation, is consistent with what the reference later found (98.52 m and 890 m³/s for 2008 under optimised rules).
- It frames the pre-release "point of no return" as the moment inflow reaches the cap.
- It proposes frequency-analysing the annual maximum excess volume above 550 and 700 m³/s directly.
- Its state-based (Z, I, trend) release chart is computed offline from the design-set envelope, so operators can act without knowing the flood size.
- It gives fixed opening and closing order with hysteresis.
- It sets up the current rule as a baseline and tests leave-one-event-out.
- It accounts for local inflow from the steep 24% residual catchment.

**Answer 1, missing:**
- No rule sets for frequent floods (10%/5%), so it cannot show that small floods stay within the riverbed.
- No check that the simplified operator table still performs.
- It does not explicitly compare against the original 1969-shape design rule.

**Answer 2, good:**
- It treats the water-balance dam inflow as rating-independent.
- It treats the gauge–dam mismatch as signal, not noise.
- Its frequency analysis is regional and historical, with QdF over 1–30 days.
- Its escalation uses projected level with the upper forecast band and cumulative observed volume (V_obs), which comes close to the reference's volume sub-classes.
- Frequent floods are included in the test ensemble.
- It checks the simplified operator table against the optimised rule.
- It covers stilling-basin and symmetric-opening constraints.
- It reports structural or policy fallbacks as findings.

**Answer 2, wrong or missing:**
- The opening 2008 mass balance ignores that only the volume above the release cap must be stored. It suggests 2008 was manageable within 98.20 m at 550 m³/s with early release, which a correct excess-volume estimate (and the reference outcome) contradicts. This biases the diagnosis toward trigger timing.
- No explicit search for the critical shape or duration.
- No baseline comparison against the current and original rules.

**Reference elements neither answer covered:**
- Choosing the design shape by the compactness coefficient γ = V/(D(Qmax − Qb)), with duration derived from γ.
- Grouping outlets into equivalence classes and optimising open and close level pairs (H′, H″) per class, as in the reference. Both answers optimise generic stage thresholds instead.
- An explicit forecast-horizon limitation: volume classes need 5–10 days, against a 3-day forecast. Both answers touch on lead time but do not state this.
- Water-balance closure on 2010 as well as 2008. Answer 1 only lists 2010 among the historical events.
