### Answer 1 (V04-3ln7)

- **KQ.** Identifies the deciding questions: the shape of forecast error (flow-dependence, bias, temporal persistence, timing), the sparse high-flow tail, how to propagate uncertainty into the joint optimisation, whether simple Gaussian/i.i.d. assumptions understate tail level risk, and how to pick one plan. Adds useful questions the reference does not stress: cross-river error correlation, whether the "2021 precipitation" basis means perfect-rainfall forecasts, missing Luhun error archive. The binding-constraint question (storage vs. capacity/ramp/end level) is raised and settled sensibly. Selection under fuzzy preferences is handled through elicited weights and sensitivity rather than an explicit fuzzy method, which is acceptable.
- **TC.** No substantive errors. The fixed-volume framing (start and end at FLL means operation only redistributes timing) is correct; NSE critique is correct; NSGA-III with three objectives (GX level, LH level, HSG peak), Muskingum routing, penalty/repair for end level all sound. Statement that entropy/CRITIC weights measure variability not importance is correct. Minor looseness: transformation choice (Box-Cox needs positive data; residuals need Yeo-Johnson or NQT on flows) is not spelled out.
- **ME.** Ordered and executable: build and verify simulator, characterise errors, propagate ensembles (open and closed loop), ladder of error assumptions with same seeds and one-feature-at-a-time isolation, deterministic vs. robust (95th percentile/CVaR, chance constraints) vs. rolling optimisation evaluated on an independent ensemble, then screening and ranking. Gaps: no explicit validation of the error model itself (prediction-interval coverage on historical floods, normality/SK-KU checks after transform); no handling of release oscillation (sawtooth) in the evolutionary search.
- **PR.** Specific: drawdown limit, ramp, capacity at level, travel-time dependence of the 20 h offset, second-peak risk on recession, local inflow uncontrollable, need for agency thresholds. Less concrete on operational procedures (triggers, fallback rules, institutional approval) than Answer 2.
- **CA.** Very well scoped. Assumptions A1–A6 are explicit, high-flow error explicitly labelled as extrapolated, refuses to give numbers it cannot support, provisional judgments with stated falsifiers.
- **RC.** Covers flow-dependent bias and spread, temporal (AR) dependence, transform to Gaussian, a ladder of error cases from simple Gaussian to full conditional model, ~1000 Monte Carlo scenarios, NSGA-III with the same three objectives and constraints, Muskingum, repair of end-level, a 95th-percentile robust model compared with the deterministic one on an independent ensemble, TOPSIS ranking. Missing: PI coverage validation, SK/KU checks, odd/even release interpolation, search corridor, fuzzy-AHP style weighting.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1 – good.** Correct fixed-volume framing; recognises that NSE > 0.95 hides peak and volume error; isolates which simplification (heteroscedasticity, autocorrelation, bias, cross-correlation) drives understatement; three-objective NSGA-III matching the reference; deterministic vs. robust (95th percentile) vs. rolling formulations evaluated on an independent ensemble; clear coordination logic (stagger Guxian before Luhun, Luhun pre-release in the 3 m margin, staggered recession to avoid a second peak); hard safety screen before weighting.
**Answer 1 – missing or weak.** No validation of the probabilistic forecast itself (interval coverage on historical floods, skewness/kurtosis after transformation); no fix for oscillating release sequences; little operational procedure (triggers, fallback, approval path); transformation choice not matched to signed residuals.

**Answer 2 – good.** Mass-balance quantification of what can bind; volume-floor argument tying the end-level requirement and window length T to the achievable Heishiguan peak; correct variance-inflation demonstration of why serial correlation drives volume and level error; matched-hindcast requirement for the archive; M0–M4 ladder with distribution testing and coverage checks; tail-percentile Monte Carlo sizing; CVaR objectives; rolling horizon with explicit triggers and Luhun pre-release point of no return; baselines and out-of-sample events; resolution check merging options within noise; shadow-mode adoption.
**Answer 2 – missing or weak.** Some provisional magnitudes lean on illustrative parameters; no sawtooth/odd-period handling; computational burden of scenario-based optimisation with 10,000 scenarios not discussed; does not note that a level objective ignores remaining storage.

**Reference elements neither answer covered.** Optimising only odd-period releases and interpolating even periods to suppress sawtooth patterns; a search corridor bounding the decision space; GA fitting of linear mean/sd functions within each flow group (both use generic conditional models instead); explicit SK/KU acceptance thresholds; repair operator applied after ranking; the computational cost limitation of the uncertainty-based model for real-time use; the observation that the 3 m drawdown limit at Guxian leaves little optimisation room; the caveat that a level objective ignores remaining flood storage (Answer 1 only touches it by offering "use of flood storage" as an alternative objective).
