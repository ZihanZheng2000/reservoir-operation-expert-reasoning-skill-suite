## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### KQ (key questions)
- **Answer 1:** It finds the deciding questions: the reliability of the extrapolated rating above 500 m³/s, the gauge/dam volume mismatch, volume over peak (storage is small next to flood volume), shapes including multi-peak, the climate allowance on volume, and real-time rules when flood size is unknown. It settles them with a provisional table and "what would change this" conditions. Its framing of whether 2008 was manageable rests on loose arithmetic (see TC), so one of the deciding questions is settled wrongly, although it is labelled a hypothesis.
- **Answer 2:** It finds the same set and states them as three explicit deciding questions: whether the 1% riverbed objective is feasible at all, how much the rating changes the design volume, and which observable state triggers each tier. It settles them more sharply. The excess-volume-above-cap test, the critical duration and the "point of no return" for pre-release go straight to what decides the study. It also raises separating an operating failure from a forced outcome in 2008.

### TC (technical correctness)
- **Answer 1:** The storage arithmetic (550/665 mil. m³, 47.5 and 60 mil. m³/day) and the weir estimates are right. The main slip is the 2008 balance. It adds 10 days × 550 m³/s of release to the storage and the pre-release and concludes that 2008 was "roughly manageable within the riverbed". This ignores hydrograph shape: you cannot release 550 m³/s on days when inflow is below 550 without drawing down, and the peak days need storage for inflow above the cap. The conclusion is probably wrong; the reference's optimized 2008 run still needed about 890 m³/s. It is flagged as a hypothesis, so the slip does not change the design. Otherwise it has no real errors.
- **Answer 2:** It uses a correct triangular excess-volume formula V(1 − q/Qp)² and gets 2008 excess > 650 mil. m³. This is consistent with the published outcome that even good operation exceeds the riverbed in 2008. The pre-release rate (386 m³/s net), the probability arithmetic, the Monte Carlo SE and the correlated-error variance are correct. The bottom-outlet head of 25 m and the travel-time range are assumptions and are labelled as such. Only small issues remain: the triangular-shape idealisation and an illustrative 0.1% volume.

### ME (method)
- **Answer 1:** The order is complete: rating repair, a water-balance inflow, gauge-to-dam routing, FFA of peak, QdF volumes and a copula, a shape set, a staged rule, a simulator, 2008 replay validation, an ensemble with forecast errors, robust parameter search, a simplification check and single-outlet-failure sensitivity. It is executable. The rule parameterisation (the projected-level test) is less explicitly tied to per-outlet open/close levels.
- **Answer 2:** It has all of the above, plus a baseline encoding of the current rule, a closed-loop test, information conditions (no, real and perfect forecast), leave-one-event-out, a synthetic ensemble for exceedance probabilities, a critical duration, a quantile ordering check, and a Z–I release chart computed offline. This method is more complete and has stronger verification.

### PR (practicality)
- **Answer 1:** It names concrete constraints: the flap-gate 0.5 m limit, stilling-basin and symmetric opening, ramp rates, transboundary coordination, restricted forecasts, and the operator products (decision table, opening tables, nomogram, log).
- **Answer 2:** It covers the same points and adds a minimum interval between gate steps (hysteresis), downstream local inflow at the control point, a forecast fallback to state-only triggers, false-alarm refill costs, shadow-mode trial and reversion triggers, and warning lead times. These are realistic and specific.

### CA (calibration)
- **Answer 1:** Assumptions A1–A4 are explicit, illustrative numbers are flagged, and the climate-on-volume interpretation is put to the regulator. It is well scoped. It slightly overstates its 2008 diagnosis.
- **Answer 2:** Its assumptions are explicit and it says plainly that the gauge-to-dam transfer rests on one event. The institutional names (CONSIB, Apele Române and so on) go beyond the brief, but they are hedged ("as far as I know, please confirm"). The provisional conclusions are well hedged.

### RC (reference coverage)
- **Answer 1:** It covers the rating re-evaluation and a hydraulic routing model of the reach, univariate and bivariate frequency with uncertainty, volume thresholds, a multiple-shape set including 2008 and 2010, a simulation-optimization of thresholds, rules per AEP with and without CC, an adaptive framework with forecast-based escalation, a 10-day rainfall flag, 2008 water-balance validation, a multi-peak test and outlet failure. It does not baseline against the original design rules or the 2008 operator rules as a benchmark, and it has no compactness or critical-shape criterion.
- **Answer 2:** It has similar coverage, plus an explicit baseline against the current/2008 operator rule, a critical-duration selection that stands in reasonably for the compactness criterion, and P10 in the rule. It also does not compare against the original 1969-shape design rules.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good:**
- It frames the problem correctly as volume-driven.
- It rebuilds the rating with a hydraulic model and an uncertainty band, and uses a reservoir water balance as an independent volume.
- Its climate table applies +20% to the 1% case and puts the 0.1% uplift only into a stress test.
- It has a multi-peak 15–30-day case.
- The staged escalation runs on a projected-level test, so it works without knowing flood size.
- The opening order is turbines → central bottom → lateral bottom → flaps, with reverse closing and storage recovery.
- It does a robust parameter search and checks the simplified table version of the rule.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong:**
- Its 2008 "manageable within riverbed" arithmetic is flawed. It ignores shape and the inflow below the cap.
- It does not formally test whether the 1% riverbed objective is feasible at all.
- The rules are not explicitly parameterised as per-outlet open and close levels.
- It does not benchmark against the original design-phase rule.
- It does not discuss the 3-day forecast horizon versus the longer horizon that volume classification needs.

**Answer 2, good:**
- It has an explicit feasibility test (excess volume above 550/700 m³/s) and proposes frequency analysis of the annual excess volume.
- It identifies the pre-release point of no return.
- Its 2010 drawdown-time arithmetic shows that storage recovery between peaks is hardly possible.
- It has a per-mode, per-outlet table with entry and exit conditions and hysteresis.
- The release chart in level and inflow is computed offline from the design set. This is a sound equivalent of the reference's adaptive decision levels.
- It encodes a baseline current rule and a perfect-forecast upper bound, which mirrors the reference's comparison of adaptive operation with perfect foresight.
- It has extensive stress tests: a jammed flap and a lost outlet (as in 2008), the 2010 sequence followed by a 1% flood, and both 2008 ratings.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong:**
- The illustrative outlet head and the 0.1% volume are speculative, though flagged.
- The institutional detail goes beyond the brief.
- It has no explicit comparison with the original 1969-shape design rules.
- The rules are framed as release targets via the chart rather than explicit per-outlet open and close levels (H′/H″). They are equivalent but less directly operator-native.

**Reference elements neither answer covered:**
- The compactness coefficient γ = V/(D(Qmax − Qb)) as the criterion for choosing the critical observed shape, with duration derived from it. Answer 2's critical duration is a partial substitute.
- An explicit comparison of the optimized rules against the original design-phase rules on the 1969-shape 0.1% flood.
- An optimization objective that smooths successive releases and meets an end-of-flood volume target, with open and close levels per outlet class as the decision variables.
- Volume sub-classes tied to decision levels (around 91.00 and 95.50 MASL), and the explicit limitation that the 3-day forecast horizon is shorter than the 5–10 days needed to classify volume.
- The epistemic spread across distributions reported as an uncertainty band (both mention confidence intervals, not the spread across distributions).
