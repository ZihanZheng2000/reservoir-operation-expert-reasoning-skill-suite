## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good.** It reads the operators' hourly practice correctly as MPC. It treats the inflow-so-far cap as a moving soft bound below dam safety. It insists on a causal inflow filter in hindcasts. It handles revision penalties for the hours shared between schedules and minimum gate-open time with an operator-defined end-of-flood condition. Its state-dependent terminal target addresses pre-release between peaks. Its horizon tests (6/12/24 h) and NHWL start match the reference. Its forecast cases (perfect, synthetic, naive, archived) are well designed, and the stress-test labelling is honest.

**Answer 1, missing or weak.** It has no quantitative check of storage versus event volume or of fill rates. It does not adapt weights or tolerances during the event and test that against fixed settings. It has no objective normalisation. Deployment and approval (shadow trial, fallback modes) get little attention.

**Answer 2, good.** Its back-of-envelope storage analysis shows that the reservoir can only shift and smooth releases, so free band at peak arrival is decisive. It quantifies inflow noise relative to turbine capacity. Its correlated forecast-error argument (volume error about 2.2× an independence assumption) is a real insight for band filling. It ties gate-opening timing to the 6-h window. It gives an explicit between-peak band rule based on the largest peak so far. Its closed-loop protocol, five comparators, leave-one-event-out tuning, freeze rule, fallbacks and shadow-mode adoption plan are all strong.

**Answer 2, missing or weak.** The 48–72 h horizon depends heavily on synthetic scenario continuations with no forecast information. It ranks peak outflow below stability and terminal storage, even though peak reduction is the sponsor's first goal. Its dismissal of evolutionary/GA approaches is not tested. Like Answer 1, it does not adapt weights during the event or compare against fixed weights.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered:**
- re-selecting objective weights and the highest tolerated level (SH) at each time step (GA, warm-started), compared with fixed-weight MPC baselines tuned for stability versus peak reduction;
- splitting the problem into a linearised MPC (slack variables, time-decaying change weights) plus a nonlinear evaluator that holds the complementarity-type objectives and the nonlinear height–volume curve;
- normalising objectives (MAVE-type multipliers);
- an explicit target level band [SL, SU] alongside SH;
- sensitivity to the evaluator weight on schedule changes, and tracking how the best weights shift over time (penalty heatmaps).
