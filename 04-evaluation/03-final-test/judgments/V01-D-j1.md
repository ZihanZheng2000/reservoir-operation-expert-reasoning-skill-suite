### KQ: key questions

### TC: technical correctness
- **Answer 1 (8).** I checked the arithmetic. 1,000 m³/s for one day is 0.864 × 10⁸ m³, about 0.154 m at 5.6 × 10⁸ m³/m, which is a reasonable value for TGR near 145–155 m. The quantile inflows match 25,500 × (1 + 1.28σ). The cumulative levels (1.1 / 2.2 / 3.1 / 3.8 / 4.5 / 4.9 / 5.2 m) and the Q_loc = 3,000 column are consistent. The correlated-sum variance nσ²(1 + (n − 1)ρ) is correct, and the example 7.2σ² → ×1.55, 6.5 × 10⁸ m³ ≈ 1.2 m is right. It reasonably infers that the 3-day mass balance roughly reproduces 148 m. The head and power sensitivity (about 80 m head, 1–2 m → 1.5–2.5%) is plausible. There is one minor slip: the column labelled "σ" is effectively used as the upper-quantile spread. Summing marginal per-day quantiles is also a simplification, but the answer explicitly says the cumulative error must replace it.
- **Answer 2 (7).** The ΔV formula and the trust test are sound. The order-of-magnitude check (about 8 × 10⁸ m³/day, about 25 × 10⁸ m³ in 3 days) is correct. However, "a few metres, consistent with 145–148" is loose: about 25 × 10⁸ m³ at about 5.6 × 10⁸ m³/m is about 4.5 m. The statement that Z_up(t, L, α) "is not necessarily increasing in L" is a conceptual slip. Under its own formula, only positive-bracket days are added and each day's quantile does not depend on L, so ΔV is non-decreasing in L. Longer leads are limited by the trust test, not by the bound itself. The trust test is also somewhat circular: Z_up is built from the α quantile and then tested at α. This is not wrong, but it is under-specified.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1: good.** The quantified mass-balance table shows that the 3-day case reproduces about 148 m, with diminishing returns beyond 4–5 days and a strong dependence on local inflow below the dam. It shows that correlated cumulative error matters and quantifies it. The decision algorithm is explicit, with a point-of-no-return rule and fallbacks. Its risk definitions add the excess volume at arrival and the requirement that a pre-release never makes flow worse than flow without the reservoir. It reasons about Monte Carlo sample size and runs a comparison against a perfect-forecast bound. The adoption path fits the Chinese regulatory setting, and the hydropower gain is framed realistically as coming from head.
**Answer 1: missing or wrong.** The "σ" column is effectively a quantile spread. It sums per-day marginal quantiles before admitting that the cumulative error is the correct basis. It does not frame graded-rule tolerance as an error domain (the decision is unchanged when forecast and observation fall in the same rule grade). There is no relaxed-accuracy option. Its provisional ceiling (149–150 m) is lower than what a deterministic capacity bound would give, because of the quantile choice. This is acceptable, but the answer could make clearer that the quantile and the trust test double-count conservatism.

**Answer 2: good.** It resamples joint error vectors across leads. It conditions errors on magnitude, limb and recent skill. It verifies threshold forecasts with POD/FAR and separates misses (safety) from false alarms (power). It includes a PIT/coverage calibration check of the error model. It replays without look-ahead and is explicit about the weak evidence from a 53-day season. It runs design-flood routing with and without pre-release. Its power accounting separates head from spill.
**Answer 2: missing or wrong.** It claims, incorrectly, that Z_up may not increase with L. Its Z_up arithmetic is loose. The trust-test logic is circular and under-specified. It has no comparison options for fixed longer leads or inflated or relaxed error, no perfect-forecast bound, no Monte Carlo sample size, no fallback or abort rules, and only a thin regulatory and adoption plan.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered.**
- An explicit error domain [θL, θU] built from the gap between the forecast peak and the release-rule threshold. Neither answer uses the idea that graded rules make a decision correct whenever forecast and observation fall in the same grade.
- The "probability of a correct decision" criterion with a threshold Pθ, applied by dropping lead times from the longest down, with real-time inflow as the fallback when no lead passes. Both use risk-tested trust with a max over trusted leads, which is an equivalent route, but not this formulation.
- An explicit relaxed-accuracy option (Option 3). Only Answer 1 has an error-inflation stress test.
- A per-lead goodness-of-fit test for the error distribution (chi-square). Both instead propose empirical or joint models, which is justified.
