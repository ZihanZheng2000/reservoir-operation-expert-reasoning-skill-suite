# Judgment V04-S-j1

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V04-5ahj)

- **KQ (7).** Identifies the core questions: error dependence on flow magnitude (heteroscedasticity, bias), lead time, temporal autocorrelation, cross-basin correlation, and how to carry uncertainty into joint optimization. Frames "how much does error change risk" as believed-versus-ensemble risk under the same plan, which is a sound operationalization. It does not set up as an explicit question how much a Gaussian/constant-parameter error assumption understates level risk; the issue appears only as "alternative tail assumptions" in sensitivity. It also does not ask how one plan is chosen from the Pareto set.
- **TC (8).** No substantive errors. The error-model candidates (NQT/Box-Cox plus AR, HUP/BJP, copula, quantile regression) are appropriate. Leave-one-event-out validation, PIT/CRPS and coverage are correct. The point that independent hourly sampling understates volume risk is correct. Using design/check level as the dam-safety threshold and the flood-control high level as the operating threshold is reasonable.
- **ME (8).** Ordered and executable: error model, joint scenarios (about 1000, reduced to 20-50 for optimization and checked back on the full set), system routing (Muskingum, verified on observed releases), risk metrics, strategies, held-out evaluation, then 2021 stress test and sensitivity. Verification is built in at each step. Gaps: the stochastic objective and constraints are listed as options, not fixed; no rule for picking an operating plan from the Pareto front; release-smoothness handling is only named.
- **PR (7).** Covers intervening-area flow, in-sample forecast leakage, forecast-model retraining, rolling-horizon replanning, unnecessary-release cost, end storage for supply/hydropower, and gate/administrative rules as threats. Mostly specific, but regulation and adoption are mentioned only generically.
- **CA (8).** Assumptions are stated explicitly, no reservoir values are invented, and expectations are labelled as hypotheses with stated conditions under which they would fail. Claims about absolute probabilities are deliberately weaker than claims about rankings.
- **RC (7).** Covers flow-dependent, autocorrelated residuals and transformation to normality; coverage-based validation of intervals; Monte Carlo scenarios added to the deterministic forecast; a multi-objective (NSGA-type) model over GX level, LH level and HSG peak with Muskingum routing; and an uncertainty-based optimization (percentile/CVaR/chance constraints) compared with the deterministic one. Missing: the controlled comparison of error-distribution assumptions as the main way to quantify understated risk, fuzzy MCDM plan selection, and the sawtooth/end-level repair details.

### Answer 2 (V04-8j4n)

- **KQ (8).** States three explicit deciding questions: error size and dependence in large floods (by lead time, flow class, cross-site, autocorrelation, timing); where the binding risk sits and how much of it is uncontrolled interval inflow; and whether the record can resolve the claimed differences. Also tests normality per flow class with skewed/mixture alternatives and brackets that assumption, which comes closer to the reference's Gaussian-understatement question. Pareto-plan selection is handled only loosely ("compromise after safety screen").
- **TC (7).** Mostly sound: union/joint probability bounds, Monte Carlo SE, travel time, the two-site variance 3σ², and the CVaR and chance-constraint formulations. Two slips. (i) Var(sum) = nσ²(1+(n−1)ρ) is the equicorrelated formula but is presented for "lag correlation ρ". For AR(1) errors with ρ = 0.8 and n = 24 the factor is about 7, not 19.4, so the illustration overstates the inflation (the qualitative point stands). (ii) Relative error is defined as e = (f − o)/o, but scenarios are generated as forecast × (1 + e), which inverts the relation. Neither changes the design, but both would need correcting.
- **ME (8).** Complete and ordered: an executable baseline simulator checked against the 2021 record, consistent hindcasts, error statistics, copula/trajectory-resampling scenarios with faithfulness checks, response simulation with interval-peak timing cases, risk definitions, six strategies including no-forecast and perfect-forecast bounds, closed-loop out-of-sample testing, and an error-scale sweep (k = 0-2) to find switching points. Gaps similar to Answer 1: no concrete MCDM step, and the optimizer details for MPC are only sketched.
- **PR (9).** Very specific and operationally realistic: point-of-no-return logic for prerelease and detention, prerelease capped by low-side refill and by the natural-flood Heishiguan flow, abort/reversion triggers, a measurement-based fallback, 2021 starting levels and external constraints on actual operation, main-stem (Huayuankou) consideration, shadow operation, acceptance criteria at the upper confidence bound, and the approval route through the annual flood-control plan.
- **CA (8).** Assumptions are explicit, illustrative numbers are labelled as such, and institutional facts are hedged ("as far as I know"). The event-count precision argument is a good guard against overclaiming. Small deduction for the mislabelled variance illustration, which is presented with more precision than it warrants.
- **RC (7).** Covers flow-class-conditional error distributions, a normality test with non-Gaussian alternatives, autocorrelation, t-copula sampling, Monte Carlo scenarios on the deterministic forecast, coverage checks on held-out events, a noninferior set over dam risk and Heishiguan risk, and a risk-based optimization compared with deterministic practice. Missing: an explicit multi-case comparison of distribution assumptions on maximum-level percentiles, fuzzy MCDM selection, and smoothing/end-level repair. The three-objective structure is less explicit than in Answer 1.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good:** a clean separation of believed versus ensemble-evaluated versus realized risk; four dependence designs (deterministic, independent, fitted, comonotonic) that isolate the effect of cross-basin error coincidence; scenario reduction for optimization verified on the full set; Muskingum routing verified on observed hydrographs; explicit Pareto front of GX/LH level against HSG peak; rolling horizon; candid hypotheses with stated failure conditions. **Missing or weak:** no controlled comparison of Gaussian versus non-Gaussian/flow-dependent error assumptions as a headline result; no method for selecting one plan from the Pareto set under fuzzy preferences; release ramping/sawtooth and end-level handling are not designed; little on regulatory approval.

**Answer 2, good:** explicit deciding questions, including statistical resolvability with few large events; interval-inflow timing cases; an executable current-practice simulator validated on 2021; perfect-forecast and measurement-based bounds; an error-scale sweep that converts results into required forecast accuracy; concrete prerelease, detention and recession logic with point-of-no-return and abort rules; a realistic adoption path. **Missing or wrong:** the equicorrelation variance formula is mislabelled as lag-ρ autocorrelation and overstates the volume-error inflation for AR(1)-type errors; the relative-error definition is inconsistent with the scenario construction; the three-objective (GX, LH, HSG) optimization and Pareto selection are only implicit; no MCDM plan choice.

**Reference elements neither answer covered:**
- Six controlled error-assumption cases (unbiased Gaussian constant through non-Gaussian, flow-group, time-varying), each propagated through the same best deterministic plan to show how much Gaussian assumptions understate the 95th-percentile maximum level. Both touch this only as sensitivity.
- Within-group linear models of residual mean and SD as functions of forecast flow, fitted by GA, with Yeo-Johnson transform and SK/KU acceptance thresholds.
- AR order chosen by PACF (AR(2)).
- FAHP-TOPSIS (or another fuzzy MCDM) on several reservoir- and downstream-safety indicators to choose one plan from the Pareto set.
- Odd-period optimization with interpolation to suppress sawtooth releases, a search corridor, and a repair operator for end-level violations.
- An explicit observation that the uncertainty-based plan shifts risk toward the lower-risk reservoir, together with its computational cost relative to real-time use. Both answers anticipate burden-sharing by uncertainty-adjusted storage, which is a reasonable equivalent of the first part.
