# V05 Judgment (S-j1)

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** Finds the deciding questions. These are whether the high-flow rating is reliable (checked by rating ensemble plus reservoir water balance), the fact that volume rather than peak governs (1100 vs 665 mil. m³), joint peak–volume definition, the choice of critical hydrograph by reservoir response ("structure-based" return period), what the 3-day warning buys, per-outlet constraints, and whether operation alone suffices. It settles most of them in sound directions. It does not settle which flood shape is critical; it leaves that to simulation over hydrograph families. Score 8.
- **TC:** No substantive errors. Water-balance inflow = outflow + ΔS/Δt is correct. The volume deficit (~435 mil. m³) is correct. Outflow ≤ inflow on the rising limb is correct. Sedimentation reducing flood volume, cavitation at small partial openings of high-head bottom outlets, and stilling-basin limits are all sound. Some points are qualitative where a number would have tested the logic, for example the pre-release capacity, which is named as central but never sized. Score 8.
- **ME:** Ordered work packages run from data, to design floods, to reservoir/outlet model, to downstream model, to forecasts, to policy optimisation, to institutions. Verification is included: the 2008 replay with recorded gates, a perfect-foresight benchmark, robustness tests, and n-1. The method is executable, but it is broad. It offers options rather than committing (NSGA-II/Borg, MPC, continuous stochastic simulation "resources permitting"), and the policy parameterisation is less concrete than in Answer 2. Score 8.
- **PR:** Realistic and specific. It covers gate operability above the flap gates (2008), cavitation zones, gate-movement and staffing limits, power backup, stilling-basin symmetry, n-1, false-alarm cost to water uses, binational approval, an operating manual, and high-flow gaugings as monitoring. Score 8.
- **CA:** Provisional judgments are labelled and come with an explicit confirm/change table. Assumptions are listed. Dam-class return periods are left to the norms. It leans slightly toward asserting a "heavier tail than assumed" from one event, but this is hedged elsewhere. Score 8.
- **RC:** Covers the rating-curve re-evaluation and gauge–dam volume mismatch (water balance), volume plus shape plus peak, the 2008 shape and multi-peak shapes, multiple distributions with uncertainty bands, climate-change scenarios, bivariate analysis, simulation-optimisation of outlet rules, the 2008 replay against actual operation, gate-failure limitation, and the perfect-foresight gap. It misses three things: a compactness or shape criterion for the critical hydrograph, explicit open/close levels per outlet class as decision variables, and AEP-specific rule sets with in-flood decision points or volume classes. Score 7.

### Answer 2
- **KQ:** Explicitly states the three deciding questions: the new design and check floods in peak and volume under rating and record uncertainty; whether the outlets can pass the check flood with N−1; and what the 3-day warning buys. It also raises a decisive question Answer 1 misses: the 2008 starting level and actual operations, which separate a hydrological problem from an operating one. It settles each with quantitative reasoning. Like Answer 1, it does not pin down a critical-shape criterion. Score 9.
- **TC:** The arithmetic is correct throughout. 435×10⁶/(7·86400) ≈ 720 m³/s. 100 m³/s over 72 h ≈ 26 mil. m³. The risk figures 1−0.99⁵⁰ ≈ 39%, 4.9% and 0.5% are right. The correlated-error variance nσ²(1+(n−1)ρ) = 7.2σ² is right. The Monte Carlo sample size ≈ 1/(0.09p) is right. The rating-bias direction reasoning is right. One small slip: the "floor" rule (the check flood is not smaller than 2008 scaled by the ratio of quantiles) is muddled and somewhat circular. Score 9.
- **ME:** Complete and ordered: Part A (data, regional and historical information, joint fit, design events), Part B (system limits, downstream travel time, baseline simulator replaying 2008), Part C (safety constraints first, a staged rule table with calibratable thresholds, an outlet-allocation constraint layer, forecast skill and error model with fallback and abort, MPC as comparator), Part D (baselines, closed-loop replay, out-of-sample split, stress tests, MC resolution, mass-balance plausibility). It is highly executable with verification built in. Score 9.
- **PR:** Very specific. It addresses the starting level, refill checks for multipurpose use, N−1, gate-movement count tied to staffing, symmetric bays, drawdown-rate limits, tributary timing at downstream control points, a rule executable by operating staff and approvable as a regulation, shadow-mode trial, reversion triggers, interim measures, and Ukrainian data sharing. Score 9.
- **CA:** Numbers not in the brief are marked as assumptions. Uncertainty about institutions and standards is stated openly ("as far as I know", "must be confirmed"). There is an assumptions-and-effects table. The provisional stance that 2008 is "no rarer than the new design flood" is a strong position from one event, though it is argued and flagged as weak evidence. Score 8.
- **RC:** Covers the rating re-evaluation and water-balance check, peak-plus-volume with multi-day volumes, copula joint analysis, multiple shapes including 2008 and double peaks, upper credible bounds, simulation-optimisation of thresholds and gate sequence, the 2008 replay against the current regulation, gate failure, and the perfect-foresight comparison. Climate change appears only as a nonstationary sensitivity, which is weaker than the reference's CC-uplifted design floods. It also misses the compactness/shape criterion, AEP-specific rule sets with decision points, and a comparison with the original design-phase rules. Score 7.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good:**
- A two-tier design basis: downstream-protection tier and dam-safety tier.
- The rating-curve ensemble is propagated through the frequency fit, with the water balance as an independent "gauge" and volume agreement used as the test.
- A structure-based return period selects the critical hydrograph by reservoir response.
- Continuous stochastic simulation is offered as a cross-check.
- Explicit climate scenarios.
- Detailed per-outlet hydraulics, including operability above the flap gates.
- Outlet operating tables keyed to level and target release.
- An outcome taxonomy that allows "operation alone is insufficient".

**Answer 1, missing or weak:**
- The pre-release potential is never quantified.
- No baseline is drawn from the 2008 initial level.
- The optimisation is generic (multi-objective EA) and does not specify which rule parameters per outlet class are decision variables.
- No AEP-specific rule sets or in-flood decision points for when the AEP is unknown at warning time.

**Answer 2, good:**
- Quantitative framing that shows pre-release below the flood-limited level is marginal for a 2008-type flood.
- Explicit attention to the 2008 starting level and the operations actually carried out.
- A correlated forecast-error model, a fallback to measurement-based triggers, an abort rule, and a "point of no return".
- A three-way value-of-forecast comparison: none, real, perfect.
- An N−1 constraint layer for gate allocation.
- Out-of-sample testing.
- Mass-balance plausibility checks.
- A realistic adoption path: shadow mode, interim measures, binational approval.

**Answer 2, missing or weak:**
- Climate change is treated only as a nonstationarity sensitivity.
- The check-flood "floor" rule is confused.
- No critical-shape criterion such as compactness.
- No comparison with the original design-phase rules.

**Reference essentials neither answer covered:**
- An unsteady hydraulic routing model of the upstream reach (Rădăuți–Prut to the dam) used to re-evaluate the rating iteratively and to calibrate roughness. Both answers propose only a local gauge-reach model or a water balance.
- A flood-shape compactness coefficient γ = V/(D(Qmax−Qb)) to pick the critical shape and derive duration.
- Per-outlet-class open/close levels (H′, H″) as explicit optimisation variables, with smoothing and end-volume penalties.
- AEP-specific rule sets (10/5/1/0.1%, with and without CC) combined into an adaptive framework with forecast-based decision points at fixed reservoir levels and volume sub-classes.
- A longer-horizon rainfall lookup table to extend the 3-day lead time.
- Comparison with the original design-phase rules (the 1969 shape).
