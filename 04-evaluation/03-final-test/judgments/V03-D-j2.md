# Judgment V03-D-j2

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (V03-3gyj)

- **KQ (8).** Finds the deciding questions: feasibility of holding both 180.69 m and 1000 m³/s for a ~100-year flood; how to build a forecast when none is operational; how to represent error (by lead time, magnitude, autocorrelation, peak bias/timing); how to define "sensible storage use" over the event; how to define risk across scenarios; how to pick one plan. It settles most of them concretely. The weak spots are the MPC horizon (6–12 h) next to an expected useful lead of only a few hours, and the absence of any quantitative first pass on ramping cost or lead time.
- **TC (8).** No real errors. The 13% capacity discrepancy is right, and so is the V_excess volume check against storage from the curve. Chance constraints and CVaR, the dam-safety override at 181.34 m and the state-dependent outlet ceiling are all handled correctly. One small inconsistency: the "value of forecast information" is defined as P1 vs P2 under perfect forecast, but P1 and P2 are different policy families, so the comparison mixes policy structure with information.
- **ME (8).** The order is clear and executable: data checks, perfect-foresight bound, information state and forecast with leave-one-event-out CV, policy families P0–P3, closed-loop Monte Carlo over an error grid, held-out test, scaled-event stress test, then selection. Verification comes from the perfect-foresight gap, the hindcast-calibrated error model and the single out-of-sample test. It lacks a Monte Carlo sample-size argument and does not check error-model coverage (PIT or band coverage).
- **PR (7).** It is specific about the unknown ramping limit, the outlet capacity at the flood-limit level, the control point, confidentiality, pre-release costs, the recovery time before a second typhoon, and a one-page operating chart. Regulation, approval and operator fallback are handled more generically.
- **CA (8).** Well scoped. The capacity discrepancy is not resolved by fiat. The expectation about MPC is labelled provisional. It leaves open whether 2703 m³/s belongs to the test event. The assumption table states what would change each judgment.
- **RC (7).** It covers the forecast build with uncertainty scenarios and an event-wide storage-use metric (utilisation ratio plus storage used at the inflow peak, framed as aggressive vs cautious failure). It defines probability and duration risk objectives over scenarios and builds a multi-objective Pareto set with an evolutionary optimizer. Deterministic MPC (P2) vs chance-constrained MPC (P3) vs perfect foresight is a close analogue of MOSEM / MOROU / MOOB. Minimax regret for robustness is effectively the reference's STP idea, and an entropy/TOPSIS cross-check stands in for AHP-entropy. It misses combining several forecast models and an ablation of the storage-use objective.

### Answer 2 (V03-v5l5)

- **KQ (9).** It identifies three deciding questions (storage vs a 100-year flood, ramping and outlet cost, forecast lead and error structure) and settles each with checkable arithmetic: storage per metre, mm-equivalents, the 30.8 mm/h peak as about 2530 m³/s, ramp catch-up storage, and how error correlation inflates volume uncertainty. It also settles the conditional-vs-annual risk distinction, the control point and the switch logic.
- **TC (8).** The arithmetic is almost entirely correct: 0.296 Mm³/mm, 3.6 Mm³/h ≈ 12.2 mm, ramp triangle ≈ 8.8 Mm³, MC n ≈ 1900, 0.13–0.15 m per Mm³, pre-release loss ≈ 12% of initial storage. There is one slip. The correlated-variance formula 6σ²(1+5ρ) assumes equal correlation between all pairs (exchangeable), yet the error is described as an AR(1) lag correlation. Under AR(1) the variance is about 24.5σ², not 30σ², so the SD is about 7.1 rather than 7.9 Mm³ and the inflation is ×2.0, not ×2.2. That does not change the design. Reading 31.4 vs 35.7 as "to 180.69" vs "to 181.34" is clever, is flagged as a hypothesis, and is to be checked against the curve.
- **ME (8).** It is executable and ordered: data assembly, a presumed baseline when the actual rule is missing, event split, a conceptual-reference forecast, an error model with PIT and coverage checks, an error grid with a sample-size justification, scaled floods, four policy families including scenario-based rolling optimization, and a resolvability check against MC standard errors. Gap: it never says how the multi-objective noninferior set is generated (only "grid or evolutionary search").
- **PR (9).** Very specific and realistic: presumed conventional dispatch rule, why historical operation is not a fair comparator, the pre-release refill cap, gate-out and capacity-coefficient stress tests, reversion triggers, shadow-mode trial with acceptance criteria, downstream warning lead, and the approval chain under the annual flood-season operation plan.
- **CA (8).** Assumptions are explicit, including the 200 m³/s/h ramp, which is labelled a planning assumption, and the capacity reading, which is labelled a hypothesis. Illustrative feasibility cases are labelled as such. It warns against claiming rankings the data cannot support. The description of Chinese approval is general but hedged.
- **RC (6).** It covers forecast build and error scenarios, risk over scenarios (probabilities plus severity and CVaR), event-wide storage use (idle storage plus premature filling relative to perfect foresight, which is a strong substitute for RRCA's too-risky/too-conservative balance) and choosing one plan (screen, noninferior set, lexicographic rule, weight sensitivity). It is weaker on how a candidate's scenario performance is collapsed into one fitness value for optimization: it offers an upper-quantile forecast and "all or nearly all scenarios", but no regret or worst-case fitness. It has no explicit deterministic-forecast vs robust comparison. It does not use a formal multi-objective optimizer to produce the Pareto set, and like Answer 1 it does not combine forecast models or ablate the storage objective.

## 2. Notes

**Answer 1, good.** Its framing as a sequential decision under an explicit information state is correct. It puts perfect-foresight bounds and a volume feasibility check first. Its error model is heteroscedastic, autocorrelated and includes peak bias and timing. P2 vs P3 maps cleanly onto the deterministic-vs-robust contrast. It reports the break-even error level and separates under- and over-forecast effects. Selection uses minimax regret and an entropy/TOPSIS cross-check with weight sensitivity. It adds a recovery-time requirement before a following typhoon.

**Answer 1, missing or weak.** The MPC horizon is longer than the expected useful lead. There is no quantitative feel for ramping cost or for how error correlation inflates volume uncertainty. Error-model coverage is not checked, and MC sample size is not justified. There is no forecast-model combination, no ablation of the storage metric, and only light treatment of adoption and regulation.

**Answer 2, good.** Its back-of-envelope reasoning tells the reader what decides feasibility: the peak-hour runoff above about 12 mm/h, not the total volume. It quantifies how ramping from generation flow eats storage. It shows that ignoring error correlation understates volume uncertainty. The storage-use measures are idle storage at peak release, and premature filling relative to perfect foresight. It keeps conditional and annual risk apart. Its hourly stage logic includes a point-of-no-return and an early switch that spreads the excess. Practicality is strong: a presumed-baseline caveat, a shadow-mode trial, reversion triggers and downstream warnings.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong.** The AR(1) variance arithmetic uses an exchangeable-correlation formula, which overstates the variance by about 20%. There is no explicit worst-case or regret aggregation across scenarios to rank candidates. There is no named multi-objective optimizer or Pareto generation step. It does not compare a deterministic-forecast optimization against the robust one, does not combine forecast models, and does not ablate the storage objective.

**Reference elements neither answer covered.**
- Combining several forecast models (stacking ensemble on out-of-fold predictions) into one deterministic forecast.
- An explicit ablation that removes the whole-event storage-use objective to show its effect.
- A single normalized whole-event index of inflow vs remaining flood storage with a target value (RRCA). Both use substitute metrics instead, which is acceptable.
- Generating scenarios by perturbing the hyperparameters of trained forecasters with an NSE screen. Both instead use calibrated statistical error models, a justified and arguably better alternative.
