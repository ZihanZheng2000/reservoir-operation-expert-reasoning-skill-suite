## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:** It correctly diagnoses that the upper reservoir binds and that (b) is dominated. Its numbered assumptions A1 to A7 map cleanly to the triggers. It is honest that the volume margin between (a) and (c) is small. It specifies a scenario routing check with tributary timing cases. Its worst-case city check with no help from lower-reservoir storage is sound.

**Answer 1, missing or weak:** It does not compute the latest start time for upper prerelease, so the cost of waiting is only asserted. There is no city-flow figure for the (c) catch-up path. The prerelease-phase city cap of 4,500 to 5,000 is loose. It does not name an approval authority or an operating-manual basis, and it has no early-arrival abort rule that accounts for water already in transit. The "6,000 minus local inflow" lower-release ceiling has no margin.

**Answer 2, done well:** Its point-of-no-return table is the strongest decision tool in either answer. Its plan arithmetic is consistent across both dams with correct lags. It compares the options quantitatively, including the (c) pass-through of about 4,100. Its tributary timing table and abort rule recognise that water released in the previous 10 h is irrecoverable. It names the authorization and logging route explicitly and gives a fallback toward (b) for gate or ramp failure.

**Answer 2, missing or weak:** Its confidence is somewhat overstated. It does not acknowledge that (c) is probably adequate in volume terms, so it understates the moderate stakes. Its tributary correlation is assumed. The trigger "upper bound below 150" is not meaningful. It gives no explicit check of local inflow below the lower dam, only an assumption of none. It has no formal multi-scenario routing run before the next forecast.

**Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had:**
- An explicit check that even under (c) the high-end case (400) is manageable in volume. With about 150 free, the upper dam must pass about 250 million m³ over 72 h, roughly +1,000 m³/s above base. That frames the decision as margin and peak management, not averting a flood.
- The dam-safety and design-flood rules and the flood-limit-level and seasonal rules under which drawdown is permitted. Prerelease decisions usually need basin or provincial authority approval and notification of downstream jurisdictions.
- Limits on rate of change and bank stability (drawdown-induced bank or slope failure), plus navigation, hydropower dispatch and ecological flow constraints on the higher releases.
- A refill-risk statement. Draining to the flood-limit level should be recoverable from the low-side forecast volume. Answer 2 touches this only for the joint-use case.
- Forecast error on arrival time: what happens if the flood arrives at hour 20 to 24, quantified against each plan.
- A sequencing point: the lower reservoir's extra release should start no later than when the upper prerelease reaches it, and lower releases should be coordinated so that city flow does not step up while tributary gauges are rising.
