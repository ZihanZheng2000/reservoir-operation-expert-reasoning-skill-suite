# Judgment U5-r3-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own work)

- State: upper free 0.40x300 = 120 Mm3 (180 occupied); lower free 0.85x500 = 425 Mm3 (75 occupied). Lower inflow now = upper release lagged 18 h (~1,500) + tributary 300 = ~1,800, so the lower dam is already drawing down at ~700 m3/s (~2.5 Mm3/h) and will be essentially empty in ~30 h. So (b) is largely what is already happening.
- Upper net drawdown now 300 m3/s gives only ~32 Mm3 in 30 h, so ~152 Mm3 free at flood arrival, below the central 250 and well below 400. The upper reservoir is the binding element.
- Volume check: 250 Mm3 / 72 h = ~965 m3/s mean, below the current inflow of 1,200, so the forecast must be volume above base flow (or a different accounting basis). This must be confirmed.
- Emptying 180 Mm3 in 30 h needs ~2,870 m3/s, in 24 h ~3,300 m3/s, both far below 8,000. City flow from upper 3,000 + tributary peak 1,500 is ~4,500 even if passed straight through, below 6,000.
- Timing: upper prerelease made in the next 30 h reaches the lower dam at h18-48 and the city at h28-58. That is ahead of the upper flood wave (at the lower dam from ~h48). The only overlap risk is with a tributary rise, which the lower reservoir can buffer.
- Regret: water above the flood-limited level is not conservation water. Releasing it down to that level costs little (some hydropower head). Going below the level needs a separate, authorized decision.
- Cost of waiting: about 12 of the 30 h window is lost, and the later catch-up release is steeper (~3,800-4,000 m3/s). There is a point-of-no-return estimate under practical release and ramp limits.
- Recommendation: (a) limited to the flood-limited level, with the upper dam as the priority. The lower dam is coordinated to hold its flood-limited level and to compensate for the tributary. A city operating ceiling below 6,000 is kept.
- Triggers: forecast rises or peak is concentrated; tributary forecast; early observed rise; forecast drops or turns out to be total inflow; local inflow below the lower dam; reach constraints; gate failure.
- Missing information: hydrograph shape and volume basis, tributary forecast, local inflow, operating rules and value of flood-pool water, ramp, drawdown and inter-dam limits.
- Practice: one joint schedule under headquarters authority, downstream warnings, approval to go below the flood-limited level, logging, and a fallback to measured-flow rules.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:**
- Point-of-no-return reasoning under practical release limits.
- Dominance table across the alternatives.
- Explicit restriction to the flood-limited level, with a separate decision for going below it.
- City operating ceiling of 4,500.
- Concrete warning content.
- Handling of the dynamic flood-limited level (cap at ~150 Mm3).
- Approval and accountability paragraph.

**Answer 1, missing or weak:**
- No scenario routing (volume x tributary timing) to verify the plan before h12.
- No explicit compensation rule for the upper release against a tributary rise; Answer 2 has one.
- The regret is understated: hydropower head and refill risk are not zero.
- "No serious risk under any alternative" should be stated as conditional on hydrograph peakedness.
- No lower-reservoir reserve threshold.

**Answer 2, done well:**
- Clear timing logic showing prerelease arrives before the upper flood wave.
- Explicit pass-through safety check with the tributary at maximum.
- Coordinated scenario-routing verification.
- A tributary compensation rule (upper + tributary ≤ planned lower release).
- A hard lower-reserve threshold of 300-400 Mm3.
- Head-dependent capacity flagged.
- Well-calibrated framing.

**Answer 2, missing or weak:**
- No point-of-no-return estimate for waiting.
- The partial upper target (85-90% free) is not justified against its own A1 logic.
- Inter-dam reach safe flow appears only generically in A4.
- Approval authority and the regulatory step for drawdown below the flood-limited level are not specified.
- No abort or fallback for a missing or late forecast, or for gate failure.

**Points an expert answer must contain that neither had:**
- An explicit statement of the forecast-update cadence relative to the lead time. Only about two updates (h12, h24) arrive before the flood, so the h24 update is the last one usable for upper-dam action. Each step should be tied to a decision checkpoint at h12 and h24.
- A reverse calculation of the maximum upper flood the system can absorb without exceeding 6,000 at the city under realistic peak shapes, for example via a triangular hydrograph peak estimate. This should be stated as the threshold forecast value at which going below the flood-limited level becomes necessary.
- Dam-safety priority: upper dam safety outranks downstream targets, and the upper dam's maximum release during the flood may be dictated by its own dam-safety rule curve.
- Refill and end-of-season considerations, plus hydropower and water-supply loss, as a quantified regret term.
- Forecast skill and lead-time uncertainty on the 30 h arrival estimate itself, not only on the volume.
