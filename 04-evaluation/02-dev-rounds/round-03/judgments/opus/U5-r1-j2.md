## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:** the point-of-no-return calculation that turns "wait 12 h" into a concrete release penalty; the side-by-side (a)/(b)/(c) table; the time-stamped coordinated schedule with the arrival of upper water at the lower dam; the abort rule, including water in transit; the cap on low-regret prerelease (about 150 Mm³) if the storage is joint-use; and the interim city cap. **Missing or wrong:** the tributary-coincidence row is confused about what the lower reservoir passes and stores; there is no formal multi-scenario routing check before the hour-12 decision; it is somewhat overconfident about robustness while the forecast basis is unresolved; the "natural flood" comparison is unsupported.

**Answer 2, done well:** an explicit verification step (a scenario grid with pass criteria); a lower-storage reserve threshold (300–400 Mm³) as a hard limit; the tributary ranked as the key uncertainty, with a rule that tributary control takes priority over the lower reservoir's storage; clear calibration language; assumptions labeled and tied to triggers. **Missing or wrong:** the catch-up comparison understates the cost of waiting (it targets 260 rather than full evacuation); a small timing slip on how long the lower reservoir takes to clear; the early-arrival protocol is thin and ignores water in transit; no explicit authority or approval chain; warnings lack flows and times.

**Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had:**
- The regulatory basis for forecast-based prerelease. That means checking whether the operating rules allow it within this forecast lead time, the allowance for forecast error, and the rule that prerelease volume must be refillable by the low-end forecast. In practice, that bounds the prerelease by the lower bound of the forecast minus the forecast error, and requires basin-authority approval for any deviation from the rule curve.
- Dam safety at the upper reservoir under the high-end case. The 400 Mm³ is set against 300 Mm³ of flood pool plus surcharge or spillway capacity, with the minimum flood-phase release needed to avoid encroaching on surcharge.
- Forecast skill at a 30 h lead: arrival-time error as well as volume error. The value of the 12 h update should be stated as the reduction in uncertainty it is expected to bring.
- A preset "minimum no-regret" component, distinguished from a "conditional" component that is committed only after the hour-12 forecast.
- Channel attenuation over the 18 h and 10 h reaches, and limits on how fast stage may change for bank stability.
