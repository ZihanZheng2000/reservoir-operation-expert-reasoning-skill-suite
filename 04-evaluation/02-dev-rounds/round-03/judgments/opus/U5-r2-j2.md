**Answer 2, missing or wrong:**

- The escalation trigger "central > 300 (the upper's entire flood storage)" mixes the above-base-flow volume basis with storage capacity without restating the basis.

**Points an expert answer should contain that neither had:**
- **Forecast schedule vs. decision windows.** There are two forecast updates (t+12, t+24) before arrival and about four before upper floodwater reaches the city (t+58). The decision should be framed as a staged, sequential policy whose stage-2 options are preserved, not as a one-shot choice.
- **Forecast skill and uncertainty.** Use the historical skill of 30 h volume forecasts and the probability level of the 150-400 range (for example, whether it is a 90% interval). Translate it into a risk-based prerelease volume, such as the prerelease that the low-end flood can refill (a refill-guarantee rule). Answer 2 touched on this only for the carry-over case.
- **Channel capacity and damage thresholds.** The reach between the dams and below the lower dam needs explicit stage/damage thresholds. Neither gave a flow at which nuisance flooding or bank failure starts.
- **Gauge-based verification in real time.** Track observed upper inflow, tributary flow and city flow against the routed plan, with defined deviation tolerances that trigger re-planning.
- **Regulatory framing.** Prerelease below the flood-limit level, or "pre-emptive" evacuation, usually needs approval from the river-basin or flood-control authority under the approved flood-control plan. Liability and notification duties for man-made flow increases also apply.
- **Post-flood refill and rule-curve return.** After the event, the reservoirs must refill and return to the rule curve, with the conservation-storage consequences if the flood underperforms.
