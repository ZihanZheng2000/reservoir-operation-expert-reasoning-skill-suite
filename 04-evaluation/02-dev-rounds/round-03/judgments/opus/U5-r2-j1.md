## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:**
- Quantified point of no return: about 10.4 h at full evacuation, a latest start around t+19 to t+20, or t+13 to t+14 if the flood arrives at t+24.
- The catch-up cost of (c).
- An explicit drift table showing that (c) is not "do nothing".
- A clear upper-led framing: the lower matches inflow and stays at 100% free.
- Abort rules and a missing-forecast fallback.
- Headquarters authority and recording.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong:**
- It does not state explicitly that prerelease must stop at the flood-limit level.
- It overstates that the flood-pool status is the only flip item.
- It assumes the evacuation finishes before the tributary rises. It does not consider that the tributary storm is likely correlated with the upper flood and could arrive during the t+18 to t+48 transit window.
- Its city-margin argument says little about local inflow below the lower dam, beyond listing it as an assumption.

**Answer 2, done well:**
- Assumption A2 (never draw below the flood-limit level).
- Explicit hard limits: city at 5,000 m³/s during prerelease, and a reserve floor for the lower.
- A worst-case pass-through check for the city.
- The tributary forecast identified as the top information gap, with hourly gauge reporting requested.
- Honest calibration that the volume margin between (a) and (c) is small.
- Noting that lower heads reduce discharge capacity, which argues for evacuating early.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong:**
- No point-of-no-return or early-arrival timing analysis, so the cost of waiting is only qualitative.
- The lower clearing time is slightly optimistic.
- The worst-case tributary is taken as 1,500 instead of 1,800 total.
- Thinner on decision authority, the case of one gate out of service, and the missing-forecast fallback.
- Its early-arrival trigger ("stop increasing upper release") is not reconciled with the cost of having under-evacuated.

**Points an expert answer needs that neither answer had:**
- The tributary storm is likely produced by the same weather system and may peak before the upper flood. The most dangerous coincidence is therefore tributary peak + upper prerelease in transit + upper flood pass-through, around t+30 to t+60. The upper prerelease rate should be tied to an observed tributary gauge trigger, not only to the forecast.
- An estimate of the upper's required in-flood release. For example, an excess of 250–400 million m³ less about 150–260 free means passing about 0–250 million m³ over the event; spread over 48–72 h, that is about 0–1,400 m³/s above base. This should be checked against the space left at the lower dam for the tributary.
- A probabilistic framing of the 150–400 range (what exceedance the bounds represent), and the value of the 12-h update relative to the forecast's lead-time skill.
- Use of surcharge storage, and the dam-safety order of priority if volumes exceed 400 million m³.
- Hydropower or water-supply losses expressed concretely as the regret cost of (a), and the formal approval step for any release above normal operating-rule rates.
