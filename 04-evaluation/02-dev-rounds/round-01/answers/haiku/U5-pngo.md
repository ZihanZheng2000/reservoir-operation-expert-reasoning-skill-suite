# Reservoir Flood Response Recommendation

## Recommendation: Option (b) with conditional escalation protocol

**Preferred immediate action:** Lower reservoir begins controlled prerelease now. Upper reservoir maintains current release rates and monitoring stance, with escalation decision at 12-hour forecast update.

---

## Summary of Reasoning

### Current Safety Margin Assessment

**Available flood storage capacity:** 545 million m³ (upper 120M + lower 425M)
- Forecast inflow over 72 hours: 250M m³ (worst case 400M m³)
- **Conclusion:** Sufficient storage exists for even worst-case forecast

**Current city-side flow status:**
- Lower dam release: 2,500 m³/s
- Uncontrolled tributary baseline: 300 m³/s
- Current total outflow toward city: ~2,800 m³/s
- Safe flow threshold: 6,000 m³/s
- **Current buffer:** 3,200 m³/s margin

**Existing prerelease state:**
- Upper reservoir is already releasing more than inflow (1,500 m³/s vs 1,200 m³/s inflow = 300 m³/s active drawdown)
- Lower reservoir is also in drawdown mode (2,500 m³/s release vs ~1,800 m³/s inflow = 700 m³/s drawdown)
- **Conclusion:** Both reservoirs are already in preparation mode; upper is not sitting idle

### Reasoning for Option (b) over Options (a) and (c)

**Why not option (a) — both prerelease now?**
1. **Redundant upper action:** Upper is already actively drawing down (300 m³/s); increasing its release further depletes scarce upper storage (only 120M m³ free) without proportional benefit since the 18-hour lag means upper's water won't reach the city until well after the next forecast arrives
2. **Storage preservation:** Upper's 40% free share is the bottleneck in a worst-case scenario. If forecast updates show volume >350M m³ or peak inflow >5,000 m³/s, we need that upper storage available
3. **Forecast value in 12 hours:** We will have vastly better hydrograph data and tributary detail before water even reaches the lower dam (18 hours remain). Committing upper to full prerelease now eliminates flexibility when we know more

**Why not option (c) — wait 12 hours?**
1. **Lower dam is the city's immediate protection layer:** It sits only 10 hours upstream. Prerelease from lower creates immediate buffer for city safety
2. **Lower has the capacity:** 425M m³ free storage is abundant; moderate prerelease is affordable even if forecast worsens
3. **Asymmetric risk:** If we wait and the uncontrolled tributary spikes or the upper forecasted peak is sharper than expected, we lose the margin that lower's prerelease would have created. Prerelease from lower now is a low-cost hedge (we can always refill it with tributary flow in the next 18 hours)
4. **Operational signaling:** Starting lower prerelease now sends a clear signal that flood response is active and keeps operations staff in a responsive posture

**Why option (b) balances risks:**
- Activates the reservoir most directly responsible for city safety (lower)
- Preserves flexibility in the storage-constrained upper reservoir
- Costs little operationally (lower has 85% free storage; moving from 2,500 to 3,000 or 3,200 m³/s is a modest increase)
- Maintains the ability to escalate to both-reservoir prerelease if the 12-hour forecast warrants it

---

## Immediate Actions to Take

1. **Lower reservoir:** Increase controlled release from 2,500 m³/s to ~3,000–3,200 m³/s over the next 1–2 hours (modest prerelease, not aggressive)
   - Monitor tributary inflow continuously
   - Target: maintain 2,500+ m³/s buffer from the 6,000 m³/s safe threshold

2. **Upper reservoir:** Hold current release at 1,500 m³/s (no change); continue inflow monitoring
   - Prepare contingency to increase to 2,500–3,000 m³/s if triggered by forecast update
   - Request real-time monitoring of any inflow acceleration

3. **Forecast operations:** Prioritize next forecast update; request:
   - Detailed tributary inflow forecast (hourly or 3-hourly intervals for next 48 hours)
   - Revised cumulative 72-hour upper-reservoir inflow estimate with timing distribution (is it front-loaded, back-loaded, or steady?)
   - Peak inflow rates and timing to each reservoir

4. **12-hour decision point (mandatory escalation check):**
   - If updated forecast shows >350M m³ cumulative or tributary peak >1,200 m³/s → escalate to option (a) (both reservoirs full prerelease)
   - If forecast improves or shows later timing → maintain option (b) or consider holding lower prerelease
   - If forecast confirms 250M m³ ± 50M m³ → continue option (b)

---

## Key Assumptions

1. **Forecast accuracy:** The 250M m³ (range 150–400M m³) represents a reasonable estimate, not a fast-moving envelope where the true value is already at the high end of range. *If the forecast is already converging toward 400M m³*, this assumption fails and recommendation shifts immediately to (a).

2. **Tributary controllability:** The uncontrolled tributary's behavior can be estimated or bounded within 12–24 hours. *If tributary has produced surprise 1,500+ m³/s peaks before reaching lower dam without warning*, this assumption is weak.

3. **Release-rate flexibility:** Both dams can adjust release rates in 30-min to 2-hour windows without cascading operational constraints (tailwater impacts, water-supply conflicts, hydropower). *If releases are locked to power-generation schedules*, operational flexibility is limited.

4. **Current release sustainability:** The current upper release (1,500 m³/s) and lower release (2,500 m³/s) do not mask an underlying constraint (e.g., low tailwater requiring limit, ongoing drought stress). These rates are presented as current operational states, not emergency maxima.

5. **Distance/timing calculations:** The 18-hour lag (upper to lower) and 10-hour lag (lower to city) are accurate. The flood front does not exceed these via tributary bypass or unscouted channels.

---

## Threshold Information That Would Change the Recommendation

**Immediately escalate to option (a) — both reservoirs full prerelease:**
- Forecast update shows cumulative inflow >350M m³ over 72 hours (approaching upper's 120M m³ free capacity plus normal inflow absorption)
- Forecast shows peak inflow rate to upper >5,000 m³/s (approaching release capacity limits and requiring maximum attenuation)
- Tributary forecast shows confirmed peak >1,200 m³/s during the critical 18–36 hour window
- Real-time inflow to upper exceeds 3,000 m³/s (well ahead of forecast timeline)

**Shift to option (c) or hold option (b) with reduced lower prerelease:**
- Forecast update shows cumulative <200M m³ and peak tributary <600 m³/s (comfortable margins without prerelease)
- Real-time observations show the flood arrival time slipping beyond 36 hours (buys time for better information)

---

## Most Critical Missing Information

**The complete inflow hydrograph and tributary forecast timing are essential.** Without these, the recommendation above is contingent on three unknowns:

1. **Timing of peak inflow to upper reservoir:** If the peak inflow arrives within 24–30 hours (before forecast update and while only upper can respond), we may need option (a) now. If the peak is distributed or delayed beyond 36 hours, option (b) is safer. **Effect:** Could shift recommendation from (b) to (a) if peak is sharper and earlier than baseline forecast implies.

2. **Tributary contribution pattern:** The uncontrolled tributary currently 300 m³/s can reach 1,500 m³/s in storms. *When does it peak relative to the upper-reservoir inflow peak?* If the tributary peaks 18–24 hours from now (coinciding with upper's peak reaching lower), combined flows could exceed lower's capacity to attenuate. **Effect:** Could make option (b) insufficient; would require both-reservoir prerelease.

3. **Inflow distribution within 72-hour window:** Is 250M m³ evenly distributed (average 3,900 m³/s over 72 hours) or front-loaded (peak 8,000+ m³/s in hours 24–36)? Uniform distribution requires far less urgent prerelease than a front-loaded hydrograph. **Effect:** Front-loaded distribution strongly favors option (a) now; back-loaded distribution supports option (b).

**Operational consequence of missing information:** The 12-hour forecast update will likely resolve all three unknowns to sufficient precision. If the current 1-hour decision window is genuinely constraining (i.e., we must commit to a course now), option (b) is the minimum-regret choice: it activates city protection without gambling away upper storage. If the decision can be delayed to the next forecast cycle (12 hours), option (c) is preferable.

## Conclusion

**Immediate decision:** Proceed with option (b). Lower reservoir begins controlled prerelease (2,500 → 3,000–3,200 m³/s). Upper reservoir holds at 1,500 m³/s, with full escalation readiness. Mandatory decision review at 12-hour forecast update or if real-time inflow data triggers an escalation threshold. The recommendation preserves operational flexibility, hedges against the most-likely adverse scenarios with low cost (lower's abundant free storage), and defers the costly choice (using upper's scarce storage) until more information is available.

The upper reservoir's current drawdown (already 300 m³/s below inflow) reflects prior judgment that preparation is warranted; escalating upper's prerelease now would be over-committing a scarce resource (120M m³ free) when the lower reservoir is both more immediately relevant to city safety and better positioned to handle prerelease operationally.
