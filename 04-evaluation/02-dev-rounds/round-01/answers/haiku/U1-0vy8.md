# Study Design: Staged Flood-Limited Water Levels for Multipurpose Reservoir

## Research Question

Can seasonal (staged) flood-limited water level (FLWL) bounds replace a single fixed FLWL at this monsoon-basin multipurpose reservoir in India while maintaining or improving flood safety at the downstream town (40 km) and increasing water-use efficiency for irrigation and hydropower?

---

## Stage 1: Define the Problem

### 1.1 Current Rule and Baseline

**Assemble and encode the baseline:**
- Document the current fixed FLWL and current operating rule in executable form (simulate 45 years of daily inflows to establish baseline spill, refill performance, and current flood-risk characteristics)
- Record all characteristic reservoir levels: current FLWL, design/check flood levels (tied to 100-year and higher return periods), conservation level (minimum for irrigation/power), and crest/PMF level
- Define the downstream safety threshold: safe discharge capacity at the town (40 km), maximum safe stage, and any existing flood-protection measures
- Assemble historical operating records (inflows, releases, observed levels) for 2–3 major flood events to validate the simulator against real operations
- Identify two-sided failures of the fixed FLWL:
  - *Waste/underutilization:* spill and water loss in small to medium floods or outside peak monsoon season
  - *Underprotection risk:* whether the fixed level creates safety headroom only for the design flood, leaving vulnerability to larger or faster-rising events

### 1.2 Why Staged Levels Could Help

- A lower FLWL outside peak monsoon season could allow more water storage when inflow is below irrigation demand, improving water-use efficiency
- A higher FLWL during the peak monsoon (when inflow is naturally abundant) could preserve flood-control storage without sacrificing conservation
- The 1–5-day inflow forecasts (already available) could allow rule-changes timed to approaching flood risk, reducing unnecessary prerelease in false-alarm conditions

## Stage 2: Understand Uncertainty and Forecasts

### 2.1 Forecast Reliability and Operational Timeliness

**Assessment needed:**

1. **Lead-time reliability analysis** on the 8 years of archived forecast records:
   - Stratify by monsoon phase (pre-monsoon, main monsoon, post-monsoon; roughly June–September and surrounding months)
   - For 1–5-day lead times, compute mean bias, standard deviation of error, and qualified rate (fraction within ±20% of observed) at each lead time
   - Identify the reliable forecast horizon (RFH): the longest lead time at which relative error is typically <20%
   - Test whether forecast skill is stationary across the 8-year record or varies by year or event type

2. **Drawdown feasibility**:
   - If the monsoon-average RFH is 3–4 days, can the reservoir draw down from a proposed seasonal upper level to a lower level within 3 days?
   - Calculate the maximum drawdown rate (release minus average inflow) at each monsoon phase and check against observed discharge capacity

3. **Decision points** for switching between seasonal FLWL bounds:
   - Example: pre-monsoon (May–May 31): lower FLWL; early monsoon (June–July 15): medium FLWL; main monsoon (July 15–Sept): higher FLWL; recession (post-monsoon): staged return to pre-monsoon level
   - For each transition, compute the required prerelease volume and check timing against the available lead time

### 2.2 Forecast-Informed Risk

- Generate scenario trees or ensemble distributions from the 8-year forecast archive:
  - For each historical monsoon, resample 100+ forecast scenarios around the observed inflow trajectory
  - Preserve cross-time correlation in forecast error (adjacent leads are not independent)
- Run 10–15 major historical floods (selected from the 45-year record) with the 8-year forecast record to measure forecast accuracy during extreme events—accuracy may degrade during outlier floods

### 2.3 Simulate Reservoir and Downstream Response

- Build a daily mass-balance simulator:
  - Storage update: S(t+1) = S(t) + Inflow(t) − Release(t)
  - Spillway capacity: once level exceeds emergency/design level, releases are constrained by spillway capacity and flow control
  - Downstream routing: route reservoir releases with any travel time or channel storage to the town 40 km downstream
  - Collect: daily storage, level, release, spill, and downstream flow for every scenario

## Stage 3: Design and Compute Plans

### 3.1 Define Safety Thresholds and Acceptable Risk

**Establish safety constraints:**

1. **Reservoir-side failure**: define the maximum safe water level at the dam. Usually this is a design-flood level (e.g., corresponding to a 0.1% annual exceedance probability or 1000-year design flood) plus a freeboard allowance. Let's call this Z_max.

2. **Downstream failure**: define the safe discharge (and/or safe stage) at the town. This is the capacity of the downstream channel/levees without overtopping. Let's call this Q_safe.

3. **Acceptable risk benchmark**:
   - Option A (conservative): no exceedance of the downstream safe discharge under the proposed staged rule during any historical flood event (deterministic test)
   - Option B (risk-based): the annual probability of exceedance of Q_safe under the staged rule should not exceed the annual probability under the current fixed FLWL (maintain baseline safety)
   - Recommendation: Use both; Option A for screening candidate rules, Option B for comparing final candidates

### 3.2 Propose Candidate Staged FLWL Schemes

**Typical approach (informed by the papers):**

- **Scheme 1 (Baseline)**: keep the current fixed FLWL year-round (control)
- **Scheme 2 (Seasonal 2-phase)**: 
  - Pre-monsoon & post-monsoon (May, September-October): lower FLWL (e.g., current FLWL − 0.5–1 m)
  - Main monsoon (June–August): higher FLWL (e.g., current FLWL + 0.3–0.7 m)
- **Scheme 3 (Seasonal 3-phase)**: 
  - Pre-monsoon: lower FLWL
  - Early monsoon (June–July 15): medium FLWL
  - Main monsoon (July 15–Sept): higher FLWL
  - Post-monsoon: lower FLWL

**Feasibility checks for each scheme:**
- At each proposed seasonal transition, check: can the reservoir level change by the required amount within the lead time using available release capacity?
- Check: does the higher FLWL leave enough freeboard for the design flood?
- Check: will pre-release to enter the higher FLWL during early monsoon introduce risk if the forecast is wrong?

### 3.3 Forecast-Based Operating Logic

**Adaptive branching:**

- Stage 1: Current forecast (1–5 day lead time):
  - If the maximum forecasted inflow over the next 5 days exceeds a critical threshold (e.g., 50th percentile inflow for the season), switch to flood-control mode: release aggressively to reach the seasonal FLWL, do not try to refill
  - If forecast inflow is below the critical threshold, allow retention near or above the seasonal FLWL
  
- Stage 2: Rolling update (daily):
  - Each day, update the forecast and re-assess storage targets; implement only the immediate release, then re-optimize tomorrow
  - If forecast error is detected (actual inflow persistently above/below the forecast), gradually correct the release rather than jumping

### 3.4 Trade-offs and Criteria

**Evaluation objectives (to be traded off):**

1. **Flood safety**: P(downstream discharge > Q_safe) and P(reservoir level > Z_max) during major events
2. **Water-supply benefit**: cumulative water retained during dry/post-monsoon periods for irrigation (measured as total inflow utilized vs. spilled)
3. **Hydropower benefit**: increased storage availability during generation season (if applicable)
4. **Operational burden**: frequency of FLWL changes, number of prerelease events, complexity for operators

**Selection principle:**
- Safety is non-compensable: all candidate schemes must maintain flood safety at or better than the baseline
- Among schemes that meet the safety constraint, prefer the one that maximizes water-use efficiency (least spill + best refill)

## Stage 4: Test and Evidence

### 4.1 Test with Historical Events and Information Conditions

**Baseline comparison (identical conditions):**

1. **Hindcast simulation over 45 years**:
   - Run Scheme 1 (fixed FLWL) and Schemes 2 & 3 (staged FLWL) under the same 45-year inflow record
   - Compare:
     - Annual maximum reservoir level (is Z_max exceeded?)
     - Annual maximum downstream discharge (is Q_safe exceeded?)
     - Annual spill volume (total wasted water)
     - Irrigation season storage on Sept 30 (refill metric)
     - Number and timing of prerelease events per year

2. **Major event replay** (3–5 large floods from the historical record):
   - For each major event, show 30-day traces of:
     - Reservoir level trajectory under each scheme
     - Downstream discharge under each scheme
     - Forecasted inflow vs actual inflow (to show when the forecast was correct/wrong)
   - Narrative: explain mechanism (e.g., "Scheme 2 released water early because the forecast signaled a major monsoon surge; the actual peak was 2 days later, and the reservoir had recovered to near the seasonal FLWL by then without spilling")

### 4.2 Robustness and Sensitivity Tests

**Critical uncertainties:**

1. **Forecast accuracy degradation**:
   - Repeat the hindcast using downgraded forecast ensembles (e.g., forecast error doubled) or using only a 2-day lead time instead of 5 days
   - Do the staged schemes still maintain safety?

2. **Hydrological nonstationarity**:
   - Divide the 45 years into two sub-periods (first 22 years vs last 23 years)
   - Re-run schemes on each period separately to check robustness across wet/dry regimes

3. **Rule parameter sensitivity**:
   - Sweep the seasonal FLWL bounds (±0.2 m from the proposed values)
   - Sweep the forecast-based release threshold (peak-inflow quantile 40%, 50%, 60%)
   - For each parameter set, record the risk metrics and benefits; identify which parameters matter most

### 4.3 What Counts as Sufficient Evidence?

**For operational recommendation, the staged FLWL should:**

1. **Maintain or reduce flood risk**:
   - P(downstream Q > Q_safe) under staged FLWL ≤ P(downstream Q > Q_safe) under fixed FLWL over the 45-year period
   - No exceedance of the reservoir's design-flood level under any major historical event
   - Confidence: test over at least 3 representative large floods (100-year or larger) with a clear mechanism explaining why the staged rule is at least as safe

2. **Demonstrate water-use efficiency gain**:
   - Measurable reduction in annual spill: e.g., 10%+ reduction in average annual spill volume (e.g., 5–10% of total annual inflow)
   - Improved refill by end of irrigation season: e.g., storage on Sept 30 is 5–10% higher under the staged rule
   - Both metrics should be consistent across the 45-year period and robust to ±0.2 m changes in the FLWL bounds

3. **Demonstrate operational feasibility**:
   - Prerelease volume required to switch into the higher seasonal FLWL can be discharged within 3 days (the RFH) without exceeding downstream safe capacity
   - No false alarms: cases where an unnecessary prerelease occurs because the forecast predicts a large inflow that doesn't materialize should be <10% of prerelease events
   - Fallback procedure: specify what the operator should do if a forecast-based release fails to bring the level down in time

4. **Show robustness**:
   - Water-use gain holds even if forecast accuracy degrades by 50%
   - Safety holds even if only a 2-day reliable forecast is available (not 5 days)
   - The benefit is not sensitive to ±0.2 m perturbations in the proposed seasonal FLWL values

### 4.4 Deployment and Adoption Readiness

**Before recommending a change to the operating manual:**

- Consultation: present the proposed staged rule to irrigation and power operators, the flood-control authority, and the town's flood-management officials
- Pilot testing (if feasible): run the staged rule in "shadow mode" (compute but do not implement for one monsoon) to detect any unexpected issues
- Training: ensure operators understand the seasonal FLWL schedule and the forecast-based logic for switching
- Emergency clause: establish a fallback to the fixed FLWL if forecast quality degrades unexpectedly or if a major malfunction occurs

## Data and Methods Summary

| Component | Data Required | Method |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Baseline rule | 45 years daily inflows, level–storage, level–discharge curves | Encode current rule in simulator; hindcast 45 years to establish baseline spill/refill/risk |
| Forecast quality | 8 years of forecast & observation pairs, 1–5-day leads | Compute bias, error variance, qualified rate by lead time; define RFH |
| Staged FLWL design | Monsoon climatology, downstream safe capacity | Propose 2–3 candidate schemes; check feasibility (drawdown time, discharge capacity) |
| Safety thresholds | Design flood level, downstream levee capacity | Define Z_max and Q_safe; set acceptable-risk benchmark as ≤ baseline risk |
| Simulation | 45-year inflow, 8-year forecast archive (for scenarios), reservoir curves | Daily mass-balance simulator with spillway/routing; run hindcasts with perfect hindsight and with actual forecasts |
| Robustness | 45-year record split into sub-periods, synthetic degraded forecasts | Holdout test (1st half vs 2nd half); sensitivity to forecast accuracy and rule parameters |
| Mechanism | Major flood event inflows, forecasts, releases | Trace-based narrative explaining how the staged rule outperforms or matches the fixed rule |

## Key Assumptions and Limitations to Track

1. **Forecast assumption**: the 1–5-day forecasts available are representative of what will be available for operational deployment (training period 2015–2023 may not reflect all flood types)
2. **Fixed downstream capacity**: assumes the town's safe discharge capacity (Q_safe) does not change and no new protection infrastructure is built
3. **Stationarity**: the 45-year inflow record and 8-year forecast error distribution are assumed representative of future conditions (not accounting for climate change)
4. **Operational simplicity**: the staged FLWL rule is assumed implementable by the existing operator team without major automation or new personnel training burden
5. **Negligible intermediate inflow**: assumes that local tributaries joining between the dam and the town (40 km downstream) contribute negligibly (this should be verified)

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Immediate** (Week 1–2): Assemble and validate the 45-year inflow and 8-year forecast archive; encode the baseline rule in a simulator
2. **Pre-screening** (Week 3–4): Test forecast lead times and propose 2–3 candidate seasonal FLWL schemes; conduct feasibility checks
3. **Hindcast simulation** (Week 5–8): Run the full 45-year hindcast with each candidate scheme; compute flood risk and water-use metrics
4. **Robustness testing** (Week 9–10): Conduct sensitivity tests, holdout tests, and the narrative analysis of major events
5. **Consultation and decision** (Week 11–12): Present findings to the operating authority and decide whether to recommend adoption, further research, or pilot testing

**This study design should require 12–16 weeks with a team of 2–3 people familiar with reservoir hydrology and operations.**
