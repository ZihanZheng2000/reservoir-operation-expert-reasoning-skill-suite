# Judgment U3-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- Verdict: do not adopt on this evidence. The 18% is conditional on the error model and the single design hydrograph. "95% confidence" here measures Monte Carlo spread under an assumed model, not confidence about the real reservoir.
- Error model: pooling mixes many low-flow, short-lead days with a few flood, day-3 errors. Errors are heteroscedastic, lead-dependent and seasonal. A normal fit has thin tails and is symmetric, and it misses the typical systematic under-forecast of large peaks. Twelve years hold no 100-year event, so the tail is extrapolated. The analyst should also ask whether the archive is operational forecasts or hindcasts.
- Independence, with a calculation. If daily errors with SD σ are independent, the SD of the 3-day volume error is σ√3 ≈ 1.73σ. If they are fully correlated, it is 3σ. Independent sampling therefore understates the volume-error SD by about 42% (a factor of √3) in the persistent-bias case. In general, Var = σ²(3 + 4ρ₁ + 2ρ₂). The prerelease volume is set by forecast volume, so this bias favours the new rule.
- Monte Carlo arithmetic. Out of 5,000 runs, the worst 1% is 50 runs and the worst 0.1% is 5 runs. Tail estimates are therefore weak. The comparison should be paired, and convergence should be shown.
- Feasibility arithmetic. Creating a prerelease volume V in 72 h needs a mean extra release of V / 259,200 s. For example, 60 million m³ (10% of total storage) needs about 231 m³/s. That figure must be checked against outlet capacity, ramping limits and safe downstream channel capacity.
- Metrics: maximum pool level and dam-safety exceedance, downstream stage with tributary coincidence, the false-alarm and refill cost, and second-storm exposure.
- Validation: backtest on the forecasts as actually issued, baseline replay, out-of-sample tuning, and several hydrograph shapes and return periods. Bounds from perfect-forecast and no-forecast runs. The breakeven forecast skill.
- Practice: dam-safety regulator approval, an amendment to the operating manual, prerelease caps tied to forecast reliability, a fallback or revert trigger, shadow operation, and post-event review.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** It finds almost every deciding issue: pooling, normal tails and bias, independence across days and leads, hindcast versus operational forecasts, the meaning of "95%", the single design hydrograph, initial pool, peak outflow as an incomplete metric with maximum level, false alarms and refill, baseline fairness and in-sample tuning, and the backtest. It does not check whether the prerelease can physically be completed within the 3-day lead time given outlet and downstream capacity. That is a deciding question for a forecast-based prerelease rule. It also does not ask for perfect-forecast and no-forecast bounds.
- **TC:** The concepts are correct. It notes that independent noise cancels and that correlated error does not, though it does not quantify this (√3). The tail arithmetic (worst 1% ≈ 50 runs) is correct. The critique of the confidence claim is correct and precise: it is conditional on the model, shrinks with the number of runs, and its estimand is ambiguous. It states with some confidence that pooling understates the flood-day error. That is usually true for absolute errors but is not shown, which is a minor issue.
- **ME:** The plan is executable and well ordered: error re-characterisation (stratify, test tails, model correlation, block bootstrap by year or event), rebuilt simulation (paired runs, convergence, multiple shapes), broader metrics, robustness scaling (×1.5, ×2, outage), validation, and a restated claim. It has a priority order. It does not include a breakeven skill analysis, though the robustness scaling partly substitutes for one.
- **PR:** Strong. It covers the dam-safety authority, regulatory limits on prerelease, downstream warning and liability, decision criteria agreed in advance (no increase in dam-safety exceedance), shadow operation, revert triggers, post-event review and periodic re-estimation of the error model. It lacks the lead-time and outlet-capacity feasibility check.
- **CA:** It is well scoped. Assumptions are explicit (flood-control storage, initial pool and spillway capacity are unknown). It says the 18% "may be real" and that the evidence is "an indication". It asserts the direction of the pooling bias somewhat firmly.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- The variance arithmetic for independence. For daily SD σ, the SD of the 3-day volume error is σ√3 under independence and 3σ under full correlation. The model therefore understates volume uncertainty by up to about 42%, or more generally Var = σ²(3 + 4ρ₁ + 2ρ₂). The firm's own data can estimate ρ.
- An order-of-magnitude feasibility calculation: required prerelease volume / 259,200 s, e.g. 60 million m³ ≈ 231 m³/s mean extra release over 3 days. Compare this with outlet capacity and the bankfull or safe channel flow. The available flood-control fraction of the 600 million m³ total must also be obtained.
- An explicit statement that a 12-year record gives very few independent flood events. The effective sample size for flood-regime errors may be fewer than about 10–20 events, so any stratified fit needs a pooled-tail or regional approach with stated uncertainty.
- A worst-case or dominance check. In the runs with the largest under-forecast, the new rule should not leave the pool lower than the current rule's minimum safe margin when the peak arrives, and should not raise the maximum pool above the current rule's value. This should be a formal acceptance test.
- Risk-based integration: weight results across return periods (expected annual damage or the change in exceedance probability of critical stages), rather than judging on the 100-year event alone.
