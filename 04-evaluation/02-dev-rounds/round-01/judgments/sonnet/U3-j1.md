# Judgment U3-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- The "95% confidence" figure is at best Monte Carlo sampling confidence that is conditional on the assumed error model. It leaves out parameter uncertainty (12 years of data), model-form uncertainty (normality and independence), design-flood uncertainty and simulator uncertainty. The estimand has to be defined: mean reduction, a quantile, or the share of runs in which the new rule is better.
- Independence calculation. Take a 3-day forecast volume with daily error sigma. If the errors are independent, the 3-day volume error has standard deviation sqrt(3)*sigma ≈ 1.73 sigma. If they are fully correlated (whole-event bias), it is 3 sigma. Independent sampling therefore understates the event-volume error by about 42% (a factor of about 1.7). That is the quantity a prerelease decision depends on. Monte Carlo precision is high: for p = 0.95 and n = 5000, SE ≈ 0.003. So a tight interval says nothing about validity.
- Pooling: errors are heteroscedastic (they grow with flow), they grow with lead time, and they vary by season. Flood-peak forecasts are often biased low and heavy-tailed. Twelve years almost certainly contain no 100-year event, so the error model is extrapolated.
- Design flood: a single hydrograph shape. Volume, shape and initial pool matter. Several return periods are needed, including beyond-design and check floods.
- Metrics: maximum pool level and dam-safety freeboard, downstream flow and stage against safe channel capacity, false alarms, refill, and losses to water supply and hydropower. Paired comparison of the two rules, with the tail of runs in which the new rule is worse.
- Feasibility: can the prerelease volume actually be evacuated within the 3-day lead time, given outlet capacity and the safe downstream discharge? For example, Q_safe × 3 × 86,400 compared with the needed flood space within the 600 million m³ total.
- Validation: backtest on real issued forecasts, operational rather than hindcast. Check the baseline rule's fidelity and the simulator's calibration. Out-of-sample testing of the tuned rule.
- Stress test to a breakeven forecast skill. Fallback rule, approval by the regulator and the dam-safety authority, shadow or pilot operation, revert triggers.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** Finds nearly all the deciding questions:
  - the conditional meaning of the 95% figure and its undefined estimand;
  - pooling, normality, independence and extrapolation to the 100-year flood;
  - design-flood shape and volume;
  - maximum level versus outflow;
  - false alarms and refill;
  - feasibility within the lead time.

  It also adds information bounds (no forecast versus perfect forecast) and a fixed-margin prerelease control to isolate the value of the forecast. These are strong points. It does not quantify how much independence understates the error. It misses hindcast versus operational forecasts and in-sample tuning.
- **TC:** No errors. It is careful about the direction of the pooling bias (relative versus absolute errors), which is more correct than a blanket claim. It does no arithmetic: there is no sqrt(n) illustration and no prerelease-volume check.
- **ME:** The program is executable and roughly prioritized: backtest, rebuild the error model (copula, VAR, trajectory resampling), stress test to breakeven accuracy, safety metrics, wider floods, feasibility, independent re-run. Its gaps are no event- or year-block bootstrap for parameter uncertainty and no explicit acceptance criteria.
- **PR:** Covers feasibility within the 3-day lead time (outlet capacity and ramping), downstream travel time and local inflow, a fallback rule, approval by the authority, and a staged pilot. It is less specific than Answer 2 on shadow operation, revert triggers and pre-agreed decision criteria.
- **CA:** Well scoped. It states its assumptions explicitly, does not claim a direction for the pooling bias, and presents the 18% as model-conditional. The vague references to "papers have reported" are a minor weakness.

### Answer 2
- **KQ:** Covers the same core set: pooling, normality, independence and cross-lead correlation, sample size, design-flood shape, initial conditions, metrics, false alarms and the meaning of the 95% figure. It adds several deciding points:
  - hindcast versus operational forecasts and changes to the forecast system over the 12 years;
  - timing (phase) errors that additive noise cannot capture;
  - how the synthetic forecast is built ("truth plus noise");
  - in-sample tuning and overfitting;
  - tail convergence (the worst 1% is about 50 runs).

  It does not explicitly check whether the prerelease volume can be evacuated within the lead time, and it lacks the perfect-forecast and no-forecast bounds.
- **TC:** Correct overall. It says the pooled fit "will understate the error in a flood at day 3". That is plausible under absolute errors but stated more firmly than the evidence allows. The "worst 1% ≈ 50 runs" check is right. It gives no quantified effect of independence.
- **ME:** Concrete and ordered:
  - stratify the errors and test relative versus additive models;
  - tail-focused fit tests;
  - copula, AR or event-block bootstrap of the errors;
  - bootstrap parameter uncertainty by year or event, not by day;
  - replay of the largest events;
  - paired simulation with tail convergence;
  - continuous simulation for downside costs;
  - error scaling (×1.5, ×2) and forecast outage tests.

  Slightly more executable than Answer 1.
- **PR:** Names specific provisions: dam-safety authority review, downstream notification and liability, decision criteria agreed in advance (no increase in the probability of exceeding the dam-safety level), shadow operation over seasons, revert triggers, post-event reviews, re-estimation of the error model when the forecast system changes, and a fail-safe fallback to the current rule. Its weakness is that it does not check physical feasibility within the lead time.
- **CA:** Mostly scoped to the evidence. "Many jurisdictions limit prerelease rates" is an unsupported generalization. It is slightly more assertive than Answer 1 about the direction of the pooling bias.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:**
- information-value bounds (perfect and no forecast) and a fixed-margin control rule;
- breakeven forecast accuracy;
- maximum level as the dam-safety metric that can trade off against outflow;
- feasibility of the prerelease within the 3-day lead time, given outlet and ramping limits;
- a careful, undecided treatment of the direction of the pooling bias.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong:**
- No hindcast-versus-operational distinction and no check on forecast-system versions.
- No in-sample tuning or out-of-sample test of the rule itself.
- No block or event bootstrap for parameter uncertainty.
- No timing-error component.
- No pre-agreed acceptance criteria, shadow mode or revert triggers.
- Unspecific literature references.

**Answer 2, done well:**
- operational versus hindcast forecasts and forecast-system change;
- timing error and the "truth plus noise" construction;
- event- or year-block bootstrap;
- tail-convergence arithmetic (50 runs in the worst 1%);
- in-sample tuning;
- continuous simulation for downside costs;
- error-scaling and outage tests;
- concrete governance (pre-agreed criteria, shadow operation, revert triggers, re-estimation).

**Answer 2, missing or wrong:**
- No check that the prerelease volume can physically be evacuated within 3 days, given outlet capacity and safe downstream discharge.
- No perfect-forecast or no-forecast bounds, and no breakeven skill as a single decision number. It only scales errors.
- The direction of the pooling bias is asserted rather than conditioned on whether errors are relative or absolute.
- An unsupported claim about regulation.

**Points an expert answer must contain that neither had:**
- A quantified effect of independence. For a 3-day volume, sigma_indep = sqrt(3)·sigma ≈ 1.73 sigma, against 3 sigma under full correlation. Independent sampling understates the event-volume error by about 42%, and that is the error that governs prerelease sizing.
- The Monte Carlo SE for the 95% figure (≈ 0.003 at n = 5000), showing that the "confidence" reflects simulation size, not validity.
- A worked feasibility check of the prerelease volume (safe release × lead time × 86,400 s) against the flood-control space within the 600 million m³.
- An asymmetric framing of risk. Under-forecasts cause insufficient prerelease and a higher pool and dam-safety risk. Over-forecasts cause water loss and possibly prerelease-induced downstream flooding. The acceptance test should be one-sided on dam safety.
- An explicit re-check of design-flood and spillway adequacy if the rule changes the flood-control pool allocation.
