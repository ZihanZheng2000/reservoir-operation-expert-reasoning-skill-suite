# Judgment U5-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own working)

- Storage: Upper 120 M m³ free, 180 M m³ occupied (most it can still gain). Lower 425 M m³ free, 75 M m³ occupied. System free 545 M m³, more than the 400 M m³ high case.
- Current trends: upper net drawdown 300 m³/s, which is 32.4 M m³ over 30 h, giving about 152 M m³ free at arrival. Lower inflow is about 1,800 m³/s (1,500 upper release + 300 tributary, if steady), so net drawdown is 700 m³/s, which is 75.6 M m³ over 30 h. The lower dam reaches 100% free in about 30 h on its own. Extra prerelease at the lower dam therefore adds almost nothing, which makes (b) close to moot.
- Upper prerelease sizing: raising release by ΔQ for 30 h gains ΔQ x 0.108 M m³ per m³/s. 2,000 m³/s gives about 206 M m³ free. About 2,870 m³/s gives the full 300 M m³ free. Even fully empty, the upper dam cannot store the 400 M m³ high case, so it must pass flood water downstream in any case. The real question is routing: peak outflow and timing at the lower dam, not volume storage alone.
- Cost of (c): after 12 h at 1,500 m³/s the upper dam has about 133 M m³ free, with 18 h left. The same target then needs a higher release rate, which arrives at the lower dam closer to the flood and to any tributary storm.
- Timing: flood at upper dam at about 30 h, at lower dam at about 48 h, at the city at about 58 h or later. The tributary responds to the same storm and may peak before the routed upper flood. Prerelease water reaches the lower dam between 18 and 48 h.
- Regulation: storage that occupies flood-control space above the flood-limited level normally has to be evacuated by rule. Drawing the upper reservoir back toward its flood-limited level is therefore a mandated, low-regret action rather than "lost water", and refill is not allowed in flood season. A good answer also gives a lower-dam release cap of 6,000 m³/s minus local inflow, downstream warnings for prerelease rises, a dispatch order and authority, and an interim forecast request.
- Conclusion: (a) in the form of an upper-led prerelease with the lower dam held, with explicit triggers on volume, timing, tributary coincidence and local inflow.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (U5-8124)
- **KQ:** It finds the deciding questions. The upper dam is the scarce, time-critical reservoir. The lower dam's 75 M m³ ceiling makes (b) weak. It counts the 12 h lost lead time under (c), tributary coincidence, and local inflow below the lower dam. It says "volume alone is not a city-level problem." It frames the upper dam as having a "shortfall" (storage vs. volume), but it does recognise that the lower dam catches the pass-through. It does not settle the peak-routing question, and it says why it cannot. Score 7.
- **TC:** The core arithmetic is correct: 152 M m³ status quo; 2,500 m³/s gives net 1,300 m³/s, 140 M m³, 260 M m³ free; 75 M m³ lower headroom; 3,500 m³/s city margin. There are small slips. A 12 h arrival error at 1,300 m³/s net is about 56 M m³, not 47. The "900 vs 1,500 extra" catch-up figures are loose; the real figures are about 1,000 vs about 1,660. Neither slip changes the conclusion. The implied loss under the low case (about 110 M m³) is framed as mostly recoverable by refill, which is doubtful in flood season. Score 7.
- **ME:** It gives an ordered, executable plan: a staged ramp to 2,000 then 2,500 m³/s; a lower dam held and then matched to inflow at +18 h; information requests; pre-agreed triggers so the 12 h decision is a threshold check. It has a rule of thumb for the tributary coincidence. There is no explicit storage or routing verification test. Score 7.
- **PR:** It covers ramping limits, reach capacity, minimum releases, the flood-limited level floor, logging and agreeing triggers with headquarters, and escalation to the decision authority. It gives no downstream or public warning for the raised releases, and it does not ask for an interim forecast. Score 6.
- **CA:** Assumptions are explicit (no local inflow, steady tributary, discretionary releases). Thresholds are labelled as provisional. Refill recoverability is marked as assumed. Score 8.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:** correct core storage and drawdown arithmetic; a clear reason (b) is the "wrong reservoir"; the explicit 3,500 m³/s city margin and the local-inflow assumption stated up front; a good trigger table that includes local inflow, a second event, and upper-dam infeasibility; a decision framing that is cheap, reversible and early.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong:**
- The "shortfall" framing implies the upper dam must store the whole volume, but it can pass the flood through to a lower dam with 425 M m³ free.
- The 12 h arrival-error drawdown figure (47 should be about 56 M m³) and the catch-up release figures are loose.
- It assumes refill is possible, which is doubtful in flood season.
- There is no downstream or public warning for higher releases.
- It does not request an interim forecast.
- It has no quantitative storage or routing check at the lower dam.

**Answer 2, done well:** it frames the upper dam as a pass-through with routing risk; the timing chain to the city is explicit; it runs a correct coincidence storage check; its storage test is a usable trigger; its practical steps are stronger (state confirmation, emergency-service alert, 6 h interim forecast, named deciders, supply and hydropower trade-off).

**Answer 2, missing or wrong:**
- The cost of waiting (about 4,000 m³/s, 2.5x) is wrong against its own 206 M m³ target, which overstates the case against (c).
- The "90 M m³ more than status quo" figure is wrong; it should be about 54.
- Its upper-end trigger ("exceeds 400") equals the current upper bound, so it barely acts as a threshold.
- The moderate target (about 206 M m³) is not tied to any routing calculation of what upper peak outflow it would allow.

**Expert points neither answer had:**
- Storage above the flood-limited level in the upper reservoir is normally required to be evacuated by the operating rules. Returning toward the flood-limited level is therefore low-regret and largely regulation-driven, not discretionary "lost water". The refill argument does not apply in flood season.
- Even fully empty (300 M m³), the upper dam cannot hold the 400 M m³ high case. The decision variable is upper peak outflow and its timing at the lower dam, so a crude peak estimate from the volume and a typical hydrograph shape should be made.
- The prerelease timing must be planned against the tributary. Prerelease water reaches the lower dam between 18 and 48 h. The tributary, driven by the same storm, may peak before the routed upper flood. So prerelease should be front-loaded and tapered before the expected tributary rise.
- Downstream channel-capacity and public warnings are needed for the prerelease itself, not only for the flood peak.
- The upper dam's surcharge and dam-safety level should define a hard trigger to switch to dam-safety operation.
