## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Pre-release (drawdown) capacity method. The water above 152 m must be released after the decision chain ends and before the flood arrives, without exceeding the town's safe discharge.
- Allowable release = 5,000 - 1,200 = 3,800 m3/s, because the ungauged intervening flow must be assumed coincident. The 7,500 m3/s capacity does not bind.
- Net drawdown = 3,800 - 1,500 = 2,300 m3/s. Effective time = 36 - 8 = 28 h = 100,800 s. Removable volume is about 231.8 Mm3, which is more than 180 Mm3. In theory, then, 154 m or slightly above. The level above 154 m cannot be fixed because the storage curve above 154 m is not given.
- Degraded cases:
  - With the 6 h update lag (22 h), removable volume is about 182.2 Mm3, roughly a 1% margin.
  - Break-even inflow at 28 h is about 2,014 m3/s; at 22 h it is about 1,527 m3/s.
  - The 36 h lead time may refer to the peak rather than the start of the rise. The reservoir must be back at 152 m when inflow passes the allowable release, not when the peak arrives.
- Verdict: 154 m is a theoretical ceiling, not a standing operating limit. Use a lower provisional ceiling, or a forecast-gated conditional ceiling with a fallback.
- Margins to cover: forecast update lag; forecast timing and volume error and missed events; chain time and gate ramp; inflow rising during drawdown; uncertainty in the intervening flow; release capacity by head and with one gate out; downstream warning and ramp limits; drawdown-rate limits for dam and bank safety; storage-curve and level-measurement error.
- Verifications:

  - historical event replay;
  - design and check flood routing from the raised level in the missed-forecast case;
  - risk compared with the current 152 m rule;
  - sensitivity tests;
  - drills;
  - regulatory approval (dynamic flood-limited level control needs approval from the flood-control authority).
- Missing information: the storage curve, the discharge curve, gate details, travel time, the basis of the 1,200 m3/s figure, forecast skill, design and check hydrographs, flood-season staging, regulatory route, and the benefit of the extra storage.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had

- Name the method and its regulatory basis: dynamic control of the flood-limited water level by the forecast pre-release capacity method. Approval runs through the basin or provincial flood-control headquarters and the water administration, and the upper limit must be revised in the approved operating rules.
- Check that the pre-release itself does not cause harm. The 2,300 m3/s of pre-release must be routed to the town with the forecast storm's own intervening rise. Downstream river users and people on the banks need warning about a man-made rise in flow during dry weather.
- Bound the intervening flow probabilistically. The 1,200 m3/s figure should carry a return period that matches the town's flood-protection standard. A coincidence analysis of the intervening peak and the reservoir flood is needed before the 3,800 m3/s cap can be relaxed or tightened.
- Explicitly require that the raised level does not raise the design flood level or the check flood level, and does not lower the downstream protection standard. These are the standard acceptance criteria for a dynamic control range.
- Stage the limit across the flood season (pre-flood, main flood, late flood), with the typhoon season possibly kept at 152 m.
