# Judgment U1-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- A research question that treats flood safety as a non-compensable constraint (non-inferiority against the current rule and against the dam's design/check flood standard), with water-use gain as the objective.
- **Stage division from data.** Use the 45-year daily series: POT and annual-maximum timing, circular statistics or change-point/Fisher optimal partition, variability of monsoon onset and withdrawal, and few stages (2–3).
- **Seasonal design floods consistent with the annual standard.** This is the deciding technical point. If each stage is designed to the same return period from its own seasonal series, the annual exceedance probability rises: P_annual = 1 − Π(1 − p_i). The stage frequencies must therefore be allocated so that the combined annual risk does not exceed the annual standard, or each stage flood must be checked against the annual design flood. Flood routing of the stage design and check floods (in India, per CWC/IS 11223 criteria, often PMF/SPF for large dams) must start from the staged FLWL, and the maximum level must stay at or below the design/maximum water level.
- **Transition and drawdown feasibility, with a calculation.** The extra storage ΔV between the staged and fixed FLWL (≈ A·Δh from the level–storage curve) has to be released within the reliable lead time at a rate that stays below both the outlet capacity and the downstream safe discharge minus local inflow. Required time t ≈ ΔV / (Q_release,max − Q_in). Compare t with the forecast lead time and its skill on large events.
- **Downstream conditions.** Safe discharge at the town and local inflow between dam and town. Travel time over 40 km is hours, not days, so a daily step is too coarse and needs peak-to-daily ratios or sub-daily checks.
- **Forecasts.** An 8-year error model by lead time and magnitude, a separation of no-forecast and forecast-informed tiers, synthetic forecasts for the other years, and a perfect-foresight upper bound.
- **Benefits.** Irrigation reliability, hydropower (head and energy), spill, and refill probability, reported by year type (dry/normal/wet).
- **Robustness.** Out-of-sample testing, stochastic ensembles, onset shifts, non-stationarity, and degraded operation.
- **India-specific practice.** Approval of rule-curve changes through the state water resources department and the CWC, with obligations under the Dam Safety Act 2021 (NDSA/SDSO); trial operation, a fallback rule, and coordination with the downstream district warning system.
- **Sufficiency criteria** set in advance, with graded recommendations (change / pilot / no change).
