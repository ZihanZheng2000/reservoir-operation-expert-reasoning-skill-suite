# Judgment U1-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's reference)

- A research question framed as a safety-constrained comparison: staged FLWL against the current fixed FLWL, with flood safety as a non-inferiority constraint and water-use benefit as the objective.
- Seasonality analysis to set the stages. Use flood-timing statistics such as POT dates, circular statistics, Fisher optimal partition or change-point methods on the 45-year daily series, and account for variability in monsoon onset and withdrawal.
- A stage-wise design flood frequency analysis (peaks and 1/3/5-day volumes) with uncertainty, reconciled with the official design flood. In India this is usually SPF/PMF-based per CWC/BIS practice.
- A flood-routing check for each stage. Start the stage design flood (and the check flood) at the proposed staged FLWL and verify that the maximum level stays below the design and check flood levels, and that the release stays below the town's safe discharge net of intermediate local inflow.
- An explicit drawdown and pre-release feasibility calculation:
  - Drawdown time T = ΔV / (Q_release,allowed − Q_in), where ΔV = S(Z_staged) − S(Z_base) comes from the level–storage curve and Q_release,allowed = min(level–discharge capacity, Q_safe,town − Q_local).
  - Compare T with the effective lead time: the reliable forecast lead time minus decision and gate-operation delay, plus or minus the 40 km travel time (a few hours to about 12 h).
  - Equivalently, the maximum allowable rise is ΔV_max = (Q_allowed − Q_in) × T_eff. This is the core calculation that links the 1–5 day forecasts to how high a stage level can go.
- A forecast-error-based risk analysis. Fit an error model by lead time and magnitude from the 8 years, run Monte Carlo or synthetic forecasts over 45 years, and report the risk of exceeding the design level or the safe discharge against a benchmark.
- Benefit evaluation of refill probability, spill, hydropower energy and head, and irrigation reliability, as distributions and in dry years.
- Data gaps and fallbacks: downstream safe discharge, local inflow, operation records, demand and plant data, and sub-daily data.
- Sufficiency criteria fixed in advance: non-inferiority with uncertainty, out-of-sample results, robustness, operability, a fallback to the fixed rule, and the regulatory route (CWC guidelines on rule curves, the Dam Safety Act 2021, the state irrigation or water resources department), with a pilot and monitoring.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** It identifies the deciding questions explicitly: safe discharge versus outlet capacity including local inflow, drawdown achievable within lead time, and late- or early-season extremes. Step 3 sets out the core feasibility logic as "ceilings" (time, space, downstream, transitions), and it separates the no-forecast rule from the forecast layer. It settles most questions well. It does not turn the time ceiling into the drawdown formula.
- **TC:** The concepts are correct throughout: daily data understate peaks, reconstructed inflow is smoothed, and zero exceedances in a sample are not zero risk. The wording of the "Time" ceiling is muddled ("the level above the lowest stage level can be restored by the next-stage level"). It constrains every stage to be no lower than the current FLWL. That is a defensible design choice, but it rules out compensating by lowering the core-season level. There is no arithmetic and no explicit ΔV/ΔQ expression. None of this changes the conclusion.
- **ME:** The steps are ordered and executable: baseline encoding and validation, stage-wise regime analysis, feasible ceilings, then a small-parameter search with full simulation. The event set includes scaled, stochastic, initial-state, boundary-shift and worst-case transition floods. It adds forecast characterization, a comparison of no / archived / perfect forecasts, holdout testing, a second-method check and bootstrap. The work plan has decision points, including an early stop. The main gap is that the drawdown quantity is never written as a calculation.
- **PR:** It mentions agreeing criteria with the operating authority, a pilot with monitoring, an explicit fallback to the current rule, post-event review and a measurement-based fallback when forecasts fail. It has no Indian regulatory specifics (CWC, Dam Safety Act, state approval), no degraded-operation test (gate out), and no limit on the release ramp rate.
- **CA:** Very well calibrated. It treats a negative result as a valid outcome, states that thresholds are placeholders, grades recommendations by evidence level, gives a "what would change my judgment" section, and refuses to report energy without plant data.

### Answer 2
- **KQ:** It covers seasonality, the downstream threshold, local inflow, the forecast tiers and worst-timing floods. It recognises the 40 km routing and timing issue. It has no explicit drawdown and lead-time feasibility step tied to the level–discharge curve. The "space" check (a stage design flood routed from the staged level) appears only implicitly in the stress tests. It finds most deciding questions but settles some loosely.
- **TC:** Mostly correct. It flags sedimentation-updated curves, the smoothing of back-calculated inflow and travel time of about one day or less. It suggests valuing energy at a constant price and also reporting in physical units, a minor inconsistency. "Exceedance of normal-high or design flood level" is a loose use of terms. There is no calculation.
- **ME:** It is executable: diagnostics, simulator, a structured candidate family with transition and filling-rate rules, a constrained search with cross-validation by blocks of years, two forecast tiers, synthetic forecasts that preserve autocorrelation, and stress tests including degraded operation and non-stationarity breakpoints. It has no explicit feasibility ceilings per stage before the search, and its verification relies more on operator review.
- **PR:** Somewhat stronger on operations. It includes degraded-operation tests (gate unavailable, delayed response), release rate-of-change as a hazard, upstream submergence, sedimentation, minimum environmental releases and water-sharing agreements. It names the dam-safety authority, approval and a trial period, and requires operability and a forecast fallback. It is still not specific to Indian regulations.
- **CA:** Good. It states that screening-level conclusions are likely with current data, gives tiered recommendation outcomes, says "evidence for the decision, not the decision", and computes EAD only if a damage curve exists. One slightly unsupported item: "a trend indicating the flood season is getting less predictable" as an automatic no-change criterion.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:**
  - Explicit per-stage ceilings (time, space, downstream, transitions) before optimization.
  - Safety treated as non-compensable with uncertainty bounds.
  - Onset and withdrawal shift tests (±1–3 weeks).
  - A comparison of no / archived / perfect forecasts.
  - Peak-to-daily-mean ratio handling.
  - Evidence-graded recommendations and an early-stop decision.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - The time ceiling is not written as T = [S(Z_staged) − S(Z_base)] / (Q_allowed − Q_in), and is not compared with the effective lead time net of 40 km travel time and gate-operation delay.
  - The wording of the time ceiling is garbled.
  - The constraint that every stage stays at or above the current FLWL excludes the classic option of lowering the core stage to fund higher shoulder stages.
  - No degraded-operation or gate-failure test, and no limit on release ramping.
  - No reference to Indian institutions (CWC rule-curve guidance, Dam Safety Act 2021 / state dam safety organisations, IMD/CWC forecasting) or to SPF/PMF design-flood standards.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:**
  - Transition and filling-rate rules.
  - Degraded-operation stress tests.
  - Release rate-of-change and pass-through events as downstream indicators.
  - Upstream submergence and sedimentation constraints.
  - Synthetic forecast errors that preserve autocorrelation.
  - A tiered recommendation standard with a clear regulatory status statement.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - No explicit feasibility calculation linking ΔV, release capacity, downstream safe discharge and forecast lead time.
  - No explicit routing of the stage design flood and check flood from the staged FLWL to the design level.
  - Monetization advice is internally inconsistent.
  - The "less predictable flood season" no-change criterion is unsupported.
  - No perfect-foresight upper bound.
  - Indian regulatory specifics are missing.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- The worked drawdown and pre-release calculation: ΔV_max = (min(Q_capacity(Z), Q_safe − Q_local) − Q_in) × T_eff, with T_eff = reliable lead time − decision and gate delay − travel time to the town. This gives the maximum staged FLWL rise from the level–storage curve. A short illustrative number would show the method.
- Lead-time selection from the forecast archive. Choose the lead time at which forecast error on large events is acceptable, using a conservative error quantile. This sets T_eff rather than assuming 5 days.
- An explicit stage-wise routing of the design and check floods starting from the staged FLWL, using the level–discharge curve. This verifies the maximum level against the design and check flood levels and against the dam crest freeboard.
- A named risk-benchmark formulation, for example a staged-rule exceedance risk no greater than the fixed-rule risk at the design return period, or a stated acceptable risk such as 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ for the dam, to be agreed with the authority.
- Indian institutional context: CWC guidelines for rule curves and reservoir operation, the Dam Safety Act 2021 and the National/State Committees on Dam Safety, the state water resources department as approver, and IMD/CWC flood forecasts as the operational forecast source.
- Formal seasonality-partition methods (Fisher optimal partition, change-point or circular statistics on POT dates), with a minimum length for each stage.
