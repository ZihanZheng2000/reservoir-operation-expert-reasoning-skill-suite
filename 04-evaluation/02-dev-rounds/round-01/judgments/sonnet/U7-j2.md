# Judgment U7-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- Representation: reservoir storage state with mass balance; level-storage table; release bounded by Qcap(h) at the level actually reached, evaluated conservatively inside the step; a reduced linear routing surrogate (lag plus attenuation, Muskingum or impulse response) inside the optimiser, with the calibrated model kept outside as verifier; ungauged tributaries as an uncertain lateral inflow, estimated by residual (control-station observed flow minus routed release) and correlated with the dam-inflow error because the same storm drives both.
- Method: multi-stage scenario (tree) stochastic MPC with non-anticipativity on the first-stage release, risk handled by chance or CVaR constraints, safety ordered lexicographically, re-solved every 6 h with only the first step applied. It should be argued against SDP/SDDP (curse of dimensionality, forecast state hard to encode), deterministic ensemble-mean MPC (hides the tail), independent per-member optimisation (no single implementable release), metaheuristics and RL (no feasibility guarantee, variable runtime, few extremes to learn from), a full hydrodynamic model inside the loop (runtime), and the current rule curve alone (no use of the forecast).
- Calculations, at order of magnitude:
  - Travel time over 120 km. A flood-wave celerity of about 1-2 m/s gives roughly 17-33 h, so 3-6 steps of 6 h. This sets the minimum useful horizon and how much the tributaries erode lead time.
  - Problem size. 51 scenarios × about 40-80 steps × a few variables gives an LP of order 10^4 variables, which solves in seconds. The 30-min limit is therefore set by data handling, the full-model check and any MILP gate logic, not by the LP itself.
  - Tail resolution. With 51 members, one member is about 2%, so smaller exceedance probabilities cannot come from the ensemble alone.
  - Hindcast cycles. 20 yr × 365 × 4 is about 29,000 cycles, which is enough to time runtime percentiles. There are few independent large floods, so reliability statistics on extremes are weak.
- Verification:
  - Runtime percentiles on production hardware, with an anytime fallback.
  - Full-model and exact-curve replay with zero hard-constraint violations.
  - Closed-loop hindcast against the current rule, historical operations, deterministic MPC and perfect foresight.
  - Cross-validated tuning, empirical violation rate compared with the nominal rate, and stress tests.
  - Ensemble reliability by lead time, and consistency of recommendations between cycles.
- Implementability:
  - Gate-step rounding and hold times.
  - Explanation of the binding constraint and the share of members that exceed it.
  - A permissible band.
  - Override logging.
  - Shadow mode and a staged regulatory adoption.
  - A "no worse than the current rule" safety requirement and a rule-based fallback.
- Unknowns: parameterise them, use conservative defaults, run sensitivity tests and set revisit triggers.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: what was done well

- Treats level-dependent capacity as a time-feasibility problem and approximates it conservatively.
- Separates the reduced internal model from the full verifier, and calibrates the surrogate's high-flow error into a margin.
- Estimates the tributaries by inverse mass balance and preserves their dependence (copula or shuffle).
- States the 2% tail-resolution limit of 51 members.
- Makes horizon length dependent on hindcast skill.
- Measures consistency of recommendations between cycles.
- Sets per-requirement pass criteria and an explicit order of work.

### Answer 1: what was missing or wrong

- No numeric travel-time or LP-size estimate.
- Uncited references to "papers".
- No per-cycle audit log for the regulator.
- The tributary residual's contamination by routing and rating error is not discussed.
- No explicit "do-no-harm" limit on pre-release, i.e. pre-releases themselves must stay below the non-damaging downstream flow.

### Answer 2: what was done well

- Non-anticipativity is stated explicitly.
- A variable time grid.
- Correct convexity reasoning for weir-type capacity.
- Uses all 51 members before reducing.
- Calibrates empirical against nominal violation rates with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- A guard-rail envelope with rule-based rejection.
- Re-solves with the override fixed.
- A full reproducibility and audit store.
- Unknowns given an owner, a default and a revisit trigger.

### Answer 2: what was missing or wrong

- No analysis of forecast skill by lead time to justify the 10-day horizon.
- No sweep of tree size and horizon.
- No measure of how stable recommendations are between cycles.
- No numeric travel time.
- A loose solve-time claim, and MILP runtime treated as predictable.
- No permissible release band for operators.
- Residual error sources in the tributary series are not discussed.

### Expert points that neither answer had

- An order-of-magnitude travel-time estimate over 120 km (about 17-33 h at 1-2 m/s celerity, i.e. 3-6 steps of 6 h). It sets the minimum horizon and shows how much tributary flow arriving downstream of the dam can only be handled by margin.
- An explicit problem-size calculation (LP of order 10^4 variables). It shows the 30-min budget is dominated by data handling, the full-model check and MILP gate logic, not by the LP.
- An explicit hindcast count (about 29,000 cycles) versus the much smaller number of independent large floods. Runtime statistics are therefore robust while extreme-event reliability is not.
- The residual tributary series includes routing-model error, control-station rating error and dam-release measurement error. It must be separated or at least bounded, and the reservoir inflow "observations" are themselves back-calculated from storage change.
- A pre-release safeguard: forecast-based pre-releases must not themselves exceed the non-damaging downstream flow, and a "release regret" check must cover false alarms, given the regulator's liability concerns.
- The "do not exceed natural flood peak" or induced-flood principle often required by regulators as an explicit constraint.
