# Judgment U5-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Storage state: upper 120 M m³ free / 180 occupied; lower 425 free / 75 occupied; system 545 free vs 150-400 forecast. Upper alone is short even in the low case (120 < 150). Lower can gain at most 75 M m³ more by prerelease.
- Current balances: upper net drawdown 300 m³/s, about 32 M m³ in 30 h, so about 152 free at arrival. Lower inflow is about 1,800 m³/s (1,500 lagged 18 h plus 300), so its net drawdown is 700 m³/s and it reaches 100% free in about 30 h anyway. Option (b) therefore buys little.
- Prerelease sizing at the upper dam: getting the upper dam to 100% free in 30 h needs about 2,870 m³/s. 2,000 m³/s gives about 206 free and 2,500 m³/s about 260 free.
- Timing: water released from the upper dam now reaches the lower dam at about +18 h, before the flood reaches it at about +48 h, and raises the lower dam's inflow. Flood onset at the city is about +58 h, and there are two forecast updates (+12 h, +24 h) before the flood arrives at the upper dam.
- Downstream capacity: the lower dam can pass up to 6,000 m³/s (less any local inflow). That is about 3,500 m³/s above its current release, a large capacity compared with a mean flood rate of 580-1,540 m³/s. So the city risk comes from peak and coincidence (tributary up to about 1,800 m³/s), not from volume.
- Asymmetric regret, reversibility, explicit triggers, upper-dam safety against an unknown peak, and the authority and approval chain with downstream warning.

It does not note that the upper prerelease reaches the lower dam at +18 h and changes the lower dam's balance. It also does not exploit the lower dam's large pass-through margin to the 6,000 m³/s limit. Score 7.
- **TC**: Most arithmetic is correct: 86 M m³ at 2,000 m³/s, 2,900 m³/s for full drawdown, and the 150 h fill check. However, the option-(c) argument uses a wrong catch-up figure. ## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well**: states the storage asymmetry clearly. Argues well for the lower reservoir as the information buffer with the shortest lag. Gives an explicit storage-test trigger. Includes a city alert and an interim forecast request.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong**:
- The catch-up release for option (c) is miscomputed (about 2,300 m³/s, not about 4,000), which inflates the case against waiting.
- The extra loss over the status quo is overstated (about 54 M m³, not 90).
- The answer ignores that the raised upper release reaches the lower dam at +18 h and changes when the lower dam reaches its flood-limit level.
- It does not use the 3,500 m³/s margin between the lower dam's current release and the safe flow.

**Answer 2, done well**:
- Recognizes that system volume is not the binding problem.
- Accounts for the upper release arriving at +18 h and matches the lower release to it.
- Lists the arrival-time reliability, the possibility of a longer flood, and reach capacity.
- Gives provisional thresholds labelled as such.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong**:
- Small arithmetic slip (47 should be about 56 M m³).
- Little on downstream public warning.
- In the tributary row, the instruction to "reduce or stagger upper release" is not reconciled with the shortage of upper storage, which needs release.
- No upper-dam safety check against an unknown peak.

**Points an expert answer must contain that neither had**:
- Explicit use of the lower dam's sustained pass-through capacity. About 6,000 m³/s over 72 h is about 1,500 M m³, far above the forecast volume, which shows the decisive risk is peak and coincidence timing, not volume.
- A timing sketch of the likely coincidence at the lower dam. The tributary storm probably peaks near +30-40 h, ahead of the upper pass-through at about +48 h or later, so lower-dam space should be reserved for the tributary first.
- An upper-dam safety check. The upper dam can pass 8,000 m³/s; a peak above release capacity plus remaining storage is the real dam-safety risk.
- Post-flood refill and water-supply risk if the low case verifies.
- The formal approval chain for changing releases, notification of downstream users and the reach between the dams, and a pre-agreed fallback if communication or forecasts fail.
- Use of an ensemble or probabilistic reading of the 150-400 range to set the size of the prerelease.
