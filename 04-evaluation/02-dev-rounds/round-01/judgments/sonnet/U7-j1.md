# Judgment U7-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (my own reference)

- Representation: reservoir mass balance on storage, level-storage table, release bounded by level-dependent capacity Qcap(h(S)); a reduced (linear/lag-attenuation) routing surrogate of the 120 km reach calibrated against the full model; the two ungauged tributaries as an uncertain lateral inflow, estimated from control-station residuals, with dependence on the same storm that drives dam inflow.
- Deciding questions: (a) the level-dependent capacity makes pre-release time-critical and makes R <= Qcap(S) potentially non-convex, so it needs a conservative inner linearisation plus exact re-simulation; (b) the dam-to-station travel time. Rough check: 120 km at a flood-wave celerity of about 1-3 m/s gives about 11-33 h. That is 2-6 six-hour cycles, so the horizon and the downstream constraint must carry a lag and the tributaries erode the effective lead time; (c) how to use 51 members within 30 min (scenario tree or reduction with tail retention and non-anticipativity of the first decision); (d) ensemble reliability by lead time, from the hindcasts; (e) risk metric and acceptable risk level. With 51 members the resolution is 1/51, about 2%, so rarer tail risks need stress events or margins.
- Method choice: multi-stage stochastic or scenario-tree MPC as an LP/QP/MILP, with rule-based guard rails and fallback. Contrast with SDP/SDDP, deterministic MPC on the mean, per-member optimisation, metaheuristics, RL, and the full hydraulic model kept inside the loop.
- Verification: timing distribution over the full hindcast against a budget split; replay through the full model and exact curves with zero hard violations; closed-loop hindcast against baselines, including a perfect-foresight bound; out-of-sample tuning; risk calibration; stress tests; cycle-to-cycle stability; gate realisability; shadow mode, logging, override, regulator documentation.
- Unknowns: an assumption register with conservative defaults, sensitivity ranking and revisit triggers. This also covers the cost of a false-alarm pre-release and the rule that pre-release itself must not exceed the non-damaging downstream flow.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** the precise non-convexity diagnosis of R <= Qcap(S) and its conservative start/end-of-step bound with SLP refinement; the residual-based tributary series, with the closed-reach assumption stated; regulator rules as hard guard rails with automatic rejection; an override that is re-solved to show its consequence; risk-parameter calibration so the empirical violation rate matches the nominal one, with a bootstrap CI.
- **Missing or wrong:** no estimate of the dam-to-station travel time, or of how it splits into forecast cycles; no analysis of which lead times have usable skill (it just uses 10 days); no mention that 51 members cannot resolve probabilities below about 2%; no dependence structure between tributary error and the dam-inflow ensemble beyond "condition on the same events"; no cycle-to-cycle stability metric; the problem-size figure is loose; pass criteria are only partly explicit.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** the travel time/horizon linkage; the ~2% tail-resolution limit; skill by lead time limiting the useful horizon; a copula/shuffle dependence between tributary and inflow errors; a scenario-tree argument against per-member and single-sequence formulations; a tree-size/horizon sweep; explicit pass criteria; recommendation stability across cycles; a regulator worked example; a full order of work.
- **Missing or wrong:** unsourced "papers found..." claims; the convexity condition of the capacity constraint is less precisely stated; no automatic envelope-rejection rule; no numeric travel-time bound; it notes that the real-time residual estimate of the tributaries is lagged by the routing time only implicitly.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- A rough travel-time calculation: 120 km at about 1-3 m/s gives about 11-33 h, or 2-6 cycles. It should state that the current release affects the station only after this lag. Real-time tributary estimates from the control-station residual are therefore themselves lagged and need a short-range tributary forecast or a persistence/recession assumption.
- A residual-error decomposition: the residual-based tributary series also absorbs routing-model error, dam-inflow estimation error and rating error at the control station. These must be separated or bounded, or the margin will be mis-sized.
- A no-harm constraint on pre-release: forecast-based pre-release must itself stay below the non-damaging downstream flow, including tributary flow. The answer should also cover the false-alarm cost: lost conservation storage, and refill probability if the flood does not come.
- A numeric time-budget split, for example ingest 3 min, reduction 2 min, solve at most 10 min, full-model check 8 min and report 2 min, with a sizing of the LP (scenarios x steps x variables).
- Explicit compliance with the approved flood-control rule curve or dispatch rules, and how deviations from the rule are authorised (who signs, under what conditions).
