## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Binding release limit is downstream, not gate capacity: 5,000 − 1,200 (ungauged, assume coincident) = 3,800 m³/s. The 7,500 m³/s capacity is not binding. Releasing at 7,500 would put up to 8,700 m³/s at the town.
- Net drawdown rate at typical inflow: 3,800 − 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s.
- Nominal effective time: 36 − 8 = 28 h = 100,800 s, so 2,300 × 100,800 ≈ 231.8 Mm³ can be drawn. That is more than 180 Mm³, so the nominal theoretical ceiling is at or above 154 m. The exact level cannot be stated without the storage curve above 154 m (about 154.6 m only if storage is linear at 90 Mm³/m).
- Degraded timing: the 6 h update cycle can cost up to 6 h, giving 22 h and about 182.2 Mm³. That is almost exactly 180 Mm³, so 154 m has about 1% margin. Further erosion comes from the rising limb arriving before the "flood" time, gate ramp-up, inflow above 1,500 and forecast error. All of these push the safe value below 154 m.
- Conclusion: 154 m is at best the theoretical ceiling. It is not a standing operating limit. Any level above 152 m must be conditional, forecast-gated, tied to inflow, and backed by a fallback, and set only after verification and approval.
- Margins: lead time and update cycle; decision-chain time; gate ramp and staged warnings; inflow rise and forecast error (volume, timing, missed events); intervening-catchment uncertainty and coincidence; travel time and routing; gate outage; storage-curve error; level-measurement error; drawdown-rate limits for slopes and banks; dam-safety levels.
- Verifications: time-stepped water balance with the real curves; design and check flood routing starting from the raised level, including a missed-forecast case; historical forecast hindcast and event replay at 6 h updates; downstream routing; risk comparison against the 152 m rule; sensitivity analysis; drills; regulatory approval (dynamic flood-limited-level control).
- Missing information: storage and discharge curves, gate data, travel time, basis of the 1,200 figure, forecast skill, pre-flood inflow distribution, design and check flood hydrographs and dam-safety levels, flood-season staging, approval route, and the benefit of the extra storage.

### Points an expert answer needs that neither had

- **Chinese regulatory framing.** Raising the level above the design flood-limited level is a dynamic flood-limited-level control scheme. It needs a formal demonstration report and approval by the competent flood-control headquarters or basin authority. It is usually staged by sub-season (pre-flood, main flood, late flood).
- **Forecast coverage of the intervening catchment.** The forecast must also cover rainfall over the intervening catchment (quantitative precipitation forecast). That catchment's storm response may begin before the reservoir flood. The 3,800 m³/s cap may therefore bind for the whole window, and early-window credit above 3,800 may not be available.
- **Release capacity above 152 m.** Capacity at 154 m exceeds 7,500 m³/s. This confirms that the downstream limit is binding at every level in the band. It also means emergency release capacity is not the constraint in the failure case. The dam-safety check is.
- **Drawdown deadline as a rule.** Turn the deadline into an explicit operating rule: a required level-versus-remaining-lead-time curve (a "drawdown guide curve"). Each 6 h update checks the current level against it. This replaces a single fixed ceiling.
- **Downstream flood standard.** The town's flood-protection standard (return period) is the benchmark for acceptable risk. It is needed to state the acceptable residual risk of a higher starting level.
