# Judgment U6-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- Treat downstream flood safety (and dam safety / freeboard) as a constraint, not a tradeable objective. The benchmark is the city's protection standard or the current rule's risk, set by the flood authority before ranking.
- Calculations:
  - 1.0% equals a 1-in-100-year return period. 1.3% is about 1-in-77, and 0.9% about 1-in-111.
  - B's +0.3 points is a 30% relative increase.
  - Over 40 years the expected exceedance count is 0.40 for the current rule and 0.52 for B. The chance of at least one exceedance is 1-0.99^40 ≈ 33% against 1-0.987^40 ≈ 41%. Over a 50-year horizon it is about 39.5% against 48%.
  - If the city is protected to a 100-year standard, B as tabled breaches it.
  - A 40-year record cannot statistically separate 0.9 / 1.0 / 1.3%, so the estimates come from the model tail. Intervals and paired differences on common inflow sequences are required.
- Dominance check:
  - No scheme dominates the current rule.
  - C is nearly but not strictly dominated by A: it has lower generation, worse irrigation and worse navigation, but slightly better flood risk.
  - A is flood-neutral and transfers water from irrigation and navigation to power.
  - B improves three indicators but carries the flood penalty.
- Mechanism check: why does B improve irrigation and navigation (more stored water at the end of the flood season, so refill is easier)? Why does A lose them (power-driven drawdown or release pattern)?
- Severity, duration and tail metrics. Dam-safety margin under the design and check floods. Forecast-based pre-release feasibility (lead time, release capacity, downstream safe discharge).
- Recommendation:
  - Keep the current rule now.
  - Drop C.
  - Redesign B (staged or dynamic flood-limit level, forecast-triggered drawdown) to bring its risk back to at most the current rule's.
  - A is conditional on compensating irrigation and navigation.
- Conditions: regulatory approval of any change to the flood-limit level, a trial period with reversion triggers, flood-authority override in operations, monitoring and post-event review, benefit-sharing.
- Stakeholders with explicit roles:
  - The flood authority and the dam-safety regulator hold gates or vetoes.
  - Hydropower is the proposer, not the evaluator.
  - Irrigation and navigation define their own metrics.
  - Also involved: the forecast service, the grid operator, the environment agency, the public, and independent review.
  - Settle the framework before the numbers.

  - a temporary modification of the operating manual for one or two seasons, with predefined revert triggers;
  - a fallback to a measurement-based rule if forecasts fail;
  - a safety gate held by the flood authority and the dam-safety regulator;
  - post-flood review;
  - compensation or benefit-sharing.

  It is less explicit than Answer 2 on formal regulatory approval of the flood-limit-level change and on override authority in real-time operation.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had

- **Return-period and horizon risk:**
  - 1.0% is a 100-year return period and 1.3% is about 77 years.
  - The probability of at least one exceedance over 40 years is about 33% against about 41%, and over 50 years about 39.5% against about 48%.
  - This makes the 30% relative increase tangible for the city.
- **Explicit compliance test:** if the city's statutory flood standard is 1-in-100, B as tabled is non-compliant regardless of benefits. Only the competent authority can change that standard. The commission cannot.
- **A quantified drawdown-feasibility check for any higher flood-season level:** the extra storage between the current and raised levels must be releasable within the reliable forecast lead time without exceeding the downstream safe discharge. This is the core basis of dynamic flood-limit-level practice.
- **A minimum-detectable-difference statement:** given the simulation ensemble size, state how many synthetic years are needed to resolve a 0.3-point difference, for example via a binomial standard error on the paired difference.
- **Season-phased (staged) flood-limit levels:** set by flood sub-season from the seasonal flood statistics, as a named, standard engineering route for redesigning B, with refill timing tied to the irrigation-storage indicator.
