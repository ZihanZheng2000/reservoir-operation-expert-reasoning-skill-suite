# Judgment U7-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- **Representation.** A reservoir mass balance on storage, with level–storage h(S) and a state-dependent release bound R <= Qcap(h(S)). Because Qcap rises steeply with level, this bound is the main nonlinearity. The answer must say how it is handled: a piecewise-linear or storage-indexed approximation inside the optimiser, and the exact curve in a check afterwards. The 120 km reach needs a fast routing surrogate (Muskingum, linear-reservoir cascade or integrator-delay). It should be identified from the calibrated model, and the calibrated model should re-simulate the chosen release. The two ungauged tributaries are uncertain lateral inflows. Their history can be estimated from the 20 years of control-station observations minus the routed dam outflow (a residual record). That record gives an error model and supports a real-time update from the current residual.
- **Key calculation: travel time and time step.** A flood wave celerity of about 1.5–3 m/s over 120 km gives roughly 11–22 h of travel. So a release change reaches the control station half a day to a day later, while tributary inflow enters with less lead time. That decides two things: (a) a 6 h decision step is about the coarsest acceptable, and the internal routing step should be 1–3 h; (b) the forecast lead time that matters is about 1–3 days plus the routing lag, not "10 days, so yes".
- **Method.** Ensemble or scenario-tree stochastic MPC (receding horizon), with non-anticipativity on the first release. Scenario reduction (51 members down to about 10–20) is used only if the solve time requires it. Risk enters through CVaR or a chance constraint on downstream flow and on the dam's top-of-flood-pool level. Reasons for rejecting the alternatives: SDP suffers from dimensionality and cannot use the ensemble directly; deterministic MPC on the ensemble mean ignores spread; evolutionary or simulation search over the full routing model is too slow and hard to reproduce; reinforcement-learning or black-box policies are hard to explain to a regulator; fixed rules cannot use forecasts.
- **Verification.**
  - Computing time: worst-case wall-clock time for the whole pipeline on the target hardware, including the full-model re-simulation.
  - Physical constraints: replay the recommended releases through the exact curves and the calibrated routing model, and count any violation caused by the linearisation.
  - Reliability: closed-loop hindcast over the 20 years against the existing rule or historical operation, measured by peak reduction, exceedances and end-of-event storage. Also check forecast reliability with rank histograms and CRPS, and test sensitivity to forecast bias and tributary error.
  - Implementability: discrete gate settings, ramp rates, a release held through each 6 h block, and compliance with the approved flood-control dispatch rules.
- **Practical provisions.** The tool is advisory and the operator can override it, with an audit trail. Fallback is the regulator-approved rule curve when inputs or the solver fail. Recommendations must stay inside the approved operating envelope.
- **Unknowns.** The safe downstream discharge or stage, ramp-rate limits, gate failure cases, minimum flows and the regulatory priority order are not given. They should be held as explicit parameters, treated conservatively, run through sensitivity tests and confirmed with the owner before the design is fixed.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (scenario-tree convex MPC)

- **KQ:** Finds most of the deciding questions:
  - how to reduce the ensemble;
  - non-anticipativity of the current release;
  - a fast routing surrogate;
  - tributaries as a residual from the control-station record (a Kalman filter on residuals);
  - extrapolating the capacity curve to extreme levels;
  - forecast bias.

  It does not settle travel time against the decision step. It claims "forecast lead time 10 days, so yes" for implementability, which misses the 11–22 h routing lag. It also does not settle the non-convexity of the capacity bound. Score 6.
- **TC:** Structure is mostly sound: mass balance, release bound depending on level, ramp limits, non-anticipativity, a 40-step horizon. The errors:
  - It claims piecewise-linear curves make the problem convex. Whether R <= Qcap(h(S)) is convex depends on curvature and is not checked.
  - A chance constraint P(Z > Zsafe) <= p is put inside a "convex QP" with no CVaR or integer reformulation.
  - The objective weights are muddled: "α favors storage (avoid spill)" in the pre-release stage, which contradicts pre-release drawdown.
  - The Monte Carlo timing (40,000 runs at 30 s each) is arbitrary.
  - Lumping tributaries at the control station ignores where they enter.

  These are real slips, but the recommended method survives them. Score 5.
- **ME:** Ordered and executable: tree construction, formulation, solver (CVXPY/Clarabel) with estimated problem size, solver-timeout fallback, and a phased build. Verification is detailed: timing profiles, a 20-year constraint audit, hindcast replay, degraded-forecast tests, missing-forecast tests. Gaps: no re-simulation of the chosen release through the calibrated nonlinear model, and no baseline comparison against the current rule. Score 7.
- **PR:**
  - A measurement-based fallback rule.
  - Gate increments rounded to what the gates can actually set.
  - A confidence flag when fewer than 70% of members agree.
  - Explanation text written for the regulator.
  - An operator comprehension trial.
  - Parallel operation over 1–2 flood seasons.
  - Human override and post-event audit.

  Missing: compliance with the approved dispatch or rule-curve envelope, and regulatory sign-off. Score 7.
- **CA:** Invents material the question does not give:
  - unexplained references ("E23-R4", "E17-R2");
  - a named forecast system (HEFS);
  - a hydraulic model (SOBEK);
  - "NSE ≈ 0.97 on similar systems";
  - "adds ~20% uncertainty";
  - rainfall data that was never provided.

  The assumptions list in Section 8 is good. Score 4.

### Answer 2 (ensemble quantile optimisation of a 1–3-parameter release)

- **KQ:** Finds the need to propagate all 51 members, to respect the level-dependent capacity, and to use a risk quantile. It also raises rating-curve and routing-validity uncertainty. It misses:
  - recourse over the horizon (a single rate or ramp across 10 days is open-loop);
  - reservoir level and dam safety as a trade-off, since the objective is downstream peak only;
  - travel time;
  - the residual-based tributary estimate. Tributaries are treated as "base flow" estimated from low-flow data, which is the wrong regime for floods.

  Score 5.
- **TC:**
  - It proposes SQP with finite differences on a quantile-of-maximum objective, which is non-smooth. Finite-difference SQP is a poor fit; the discrete screening it also offers is better.
  - It says a calibrated ensemble brackets the observed peak "~50% of the time". With 51 members the observation should fall inside the ensemble range about 96% of the time (about 49/52); 50% is the median-exceedance rate. It then sets a 60–70% bracketing pass criterion.
  - "Fresh ensemble members improve forecast quality within the 30-minute window" is confused.
  - It lists "MPC on the full ensemble" as a rejected alternative even though its own method is an ensemble receding-horizon scheme.
  - The "<5 minutes" claim is plausible.

  Score 4.
- **ME:** Executable, because simulation-based screening of candidate releases with the calibrated model is simple and fast. Verification of forecast reliability is good: rank histogram, CRPS, spread–skill, a quantile exceedance check. Gaps:
  - the decision parameterisation and the horizon trajectory are undefined;
  - constraints are enforced by "penalty or inequality" without saying which;
  - there is no closed-loop hindcast against a baseline;
  - there is no fallback.

  Score 5.
- **PR:** A good explanation template that includes alternative releases and their outcomes. Advisory, then shared, then supervised use, with override and an audit trail. Weaknesses:
  - a 1–2 week advisory pilot is too short to see floods;
  - "full automation" is suggested for a regulated flood release;
  - no fallback rule;
  - no gate or ramp mechanics beyond a mention;
  - no regulatory rule compliance.

  Score 6.
- **CA:** Fewer invented facts, and it uses the given calibrated model as given. However, it declares "Feasible… technical approach is sound" without evidence. It insists the nonlinear relationships are encoded "without approximation" while also proposing SQP. It calls Phase 1 "Synthetic Gates", a confused label. The ±5/10% rating sensitivity and NSE thresholds are reasonable placeholders. Score 6.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1 — done well:** a scenario tree with non-anticipativity of the first release and recourse later; reduction from 51 members with a scaling test; a solver-timeout fallback; tributary error from control-station residuals with a Kalman update; a conservative lower bound on extreme-level capacity with sensitivity tests; lead-time-dependent risk; a degraded-forecast stress test; parallel running over flood seasons.

**Answer 1 — missing or wrong:**
- Drop the invented references, HEFS, SOBEK, the NSE values and "~20%".
- Show how the state-dependent bound R <= Qcap(h(S)) stays convex, or use successive linearisation followed by an exact-model check.
- Reformulate the chance constraint, for example as CVaR or with scenario indicators.
- Fix the α stage logic so pre-release means drawdown.
- Route tributary inflows at the points where they enter.
- Replace "lead time 10 days, so yes" with a travel-time argument.
- Add a closed-loop hindcast against the existing rule.
- Bind recommendations to the approved dispatch rules.

**Answer 2 — done well:** uses the calibrated routing model directly on all 51 members, which is honest and simple. A quantile risk objective with an exceedance-rate check. Thorough checks of forecast reliability (rank histogram, CRPS, spread–skill). An explanation template showing trade-offs between alternative releases. Advisory and shared rollout with an override audit trail. Explicit sensitivity tests on the rating curve and routing model.

**Answer 2 — missing or wrong:**
- No recourse: a single rate or ramp across 10 days is not a credible flood policy. It needs a staged or tree trajectory with only the first block committed.
- The objective ignores reservoir level and dam-safety risk.
- Tributaries are wrongly treated as low-flow base flow. They should be estimated from control-station residuals.
- SQP on a non-smooth quantile-of-maximum objective; use screening or a CVaR linear program.
- The bracketing statistic is wrong: about 96%, not 50%.
- No fallback rule. A pilot of a few weeks and a "full automation" end state do not fit a regulated release.
- No gate or ramp discretisation, and no compliance with approved rules.

**Points an expert answer needs that neither answer had:**
- The travel-time calculation (about 11–22 h over 120 km) and what it implies for the decision step, the internal routing step and the lead time that actually matters.
- Re-simulating each recommended release through the exact level curves and the calibrated routing model, to catch violations caused by the surrogate or linearisation.
- A closed-loop 20-year hindcast benchmark against the current approved rule: peak reduction, exceedance counts, end-of-event storage.
- Constraining recommendations to the regulator-approved flood dispatch rules and envelope, with documented authority for any deviation.
- An explicit list of unknown engineering constraints with conservative placeholders and owner confirmation: safe downstream discharge or stage, ramp limits, gate outages, minimum flows.
- Holding the release constant over each 6 h block, and limiting release changes to protect downstream areas.
