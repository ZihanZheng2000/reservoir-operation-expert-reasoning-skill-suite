# Judgment U3-j2 (Answer 1 = U3-8emy, Answer 2 = U3-tcdg)

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's reference)

1. **Error model misspecification.** One pooled normal fit ignores several things. Errors grow with lead time (day 1 vs day 3 of a 3-day forecast). Errors are heteroscedastic, because their size scales with flow magnitude, so relative or log errors are more suitable. Errors differ by season and flood type. Flood peaks tend to be under-forecast, so the errors carry systematic bias. Tails are heavy. An additive normal can also produce negative inflows. The 12-year record holds very few large floods (expected count of 100-year events = 12 x 0.01 = 0.12; P(at least one) = 1 - 0.99^12 ≈ 11%). The error behaviour at 100-year magnitudes is therefore extrapolated.
2. **Independence across days and lead times.** Real errors are autocorrelated within a forecast issue and across successive issues. The variance of the 3-day volume error is 3σ² when errors are independent and up to 9σ² when they are fully correlated. Independent sampling can therefore understate the SD of the volume error by up to √3 ≈ 1.7. Volume error is what drives prerelease decisions, so this matters most.
3. **What "95% confidence" can mean.** With 5,000 runs, Monte Carlo noise is tiny (SE of a proportion near 0.95 is about √(0.95·0.05/5000) ≈ 0.003). So the 95% figure only reflects variability *inside the assumed error model*. It excludes uncertainty in parameters, model structure, design-hydrograph shape, initial storage, and the routing/storage model. "95% confidence" is therefore conditional and overstated.
4. **Metric and comparison design.** The 18% figure needs a definition: mean, median, or a tail percentile of peak outflow. Both rules should be run on the same scenarios (paired runs, common random numbers). The tail of the outflow distribution matters more than the mean. Results should also be reported for several hydrograph shapes and return periods.
5. **Prerelease-specific risks.** Over-forecasts cause false-alarm releases. Those releases can themselves exceed downstream channel capacity, and if the flood does not come the reservoir may fail to refill (supply, hydropower). Under-forecasts give too little prerelease. A scale check helps: the prerelease volume should be compared with the 600 million m³ storage and the flood-control pool. For example, an extra 1,000 m³/s for 3 days ≈ 259 million m³, about 43% of total storage. The key test is whether the prerelease can be released inside the forecast window without breaching downstream safe discharge and ramping limits.
6. **Dam safety.** Report maximum reservoir level and freeboard, not just outflow. Test the check flood/PMF and cases where the forecast fails, so that a rule reducing outflow does not raise dam-safety risk.
7. **Required evidence.** A hindcast on the 12 years of actual forecasts. Stratified, heteroscedastic, correlated error models (AR, copula, or empirical block/analogue resampling). Sensitivity to error inflation and to the distribution family. Perfect-forecast and no-forecast bounds. Several design-flood shapes and sequential floods. Forecast-system stationarity.
8. **Practicality.** An operating manual with forecast-confidence triggers. A fallback to the current rule or an observation-based override when the forecast fails. Downstream warning and coordination. Regulator or dam-safety authority approval. A shadow-mode pilot, independent review, and monitoring with reversion criteria.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- An explicit statement that "95% confidence" is conditional on the assumed error model. With 5,000 runs, Monte Carlo SE is about 0.003, so the figure says nothing about model, parameter or hydrograph uncertainty.
- Quantification of the independence error. The variance of the 3-day volume error is 3σ² under independence versus up to 9σ² under full correlation, so the SD may be understated by up to about 1.7x.
- Heteroscedastic, flow-proportional or log-space errors. An additive pooled normal can give negative inflows and understates absolute errors at flood peaks. Systematic under-forecasting of peaks should be checked.
- A prerelease volume and feasibility check against the 600 million m³ storage and the flood-control pool. Whether the needed prerelease can be passed within 72 h below downstream safe discharge (e.g., 1,000 m³/s extra over 3 days ≈ 259 million m³ ≈ 43% of storage).
- An explicit false-alarm vs. miss analysis: the downstream damage caused by unnecessary prerelease, and the water-supply or hydropower loss if the reservoir does not refill.
- Dam-safety outcomes (maximum level and freeboard under the design flood and the check flood/PMF, with forecast failure), not only maximum outflow.
- Paired runs of both rules on identical sampled scenarios (common random numbers), with the result reported as the distribution of the paired reduction, including its lower tail.
- Formal adoption provisions: an operating-manual revision with forecast-confidence triggers and release caps, approval by the dam-safety or regulatory authority, downstream warning protocols, and a shadow-mode season before operational use.
