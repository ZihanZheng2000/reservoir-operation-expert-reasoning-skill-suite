# Judgment U2-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- **Effective pre-release window.** The forecast lead time is 36 h. Subtract the 8 h decision, warning and gate chain to get 28 h. The worst case also subtracts the 6 h update interval, because a flood can first appear just after an update. That gives about 22 h.
- **Allowable dam release during pre-release.** The release is bounded by the downstream constraint, not by the 7,500 m³/s capacity: 5,000 − 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s. Capacity is not binding at this rate, but it should be checked at lower levels.
- **Net drawdown rate.** The release must be net of inflow: 3,800 − 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s.
- **Releasable volume.**
  - 28 h: 2,300 × 28 × 3,600 ≈ 231.8 million m³. This exceeds 180 million m³, so the theoretical maximum level lies above 154 m. Linear extrapolation at 90 million m³/m gives about 154.6 m, but the real stage–storage curve above 154 m is needed.
  - 22 h: about 182 million m³, so about 154.0 m.
  - Drawing down 180 million m³ needs about 21.7 h at 2,300 m³/s net.
- **Judgment on 154 m.** 154 m sits essentially at the theoretical limit with no margin. It should not be adopted directly as the operating upper limit without margins and verification.
- **Margins to consider:**
  - forecast magnitude and timing error, and missed or late detection;
  - inflow rising above 1,500 m³/s during the pre-release window;
  - timing of the intervening-catchment flow and the dam-to-town travel time;
  - gate ramp rates and limits on downstream rate-of-rise;
  - bank and dam-slope stability limits on drawdown rate;
  - stage–storage curve error, for example from sedimentation;
  - reduced outlet capacity at lower heads, or a gate out of service;
  - the update-interval loss.
- **Verifications:**
  - Route design and check floods starting from 154 m, and confirm that the design flood level, check flood level and downstream protection standard are not worsened.
  - Replay historical floods, including back-to-back events and fast-rising events.
  - Evaluate forecast skill and grade, with hindcasts.
  - Run a risk analysis of pre-release failure.
  - Check the downstream channel and warning arrangements.
  - Obtain approval from the competent flood-control authority, since dynamic FLWL control requires approval.
- **Missing information:**
  - stage–storage and stage–discharge curves above and below 154 m;
  - forecast accuracy statistics;
  - flood hydrograph types and rates of rise;
  - intervening-catchment hydrograph and timing;
  - channel routing;
  - characteristic dam levels and the design standard;
  - seasonal staging;
  - regulatory requirements.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1

- **KQ: 3.** It misses the deciding question: that the downstream constraint (5,000 − 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s) sets the release, and that the window is lead time minus latency. It treats the 8 h latency as the drawdown window and the 7,500 m³/s capacity as the release. It then frames the downstream problem as an "unavoidable 3,700 m³/s excess", which misreads the operating logic. The reservoir is not obliged to release 7,500 m³/s.
- **TC: 2.**
  - It uses the wrong window: 8 h instead of 28 h, or 22 h in the worst case.
  - It uses the wrong release limit, ignoring the downstream safe discharge.
  - It treats 172.8 as "approximately equal" to 180, although 172.8 < 180 means the drawdown is actually infeasible by its own numbers.
  - The 153 m rationale contains incoherent arithmetic ("saves ~55 million m³ ... 9 hours ... 1-hour margin").
  - The conclusion "theoretical max ≈ 154 m" lands near the right value only by accident.
- **ME: 4.** The verification lists are reasonable: hydrograph characterisation, forecast skill (POD/FAR), timing of the gate chain, the stage–discharge curve and stress scenarios. But the core method is wrong. There is no flood-routing check of design and check floods starting from 154 m.
- **PR: 5.** It gives tiered alert levels, seasonal reduction, conditions and annual re-evaluation. Regulatory compliance is mentioned generically. The staged levels (153, 153.5, 152.5) are not derived from anything.
- **CA: 3.** It invents a Three Gorges precedent that is factually wrong: "typical operating level ~140 m" is below dead or flood-limited operation, and it is used as "regulatory precedent". The recommended 153 m and the thresholds (POD > 0.85, 90th percentile) are asserted without basis. It also claims "exactly enough" capacity when its own numbers show a shortfall.

It omits the update-interval loss (worst case about 22 h). Crucially, it omits subtracting the 1,500 m³/s inflow, so the deciding net-drawdown question is settled wrongly.

Regulation is listed as missing information, but it does not name the specific approval route for dynamic flood-limited-level control, and it gives no fallback beyond "use 152.5 m".

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had

- **The correct net calculation:** (5,000 − 1,200 − 1,500) × (36 − 8) h ≈ 232 million m³, or about 182 million m³ with the 6 h update-interval loss. Hence 154 m sits right at the theoretical limit under worst-case detection timing, with zero margin.
- **An explicit statement** that the theoretical maximum above 154 m needs the stage–storage curve above 154 m. The 90 million m³/m figure is only an average for 152–154 m.
- **Inflow rising on the rising limb** during pre-release, not a constant 1,500 m³/s. This shrinks the net rate quickly.
- **Flood routing** of design and check floods, and of historical floods, starting from 154 m. This verifies that the design and check flood levels and the downstream protection standard are unchanged. It is the standard dam-safety test for raising the flood-limited level dynamically.
- **The risk of a failed pre-release** (a missed or underforecast flood) and its consequences.
- **Constraints on drawdown rate** from bank and slope stability, and limits on downstream rate-of-rise.
- **Dam-to-town travel time** and how it phases with the intervening-catchment peak.
- **Staging by flood season**, for example main versus early or late flood season.
- **Formal approval** by the competent flood-control authority as a dynamic flood-limited-water-level control scheme, with a mandatory fallback: return to 152 m whenever forecast conditions or data quality degrade.
