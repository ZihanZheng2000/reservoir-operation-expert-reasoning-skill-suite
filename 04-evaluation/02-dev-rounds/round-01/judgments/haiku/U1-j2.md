# Judgment U1-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

1. **Correct staging concept.** Seasonal FLWL rests on flood magnitude varying within the flood season. The main-monsoon sub-period keeps the design (lowest) FLWL. Pre- and post-main sub-periods, which have smaller seasonal design floods, may get a higher FLWL. In Indian practice this is a filling schedule: keep the reservoir low at onset, raise it to FRL as the monsoon recedes, with the post-monsoon refill as the main gain. A higher level in the peak monsoon is the wrong direction.
2. **Objective flood-season division** from the 45-year record: timing of annual and seasonal maxima, change-point, circular-statistics or fractal methods, and checks that sub-periods are stable across sub-records.
3. **Seasonal design floods.** Do a frequency analysis of maxima within each sub-period at both standards: the dam-safety standard (SPF/PMF or 1000–10000-yr for a large dam under Indian BIS/CWC practice) and the town's protection standard (e.g., 25–100-yr). Account for the uncertainty of extrapolating from 45 years. Arithmetic check: 45 years gives about 0.9 expected exceedances of a 50-yr event (P(≥1) = 1−0.98^45 ≈ 60%) and 0.045 of a 1000-yr event (P(≥1) = 1−0.999^45 ≈ 4.4%). The record cannot validate rare-event safety empirically, so design-flood routing is required.
4. **Design-flood routing from each candidate FLWL.** Use the level–storage and level–discharge curves and the operating rule. Pass if the peak level stays at or below the design/check flood levels (with freeboard) and the release reaching the town stays at or below the safe carrying capacity at the town's standard. Iterate to find the highest FLWL per sub-period that passes.
5. **Data adequacy.** Daily inflow cannot represent flood peaks or the attenuation through gates; flood hydrographs need sub-daily data. The 40 km reach (travel time of hours) needs routing, the intermediate catchment inflow, and the town's safe discharge. All of these must be requested, with a fallback such as peak/daily ratios and conservative assumptions if they cannot be obtained.
6. **Forecast role.** Use the 8-year archive to estimate error by lead time, especially for large events (few in 8 years). Derive an effective reliable lead time. Check whether the extra storage between the staged and base FLWL can be pre-released within that lead time without exceeding safe downstream discharge. Keep the forecast-based dynamic component separate from the static staged schedule.
7. **Benefit assessment.** Simulate the 45 years under both rules. Metrics: end-of-monsoon storage, irrigation deficit and reliability against real demand data, hydropower energy, and spill.
8. **Sufficiency and regulation.** Recommend only if design-flood safety is unchanged at both standards, the benefit is material and robust, and forecast-failure fallbacks exist. The route to adoption runs through the dam owner and state WRD, CWC review, and the Dam Safety Act 2021 / EAP and operating-manual update, with a trial or shadow period.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ (5):** Finds forecast reliability, drawdown feasibility, the downstream safe discharge, the reservoir-side Z_max, intermediate inflow and two-sided failure. It misses the decisive steps: objective season division, seasonal design-flood frequency analysis, and design-flood routing from each candidate FLWL. It does not flag that daily data cannot resolve flood peaks.
- **TC (3):** The core concept is reversed. It proposes a *higher* FLWL in the main monsoon and a *lower* FLWL pre- and post-monsoon. It even says a lower FLWL "could allow more water storage." It also says switching *into* the higher FLWL requires pre-release. Each of these inverts the logic of staged FLWL and would produce an unsafe main-season rule. Smaller slips: Sept 30 is called the "end of irrigation season" when it is the end of monsoon refill, and it requires testing "at least 3 floods of 100-year or larger" that a 45-year record is unlikely to contain. The dam standard (1000-yr + freeboard, PMF) is handled reasonably.
- **ME (6):** An executable sequence: encode baseline → forecast skill by lead time and phase → reliable lead time vs. drawdown time → candidate schemes → 45-year hindcast → event replay → sensitivity tests (split record, degraded forecast, parameter sweep). It also preserves autocorrelation of forecast error. Gaps: no design-flood routing, a daily mass balance only, and the rare-event safety probability is "measured" from 45 years.
- **PR (6):** Pre-release ≤ Q_safe, false-alarm rate, a fallback when drawdown fails, shadow-mode trial, operator training, an emergency revert to the fixed FLWL, and consultation with named stakeholder groups. It names no Indian regulator (CWC, state WRD, Dam Safety Act, EAP).
- **CA (5):** Lists assumptions (stationarity, representative forecasts, intermediate inflow to verify). But it cites "the papers" that do not exist in the prompt. Numeric thresholds (10% spill reduction, <10% false alarms, ±0.2 m) are arbitrary. It assumes historical operating records exist, although the prompt does not confirm them.

### Answer 2
- **KQ (5):** Explicitly addresses the unconfirmed-data problem: downstream hazard and channel capacity, the exact current rule, irrigation and power demand, and contingencies if data are missing. Recognises that the current safety margin decides whether there is room to raise levels. Like Answer 1, it lacks season division, seasonal design floods and design-flood routing.
- **TC (4):** The direction is partly right (raise levels post-monsoon), but it contradicts itself. One line says early monsoon has lower risk so "allow higher levels"; the next says "reduce levels sharply pre-monsoon." Arithmetic error: "45 years provides ~2.25 samples of 50-year events" (correct ≈ 0.9). Flood frequency stops at "100+ years" and the pass criterion uses a 100-year design level, which is well below the dam-safety standard for a large dam. It tolerates a "±10% relative change in design-event frequency," which allows a safety reduction. It treats "probability peak outflow exceeds design level" as estimable from a 45-year simulation. The line "skill <70% ... becomes fixed rule, gains much efficiency" is garbled.
- **ME (5):** Phased plan with baseline, candidate options A–C, a 45-year simulation, forecast-error sensitivity, stress tests with design-storm injection (a good instinct), and an evidence-tier ladder. But the steps are generic. The forecast component is thin: no reliable-lead-time vs. drawdown check, and "accuracy %" is undefined. Options are defined only by amplitude (0.5–2 m), with no derivation method.
- **PR (5):** Fail-safe default, stakeholder acceptance, staffing, and data contingencies by month 2. All generic. It names no regulator, operating-manual or EAP route, and no trial period.
- **CA (4):** Has a useful assumptions table. But the "Provisional Recommendation" pre-judges the study ("likely to be beneficial"; "preliminary estimates suggest 10–20% efficiency improvement is plausible") without evidence. It asserts that monsoon onset is predictable within ±2 weeks and that 45 years is "sufficient to test the hypothesis rigorously." It applies "statistically significant" to rare-event exceedance with an n too small to support it.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** Concrete forecast-skill protocol (bias, spread, ±20% qualified rate by lead and monsoon phase, stationarity). Reliable lead time vs. drawdown-time feasibility. Pre-release capped at downstream safe discharge. Correlated forecast-error scenarios. Split-sample and degraded-forecast robustness. Non-compensable safety ordering. Shadow-mode trial and revert-to-fixed fallback.
- **Missing or wrong:** The staging direction is inverted. The main monsoon must keep the base (lowest) FLWL, and the gain comes from raising it in the pre- and post-main periods. Pre-release is needed to fall from a *raised* level back to the base level, not to enter a higher one. There is no seasonal design flood or design-flood routing, and safety is tested only on the observed 45-year series. It does not flag that daily data are too coarse for peaks or the 40 km routing. Sept 30 is mislabeled. It assumes operating records exist.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** Systematic data-gap inventory tied to the "not yet confirmed" condition, with contingencies. Irrigation-demand and hydropower data identified and used for deficit and MWh metrics. The current safety margin is recognised as the gate for any raise. Design-storm injection stress test. An evidence-strength ladder and "what would change the recommendation."
- **Missing or wrong:** Contradictory staging logic. The 50-year sample arithmetic is wrong (≈0.9, not 2.25). The dam-safety standard is understated (100-yr instead of SPF/PMF or 1000-yr+). It accepts up to a 10% change in design-event frequency. It treats rare-event probability as measurable from 45 years. The forecast-to-drawdown feasibility check is missing. The provisional recommendation and the 10–20% gain estimate are unsupported. Regulatory steps are generic.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- Objective flood-season division from the timing of annual and sub-seasonal maxima, with a stability check.
- Sub-seasonal flood frequency analysis and seasonal design hydrographs at both the dam-safety standard and the town's protection standard, with confidence bounds from a 45-year sample.
- Routing those design floods from each candidate FLWL through the level–storage and level–discharge curves. Pass criteria: the design/check flood levels and the town's safe discharge are not exceeded. The highest passing FLWL per sub-period is the result.
- An explicit statement that daily inflow cannot resolve flood peaks or gate operations. Request sub-daily hydrographs, the intermediate catchment inflow, and the 40 km reach routing/travel time, with a conservative fallback if they are unobtainable.
- A clear separation between the static staged schedule and an optional forecast-based dynamic band, and a check of whether 8 years contains enough large events to trust forecast error at flood magnitudes.
- Named Indian adoption route: dam owner and state WRD, CWC/dam-safety review under the Dam Safety Act 2021, revision of the operating manual and Emergency Action Plan, a trial season, and a reversion trigger.
