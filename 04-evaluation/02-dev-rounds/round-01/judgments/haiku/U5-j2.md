## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Storage arithmetic: upper free = 0.40 x 300 = 120 Mm³ (occupied flood pool 180 Mm³); lower free = 0.85 x 500 = 425 Mm³ (occupied only 75 Mm³). Total free 545 Mm³ is more than the 400 Mm³ high-end volume. Total volume is therefore not what limits the system. The limits are the upper reservoir's storage (120 Mm³ against inflow of 150-400 Mm³), the peak and timing of the flood, and coincidence with the tributary.
- Current balance: upper is drawing down at 1,500 - 1,200 = 300 m³/s. Lower inflow is about 1,500 (lagged upper release) + 300 (tributary) = 1,800 m³/s, so lower is drawing down at about 700 m³/s. City flow is about 2,500 m³/s, assuming no local inflow below the lower dam.
- Mean of the forecast volume: 250 Mm³ / (72 x 3,600 s) is about 964 m³/s (range about 580-1,540 m³/s). This is below or near the current inflow, so the stated volume is ambiguous (total inflow or flood excess?). The peak rate is what matters, and it is unknown.
- Downstream constraint chain: lower release must stay at or below about 6,000 m³/s at the city (minus any local inflow). So during the flood, lower must store roughly (upper release + tributary of up to 1,500) - 6,000. Upper releases above about 4,500 m³/s during a tributary storm push the problem onto the lower reservoir. This is why the storage of the upper reservoir is the scarce asset.
- Timing: upper prerelease made now reaches lower at about 18 h, before the flood arrives at upper (30 h). Lower can pass it through without stress. Releases from upper reach the city in about 28 h. Waiting 12 h (option c) cuts the upper prerelease window from 30 h to 18 h. At +1,800 m³/s net, that is about 194 Mm³ of room over 30 h against about 117 Mm³ over 18 h. Prerelease at the lower dam buys little, because lower is already 85% empty and only 75 Mm³ can be removed.
- Decision: this argues for a modest, reversible upper prerelease now. That means (a) with lower kept near pass-through, or a hedged "upper first" variant. It also means weighing the cost of a false alarm (refill or water-supply loss if the volume comes in at 150 Mm³) against flood risk. Triggers at the 12 h update should be the peak-inflow estimate, a tributary forecast at or above about 1,000 m³/s coinciding with upper releases, and a volume above about 300 Mm³. Fallbacks: dispatching rules, approval from headquarters, downstream warning, ramping limits, and dam-safety limits (FLWL and surcharge).
