# Judgment U4-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's reference)

- Verdict: do not replace the approved operating rule next season. Run the model in shadow or advisory mode only.
- Physical point: peak outflow can only come down if water is stored. Volume conservation gives ΔV = ∫(Q_actual − Q_model) dt over the event. A 30% lower peak release means extra storage, so the reservoir peaks higher and has less flood space left. The answer must ask for the simulated peak water level against the flood-control and design/check flood levels, the end-of-event storage, and the risk if a second flood followed. A good answer would also sketch the check, for example: ΔV ≈ 0.3·Q_peak × duration of the reduced-release window, compared with the remaining flood-control storage.
- Information leak: a retrospective run usually uses observed inflows (perfect foresight). The model must be re-run with the forecasts that were actually available in real time, re-forecast at each step.
- Baseline: actual operator decisions are not the same as the approved rule. Operators may have deviated for downstream, gate or communication reasons. The comparison must be against the rule simulated under the same conditions, plus an optimized or perfect-forecast bound.
- Generalisation: n = 1 event. Leave-one-event-out over the 2010–2022 floods. Check whether the 2023 inflows were outside the training range (extrapolation), and whether 2023 was used to tune the model.
- If the model learned from operator decisions, it is unclear how it could beat those same operators unless it had extra information.
- Metrics beyond reservoir outflow: downstream control-point stages and flows, coincidence with tributary peaks, ramping rates, gate feasibility.
- Governance: the operating rule or dispatch plan is approved by a regulator, so changing it needs formal approval and dam-safety review. Also needed: clear authority, liability, a written fallback to the existing rule, override triggers and a post-event review.

**Points an expert answer needs that neither had:**
- A volume-balance check: extra stored volume ΔV = ∫(Q_actual − Q_model) dt, compared with the remaining flood-control storage, plus the resulting peak level against the flood-limit and design/check levels.
- The effect on a following flood, since less flood space is left.
- The paradox that a model trained on operators' decisions appears to beat those operators. That points to information leakage (observed future inflows) or extrapolation.
- A re-forecast hindcast protocol that uses only information available at each decision time.
- Explicit numeric acceptance criteria: no exceedance of the flood-limit level, downstream safe discharge never exceeded, and not worse than the approved rule on any held-out event.
- The current approved rule stays the legal operating basis until formally revised and approved by the competent authority.
