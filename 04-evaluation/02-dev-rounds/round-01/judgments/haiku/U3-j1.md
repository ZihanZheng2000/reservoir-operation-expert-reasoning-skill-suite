# Judgment U3-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

1. **The "95% confidence" means little.** With 5,000 Monte Carlo samples, the sampling error of a mean reduction is tiny, so a 95% interval built from them only measures Monte Carlo noise. That noise is conditional on the assumed error model, one design hydrograph, a fixed initial level and the fitted parameters. It says nothing about model-structure, parameter or design-flood uncertainty. The answer should also ask which statistic the 18% is (mean, median, or the reduction of a high quantile) and what P(new rule worse than current) is.
2. **Pooling hides heteroscedasticity and bias.** Errors grow with flow magnitude and lead time. Peaks are often underforecast. Errors pooled over 12 years are dominated by ordinary flows, so they understate absolute errors during a 100-year event, which is an extrapolation since about 0.12 such events are expected in 12 years. The fix is to stratify by lead time and magnitude, or use relative or heteroscedastic errors, and check bias and the tails.
3. **Independent daily sampling understates the error in the 3-day volume.** Prerelease depends on forecast volume. Var(sum of 3 days) = σ²(3 + 2(2ρ + ρ²)). With independence it is 3σ². With ρ = 0.7 it is about 6.8σ², so the SD is about 1.5 times larger. With ρ = 1 it is 9σ², about 1.73 times larger. Sustained over- or under-forecasting, which drives both failure modes, is therefore under-sampled.
4. **Both failure modes need checking.** If inflow is underforecast, prerelease is too small and the maximum water level or outflow rises. If it is overforecast, prerelease is wasted, refill fails and supply or hydropower is lost. Check that the prerelease outflow stays within the downstream safe discharge and does not itself create a flood. Report the maximum reservoir level against the design and check flood levels, not only peak outflow.
5. **Robustness testing.** Use several hydrograph shapes and return periods, including the dam-safety check flood. Vary the initial level. Include sequential floods. Hindcast both rules on the real archived forecasts, out of sample (the errors were fitted on the same 12 years). Compare against no-forecast and perfect-forecast baselines.
6. **Practical provisions.** Approval by the flood-control authority and revision of the operating manual. Forecast-quality triggers and a fallback to the current rule. Downstream warning. A shadow or pilot period with monitoring and revert criteria.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:** Copula or temporal-dependence fix; sequential floods and refill; perfect-forecast and no-forecast baselines to show how much of the possible benefit the rule captures; Monte Carlo convergence and seed checks; measurement-based fallback and pilot; a concrete question list for the owner.
**Answer 1, missing or wrong:** Invented literature statistics (50 papers, ρ > 0.7, α values, the "0.5 SD", factor 2–3) presented as evidence. It does not point out that 12 years cannot characterize errors at 100-year magnitudes. No quantification of how independence shrinks the 3-day volume error. It does not say the 95% is conditional Monte Carlo confidence. It does not check downstream safe discharge of prerelease outflow or the maximum water level against design and check flood levels. No regulatory approval or manual revision.

**Answer 2, done well:** Extrapolation argument (0.12 expected events). Separation of parametric, model-structure and Monte Carlo uncertainty. A hindcast of both rules on the actual 12-year forecasts. Nonstationarity of forecast skill. Pilot with revert triggers.
**Answer 2, missing or wrong:** Confused lead-time argument (7-day errors pooled into a 3-day product). No sequential floods, refill, or overforecast/false-prerelease cost. The hindcast is in-sample with the error fit; it needs cross-validation. No perfect-forecast or no-forecast baselines. No calculation of the variance effect. No regulatory approval or downstream safe-discharge check.

**Points neither answer had:**
- Explicit calculation: Var(3-day volume error) = σ²(3 + 2(2ρ + ρ²)). Independence understates the SD by about 1.5 times at ρ = 0.7, and by up to √3 ≈ 1.73 times at ρ = 1.
- With 5,000 samples, the 95% interval reflects only Monte Carlo noise conditional on a single model and hydrograph. Report P(new rule worse than current), the distribution of the reduction, and upper-tail metrics (for example the 95th or 99th percentile of maximum outflow and water level), not a mean of 18%.
- Errors should be modeled as relative or heteroscedastic (scaled to flow), with checks for underforecast bias at peaks.
- Checks that prerelease outflow stays at or below downstream safe channel capacity. Dam-safety checks at the check flood (for example 1,000-year or PMF), not only the 100-year event. Varying the initial and flood-limit water level.
- Formal approval by the flood-control authority, revision of the operating manual, forecast-quality and availability triggers for invoking prerelease, and downstream warning procedures.
