# Judgment U1-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's reference)

- **Correct direction of staging.** In a monsoon basin the main-monsoon sub-season needs the *lowest* FLWL, because it needs the most flood-control storage. Pre-monsoon and especially the post-monsoon/recession sub-season can carry a *higher* FLWL, because the design flood for those sub-seasons is smaller. That higher level is where the irrigation and hydropower gain comes from, through better end-of-monsoon refill.
- **Statistical flood-season partitioning.** Use the 45-year record (timing and magnitude of annual maxima and peaks-over-threshold events) to split the season, for example with circular/directional statistics, Fisher optimal partitioning, change-point methods or a fractal/relative-frequency method. Check that the split dates are stable across sub-periods.
- **Sub-seasonal design floods.** Run a frequency analysis for each sub-season. Make it consistent with the annual standard: the sub-seasonal non-exceedance probabilities must combine to the annual design probability, i.e. product of (1−p_i) = 1−P.
- **Design hydrographs.** Daily data cannot give instantaneous peaks or the rising-limb shape. The design needs peak-to-daily ratios or sub-daily records, or must state this as an explicit limitation.
- **Design-flood routing from each candidate FLWL.** Route the dam-safety floods (design and check floods, e.g. 1000-yr or PMF) and the town-protection flood (e.g. 100-yr). Use the level–storage and level–discharge-capacity curves and the existing release rule, starting from each candidate sub-seasonal FLWL. Then verify three things:
  - the peak level stays at or below the design and check flood levels;
  - the release stays at or below the downstream safe discharge;
  - intermediate inflow between the dam and the town (40 km) is accounted for.
- **Why a 45-year hindcast is not enough for safety.** The expected number of 100-yr events in 45 years is 0.45, and P(at least one) = 1−0.99^45 ≈ 36%. The expected number of 50-yr events is 0.9. So a hindcast can show benefit but cannot establish safety at the design standards. That needs design-flood routing and stochastic or synthetic floods.
- **Forecast-based dynamic control inside the staged bounds.** Estimate forecast error by lead time and season from the 8-year archive, and set the reliable lead time. Check that the pre-release volume can be discharged within that lead time at or below the downstream safe discharge. Treat the 8-year sample of extreme events as small.
- **Benefit metrics, using demand data that must be obtained.**
  - Irrigation: reliability, deficit, end-of-monsoon storage.
  - Hydropower: energy and firm power.
  - Spill reduction.
  - Refill probability.
- **Indian approval and dam-safety context.**
  - Who approves: the CWC, the state water resources department, and the dam-safety framework under the Dam Safety Act 2021.
  - Operating provisions: a revised rule curve, a trial or shadow period, a fallback to the fixed level, and triggers for review.
- **Decision criteria for recommending a change.** There must be no exceedance at any design standard, the benefit must be material and robust, and assumptions and data gaps must be stated.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (U1-ahqz)

- **KQ:** It identifies the downstream threshold, forecast skill, baseline safety margin and the need for an extreme-value analysis. However, it never partitions the flood season statistically. It never derives sub-seasonal design floods, and it never routes design floods from each candidate FLWL. Safety rests mainly on the 45-year hindcast. Its staging logic is muddled: it proposes "seasonal lowering pre-monsoon", "reduce levels sharply pre-monsoon" and "allow higher levels" in the early monsoon. That leaves the deciding question, which sub-season can safely hold a higher level, unsettled. Score 4.
- **TC:** It says "45 years provides ~2.25 design-event samples for 50-year events", but the correct figure is 45/50 = 0.9. That error overstates the evidential power of the record. The pass/fail criterion allows "±10% relative change in design-event frequency", which would tolerate an increase in risk. The phrase "forecast skill <70% … staged rule becomes fixed rule, gains much efficiency" does not make sense. The direction of staging is internally inconsistent. Score 3.
- **ME:** The four phases are ordered: characterisation, baseline, rule design, simulation, and sensitivity. Stress tests with injected design storms and model-structure uncertainty are reasonable. Several steps are still not executable: there is no partitioning method, the efficiency criteria are arbitrary ("5–10% critical months or 15% annually"), and "calibration" of a mass-balance model is vague. Score 5.
- **PR:** It lists demand data, stakeholder acceptance and fail-safe defaults generically. There is no approving authority, no Indian guidance, no shadow trial, and no specific fallback provision beyond "default to conservative level". Score 5.
- **CA:** Its "Provisional recommendation" says the staged rule is "likely to be beneficial", and it asserts that "10–20% efficiency improvement is plausible" with no evidence. It also assumes monsoon onset is predictable, calling this "likely true". Both prejudge the study. Score 3.

### Answer 2 (U1-0vy8)

- **KQ:** It defines the characteristic levels: FLWL, design and check flood levels, Z_max and Q_safe. It treats both failure modes, at the reservoir and downstream. It covers the reliable forecast horizon, drawdown and pre-release feasibility within the lead time, downstream routing, intermediate inflow (as an assumption to verify), refill (storage on 30 Sept) and nonstationarity. It too misses statistical season partitioning and sub-seasonal design floods. Above all, it gets the deciding question backwards (see TC). Score 5.
- **TC:** It has a major conceptual inversion. Section 1.2 says a lower FLWL outside peak monsoon "allows more water storage", and that a higher FLWL in peak monsoon "preserves flood-control storage". Scheme 2 then sets the FLWL *higher* in June–August and *lower* in May and Sept–Oct. That is the reverse of correct staging and would cut flood storage exactly when floods are largest. Other problems:
  - "Test over at least 3 representative large floods (100-year or larger)" is unlikely to be possible from 45 years of record.
  - Using the seasonal 50th-percentile inflow as the flood-mode trigger is arbitrary.
  - The mass-balance equation and the probability criteria are otherwise fine.
  Score 3.
- **ME:** It is ordered and executable. It covers forecast-error statistics by lead time and phase, and an error-ensemble resampling that preserves autocorrelation. Its daily simulator includes routing. It screens with a deterministic test and then compares on a risk basis. It replays major events, runs a split-sample test and sweeps parameters. The main gap is that there is no design-flood routing from candidate levels. It relies on the hindcast for safety at rare return periods. Score 6.
- **PR:** It gives specific operating provisions: drawdown-rate checks, limits on the pre-release volume and on false alarms, a fallback procedure, shadow-mode trial for one monsoon, operator training, an emergency clause reverting to the fixed FLWL, and consultation with the irrigation and power operators, the flood authority and town officials. It does not name Indian regulators (CWC, state WRD, Dam Safety Act). Score 7.
- **CA:** Assumptions are listed explicitly and largely scoped. However, it invents a forecast period ("2015–2023"), refers to "the papers" without any basis, and its target numbers (10% spill reduction, 5–10% refill) are not justified. It does not overclaim an outcome. Score 5.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:**
  - It recognises that the downstream hazard threshold is critical and currently missing.
  - It runs an extreme-value analysis on the 45-year record.
  - It stress-tests with injected design-storm sequences.
  - It uses forecast-error sensitivity and alternative distributions.
  - It ranks evidence in tiers and lists conditions that would change the recommendation.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - The direction of staging is confused. Pre-monsoon lowering and higher early-monsoon levels are not justified by flood seasonality.
  - The arithmetic is wrong: 45 years gives about 0.9 events at 50-yr, not 2.25.
  - The ±10% allowance on design-event frequency permits a loss of safety.
  - It has no sub-seasonal design floods, no design-flood routing from candidate levels, no check against design or check flood levels, no pre-release or drawdown feasibility check, no intermediate inflow, and no refill-risk metric.
  - The provisional "likely beneficial, 10–20% gain" claim is unsupported.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:**
  - It sets clear safety variables (Z_max, Q_safe) and treats safety as non-compensable.
  - It sets a reliable forecast horizon by lead time and monsoon phase.
  - It checks that pre-release can be discharged within that horizon at or below the safe downstream flow.
  - It uses an autocorrelated forecast-error ensemble.
  - It routes to the town and flags intermediate inflow.
  - It uses split-sample nonstationarity tests and a refill metric (30 Sept storage).
  - Its deployment provisions are concrete: shadow mode, fallback to the fixed FLWL, training and consultation.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - The staging is inverted. It puts a higher FLWL in the main monsoon and a lower one pre- and post-monsoon, and its stated rationale reverses what FLWL does to storage. If implemented, this would reduce flood safety.
  - It does not partition the season statistically and does not derive sub-seasonal design floods.
  - Safety at the 100-yr and 1000-yr standards rests on the hindcast or "3 floods of 100-yr or larger", which the record probably does not contain.
  - It invents a forecast period (2015–2023).
  - It does not name Indian regulators.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
1. A statistical flood-season partition (e.g. directional statistics on the timing of annual maxima, Fisher optimal partitioning or change-point detection), with a stability check on the split dates.
2. Sub-seasonal design-flood frequency analysis that is made consistent with the annual design standard (product of sub-seasonal non-exceedance probabilities = annual non-exceedance).
3. Design-flood routing, using the level–storage and discharge-capacity curves, of both the dam-safety floods (design and check flood, or PMF) and the town-protection flood, starting from each candidate sub-seasonal FLWL. The check is that peak level stays at or below the design and check flood levels and that release stays at or below the downstream safe discharge.
4. Recognition that daily inflows understate peaks. This needs peak-to-daily ratios or sub-daily data, or must be stated as a limitation.
5. An explicit statement that a 45-year hindcast cannot demonstrate safety at rare standards: P(at least one 100-yr event in 45 yr) ≈ 36%, and the expected count is 0.45.
6. A refill-probability analysis for the end of the monsoon. This is where the higher post-monsoon FLWL earns its benefit.
7. The Indian approval pathway: CWC and state WRD review of the revised rule curve, and the Dam Safety Act 2021 / dam-safety organisation, together with an Emergency Action Plan update for the downstream town.
