# Judgment U7-j2

## 0. What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- **Representation.** A reservoir mass balance on storage S, with the level h(S) taken from the level–storage curve. The release limit R ≤ Qcap(h(S)) depends on the state and must not be treated as a fixed bound. Downstream routing has a fast surrogate (linear or Muskingum-type, integrator-delay, or a linearized version of the calibrated model) for use inside the optimizer. The two ungauged tributaries are uncertain lateral inflows, estimated as residuals at the control station: observed control-station flow minus routed dam release, using the 20 years of observations. The downstream constraint is a safe flow or stage at the control station. Gate and ramp limits and a terminal or end-of-horizon storage condition are also needed.
- **Forecast handling.** Use the hindcasts to post-process the ensemble (bias, spread, reliability by lead time) before optimizing. A rank histogram and CRPS are the minimum checks.
- **Method.** Multi-scenario or tree-based MPC with non-anticipativity on the first-stage release, optionally with scenario reduction from 51 members. The alternatives to weigh are deterministic MPC (ensemble mean), SDP or precomputed policies, rule curves, and simulation-optimization with evolutionary search. The answer should explain how the nonlinear capacity curve is handled: piecewise linearization, successive linearization, or an a-posteriori check.
- **Key calculation.** A size and time budget. Example: 10 days at a 6 h step is 40 steps; at a 1 h step it is 240 steps. With 51 members (or about 10–15 reduced scenarios) there are a few thousand variables, which an LP or QP solves in seconds. Each candidate simulation-optimization run costs 51 routing runs, so 1,000 evaluations means 51,000 routing runs and needs real profiling.
- **Verification.**
  - Computational: profile against the 30-min budget with margin.
  - Physical: every recommended release is re-simulated through the full calibrated routing model and the nonlinear curves, and any surrogate mismatch is quantified.
  - Reliability: replay the 20-year hindcast in closed loop, compared with the existing operating rules and with a perfect-forecast benchmark. Report exceedance and peak-reduction statistics.
  - Implementability: gate-setting translation, a hold period between changes, and few release changes.
- **Practice.** Advisory mode, operator override with an audit log, consistency with the approved regulation or dispatch rules, and a fallback rule when forecasts or the solver fail. The regulator explanation should be framed in terms of probability of exceeding a threshold.
- **Unknowns.** Ramp limits, safe downstream flow, flood-control levels, the gate schedule and the tributary share. Use conservative provisional values, run sensitivity analyses, and state what the client must confirm.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (U7-ghqi)

- **KQ (5).** It sees ensemble propagation, the level-dependent capacity, the ungauged tributaries and the 30-min limit. It does not settle the central structural question: what the release does after the first period. Optimizing a single rate or ramp "for the next period" and then scoring 10-day peaks implies an open-loop constant release with no recourse, and this is never made explicit. Other gaps:
  - It does not discuss surrogate versus full routing inside the optimizer.
  - It has no terminal storage condition.
  - It does not trade off reservoir storage risk against downstream risk. The objective is only the downstream peak.
- **TC (4).** Several conceptual errors:
  - It treats the tributaries as "base flow", although in a flood they are storm-driven lateral inflow.
  - It says a calibrated 51-member ensemble should capture the observed peak "with ~50% probability". The correct figure is about 96%. It then sets a 60–70% bracketing pass criterion, which is inconsistent with its own quantile-exceedance test.
  - It requires "100% constraint satisfaction for all 51 members", which conflicts with a 90th-percentile objective.
  - The unit test ("does not propose a release that would raise the level") is confused.
  - The sentence about 6-hourly updates improving quality "within the 30-minute window" is muddled.
  - The runtime numbers are plausible but are stated as expectations without any basis.
- **ME (5).** It is executable as a simulation-optimization: candidate releases, 51 routing runs each, and a quantile metric, solved by SQP or grid search. It uses the calibrated model directly, which avoids surrogate error. Missing pieces: there is no future-release policy or non-anticipativity, no ensemble bias correction (only "weights"), and no comparison with current operating rules in the hindcast replay. Verification is listed but loosely tied to the method.
- **PR (6).** The explanation template, operator workshop, advisory mode, override with audit trail and display of alternative releases are good. Weaknesses:
  - It proposes eventual "full automation", which is unrealistic for a regulated flood-control dam.
  - It has no fallback rule for tool or forecast failure.
  - It says nothing about gate-setting translation or consistency with the approved dispatch rules.
  - It sets a 1–2 week advisory pilot, which is too short to see floods.
- **CA (5).** It scopes the uncertainty around the rating curve and routing well, using sensitivity tests and validity range. But it closes with a flat "Feasible" verdict and invented runtimes, and its rollout phases of weeks are not credible.

### Answer 2 (U7-b79a)

- **KQ (7).** It identifies and settles most of the deciding questions:
  - first-stage non-anticipativity with recourse;
  - a fast surrogate routing inside the optimizer;
  - scenario reduction to meet the time budget;
  - piecewise-linear curves for convexity;
  - ramp limits and a chance constraint downstream;
  - a post-horizon terminal condition;
  - tributaries estimated as a residual at the control station from 20 years of data;
  - bias correction from the hindcasts;
  - a fallback rule.

  What it misses: re-simulating each recommendation through the full calibrated model as a routine check, and benchmarking against the existing operating rules.
- **TC (5).** Some errors:
  - It calls the formulation "convex" but includes a chance constraint, which is not convex unless it is reformulated. This is not addressed.
  - The Monte Carlo arithmetic ("1,000 × 40 = 40,000 model runs") confuses time steps with runs. The correct count is 1,000 candidates × 51 members.
  - The stage weighting α is fixed by horizon day (days 1–3, 4–7, 8–10) instead of being triggered by the event state. The description of pre-release ("favors storage, avoid spill … maximize drawdown") contradicts itself.
  - It loosens risk tolerance beyond day 7. This is defensible but debatable.
  - The post-horizon design flood "enters at the control station", when it should enter the reservoir.

  The variable and constraint counts and solve times are reasonable.
- **ME (7).** The method is complete and ordered: reduced tree, convex MPC in CVXPY, a rolling single-step commitment, profiling, a 20-year constraint audit, degraded-forecast sensitivity tests and scenarios with missing or biased forecasts. The surrogate is validated against the detailed model, with a switch if NSE < 0.90. Gaps: the convexity and chance-constraint inconsistency, the tributary model's two inconsistent descriptions, and no explicit check of each decision with the full model.
- **PR (7).** It covers gate actuator increments and rounding, the fallback rule's invocation rate, low-confidence flagging, a 1–2 flood-season parallel pilot, human override and a post-event review protocol, and gives an explanation for the regulator. It still does not tie the tool to the approved regulation or dispatch rule, and it does not limit how often releases change.
- **CA (4).** It invents several facts:
  - an external reference ("E23-R4", NSE ≈ 0.97 "on similar systems") and literature claims ("E17-R2", "no SDP instances in the literature");
  - that the ensemble is HEFS and that the detailed model is SOBEK;
  - a "~20% uncertainty" contribution from the tributaries;
  - inflow magnitudes and capacity values (15,000/16,000 m³/s).

  The assumptions and limitations section at the end is good, but the invented specifics are a real calibration problem.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well.** It keeps the full nonlinear curves and the calibrated routing model in simulation, so there is no surrogate error. It uses a quantile risk metric with an exceedance check on the hindcasts, a rank histogram and CRPS, and spread versus lead time. It runs sensitivity tests on the capacity curve (±5 and ±10%) and on routing travel time. It tests whether the tributary model adds skill (with and without it), and it has a clear explanation template that shows alternatives.

**Answer 1, missing or wrong.**
- It has no recourse or future-release structure. It needs multi-stage or tree MPC with first-stage non-anticipativity, or at least an explicitly parameterized release trajectory.
- The objective ignores reservoir level risk and the end-of-horizon storage.
- Treating tributaries as base flow is wrong. They should be estimated as a residual from observed control-station flow minus the routed release.
- The ensemble bracketing probability is wrong (about 96%, not about 50%), and the pass criteria contradict each other.
- It has no bias or spread post-processing before optimization.
- It has no fallback rule, does not mention gate schedules, and does not replay the existing rules as a baseline.
- "Full automation" should be dropped, and the pilot must span flood seasons.

**Answer 2, done well.**
- Non-anticipative first-stage release with recourse, and scenario reduction justified by problem size.
- A fast surrogate validated against the detailed model.
- Ramp limits, a downstream chance constraint and a post-horizon terminal condition.
- Tributaries estimated from the control-station residual.
- Seasonal and online bias correction.
- Stress tests on missing or degraded forecasts, a fallback rule with an invocation-rate target, gate-increment rounding and a pilot across a flood season.

**Answer 2, missing or wrong.**
- The chance constraint makes the "convex" formulation non-convex. It needs a scenario-count (MILP) or CVaR reformulation, or a penalty.
- The Monte Carlo cost arithmetic is wrong.
- Stage weights fixed by horizon day are conceptually wrong and need event-state triggers. The pre-release description contradicts itself.
- The post-horizon flood should enter the reservoir, not the control station.
- The two tributary descriptions are inconsistent.
- It invents references, product names (HEFS, SOBEK) and magnitudes.
- It does not re-simulate each recommended release with the full calibrated routing model and nonlinear curves.

**Points an expert answer needs that neither had:**
1. A routine check of every recommendation by re-simulating it through the full calibrated routing model and the true nonlinear level–storage and level–capacity curves. The gap between surrogate and full model becomes an explicit acceptance criterion and a margin on the safe flow.
2. Closed-loop replay of the full 20-year hindcast, benchmarked against both the current approved operating rules and a perfect-forecast optimum. Report peak reduction, exceedance frequency of the safe flow and the flood-control level, and end-of-event storage.
3. Implementability constraints in the formulation itself: a minimum hold time between release changes, a limit on the number of changes, and translation of the release into gate settings using the gate rating at the current level.
4. Explicit consistency with the regulator-approved flood dispatch rules. The tool works inside the approved envelope, and the rule-based release is shown next to the optimized one as the default and fallback.
5. A concrete size and time budget: steps × scenarios × variables, and the number of full-model runs available within 30 min. Choose the time step in light of the travel time over the 120 km reach (on the order of hours), and check that a 6 h step is not too coarse for the downstream peak.
6. A list of provisional engineering values the client must confirm: ramp limits, safe downstream flow, flood-control and design levels, gate operating constraints, and the minimum release. Each gets a conservative default, a sensitivity analysis, and a design that can be reconfigured once the values are confirmed.
