# Judgment U5-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Storage arithmetic: upper free 0.40 x 300 = 120 Mm3 (occupied flood-control storage 180 Mm3); lower free 0.85 x 500 = 425 Mm3 (occupied 75 Mm3). System free 545 Mm3, which exceeds even the 400 Mm3 upper-bound flood volume before counting any release.
- Volume versus release: 250 Mm3 over 72 h is a mean of about 965 m3/s (400 Mm3 is about 1,540 m3/s). The upper dam needs to release only about 130 Mm3 (mean of about 500 m3/s) during the flood to stay within 120 Mm3 at 250 Mm3, or about 280 Mm3 (about 1,080 m3/s) at 400 Mm3. Volume is not the binding issue. Peak rate, hydrograph shape and timing are, and none of them is known.
- Topology: the tributary enters above the lower dam, so the lower reservoir regulates it. City flow is roughly the lower release lagged 10 h, plus any local inflow below the lower dam. It is not lower release plus tributary. Current lower inflow is about 1,500 + 300 = 1,800 m3/s, so the lower reservoir is already drawing down at about 700 m3/s.
- Value of waiting: upper releases take 18 h to reach the lower dam, and the flood reaches the upper dam in 30 h. If the upper decision waits until hour 12, 18 h of lead time remain. At about 3,000 m3/s against 1,200 m3/s of inflow, that recovers about 117 Mm3 in 18 h. The lower dam has 425 Mm3 free and can release up to about 6,000 m3/s within the city limit. Waiting 12 h therefore costs little, and prerelease is worth more in the scarce upper reservoir than in the storage-rich lower one.
- Regulatory bound: prerelease is limited to the occupied flood-control storage (about 180 Mm3 upper, about 75 Mm3 lower). Drawing below the flood-limited level loses conservation water if the forecast verifies low (150 Mm3), and needs approval.
- Main hazard: the tributary peak (up to 1,500 m3/s) could coincide with routed upper releases at the lower dam. The answer should describe compensating operation of the lower reservoir against the city's 6,000 m3/s limit.
- A defensible conclusion is a choice between (c)-with-preparation and a modest upper-focused prerelease. The answer should name an explicit trigger, such as the updated volume, peak or tributary forecast, plus the approval route, ramp-rate limits, downstream notice and a fallback.
