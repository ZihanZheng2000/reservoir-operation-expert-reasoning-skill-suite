# Judgment U2-j2 (Answer 1 = U2-wjz2, Answer 2 = U2-owja)

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Effective pre-release window: 36 h lead - 8 h decision/warning/gate = 28 h (100,800 s), assuming the lead time runs to flood onset at the dam.
- Allowable dam release during pre-release is set by the town, not the outlets: 5,000 - 1,200 (worst-case intervening inflow) = 3,800 m³/s. The 7,500 m³/s outlet capacity does not bind.
- Inflow keeps arriving, so the net drawdown rate is 3,800 - 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s. Net evacuable volume = 2,300 x 100,800 = about 232 million m³.
- 232 > 180 million m³, so 154 m passes in theory with about 52 million m³ (about 29%) to spare. Above 154 m the storage curve is not given. A linear extrapolation (90 million m³/m) gives about 154.6 m, and that number has to be labelled as an extrapolation.
- Sensitivities that decide the answer: (a) the 6-h update cycle. If the flood shows up only in the next forecast, the usable window can fall to about 22 h, giving 2,300 x 79,200 = about 182 million m³, which is roughly the 180 needed, so the margin is gone. (b) Inflow rising above 1,500 m³/s during pre-release. (c) Ramp-up time and dam-to-town travel time. (d) Whether the full 1,200 m³/s intervening inflow really coincides with the whole pre-release period.
- Conclusion: 154 m is theoretically feasible but has a thin margin. It cannot be adopted as the actual upper limit until margins for forecast error and update timing, inflow uncertainty, the ungauged intervening flow and operational delay are applied.
- Verifications: hindcast forecast skill and missed or late detections at 24 to 36 h; pre-release simulations on historical and design floods; dam-safety flood routing from 154 m for the case where the forecast fails or pre-release is incomplete (design and check flood levels must not be exceeded); downstream routing; and risk analysis of the dynamic flood-limited level.
- Missing information: the stage-storage curve above 154 m, the stage-discharge curve between 152 and 154 m, forecast error statistics, intervening-flow records or estimates, design and check flood routing results, downstream travel time and warning requirements, and the approval authority for any dynamic flood-limited level.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** It finds the 28-h window, sets the release cap at 3,800 m³/s from the town's capacity minus the intervening flow, and sees that the outlet capacity is not the constraint. It misses the deciding point that the 1,500 m³/s inflow continues during drawdown. It also does not test the 6-h update granularity. It lists the missing storage curve and structural limits.
- **TC:** It computes the gross volume, 3,800 x 28 h = 383 million m³, and gets 156.3 m. The inflow is ignored. The correct net volume is about 232 million m³, which gives about 154.6 m, and the linear extrapolation is not flagged. The "1,786 m³/s required release" is also a net figure presented as the release; the true release needed is about 3,286 m³/s, still under 3,800. The "0.64 m locked-in rise" double-counts the 8 h that was already removed from the window. The most serious error is in the margin case (4,000 m³/s safe limit, 2,800 m³/s release). Done correctly, it nets only 1,300 m³/s x 28 h = about 131 million m³, which is less than 180, so 154 m would fail. The answer instead reports 155.1 m. This error changes a conclusion.
- **ME:** The verification programme is well ordered and executable. It covers historical and synthetic flood simulation, forecast hindcasts by lead time, latency timing, downstream fragility, joint risk, stress tests and seasonal review, with acceptance criteria. It lacks explicit dam-safety flood routing from 154 m for the case where the forecast fails.
- **PR:** It covers coordination with downstream authorities, warning protocol, ramp rates, staged decision rules, field testing and annual review. Regulation and approval appear only as questions. There is no fallback rule for a missed forecast.
- **CA:** It hedges reasonably ("tentatively yes") and lists missing data well. But it claims 154 m is safe by a wide margin using numbers that are wrong. The 153.0 to 153.5 m recommendation comes from a flawed 0.64 m deduction. The 90 to 95% capacity and 20 to 30% underforecast margins are asserted without basis.

### Answer 2
- **KQ:** It treats 8 h, the decision delay, as the drawdown window when it should be 28 h. It ignores the downstream safe discharge as the binding constraint on release and drains at 7,500 m³/s. It accounts for the continuing inflow, which is to its credit. It raises pre-flood inflow variability, intervening-flow timing and storage above 152 m.
- **TC:** It contains several errors that change the conclusion. (1) The window is wrong: 8 h instead of 28 h. (2) The release of 7,500 m³/s would put 8,700 m³/s through the town during pre-release. (3) It finds 172.8 < 180 million m³ and still concludes that drawdown is possible, so its own arithmetic shows infeasibility. (4) It says the town faces 3,700 m³/s of excess "even with perfect reservoir operation", which misreads the purpose of the reservoir. (5) The "9 hours of outflow / 1-hour margin" rationale does not add up. (6) Its Three Gorges description, an operating level of about 140 m against a 145 m flood-limited level, is factually wrong.
- **ME:** Its verification lists are reasonable: hydrograph characterization, forecast skill and false alarm or missed-event rates, gate chain timing, level-dependent capacity and downstream routing. It has no acceptance criteria, and the method is built on the wrong core calculation.
- **PR:** It proposes alert levels, a seasonal reduction, conditions such as POD > 0.85, a fallback level of 152.5 m, a "rising forecast" trigger, regulatory compliance and a gauge for the intervening catchment. The level values themselves are not justified.
- **CA:** It invents a regulatory precedent. It asserts a theoretical maximum of "approximately 154 m" from inconsistent numbers. The proposal to draw to 151.5 m has no basis. Its recommended levels are not derived from anything.
