- Differences from the current rule: A +0.3 bn kWh (+3%), 0 pp flood, -2 pp irrigation, +2 days; B +0.5 bn kWh (+5%), +0.3 pp flood (30% relative; return period 100 -> ~77 yr, 1/0.013 = 76.9), +3 pp irrigation, -3 days; C +0.2 bn kWh, -0.1 pp flood, -7 pp irrigation, +6 days (+50%).

- Consistency check on the table (A raises generation but lowers refill; B improves three objectives at once), validation of the simulator against history, forecast dependence.
- Recommendation: keep current rule for now; A as lead candidate with conditions; B rejected as proposed but a redesigned B (staged / forecast-based flood-limit level) requested; C dropped; explicit adoption conditions, triggers, fallback, staged trial.
- Stakeholders with defined roles: commission, flood authority (veto on safety), dam-safety regulator, proponent (burden of proof), irrigation, navigation, forecasting agency, grid, rule-approving ministry, environmental agency, independent review, public; criteria agreed before results.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- A quantified statement of resolvability: e.g., a binomial standard error for a 1% estimate from N simulated years (about sqrt(0.01*0.99/N)), showing that tens of thousands of paired synthetic years are needed to separate 1.0% from 1.3% or 0.9% with confidence, and that the 40-year empirical record alone (SE ~1.6 pp) cannot.

- Concrete trigger examples (forecast inflow threshold and lead time for mandatory pre-release to the current flood-limit level; maximum drawdown rate) and a dynamic/staged flood-limit level specification for B'.
