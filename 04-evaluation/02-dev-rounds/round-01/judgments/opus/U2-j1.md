# Judgment U2-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Binding release is set downstream, not by the gates: Q_rel,max = 5,000 − 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s (< 7,500 m³/s, so outlet capacity does not bind). The 1,200 m³/s is assumed to coincide with the drawdown.
- Net drawdown rate = 3,800 − 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s (≈ 8.28 Mm³/h).
- Effective window = 36 − 8 = 28 h → ΔV = 2,300 × 28 × 3,600 ≈ 231.8 Mm³, which is more than 180 Mm³. With 90 Mm³/m extrapolated, the theoretical maximum is about 154.6 m, or a little lower if the curve widens. The storage curve above 154 m is not given, so this must be flagged.
- Time to evacuate 180 Mm³ = 21.7 h; with the 8 h chain, the flood must be detected about 30 h ahead.
- Sensitivities: worst-case 6 h update timing gives 22 h → 182.2 Mm³ (≈1% margin). A rising pre-flood inflow cuts the net rate. Release ramping, travel time and the timing of the intervening flow, and forecast error (timing, volume, misses) also reduce the margin. Taken together, 154 m is a break-even level, not a level with a safety margin.
- Asymmetric consequence: if the pre-release fails, the design and check floods start from 154 m. Routing must show the design and check flood levels, freeboard and the downstream protection standard are not degraded.
- Verifications: forecast hindcast by lead time (hit, miss and false-alarm rates; detection lead-time distribution), time-stepped routing with realistic hydrographs, downstream channel routing, historical replay, stress tests (N-1 gate, back-to-back floods, shorter lead time), risk compared against the current 152 m rule, and seasonal staging.
- Practicality: approval for dynamic flood-limited-level control from the competent flood-control authority, pre-authorised trigger and warning protocol, fallback and abort rules, and a trial period.
- Missing information: storage curve, characteristic levels, design hydrographs, gate and ramp data, basis of the intervening-flow figure and travel time, forecast performance, meaning of the "36 h", protection standard and original design basis, approval process.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1 (U2-ncan)

- **KQ.** Identifies every deciding question:
  - downstream binding at 3,800 m³/s;
  - coincidence of the intervening flow;
  - 28 h window;
  - update-cycle loss;
  - rising inflow;
  - the trustworthy-lead-time issue;
  - ramping;
  - the forecast-failure residual check, which it explicitly calls "often the binding one".
  
  It settles them with a scenario table and a provisional range.
- **TC.** All arithmetic checks out:
  - 231.8 Mm³;
  - 21.7 h;
  - a 1,786 m³/s required net rate, quoted as ≈1,790;
  - 182.2, 181.4, 142.6 → ≈153.6 m;
  - 132.5 → ≈153.5 m;
  - the ramp loss of 1,150 × 4 h ≈ 16.6 Mm³ is correct.
  
  The "two calculations to avoid" (352.8 and 298 Mm³) are correct and instructive. It correctly treats the linear extrapolation above 154 m as illustrative.
- **ME.** A complete, ordered procedure:
  - design and check flood routing with no and partial prerelease;
  - time-stepped mass balance;
  - hindcast;
  - Monte Carlo risk against the 152 m benchmark, with dependence between local and upstream inflow preserved;
  - a candidate-level scan in 0.1–0.25 m steps, taking the highest level that passes;
  - downstream hydraulics, historical replay, stress tests, and a benefit check.
  
  It has an explicit acceptance criterion.
- **PR.** Mentions:
  - authority approval ("a model-feasible level is not an authorized one");
  - trigger rules, staffing for the 8 h chain at night, and a monitored trial;
  - an early-drawdown trigger based on QPF;
  - the refill rule after a flood.
  
  It does not name the specific regulatory instrument and gives no explicit abort or fallback dispatch rule for when a drawdown is under way and the flood arrives early.
- **CA.** Assumptions are listed explicitly, and the meaning of the 36 h lead (onset or peak) is flagged. The recommendation is scoped as provisional and conditional. The 153.3–153.6 m range is tied to the combined adverse cases but adds no explicit margin for ramping on top, which is a minor softness.

### Answer 2 (U2-30wn)

- **KQ.** Finds the same deciding questions: the downstream binding release, the coincidence assumption, 28 h versus 22 h, rising inflow, ramping, forecast error, travel time, and the asymmetric failure consequence. It adds a clear "downstream protection-standard margin". It folds the worst-case update timing into the "theoretical maximum" (154.0–154.5 m), which blurs the requested theoretical value with a realistic one.
- **TC.** The arithmetic is correct:
  - 231.8 and 182.2 Mm³;
  - 21.7 h and about 30 h detection;
  - 154.6 and 154.0 m;
  - 143 Mm³, leaving the level at ≈152.4 m.
  
  The ramping loss is described as "several million m³". A ramp of about 4 h actually costs about 16 Mm³, so this is understated but does not change the conclusion. The 110–140 Mm³ behind the 153.0–153.5 m recommendation is reasonable but loosely derived.
- **ME.** The verification list is good: hindcast replay counting failures and near-misses, design-flood routing compared with the 152 m start, forecast-skill statistics, Muskingum or unsteady downstream routing, ramping scenarios, stress tests, risk against benefit, and seasonal staging. The margin table includes "how to size it" (for example, the 90–95% lower bound of detection lead time; an N-1 gate check). It lacks an explicit acceptance criterion or benchmark and a procedure for selecting the final level.
- **PR.** Recommends:
  - treating 152–154 m as a dynamic control range;
  - allowing 154 m conditionally, by season and on a high-confidence no-rain forecast that also covers the intervening catchment;
  - a pre-authorised decision protocol;
  - checking that 8 h holds at night;
  - downstream river users;
  - a sustained 3,800 m³/s release;
  - approval by the authority.
  
  Like Answer 1, it gives no explicit abort or fallback provisions.
- **CA.** The key assumptions and what would change them are stated, and the judgment is labelled provisional. Calling 154.0–154.5 m the "theoretical maximum" while also admitting the ideal value is 154.6 m is slightly inconsistent.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: strengths

- Clean derivation, with the two common wrong calculations shown and quantified.
- A scenario table that turns each realism effect into volume and level.
- Identifies the residual forecast-failure check as possibly binding.
- A benchmark-based acceptance rule (risk no greater than the current 152 m rule) with a candidate-level scan.
- Preserves dependence between local and upstream inflow in the risk analysis.
- An early-drawdown trigger based on QPF as a route to 154 m.

### Answer 1: gaps

- The planning range of 153.3–153.6 m is taken from the combined adverse case without stacking the ramp loss and a forecast-error allowance.
- No explicit fallback operating rule: what to do when the drawdown is incomplete and the flood arrives, or when to abort a false-alarm drawdown and refill.
- The downstream protection standard of the town, routed from the raised level, is only implicit in item 4.1.
- The regulatory framework is generic.

### Answer 2: strengths

- Explicit "dynamic control range" framing with seasonal conditional use.
- Sizing guidance per margin: a percentile of the detection lead-time distribution, and an N-1 gate check.
- A separate downstream protection-standard margin.
- Notes the asymmetric risk, which calls for a margin rather than break-even.
- Constraints on downstream users during a sustained 3,800 m³/s release.
- The flood-control design basis (how 152 m was derived) listed as missing information.

### Answer 2: gaps

- The "theoretical maximum" is stated as 154.0–154.5 m, mixing the ideal and worst-case values.
- The ramping loss is understated ("several million m³" against ≈16 Mm³ for a 4 h ramp).
- The recommended 153.0–153.5 m is not derived step by step.
- No acceptance criterion or benchmark for choosing the final level.
- No explicit abort or fallback rule.

### Points neither answer had

- Naming the specific Chinese regulatory basis: the dynamic flood-limited water-level control guidance and approval by the competent river-basin or provincial flood-control headquarters. Also, that the authorised range and the trigger indicators are written into the approved annual flood-control dispatch plan.
- An explicit fallback and abort protocol:
  - if detection comes later than about 30 h, start the maximum safe release immediately and hand over to the approved flood dispatch rules from the current level;
  - rules for stopping a drawdown on a false alarm;
  - a rule for lowering the limit automatically after a near-miss.
- Using the 8 h decision window productively: a provisional partial release or ramp-up started on a preliminary forecast, so the 8 h is not wholly lost.
- Recognising that 3,800 m³/s is itself below the 5,000 m³/s safe discharge. The "warning" part of the 8 h chain may therefore be shortened for releases of this size, which affects the usable window.
- Exploiting a gauged or real-time rainfall estimate of the intervening flow, so that the release is raised above 3,800 m³/s when local flow is low. This changes the evacuable volume dynamically.
- Checking that the flood-control storage still matches the town's protection-standard flood volume, not only the peak, when starting from the raised level.
