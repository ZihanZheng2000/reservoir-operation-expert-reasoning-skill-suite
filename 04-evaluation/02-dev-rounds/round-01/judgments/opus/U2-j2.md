## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Binding release constraint is downstream, not the outlets: Q_rel,max = 5,000 - 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s (< 7,500 m³/s capacity).
- Net drawdown rate = 3,800 - 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s.
- Effective window = 36 - 8 = 28 h; volume = 2,300 x 28 x 3,600 = 231.8 Mm³ (about 29% above the 180 Mm³ in 152-154 m). Time to empty 152-154 m = 21.7 h (29.7 h including the 8 h chain).
- Level: with no curve above 154 m, linear extrapolation (90 Mm³/m) gives about 154.6 m; a widening basin gives a little less. Theoretical maximum about 154.5 m.
- Erosion of the margin: 6 h update cycle (worst case 22 h -> 182 Mm³, about 1% margin), rising inflow, release ramping, forecast timing/volume error, intervening-flow timing and travel time; these combined make 154 m infeasible as a fixed limit.
- Residual-risk check: design and check flood routing from the raised level with no pre-release; downstream protection standard; successive floods; N-1 gate.
- Verifications: hindcast forecast skill (hit/miss/false alarm, lead-time distribution), historical replay, time-stepped mass balance, downstream routing, Monte Carlo risk vs. the 152 m benchmark, seasonal staging.
- Missing information: stage-storage curve and sedimentation, characteristic levels (normal pool, design/check flood levels, crest), discharge curve and gate data, intervening catchment basis and travel time, forecast record and lead-time definition, design hydrographs, regulatory approval and acceptable-risk benchmark.
- Conclusion: 154 m is a theoretical edge, not an operating limit; adopt a lower provisional value or conditional dynamic range pending verification and authority approval.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- Check that the proposed upper limit does not exceed the normal pool level or other characteristic levels that constrain the flood-season operating level, and how the dynamic upper limit relates to them.
- Note that the dam-to-town travel time can work for the operator as well as against it: release made late in the window reaches the town after the flood begins. This needs a combined routing that includes the arriving flood itself, not only the intervening inflow.
- Partial drawdown can start during the 8 h chain (for example a staged early release within the safe channel flow and without a full warning). Treating that period as zero net drawdown is conservative, and a pre-agreed staged protocol would recover some of the time.
- Explicit false-alarm economics: repeated unnecessary drawdowns lose stored water and sustain high downstream flows. The trigger threshold must balance miss risk against false-alarm cost.
- Reference to the formal dynamic flood-limited-water-level control procedure: upper limit taken as the minimum of the pre-release-capacity limit and the limit that keeps the design and check flood levels and the downstream standard unchanged, approved and filed with the competent authority.
