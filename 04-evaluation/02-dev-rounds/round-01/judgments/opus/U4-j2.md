# Judgment U4-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own outline)

- Clear verdict: do not replace the approved rule next season; at most shadow/advisory use, with the current rule as the legal operating basis.
- What the evidence supports: feasibility and a testable hypothesis for one event. It does not show real-time achievability, superiority over the rule, safety, or generalization.
- Deciding questions:
  1. Comparator: actual 2023 decisions vs the current rule simulated under identical inputs.
  2. Information leakage / hindsight: observed inflows vs forecasts as issued. Perfect-foresight upper bound vs realistic.
  3. Closed-loop simulation with mass balance, level-storage-discharge curves, outlet capacity by level, ramp limits.
  4. Volume trade-off: a peak cut at the same inflow must come from extra storage. Rough calculation: if the actual peak outflow is Qp and the model holds it to 0.7Qp, the extra storage is roughly the area between the two outflow hydrographs, about 0.3Qp × (duration the actual outflow exceeded 0.7Qp), less any gain from pre-release. That volume must be checked against the remaining flood-control/surcharge storage and the resulting maximum level against design/check flood levels and freeboard. End-of-event storage determines exposure to a following flood.
  5. Downstream control points: timing with tributaries and duration of high flows, not dam outflow alone.
  6. Generalization: one event; 13 seasons with few large floods; extrapolation beyond the training range; whether 2023 was strictly held out; regime changes in 2010–2022 data.
  7. Mechanism: imitation learning cannot plausibly beat its teacher by 30% without explanation. What objective and constraints were used?
- Ordered evaluation: reproduction/audit, simulator validation, rule-vs-model on identical inputs with as-issued forecasts, multi-event leave-one-out, stress/design floods, forecast-error and data-failure tests, full metric set, acceptance criteria fixed beforehand.
- Practical items: shadow season, fallback triggers, override authority, accountability, regulatory/manual revision approval, data pipeline readiness, operator training, staged pilot.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** It validates the simulator against recorded 2023 releases and levels, and treats constraint violations as failures rather than clipping them. It runs a no-forecast vs perfect-forecast bracket and stress-tests to design/check flood magnitudes, with correlated underforecast runs and seed/training-window stability. The pilot is bounded to small and medium floods with automatic reversion near thresholds, and the distributional reporting includes the share of events made worse.
- **Missing or wrong:** No explicit closed-loop requirement or downstream hydraulic/routing model setup. No mention of non-stationarity or regime change in the 2010–2022 training data. No benchmark against a simple forecast-informed or MPC policy to show whether ML adds value beyond fixing the rule. No after-action review of 2023 independent of the model. Operator training, model version control and cybersecurity, and emergency-action-plan updates are thin. No order-of-magnitude storage check.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** It makes closed-loop vs open-loop explicit, names leakage via lagged features, and flags regime change. Its baseline hierarchy (rule, actual, MPC/perfect-foresight optimization) and full metric set cover dam safety, downstream, feasibility, post-event state, other purposes, and robustness. The safety architecture includes fallback, version freeze, and cybersecurity. Operator training and EAP updates are included, as is an after-action review of 2023 that could improve the current rule sooner.
- **Missing or wrong:** No replay validation of the simulator against observed levels. No sensitivity to retraining or seeds. A clipping wrapper is offered without warning that evaluation must score the unclipped policy, or the wrapper itself, as the tested policy. The bottom line's "probably hindsight-informed" slightly prejudges the question. No storage calculation.

### Points neither answer had
- **Volume calculation:** an order-of-magnitude check of the volume needed for a 30% peak reduction (about 0.3 × Qp × the duration actual outflow exceeded 0.7Qp, net of any pre-release). Compare it with the remaining flood-control and surcharge storage at the 2023 starting level, and convert it to a maximum-level rise via the level-storage curve. This quickly shows whether the 30% is physically plausible without encroaching on the design/check flood levels.
- **Starting level:** whether the simulated 2023 start level and the flood-season limiting water level matched reality. A different initial storage alone can produce a large peak difference.
- **Flood frequency:** whether the 2023 flood's return period lies inside the range of 2010–2022 events, as a concrete test of extrapolation.
- **Joint validity of the claim:** the 30% must hold together with the downstream safe-discharge constraint and the flood-season limit level. A dam-outflow peak reduction that still exceeds the downstream safe discharge, or that comes with a higher maximum reservoir level, changes the benefit judgment.
- **Rule revision path:** an explicit statement that a rule change before the next season is infeasible within normal approval timelines. Any interim use must be advisory under the existing approved dispatch plan.
