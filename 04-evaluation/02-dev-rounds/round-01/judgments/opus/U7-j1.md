# Judgment U7-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- **Representation:** a reservoir mass balance in storage, with level limits converted to storage limits; the level-dependent capacity as a state-dependent constraint R ≤ Rmax(S), approximated conservatively (inner approximation) so that planned releases can actually be made; any ungated or free-overflow spillway treated as a forced, uncontrollable release; a reduced, fast routing model of the 120 km reach identified from the calibrated model, with the calibrated model kept as the reference; the two ungauged tributaries as uncertain lateral inflows, estimated from station residuals (observed station flow minus routed release), assimilated in real time, and kept correlated with the reservoir-inflow members.
- **Timing calculation:** 120 km at a flood-wave celerity of about 1.5–3 m/s gives a travel time of about 11–22 h, so 2–4 six-hour blocks. A release decided now acts on the station mostly after the next forecast update. The tributaries enter part-way down the reach, so their contribution can reach the station sooner than the dam release. The decision step and the scenario-tree branching must resolve this. Coordinating the routed release with the tributary peak is the core downstream control problem.
- **Pre-release feasibility:** the drawdown volume ΔV needs a time of roughly ΔV / Rmax(low level). Because capacity is lower at lower levels, this time has to be compared with the lead time at which the forecast has skill.
- **Method:** a rolling, multi-stage (tree-based) stochastic MPC, with a single first-stage release, risk control through CVaR or chance constraints, dam safety as a hard constraint, a terminal storage condition, and a convex (LP/QP) formulation so it solves within 30 min. Problem size estimated: roughly 10⁴ variables.
- **Alternatives rejected for stated reasons:** rule curves, deterministic MPC on the ensemble mean, a single release path shared by all members, SDP, evolutionary or policy search, deep RL, and nonlinear MPC with the full model. Each should keep an offline or fallback role.
- **Verification:**
  - closed-loop hindcast replay using only the information available at each time;
  - post-processing and verification of the forecasts (reliability, CRPS, tails, useful lead time), with leave-years-out validation;
  - timing statistics at the 95th/99th percentile and the maximum, with timeout behaviour and a fallback;
  - re-simulation of every recommendation with the exact curves and the full routing model;
  - comparators, including a perfect-forecast upper bound;
  - stress tests beyond the record;
  - shadow mode, operator and regulator review, and an audit trail.
- **Uncertainties:** a register of explicit assumptions with conservative defaults and sensitivity runs; a check that the hindcasts come from the same forecast system as the operational one; and checks on ramping limits, gate rules and Q_safe.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1

- **KQ (key questions):**
  - Finds almost every deciding question:
    - the level-dependent capacity limits pre-release;
    - the ungauged tributaries handled by residual back-calculation and assimilation;
    - correlation between tributary flow and reservoir inflow;
    - the non-identifiability of the split between the two tributaries;
    - non-anticipativity;
    - the terminal condition;
    - the useful lead time from hindcast skill.
  - It does not work out the reach travel time or its consequence for the 6-h decision step. It also does not test pre-release feasibility explicitly, as drawdown time against forecast lead.
- **TC (technical correctness):**
  - Mostly correct. The size estimate (25 × 40 × a few variables ≈ 10⁴) is reasonable.
  - "Q_max typically concave in S" is asserted too generally. Weir flow ∝ H^1.5 is convex in head, so concavity in storage depends on the reservoir's shape. The sequential-linearization fallback mitigates this.
  - The unit test "releases never above inflow when the level is below minimum" is oddly framed.
  - The illustrative "3 % vs 18 %" figures are clearly examples, not results.
  - None of these changes the conclusion.
- **ME (method):**
  - Complete and ordered: the 6-h cycle, scenario reduction, post-processing, verification by full model and exact capacity, back-off and re-solve, closed-loop replay with comparators including perfect-forecast MPC, a scaling study, and stress tests.
  - Gap: it does not say that the first-stage release is rounded to a gate setting before it is re-checked.
- **PR (practicality):**
  - Strong provisions:
    - operator units and hold times;
    - what-if tool;
    - change log and audit trail;
    - shadow mode and operator workshops;
    - regulator agreement on hard constraints;
    - a deviation procedure;
    - degraded modes for missing data;
    - partial gate unavailability;
    - maintenance.
  - Less specific than Answer 2 on how adoption is staged under a formal temporary rule change.
- **CA (calibration):**
  - Assumptions are explicit and there is a section on what would change the choice.
  - It warns that 20 years contain few extremes.
  - It checks the tool's own risk statements for calibration.
  - It says that tributary dominance should be communicated as a real limitation.
  - It does not check that the hindcasts were produced by the same forecast system as the operational forecasts.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1

**Done well:**
- Tree-based MPC with a single first-stage release, and an inner piecewise-linear capacity constraint.
- Regime-dependent impulse-response kernels identified from the calibrated model, with a back-off margin.
- Tributary residual back-calculation, with the split between the tributaries explicitly flagged as non-identifiable.
- Joint ensemble that keeps the inflow–tributary correlation.
- A strong table of alternatives, each with a retained offline or fallback role.
- Closed-loop replay with a perfect-forecast bound and a reliability diagram for the tool's own risk statements.
- A rich register of unknowns.

**Missing or wrong:**
- No reach travel-time estimate and no link between it and the 6-h decision step.
- No explicit test of pre-release feasibility (drawdown time at reduced low-level capacity against the forecast signal lead).
- The concavity of Q_max(S) is over-generalised.
- No rounding of the release to a discrete gate setting before the full-model re-check.
- No check that the hindcasts and the operational forecasts come from the same system.
- No staged path through a temporary rule modification.
- Decision stability (reversals) is listed only as an operational-burden metric.

### Answer 2

**Done well:**
- The same sound core design.
- Lexicographic priority solve instead of opaque weights.
- Gate-setting rounding before the full 51-member re-check.
- An explicit pre-release feasibility test.
- Qualitative treatment of travel time and its effect on block timing.
- A check that the hindcast system matches the operational one.
- A tree-size convergence test.
- Forced-timeout testing.
- Decision stability as a metric.
- A simpler ensemble risk-tolerance rule named as the main competitor.
- Staged adoption under a documented temporary deviation from the operating rules.

**Missing or wrong:**
- Travel time is guessed ("about a day") rather than estimated from 120 km and a plausible wave celerity.
- It does not note that tributary inflow can reach the station before the dam release does, which constrains when the release must be cut back.
- The design-flood terminal condition may be overly conservative and could force unnecessary drawdown. Its cost should be tested.
- It does not discuss how tree reduction affects CVaR estimates computed from about 16 branches.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had

- An actual timing calculation: 120 km at about 1.5–3 m/s gives about 11–22 h, so 2–4 decision blocks. This sets how far ahead releases must be cut so that the routed release and the tributary peaks do not coincide at the station.
- An order-of-magnitude pre-release check: the required drawdown volume divided by the low-level capacity gives the time needed, to be compared with the useful forecast lead.
- Treatment of any ungated or free-overflow spillway as a forced release (a lower bound on R that depends on the level), in addition to the upper capacity bound.
- Ramp-down limits on downstream flow during recession, and their interaction with the refill target.
- A check that the hindcast ensemble size and configuration match the 51-member operational ensemble (reforecasts often have fewer members), since this affects calibration of the risk statements.
- The sampling error of tail-risk (CVaR) estimates from a reduced tree, and a check that the first-stage decision is insensitive to it.
