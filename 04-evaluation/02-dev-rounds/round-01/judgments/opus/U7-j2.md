# Judgment U7-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (my own reference)

- **Representation.** Reservoir storage state with an exact mass balance. Level–storage and level-dependent release capacity Rmax(S) written as a constraint that the optimiser cannot violate, for example a conservative piecewise-linear inner approximation or sequential linearisation. The answer must state that concavity of Rmax(S) is not guaranteed: weir flow goes as H^1.5, and S(H) also bends upward. A reduced routing model (Muskingum cascade or impulse-response kernels) identified from the calibrated model, with flow-regime dependence and an error back-off. The calibrated model stays as the verifier. Two ungauged tributaries handled as a lateral-inflow uncertainty: back-calculated from station minus routed release, updated in real time from the station residual, and kept correlated with reservoir inflow.
- **Method.** Ensemble or tree-based stochastic MPC that rolls every 6 h. Release is non-anticipative in the first stage. Dam safety is a hard constraint. Downstream exceedance is controlled by risk (CVaR or chance). A terminal condition handles the end of the horizon. Only the next block is implemented, at gate-operable resolution, and a fallback rule runs alongside. It must be justified against rule curves, deterministic MPC on the ensemble mean, SDP, evolutionary or real-time simulation-optimisation, and full nonlinear MPC.
- **Key calculations and checks.**
  - Problem size: 10 days is 40 six-hour steps or 240 hourly steps. That times roughly 10–25 scenarios times a few variables gives about 10^4 variables, an LP or QP that solves in seconds.
  - The 30-min budget must be shared with ingest and the full-model re-simulation of 51 members.
  - Pre-release feasibility: the drawdown volume achievable within lead time is limited by min(Rmax(S), Q_safe − Q_trib) integrated over the lead time. This is a real limit when capacity falls at low levels.
  - Routing travel time must be resolved by the decision step.
- **Verification.**
  - Closed-loop hindcast replay using only information available at each time.
  - Comparators, including a perfect-forecast upper bound.
  - Timing percentiles on the largest events.
  - Zero-violation checks with the full models.
  - Forecast verification (CRPS, rank histograms, Brier) and post-processing.
  - Few extreme events in 20 years, so stress tests are needed. Cross-validation of anything tuned.
  - Reliability of the tool's own stated probabilities.
  - Decision stability.
- **Practice and uncertainty.**
  - Gate settings, ramp limits and hold times.
  - An explanation package (binding constraint, risk numbers, comparison with the rule).
  - Shadow mode, regulator acceptance of the risk level, a deviation procedure from the approved operating manual, overrides and audit logs.
  - An unknowns register with conservative defaults and sensitivity-based prioritisation.
  - Willingness to fall back to a simpler forecast-informed rule if the MPC does not earn its complexity.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: done well

- It makes the low-level capacity limit drive the timing of pre-release, and it verifies pre-release feasibility directly: required drawdown time compared with the first ensemble signal.
- It names a strong, simple competitor (an ensemble risk-tolerance rule) and makes adopting the MPC depend on its gain in the replay.
- Lexicographic priorities avoid arbitrary weights.
- Rounding to a gate setting, then a full nonlinear re-check on all 51 members, then a single tighten-and-re-solve, then fallback is the right operational loop.
- It runs a tree-size convergence test on the decision.
- It measures decision stability (flip-flopping).
- It checks hindcast/operational forecast-system consistency.
- It notes that the station acts as a lagged sensor for the tributaries.

### Answer 1: missing or weak

- There is no reliability check of the tool's own risk statements, that is, whether "x % exceedance" verifies at x %.
- It does not say explicitly that 20 years hold few extreme floods, so confidence intervals per event are needed.
- It does not write the pre-release bound as a quantity: achievable drawdown ≈ ∫ min(Rmax(S), Q_safe − Q_trib) dt over the warning lead.
- There is no partial-gate-outage scenario.
- It does not discuss a what-if tool for operators.

### Answer 2: done well

- It notes that the two-tributary split cannot be identified from station residuals, and uses an area-ratio prior.
- It checks risk calibration (a reliability diagram of the tool's probabilities).
- It stratifies events by driver (tributary against reservoir inflow, multi-peak, high initial storage).
- Stress tests include partial gate unavailability and fully correlated tributaries.
- The operator interface is rich: what-if, change log, fans under both the plan and the fallback.
- It includes a regulator deviation procedure and maintainability (surrogate re-identification, annual recalibration).
- It offers the option of using the MPC offline to design a codified rule.

### Answer 2: missing or weak

- It asserts that the capacity is "typically concave in S" without justification. Weir-type capacity is often convex in head, so this should be presented as something to check.
- The size estimate uses 6-h steps while routing is at 1–3 h.
- "The only class of method" overclaims.
- There is no check that the hindcast forecast system matches the operational one.
- There is no explicit re-check after rounding to the gate setting.
- The tree-size choice is not tied to decision invariance.
- Travel time against the 6-h step, and the lag of the first block's station effect, are not discussed.
- There is no explicit test of pre-release feasibility against lead time.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had

- **A worked bound on forecast-based pre-release.** Drawdown volume ≈ Σ min(Rmax(Z_t), Q_safe − Q_trib,t)·Δt minus the inflow volume over the lead time, compared with the flood volume above the flood-limited level. This shows quickly whether a 10-day ensemble can be exploited at all, given the capacity limit at low levels and the tributary allowance.
- **An explicit end-to-end time budget calculation.** For example, 51 full-routing runs × the per-run time of the calibrated model on N cores, plus 2–3 solves, plus the re-solve, against 30 min. Without it, the full-model check could itself break the budget.
- **Gated (controlled) outflow mechanics.** Gate opening–head–discharge relationships differ from the free-overflow capacity curve. Translating a target discharge into gate openings must use the controlled-outflow rating, and spillway gate opening sequences may constrain which discharges are reachable.
- **A legal and regulatory status for pre-release below the flood-limited level, or for temporary surcharge.** This covers who authorises the release, how liability for false pre-releases (lost storage) is assigned, and how the forecast-based operation is written into the approved dispatch rules, including trigger and abort criteria.
- **Hindcast ensemble size or configuration mismatch.** Reforecasts are often run with fewer members or at a lower frequency than the 51-member operational ensemble, which affects post-processing and replay validity.
