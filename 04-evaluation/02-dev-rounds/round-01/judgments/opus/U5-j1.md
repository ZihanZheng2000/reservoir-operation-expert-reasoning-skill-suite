# Judgment U5-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Free flood-control storage: upper 0.40 x 300 = 120 million m3 (180 occupied); lower 0.85 x 500 = 425 (75 occupied); system 545.
- Upper free storage (120) is below even the low-end forecast (150). Lower free storage (425) is above the high end (400). The upper reservoir is the bottleneck.
- Current operation already evacuates both pools. Upper: net 300 m3/s (about 1.08 million m3/h), about 32 million m3 over 30 h, so about 152 free at arrival. Lower: inflow about 1,500 (lagged) + 300 = 1,800 against a 2,500 release, net about 700 m3/s, so the 75 occupied is cleared in about 30 h. Option (b) therefore adds little.
- Timing: upper prerelease made during t0 to t+30 reaches the lower dam at t+18 to t+48 and the city at t+28 to t+58. That is before upper floodwater reaches the city (about t+58 or later). The only possible overlap is with the uncontrolled tributary.
- City check: upper prerelease of 2,500 to 3,000 plus a full 1,500 tributary is 4,000 to 4,500 even if passed straight through. That is below 6,000, but the margin depends on local inflow below the lower dam, which is not given.
- Upper at about 2,500 m3/s: net 1,300, about 140 million m3 over 30 h, so about 260 free at arrival. Waiting 12 h (option c) needs about 3,100 to 3,400 m3/s over 18 h to reach the same point.
- Forecast consistency: 250 million m3 over 72 h averages about 965 m3/s, which is below the current inflow. The figure must be flood volume above base flow, or its basis must be clarified.
- Conceptual core: occupied flood-control storage lies above the flood-limit level and should be evacuated under the operating rules anyway, so prerelease down to the flood-limit level is low-regret. Drawdown into conservation storage on an uncertain forecast is not.
- Volume is not the binding constraint. Peak shape (upper dam safety and the 8,000 m3/s capacity), tributary timing, local inflow, and downstream channel limits are.
- Recommendation: upper-led (a) at moderate, reversible rates, with the lower coordinated to clear and hold its flood-limit level. Switching thresholds tied to the next forecast, the tributary and local inflow, plus operational readiness, warnings, authority and logging.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: done well
- Clear bottleneck logic: upper free storage below the low end, lower free storage above the high end.
- Strong treatment of upper dam safety: a sharp peak can approach or exceed 8,000 m3/s with little free space, so hydrograph shape is the top missing item.
- A good scenario-screen design that uses the high-end case for dam safety and the central and low cases for water cost.
- The low-regret cascade argument: water released from the upper is caught in the lower's spare space.

### Answer 1: missing or wrong
- Does not frame the occupied storage as above the flood-limit level and therefore due for evacuation by rule. It treats lower evacuation as losing water "for good", a cost the rules usually do not count against flood-pool water.
- Holding the lower at 2,500 lets it store the upper's prerelease from t+18, so it never returns to its flood-limit level before the flood. No target is given for clearing the lower's 75 million m3.
- The (c) catch-up rate is overstated (3,500 to 4,000 vs about 3,200 to 3,400).
- No gate, power or staff readiness checks, no head-dependent capacity check, no decision log, and no explicit prohibition on drawing into conservation storage.

### Answer 2: done well
- States the decisive regulatory premise (A1/A2: evacuate only down to the flood-limit level, never into conservation storage) and makes it the explicit switch to (c).
- Shows that the lower is already evacuating its 75 million m3, so (b) is dominated.
- Correct catch-up computation under (c), plus a worst-case city check of upper prerelease plus a full tributary with no help from lower storage.
- Coordinated lower plan: raise to 3,000 to 3,500 now, then pass inflow from t+18 to hold the flood-limit level.
- Specific operational readiness items and hard limits.

### Answer 2: missing or wrong
- Upper dam safety under a sharp peak (inflow near or above 8,000 m3/s with limited free space) gets less development. Hydrograph-shape scenarios are not part of the routing check.
- The tributary trigger mixes "store" and "pass" for the lower without a clear rule.
- Raising the lower to 3,000 to 3,500 now uses city channel margin while the local inflow below the lower dam is unknown. It needs a local-inflow check before the ramp.
- The approval and authority step (who signs the order) is not stated as an action.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- The correlation between the upper-basin storm and the tributary storm. They are likely the same weather system, so coincidence of the tributary peak with the upper flood (around t+48 at the lower dam) should be the design case, not just one timing option.
- A joint flood-phase compensation rule written explicitly: lower release(t) is at most 6,000 minus local inflow, with upper release(t-18) + tributary(t) minus lower storage change balancing it. Upper flood releases should be throttled against the forecast tributary arrival.
- Refill risk: a quantified check of whether the flood-pool water released now would be recovered if the event comes in at the low end, and whether the flood season permits holding water above the flood-limit level.
- The upper reservoir's design flood and surcharge or dam-safety levels, the spillway rating at the actual pool level, and the consequence if the 400 upper bound is exceeded (the residual risk the 545 to 760 million m3 system storage cannot cover at peak).
- Formal reporting to and approval from the basin or flood-control authority, with a designated fallback order if communications or the forecast update fail.
