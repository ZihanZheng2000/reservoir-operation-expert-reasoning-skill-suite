# Judgment U1-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's reference)

- A research question framed as a constrained comparison: staged FLWL schedule vs. the current fixed level, under identical inflows. Dam-side and town-side flood risk must not increase, and only then are irrigation, hydropower and refill benefits counted.
- A diagnosis of whether the fixed level actually binds under the current rule. If the reservoir rarely sits at the FLWL in the shoulder periods, the gain is small.
- Flood seasonality from the 45-year record: timing of maxima, peaks and multi-day volumes by period, stage division into 2-3 operable stages, and stationarity and boundary-stability tests.
- Stage design floods that are consistent with the annual standard. The key calculation: the stages are roughly independent, so annual non-exceedance is the product of the stage non-exceedances. If each of n stages is designed at the annual p, the combined annual exceedance is 1-(1-p)^n, about np. For 3 stages at p = 0.01 that gives 1-0.99^3 = 0.0297, about 3 times the target. Holding the annual target at 0.01 needs each stage at 1-0.99^(1/3) = 0.00334, roughly 1-in-300. Keeping the annual return period per stage is therefore non-conservative, not conservative.
- Routing from candidate levels with daily-to-instantaneous peak correction, design hydrograph shapes, and a PMF/spillway-design-flood check for dam safety.
- Town safety: safe discharge, inflow from the local catchment between dam and town, and travel time. At 40 km the travel time is likely a few hours to under a day, so daily data cannot resolve it.
- Feasibility of drawdown at stage transitions and of pre-release: ΔV ≤ (Q_safe − Q_local − Q_in) × usable lead time. A worked order of magnitude helps, e.g. 1,000 m³/s net for 1 day ≈ 86 Mm³.
- Forecast skill (8 years, few large events) treated as a secondary layer.
- Long-term historical and stochastic simulation, robustness and split-sample tests.
- Graded claim levels tied to data obtained.
- Indian regulatory context: CWC/BIS design-flood criteria (IS 11223), reservoir-operation guidance (IS 7323) and rule-curve practice, Dam Safety Act 2021 / NDSA-SDSO review, and monitored trial operation with a fallback.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1

- **KQ (8):** Finds nearly every deciding question: seasonality, whether the fixed level binds (an explicit sub-question and diagnosis step, which is a strong point), consistency of stage design floods with the annual standard, transition drawdown, town safe flow, local inflow, travel time, sub-daily peaks, and the forecast layer kept separate. It settles the risk-combination question correctly in direction but gives no numbers. It does not raise the PMF/seasonal-PMP issue for dam safety explicitly.
- **TC (8):** No conceptual errors. It correctly says that applying the annual return period to every stage raises the combined annual exceedance, and it prefers direct annual-risk checking by continuous simulation. The pre-release bound (raise only by the volume releasable within the effective lead time at or below the safe release, net of forecast inflow and minus decision and travel delays) is correct. No arithmetic is given to check, for example the factor-of-n risk inflation.
- **ME (8):** Well ordered and executable: encode and validate the rule, baseline simulation, binding diagnosis, seasonality and stage division, stage frequency analysis with bootstrap, grid search over levels, transition feasibility, continuous historical and synthetic simulation with sub-daily event routing, and a three-condition forecast comparison (none / actual / perfect). Verification is strong: split-sample, leave-one-year-out, synthetic-series fidelity checks and convergence. Gaps: no named distributions or partition methods, and no rule for allocating stage probabilities.
- **PR (7):** Gives operable outputs (a schedule of dates, levels and transition drawdown rules), a gate-outage case, ramping, an approval route through the dam owner, dam-safety review and stakeholders, a monitored trial, and a fallback to staged drawdown when a forecast misses. It is not India-specific: there is no CWC/BIS guideline, Dam Safety Act or rule-curve framing.
- **CA (9):** Very well calibrated. The hypothesis is explicitly conditional on three tests. The claim-level table says that current data support "promising" only, the benefit threshold is to be set with stakeholders, assumptions are listed, and the answer states what would weaken or reverse the case.

### Answer 2

- **KQ (7):** Covers seasonality, stage stability, seasonal design floods and risk combination, town-side data, sub-daily peaks, PMF caution, onset variability with trigger-based transitions, and forecast pre-release. The cautions about the seasonal PMF and onset variability are valuable and absent from Answer 1. It treats "does the fixed level bind / does the reservoir already refill" only in passing, as a counter-result, rather than as an up-front diagnosis. It settles the key risk-combination question incorrectly (see TC).
- **TC (6):** It states the product rule correctly. It then calls keeping the annual return period in each stage "the conservative default", giving a combined risk "slightly above the annual target". This is wrong on both counts: the choice is non-conservative, and with 3 stages the inflation is about threefold (0.0297 vs 0.01), not slight. Because this choice "directly controls how much the FLWL can be raised", the error could push levels too high. The pre-release inequality ΔV ≤ (Q_safe − Q_int − Q_in) × lead time is correct. Its other points are sound: back-calculated inflow noise, LP3/GEV/P-III, and Mann-Kendall/Pettitt tests.
- **ME (8):** Executable and ordered: data checks and trend tests, multi-method stage division with sub-period stability, seasonal frequency analysis with POT and bootstrap, a floor at the observed stage maximum, multiple hydrographs, routing with explicit acceptance conditions, historical plus stochastic plus event Monte Carlo simulation, optional multi-objective optimization, and forecast error-tail tests. Verification against history is mentioned. Split-sample checks appear only for boundaries, not for out-of-sample checks of benefits and safety.
- **PR (7):** More India-specific (CWC/BIS, PMF/SPF, IMD onset triggers), with transition buffers (lower early, raise late), decision delays of 6-24 h, gate failure, drawdown-rate limits, regulator approval and a trial period. It does not name the Dam Safety Act 2021 / NDSA or a specific fallback protocol when a forecast fails during operation.
- **CA (7):** Good graded claim levels A/B/C and "results that argue against a change". However, the framing states as fact that the single FLWL "is set by the core-monsoon design flood" and that the reservoir "often fails to fill". The safety criterion allows an unspecified "tolerance" for increased risk, and the conservative/slight mischaracterization understates risk.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** It diagnoses whether the fixed level binds before claiming gains. Safety is a hard filter and benefits cannot buy it back. Annual risk comes from continuous simulation rather than summed stage probabilities. It uses a CVaR-type tail measure. It separates the value of staging from the value of forecasts with no / actual / perfect forecast runs. Split-sample and leave-one-year-out checks are included. A clear claim-level table ties claims to data obtained.
- **Missing or weak:**
  - No numeric illustration of risk inflation (3 stages × 1% ≈ 3%) or of the per-stage allocation (≈1-in-300 each).
  - No PMF or seasonal-PMP caution for the dam-safety check.
  - No Indian regulatory framing: CWC/BIS design-flood criteria, IS 7323 rule curves, Dam Safety Act 2021 review.
  - No treatment of variable monsoon onset or withdrawal, and no option for trigger-based instead of calendar transitions.
  - No transition buffers.
  - No check on whether the daily inflow is back-calculated.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** It cautions against a seasonal PMF without a seasonal PMP. It brings in IMD onset-based transition triggers and buffers around transitions. It sets a floor of the observed stage maximum on the stage design flood. It notes noise in back-calculated inflow, decision delays and drawdown-rate limits. It gives an explicit pre-release volume inequality and graded claim levels A/B/C.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - It calls keeping the annual return period per stage "conservative" with "slightly above" combined risk. It is non-conservative, and the inflation is about n-fold. This must be corrected, and per-stage allocation or direct annual-risk simulation should be the default.
  - It does not diagnose up front whether the fixed level binds under the current rule.
  - Its safety criterion admits an unquantified "tolerance".
  - It asserts current under-filling without evidence.
  - It runs no out-of-sample test of benefits.
  - It does not separate the value of staging from the value of forecasts, e.g. with a perfect-forecast bound.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- A worked risk-combination calculation: 1-(1-p)^n, with per-stage p = 1-(1-P_annual)^(1/n), plus a statement of how much this tightens the shoulder-stage design floods.
- A worked order-of-magnitude for transition and pre-release drawdown: ΔV from ΔH via the level-storage curve, against (Q_safe − Q_local − Q_in) × usable lead time. Also a travel-time estimate for 40 km (hours, not days), which shows that daily data cannot resolve the town-side timing.
- Explicit mapping to Indian practice: FLWL expressed as a seasonal rule curve or filling schedule under IS 7323 / CWC guidance; design flood per IS 11223 (PMF/SPF by dam class); approval via the dam owner, State Dam Safety Organisation and NDSA under the Dam Safety Act 2021, with the Emergency Action Plan updated; downstream warning coordination with district authorities.
- A specific operational fallback: a defined reversion trigger to the fixed level during the trial, e.g. after a near-miss event or forecast failure, and a post-season audit protocol.
