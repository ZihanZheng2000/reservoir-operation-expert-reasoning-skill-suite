### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had

- A note that only one or two forecast updates (t+12, t+24) arrive before onset. The decision schedule should be tied to those updates and to an interim request, with t+12 set as a hard review against the point of no return.
- A check that the 1,500 m³/s upper release assumed for 18 h ago is the actual lagged inflow to the lower reservoir. The lower trajectory depends on it.
- The channel capacity and safety between the dams at about 3,000–5,000 m³/s. Answer 1 only assumes it. It also matters for the upper flood-phase release.
- An explicit compensation rule for the lower reservoir in the flood phase: release = min(6,000 − local inflow, what storage allows), with the tributary treated as uncontrolled inflow to be stored.
- An allocation rule for the joint flood-control storage between the two dams under the high scenario (how much the upper passes versus stores), with dam-safety limits at both dams.
- The refill and regret side if the forecast falls to the low end. For example, whether the reservoirs are near the end of the flood season, and whether a dynamic flood-limited level applies.
- A probabilistic framing: what probability of the high scenario justifies prerelease, given the 150–400 range.
