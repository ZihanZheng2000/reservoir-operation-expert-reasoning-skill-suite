# Judgment U3-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own outline)

- Recognise that "95% confidence" is Monte Carlo variability conditional on the assumed error model, one hydrograph and one starting level; with n = 5,000, MC standard error on a proportion near 0.95 is about sqrt(0.95*0.05/5000) = 0.003, so the number is precise but answers a narrow question. Ask whether it refers to the sign or the size of the effect, and to the mean, median or paired share.
- Error-model flaws with direction of bias: pooling across magnitude (heteroscedasticity, sigma too small at flood flows), lead time (day-3 error understated, and day 3 drives prerelease), season; normal/zero-mean hides flood-time underforecast bias and heavy tails; independent daily sampling cancels errors in the 3-day volume. Var(sum of 3) = 3 sigma^2 (1 + 2 rho) for equal pairwise correlation, so at rho = 0.7 the SD is about 1.55x what was simulated. Successive-issuance correlation and timing/shape errors are also missing. Net direction: all flatter the forecast-based rule.
- The 12-year record almost certainly lacks a 100-year event: P(at least one) = 1 - 0.99^12 = 11%. So the error behaviour at design magnitude is an extrapolation.
- Scenario design: one hydrograph, initial level, other return periods (frequent floods where prerelease harms; dam-safety design/check floods/PMF), false alarms and misses (a design-flood-conditioned study cannot see false-alarm costs), back-to-back floods.
- Physical plausibility: is an 18% cut achievable with the flood-control storage (only part of 600 Mm³), outlet capacity and downstream safe discharge within the 3-day lead? Mass-balance check.
- Metrics: max water level/freeboard, tails, probability that the new rule is worse, downstream flows caused by prerelease, refill reliability.
- Required work: stratified, heteroscedastic, dependent error model, validated against observed errors; hindcast with archived forecasts; perfect/no-forecast brackets; error-inflation break-even analysis; fair baseline and out-of-sample tuning; simulator verification.
- Adoption: acceptance criteria agreed with the dam-safety and flood-control authority, operating-manual revision approval, shadow operation, reversion triggers, downstream warning protocol, post-event review.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** It gives a thorough taxonomy of error-model flaws, including timing/shape errors and forecast-system non-stationarity, which Answer 2 covers only partly. It points out that independent sampling creates a "self-correcting" forecast stream. It recommends a real-forecast hindcast, break-even sensitivity, out-of-sample tuning, ensemble forecasts if available, expected-annual-damage weighting across return periods, and propagation of design-flood uncertainty. It has clear confirm/reject criteria.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - There is no physical-feasibility (mass-balance) test linking the 18% to flood-control storage, outlet capacity, downstream safe discharge and the 3-day lead.
  - It does not quantify the 12-year sample (an 11% chance of containing a 100-year event) or the MC standard error.
  - The correlation formula is loosely written and the SD statement is muddled.
  - It has no perfect/no-forecast bracketing.
  - The approval pathway is generic: it does not say who approves, that the current rule remains the legal basis, or what the pre-agreed acceptance criteria are. "Shadow operation" is only implied by "trial period".
  - It does not rank the deciding questions.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:**
  - It states the deciding questions explicitly and gives a direction-of-bias table for each simplification.
  - It checks the correct variance formula numerically and quantifies the 12-year record and the MC standard error.
  - Its mass-balance plausibility test is clearly flagged as illustrative.
  - It notes that design-flood conditioning hides false-alarm costs.
  - It brackets with perfect and no forecasts and adds error-inflation break-even.
  - It uses a bootstrap over events for error-model parameter uncertainty.
  - It gives a concrete regulatory pathway: pre-agreed criteria, the operating manual as the legal instrument, shadow season, reversion triggers and a downstream warning protocol.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - The mass balance uses a rectangular ΔQ × duration. It should use the hydrograph volume above the new peak, minus the flood-pool space already available under the current rule.
  - Timing/peak-shift errors and design-flood (quantile) uncertainty get less attention than in Answer 1.
  - It does not mention expected-annual-damage weighting across return periods.
  - It does not mention climate or non-stationarity of floods.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- Conditional (flow-dependent) bias modelled as a joint forecast–observation relation, for example a meta-Gaussian/NQT or Bayesian processor of the forecast. This yields the predictive distribution conditional on a large forecast, rather than an unconditional error added to a design hydrograph. Neither answer noted that adding errors to a known design hydrograph inverts the operational information flow: the operator sees the forecast, not the truth.
- That autocorrelated errors also reduce the effective sample size used to fit σ. Twelve years of daily pairs are far fewer independent samples than their count suggests, which widens parameter uncertainty.
- Separating the source of forecast error, chiefly quantitative precipitation forecast (QPF) skill at a 2–3 day lead for the basin's flood-producing storm types, and checking whether the forecast skill for the 3-day volume is sufficient relative to the prerelease volume decision. Examples are the probability of detection and false-alarm ratio at the trigger threshold.
- Explicit liability and compensation considerations for prerelease-induced downstream damage on false alarms. This is a frequent decisive institutional barrier.
- A statistical test of the paired difference (common random numbers), with the effect reported as a distribution of paired differences per run rather than a single percentage.
