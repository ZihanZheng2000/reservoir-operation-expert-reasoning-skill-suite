# Judgment U1-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- A falsifiable research question: whether within-monsoon flood hazard differs enough to justify staged FLWLs, and whether a staged rule keeps the dam and the town at least as safe as the current rule while increasing refill, irrigation and energy.
- Deciding questions: (1) is the seasonality real and stable given 45 years? (2) is the annual risk, combined across stages, kept at or below the standard and the current rule? (3) is the town (not only the dam) safe? This needs safe discharge, intermediate-catchment flow and travel time. (4) does the benefit exist at all, or does the reservoir already refill in most years?
- Risk combination: for n independent stages each designed at annual p, the annual risk is 1-(1-p)^n. For p = 1% and n = 3 that is about 2.97%, almost three times the standard. So either allocate the risk across stages (prod(1-p_i) >= 1-p) or simulate annual risk directly.
- Resolution limits: 45 years cannot test 1% risks directly. Stochastic generation is needed, with a Monte Carlo size of about (1-p)/(p e^2) years. For p = 1% and e = 10% that is about 9,900 years.
- Downstream: 40 km at about 1-3 m/s is about 4-11 h, shorter than the daily step. A sub-daily reach routing model is needed, and town flow = routed release + local inflow.
- Pre-release / drawdown feasibility: dV = A x dh, and the time is dV / (release minus inflow), with release capped by Q_safe minus local flow. Compare this with the reliable forecast lead time after allowing for decision and gate delays.
- Forecasts: 8 years is too short for the tail of the error distribution, so forecasts must not carry the safety case.
- Dam-safety check against the spillway design flood (PMF/SPF per CWC/BIS practice, IS 11223), routed from the raised start level. Sedimentation-updated capacity, gate-failure and successive-flood cases.
- Sufficiency criteria and graded claims that depend on which data are obtained. An Indian regulatory path: owner, state dam safety organisation, the Dam Safety Act 2021 and NDSA, and a revised O&M manual / rule curve. A shadow trial, reversion triggers and downstream warning.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ (7):** Identifies seasonality and its stability, combined annual risk, the reservoir-side vs town-side safety gap, forecast limits, and the "reservoir already refills" failure mode. It does not frame the benefit question as sharply as Answer 2 (the benefit exists only in years when the current rule falls short) until the counter-indicators list. It also leaves the risk-combination question loosely settled.
- **TC (6):** Most content is sound: risk product rule, GEV/LP3/P-III, trend tests, and the pre-release inequality using the net rate (Q_safe - Q_int - Q_inflow) x lead time. But it calls keeping the annual return period for each stage "the conservative default" that gives a combined risk "slightly above the annual target". That is wrong on both counts. For 3 stages at 1% the combined risk is about 3%, nearly triple the target, and the choice is the non-conservative one. This error directly controls how much the FLWL could be raised, which is exactly the point the answer itself says matters most. There is no arithmetic anywhere to check the claims.
- **ME (7):** The sequence is clear and executable: QC and baseline, stage division with several methods and a split-sample check, seasonal design floods with bootstrap CIs, routing to find the maximum FLWL, historical and stochastic simulation, forecast strand, stress tests. Gaps: no Monte Carlo sample-size reasoning, no sub-daily reach routing model is specified (only "lagged by travel time"), no common-random-numbers comparison, and no out-of-sample test of the selected schedule.
- **PR (6):** Mentions CWC/BIS guidance, regulator approval, a trial period, transition buffers, drawdown rules, gate failure and decision delays. Its Level A/B/C claim grading is useful. But the regulatory path is generic: no Dam Safety Act, no named approving body, no explicit reversion triggers, no downstream warning or district administration role.
- **CA (8):** Scopes claims well. It states plainly that the listed data cannot support town safety, grades claims by data obtained, labels its provisional expectation as testable, and flags the 8-year forecast tail. The mislabelled "conservative" risk choice is a small calibration slip as well.

Very slight tendency to state numerical anchors that might be read as site facts, but they are clearly marked as placeholders.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** clear reservoir-side vs town-side distinction; graded claim levels A/B/C; caution against a seasonal PMF without a seasonal PMP; a full stress-test list (transition shifts, upper-bound floods, hydrograph shape, sedimentation, climate, gate failure, successive floods); a forecast pre-release inequality using the net release rate; the benefit expected to concentrate in dry or late-failing years.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - Mislabels the "annual return period per stage" choice as conservative with "slightly" higher risk. It is non-conservative and gives about 3x the risk for three stages. This must be corrected, and risk allocation or whole-season annual-risk simulation made the primary test.
  - No sample-size logic for stochastic simulation, and no common random numbers.
  - No travel-time calculation or sub-daily reach routing model.
  - No out-of-sample validation of the chosen schedule.
  - Regulatory path generic: no Dam Safety Act 2021 / NDSA / SDSO, no reversion triggers, no downstream warning protocol.
  - No check that current losses actually come from the fixed FLWL, rather than demand or release capacity.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** correct and checked arithmetic throughout (risk combination, Poisson resolution, Monte Carlo size, correlated forecast errors, travel time, drawdown time); safety screens before benefits; realistic starting levels; man-made rises; an operational fallback with named triggers; a specific Indian institutional path; a mass-balance cap on benefit and a count of benefit-bearing years.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - Does not discuss that Indian spillway-design-flood checks are conventionally routed from FRL, so raising FLWL below FRL may not change the dam-safety IDF result. The binding constraint is more likely the town or the moderate-flood cushion. This should be checked rather than assumed.
  - The hydropower benefit is treated mostly as energy and firm power, without the head-effect or tailwater detail.
  - Non-stationarity handling is only a trend test plus a revisit trigger.
  - Inflow derived from the water balance (noisy back-calculated inflow) is not flagged.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- Relating the "flood-limited level" concept to Indian practice. Indian reservoirs usually operate by a monthly or 10-daily rule curve / filling schedule toward FRL, with a flood cushion, under CWC rule-curve guidance. The study should frame the staged FLWL as a revised rule curve / filling schedule and compare it with the existing curve.
- Explicit recognition that the IDF/PMF dam-safety check in Indian practice starts at FRL. The added risk from staging therefore shows up mainly at moderate floods and at the town, so the town-protection standard (often a 25-100-year flood) is the key acceptance metric.
- Quantifying the upper bound of the benefit early, before modelling: the maximum gain per year is about A x dh in the late stage, compared with live storage and with the irrigation and power shortfall in non-refill years. This screens whether the study is worth doing.
- A drawdown-rate limit for upstream slope and embankment stability during transitions, and the operating chain of who orders gate openings, given the short warning to the town.
- Interaction with upstream reservoirs or the inter-state river board, if the basin is regulated, and any water-sharing tribunal constraints on release timing.
