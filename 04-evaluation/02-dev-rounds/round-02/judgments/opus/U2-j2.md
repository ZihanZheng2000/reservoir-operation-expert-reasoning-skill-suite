# Judgment U2-j2

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Allowable reservoir release = 5,000 - 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s. The town, not the 7,500 m³/s gate capacity, is the binding limit.
- Net drawdown = 3,800 - 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s, which is 8.28 million m³/h.
- Usable time = 36 - 8 = 28 h. Releasable volume = 231.8 million m³, which exceeds the 180 million m³ by 51.8.
- Time to empty 152-154 m = 21.7 h. Adding the 8 h latency, the flood must be detected at least about 29.7 h ahead.
- Theoretical ceiling: with storage linear at 90 million m³/m, about 154.6 m. Since the reservoir widens with height, the true value is somewhat lower, and the storage curve is needed.
- The 6 h update cycle means a flood can first be detected with only about 30 h of lead. Usable time is then 22 h and the releasable volume 182.2 million m³, which leaves almost no margin at 154 m.
- Sensitivity: rising inflow on the early limb, ramping, latency spread, uncertainty in the intervening flow, and travel time.
- Verdict: 154 m is break-even under worst-case update timing, so it is not a safe fixed limit. Either a lower limit or a conditional dynamic range is needed.
- The controlling consequence is a missed or late forecast. Design and check floods must be routed from the raised start level.
- Verifications: forecast hindcast skill (detection, false alarms, timing), closed-loop back-testing, design and check flood routing from the raised level, downstream routing, N-1 gate availability, and risk compared with the 152 m baseline.
- Missing information: storage curve, discharge curves, forecast skill, basis for the intervening flow, travel time, design floods and characteristic levels, flood-season staging, procedures, and approval authority.
- Operating provisions: dynamic pre-release trigger, reversion triggers, refill rule, downstream warning for man-made releases, and approval by the flood-control authority.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: done well

- The downstream-limited release is correctly derived. The full calculation chain is shown and checked.
- It recognises that the 6 h update phase alone uses up the margin at 154 m.
- The confirmation-wait scenario links forecast reliability to the ceiling.
- It correctly treats storm-driven inflow, intervening flow and forecast error as correlated.
- It makes dam safety under a missed forecast the controlling test.
- It gives a concrete operating rule with abort and reversion triggers, and costs false alarms.
- It addresses the drawdown-rate limit for bank and upstream-slope stability.

### Answer 1: missing or weak

- It does not define the refill rule: when and how fast to go back up to the ceiling after the recession, and on what forecast.
- It does not say what share of the total flood-control storage 180 million m³ represents. That share governs how much the town's protection standard degrades.
- It assumes the 8 h chain is strictly serial, without discussing whether warning and gate preparation can overlap.
- It does not state the relevant Chinese guidance for dynamic control of the flood-limited level, beyond naming the approval authority.

### Answer 2: done well

- Same correct core calculation.
- It clearly presents 154 m as the "edge of feasibility" and stresses the asymmetric consequence.
- It proposes a conditional, staged dynamic range and allows 154 m only under defined forecast and seasonal conditions.
- A good margins table, including wind setup and waves, and the protection standard if pre-release fails.
- Downstream hydraulic routing is named explicitly.

### Answer 2: missing or weak

- No operational decision rule: no trigger tied to remaining slack, and no abort or reversion triggers.
- It does not discuss the false-alarm cost, the need to wait for a confirming forecast, or downstream warning rules for man-made releases.
- No acceptance criterion for the risk comparison, and no treatment of correlated inputs.
- It does not check drawdown-rate limits for slope or bank stability.
- It does not discuss the limited sample size of the historical record.
- The theoretical maximum is reported as a blended range rather than kept separate from the operational assessment.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had

- **Express the ceiling as a function of the current forecast.** The allowable level should be a curve or table indexed by current inflow and forecast inflow, for example as the inflow forecast for the next 36 h. The allowable starting level falls as the rising-limb inflow grows. This replaces a single fixed number.
- **A refill rule after a flood.** Raise the level back towards the ceiling only after the recession, on a forecast showing no flood within the effective lead. Set the refill rate so that the band can still be emptied if a new storm appears.
- **Protection-standard check.** State what fraction of the total flood-control storage 180 million m³ is. Check explicitly whether the town's design return-period protection is preserved when a flood starts from 154 m. This is distinct from dam safety.
- **The basis for the 36 h lead.** Separate rainfall-forecast lead from hydrological lead. Check whether the 36 h depends on quantitative precipitation forecasts, and whether its skill drops sharply beyond 24 h for convective and typhoon storms.
- **Joint occurrence of intervening flow and the reservoir flood.** Treat it statistically, for example with copula or coincidence analysis of local and mainstem peaks. This is needed to justify any value lower than the full 1,200 m³/s.
