# Judgment U4-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own outline)

- Clear verdict: no direct replacement next season; current approved rule stays the legal basis; shadow/advisory operation; staged path to formal rule revision through the competent authority.
- Evidence scoping: one retrospective event; the comparison is against actual operator decisions, not the current rule simulated on identical inputs; the result supports a hypothesis and an after-action review, not a general or safety claim.
- Deciding questions:
  1. Information leakage / hindsight: observed future inflows vs forecasts as issued; 2023 used for tuning or model selection.
  2. Closed-loop vs open-loop simulation; same initial level; simulator validated by replaying actual releases.
  3. Mass balance: a 30% lower peak outflow must be stored or pre-released. ΔV ≈ ΔQ × duration × shape factor must fit in the remaining flood-control storage below the allowed level, or come from a pre-release that a forecast reliable at that lead time could justify. Consequences: maximum level, freeboard, end-of-event storage, exposure to a second flood.
  4. Downstream effect at control points (travel time, tributary coincidence), not dam outflow alone.
  5. Baseline confounding: why operators released what they did (orders, gate outages, downstream state, wrong forecasts).
  6. Imitation paradox: a model that imitates past operators should not beat them by 30% without better information, a different objective, or a poorly handled 2023.
  7. Generalization: one event is n=1; 13 years rarely contain floods near design or check magnitude, so extrapolation risk; non-stationarity within 2010–2022 (rule changes, sedimentation, sensor changes).
- Evaluations: independent reproduction; closed-loop with forecasts as issued; comparators (actual, simulated rule, simple forecast-informed rule, perfect-foresight upper bound); leave-one-event-out; stress and design floods; failure modes; full metric set; acceptance criteria fixed in advance, judged on worst case.
- Preparations: constraint/safety wrapper, reversion triggers and named decision owner, data pipeline, version control, operator training, regulatory approval, stakeholder and emergency-warning communication.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** It has the broadest list of unresolved questions, including regime change in the training period, held-out hygiene, explainability, accountability, the after-action review of 2023 independent of the model, MPC and perfect-foresight benchmarks, and the "no silent model updates during season" rule. The full metric set covers dam safety, downstream impact, operability, post-event state, other purposes and robustness.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - No mass-balance feasibility check. It should compute ΔV ≈ ΔQ × duration × shape factor and compare it with the flood-control storage remaining below the allowed level, or with the pre-release a forecast would have had to justify.
  - It does not require the same initial level, or validation of the simulator by replaying the actual 2023 releases against observed levels.
  - The extrapolation point is not quantified (for example, the chance that 13 years contain a 100-year flood; the rule-of-three bound on failure rate from a few events).
  - Acceptance criteria are "most events" rather than "no worse than the simulated current rule in any event (worst case)".
  - Reversion triggers lack thresholds and a named decision owner.
  - It does not say that one shadow season is unlikely to contain a significant flood, so stress and synthetic testing must carry the safety evidence.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** It frames the case around three deciding questions. The mass-balance ΔV illustration and the pre-release timing test are correct. The rule-of-three and exceedance-probability arguments are correct. The four comparators are run with identical inputs, and the simulator is validated by replay. Stress tests are judged event by event on worst case. Reversion triggers have an owner. It notes that the benefit mechanism may be captured by a transparent rule revision, which would be easier to approve.
- **Missing or wrong:**
  - It does not raise non-stationarity within 2010–2022 (rule revisions, new downstream works, sedimentation changing the storage curve, sensor changes) as a reason the training data may misrepresent current conditions.
  - Multi-purpose impacts (refill, supply, hydropower, environmental flows) appear only as a question, not as acceptance metrics.
  - It does not address the gate-movement frequency limits or rate-of-rise warning implications of a reshaped hydrograph.
  - The "PMF where it applies" stress test needs a statement that the model must not be in the loop for spillway-design-level events without a hard rule override. This is implied but not stated.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- A check of the inflow series itself. Reservoir inflow for 2023 is usually back-calculated from level change and outflow, and is noisy. Uncertainty in the reconstructed inflow (and the level–storage curve, possibly outdated by sedimentation) propagates directly into the claimed 30%. The audit should bound this.
- A check that the model's schedule complies with the current rule's own mandatory provisions: required evacuation of the flood-control pool before the season, level-triggered mandatory releases, and maximum-allowed level by date within the flood season. A schedule that violates the approved rule cannot be a like-for-like comparison.
- Quantitative attribution of the 30%. The gap should be decomposed into a forecast component (perfect foresight vs forecasts as issued), a policy component (model vs simulated rule under the same forecasts) and an operator-deviation component (actual vs simulated rule). Only the policy component is relevant to the decision to replace the rule.
- A statement of the decision timeline against the season start. If audit, multi-event testing and regulatory approval cannot finish before the season, the only lawful options for next season are shadow mode, plus at most a pre-approved, transparent forecast-informed adjustment within the existing rule's envelope.
- Statistical treatment of the multi-event comparison: paired differences per event with uncertainty, rather than a count of "wins".
