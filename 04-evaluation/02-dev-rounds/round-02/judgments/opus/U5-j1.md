# Judgment U5-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Free flood-control storage: upper 0.40 x 300 = 120 Mm³ (180 occupied); lower 0.85 x 500 = 425 Mm³ (75 occupied).
- Current trajectory: upper net drawdown 300 m³/s, about 32 Mm³ over 30 h, so about 152 Mm³ free at onset. Lower inflow is about 1,500 (lagged upper release) + 300 (tributary) = 1,800 against a release of 2,500. That is a net drawdown of 700 m³/s, which clears the 75 Mm³ in about 30 h. The lower reservoir is therefore already prereleasing in effect, so (b) is nearly redundant.
- The upper reservoir is the binding storage: about 120 to 152 Mm³ free against 150 / 250 / 400 Mm³.
- Timing: upper prerelease reaches the lower dam after 18 h and the city after 28 h, which is before the upper flood (lower dam at t+48 h or later). The tributary enters above the lower dam, so the lower reservoir controls it. City flow is the lower release plus any local inflow.
- Window: emptying 180 Mm³ by t+30 h needs a net of about 1,670 m³/s if started now, and much steeper rates after a 12 h wait. This sets a point of no return.
- Forecast consistency: 250 Mm³ over 72 h averages about 965 m³/s, which is below the current 1,200 m³/s inflow. The volume basis (excess or total) must be confirmed.
- Regret asymmetry depends on whether the occupied storage lies above the flood-limited level.
- Immediate actions, coordinated releases with a city-flow cap, triggers for the 12 h update, a tributary forecast request and approval or authority provisions.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** It identifies the binding role of the upper reservoir, shows that (b) is dominated, handles the timing and non-stacking of prerelease water, and raises the volume-basis ambiguity and the flood-zone versus conservation-water condition. It sharpens the decision with an explicit point of no return (about hour 15 to 18) and a stress test showing the city is probably safe under all options. These settle the deciding questions well. It treats the tributary somewhat less centrally than Answer 2.
- **TC:** The arithmetic checks out: 180 / 6.48 is about 28 h; the post-wait rates are about 3,800 and about 5,100 m³/s; a triangular 400 Mm³ / 24 h event gives a mean of about 4,600 and a peak of about 9,300; the storage above 4,500 is about 105 Mm³; 425 / 12.6 is about 34 h; and a sustained tributary excess over 72 h is about 311 Mm³. There are minor loose points. "Peak above base flow" is mixed with absolute release thresholds. The idea of raising the upper release to 4,500 to 5,000 m³/s in the escalation trigger is not reconciled with the pool already being nearly emptied at 3,000 m³/s.
- **ME:** It gives an ordered action list, a trigger table and catch-up rates. It has no explicit routing or scenario-check procedure before the next forecast, and it does not set out a verification step.
- **PR:** It covers ramping and drawdown-rate limits, head-dependent outlet capacity, equipment, staff and power, downstream warnings with timing, and HQ authorization under the flood-control plan with logging and an override authority. It mentions dam safety at the upper dam. It does not consider the flow capacity or damage limits of the channel between the dams.
- **CA:** It is well scoped. It explicitly says the city is probably safe under all three options and frames (a) as low-regret, not decisive. Assumptions are marked as such, including the ±6 to 12 h timing. It reads the volume conservatively, and its fallback is a modest 2,500 m³/s if the flood-zone status cannot be confirmed.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1, done well:** It quantifies the point of no return and the catch-up release rates. The stress test shows that the options differ in margin, not in whether the city is safe, which makes the low-regret framing well calibrated. It notes upper dam safety, the authorization and override chain, and a modest fallback rate if the flood-zone status is unconfirmed.
**Answer 1, missing or weak:** It needs a formal scenario-routing check before the 12 h update that covers tributary timing (early, coincident, late). It does not consider the flow capacity or damage limits of the channel between the dams. The tributary is under-weighted as an information gap. The escalation rates for the upper reservoir should be reconciled with the pool already being emptied. The "peak above base" basis is unclear.

**Answer 2, done well:** It has a clear routing-check protocol and constraint checklist. The tributary is correctly ranked as the top gap. Its quantitative comparison of system storage under (a) and (c) is sound. It sets hard operating limits (a city-flow cutback near 5,000 and a lower-reservoir reserve floor). Its A4 assumption covers channel damage and evacuation.
**Answer 2, missing or weak:** It has no explicit point of no return and does not stress-test the peak under (c). It does not justify why the target is 85 to 90% free rather than a full flood pool. It names no approval authority and no deviation-authorization step under the flood-control plan. It is overconfident about the basis of the forecast volume. The reserve figure is underived.

**Points that neither answer had:**
- The 12 h forecast cycle is shorter than the 18 h travel time. Upper water released at the next decision point still clears the lower dam before t+48 h. Neither answer turns this explicitly into the latest-safe-decision time for the lower reservoir, as distinct from the upper.
- Upper prerelease water only moves storage into the lower reservoir. It gains system capacity only if the lower reservoir passes it to the city. Neither answer budgets the lower reservoir's pass-through against its own reserve for a tributary storm that arrives during the prerelease.
- They do not quantify refill risk or the hydropower and supply loss for the low-volume outcome, given that the forecast mean is below the current inflow, and they do not tie this to the reservoir's actual flood-limited level or dynamic-limit rules.
- They do not check the lower dam's own safety or its spillway rating curve when it receives upper pass-through near 8,000 m³/s.
- They do not assign named responsibilities for the cascade, such as a basin authority versus the individual dam owners, or set out a communication protocol between the two dams.
- They give no attention to rainfall over the reservoir areas and the tributary before t+30 h, which could raise the inflow during the prerelease itself.
