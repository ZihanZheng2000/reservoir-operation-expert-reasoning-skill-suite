# Judgment U6-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own working)

- Deltas vs current rule: A +0.3 bn kWh (+3%), risk 0, irrigation -2 pp, navigation +2 d; B +0.5 (+5%), risk +0.3 pp (relative +30%, return period 100 -> ~77 yr), irrigation +3 pp, navigation -3 d; C +0.2 (+2%), risk -0.1 pp, irrigation -7 pp, navigation +6 d (+50%).
- No scheme Pareto-dominates the current rule; B is the only one that worsens city flood risk, and the only one that improves all other objectives -> the crux.
- City flood safety (and dam safety) is a constraint/veto, not a tradeable weight. If the city standard is 1-in-100 (1%/yr), the current rule is already at the limit and B fails on its point estimate.
- Resolvability: 40 years at 1%/yr gives ~0.4 expected exceedances (P(no exceedance) ~0.67 at 1.0%, ~0.59 at 1.3%); estimates are model-driven; need parameter + sampling uncertainty, paired (common random numbers) differences, and enough synthetic years. Irrigation reliability from 40 seasons has SE ~4.3 pp.
- Severity/tail metrics (exceedance magnitude, duration, EAD), dam-side check (design/check flood routing from the raised start level, surcharge loss, successive floods), forecast-error realism for any pre-release scheme, pre-release must not itself create man-made exceedance.
- Bad-year metrics for conservation objectives (vulnerability, resilience), hydropower value not just kWh.
- Recommendation: keep current rule in force; take A forward conditionally; reject B as proposed but request a flood-neutral B-variant (dynamic/forecast-based or staged flood-limit level); C dominated in practice by A. Conditions: intervals, dam safety sign-off, agreed minima for irrigation/navigation, benefit sharing, shadow/trial operation, reversion triggers, flood authority retains drawdown command.
- Participation: commission, flood-control authority (veto), dam-safety regulator, city/emergency management, hydropower operator (burden of proof), grid dispatch, irrigation district, navigation authority, hydromet services, environmental agency, independent review, public; criteria fixed before results; joint fact-finding; formal approval through the legal operating instrument.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- Done well: explicit safety-first screen using the upper bound; quantitative resolvability analysis (record power, binomial SE, paired vs unpaired sample size); dam-safety routing with realistic equipment assumptions; severity/tail metrics; man-made-flood rule for pre-release; pre-release volume feasibility test; staged season and forecast-based variants; study QA checklist; shadow-mode trial, reversion triggers and audit; detailed stakeholder roles including grid dispatch and hydromet certification of hindcasts.
- Missing/weak: no vulnerability/resilience/worst-year metrics for irrigation and navigation; generation treated in kWh not revenue/firm output/peak value; no mention of upstream inundation/reservoir backwater from a higher level or sediment storage loss; mechanism for A vs B irrigation divergence asserted rather than asked as a consistency check; "unlikely" for A passing is slightly overstated.

### Answer 2
- Done well: clean trade-off table with return-period conversion; strong argument for treating flood safety as a constraint (catastrophic, irreversible, accountability); paired-difference framing; bad-year metrics (vulnerability, resilience, worst year) and hydropower value (timing, firm output); explicit consistency check on A vs B irrigation directions; contractual minima turning into constraints (90% contract -> A at the edge, C fails); B' variant; benefit-sharing uses; upstream residents; joint fact-finding and criteria-before-results.
- Missing/weak: no quantitative support for the claim that the differences are within uncertainty (no SE, no sample-size estimate); does not draw out that a 1% standard makes the current rule a binding boundary; dam-safety check only named, not specified (design/check flood routing from raised start level, gate-out, successive floods); no pre-release feasibility test or "pre-release must not cause man-made exceedance"; no shadow-mode operation or audit logging; no study-QA list (current-rule validation, closed-loop, local inflow share of city exceedances); legal instrument for adoption left generic.

### Points neither answer had
- Explicit check that the raised level stays within the legally approved flood-limited water level / flood-control plan, and that a permanent change requires revision of the approved flood-control dispatching rules (not just a commission decision), including whether the design basis of downstream levees assumed the current flood storage.
- Joint/correlated uncertainty across indicators (e.g. bootstrap of the whole indicator vector per scheme) and a multiple-comparison caution when ranking four schemes on four noisy indicators.
- Value of the generation gain set against the cost of the conditions and compensation in a simple per-year ledger (e.g. revenue of +0.3 bn kWh at a stated tariff vs irrigation crop-loss and navigation cost), to test whether A is worth the institutional cost at all.
- Rate-of-rise/drawdown limits during pre-release for downstream bank stability and navigation safety, and the interaction of navigation shortfall timing with lock operation in the transition from flood season to refill.
- Explicit consideration of whether the current rule's 1% itself already reflects a deficient safety margin given 40-year record and possible nonstationarity, which would argue for a risk-reduction target rather than "no worse than current".
