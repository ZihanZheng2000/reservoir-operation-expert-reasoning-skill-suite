- Missing/weak: no quantification of resolvability (no sample-size or binomial reasoning, no P(no exceedance in 40 yr)); dam-safety check named but not specified (design/check flood routing from the raised level, gate-out-of-service, surcharge storage); no mention of local/tributary inflow to the city or man-made rises from pre-release; no pre-release feasibility check for B′; trial lacks a shadow-mode phase and concrete legal instrument; "modest and compensable" for A asserted without dry-year evidence.

### Answer 2
- Done well: explicit deciding questions; quantified resolvability with correct arithmetic and paired-simulation sample size; dam-safety routing with realistic equipment assumptions; severity/tail measures; pre-release volume check; study-validity list (closed loop, hindcasts, local inflow, ramping limits); concrete trial, reversion triggers, legal instrument.
- Missing/weak: unpaired-SE slip for C's irrigation difference; Condition 1 (upper bound of A's absolute risk ≤ current value) is near-unachievable as written—a pre-agreed tolerance on the paired difference (as in Answer 1) is the workable form; hydropower valued mainly as kWh (less on firm power/peak value/dry-season output); fewer tail indicators for irrigation (vulnerability, consecutive failures); does not mention upstream inundation/backwater effects or sediment.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- Clarify what "raising the flood-season operating level" means for A and C: if A and C raise the flood-limit level yet show unchanged or lower city risk, the study's flood-routing or rule definitions must be reconciled—request the actual rule curves (flood-limit level, period boundaries, refill start date) for each scheme alongside the table.
- Quantify the money at stake: convert +0.3/+0.5 bn kWh to annual revenue and compare with irrigation crop-loss and navigation cost estimates to size compensation (both mention it only generically).
- Downstream risk at points other than the city (rural reaches, embankments, floodplain detention areas) and whether the "safe threshold" itself is current given channel changes.
- Reservoir-side effects of a higher level: upstream backwater, inundation of reservoir-margin land and resettlement limits, bank/landslide stability under higher sustained levels (Answer 2 touches drawdown rates only).
- Consideration of whether a 1% city exceedance probability already reflects coordinated operation with other reservoirs/detention basins in the system, and whether the change affects that joint operation.
