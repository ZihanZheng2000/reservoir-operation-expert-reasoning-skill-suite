# Judgment U7-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own reference)

- Representation: storage mass balance; level-dependent discharge capacity written conservatively (chords/inner approximation of a concave Q_max(S) stay convex and feasible; non-concave segments such as the gate-control to free-overflow transition need MILP/SOS2 or successive linearization); a fast linear routing surrogate fitted to the calibrated model, with tributary junctions at true locations; the full model kept as a verification layer.
- The deciding timing question: release travel time over 120 km at a wave celerity of roughly 1.5 to 3 m/s is 120,000/(1.5 to 3) s, about 11 to 22 h. That is longer than the 6 h update. So the first release block is committed, and tributary peaks arriving within that lag cannot be offset by cutting the release. This sets the time step, the non-anticipative first stage and the "point of no return".
- Ungauged tributaries: back-calculate the combined lateral inflow as station flow minus routed release; note that the split between the two is not identifiable; generate scenarios conditional on each reservoir-inflow member, because the same storms drive both. Independence understates risk (variance 2σ²(1+ρ) against 2σ²).
- Method: multi-stage (tree) stochastic MPC, LP/QP, a shared first-stage release, risk control (CVaR, worst-branch or chance constraint), a terminal storage condition, rolling every 6 h. Compare it with deterministic MPC on the ensemble mean, a single shared path across all members, two-stage recourse, SDP, evolutionary search, rules and RL, giving the reason each is rejected.
- Reliability limits: 51 members can resolve probabilities only down to 1/51 ≈ 2%. The 20-year hindcast holds few independent flood events, so differences between policies have wide error bars. Post-process the ensemble and check its reliability, and check that the hindcast system version matches the operational feed.
- Verification: a run-time distribution (p99 and maximum, not the mean) in flood conditions; exact-capacity and full-routing re-checks; surrogate error carried into the margin; a closed-loop leave-years-out replay against the current rule, the deterministic forecast and perfect foresight; stress tests; checking the tool's own risk statements; a timeout fallback.
- Practicality: gate-settable steps and hold times; stay within the operating manual; an audit trail and reproducible results; shadow mode; acceptance criteria agreed with the regulator in advance; reversion triggers; downstream warning. Unresolved items become configurable conservative parameters with sensitivity tests and named owners.

## 1. Reasoning per criterion

### Answer 1
- **KQ:** It explicitly names and settles the deciding questions: the release travel time against the tributary timing and the 6 h cycle (point of no return), the ensemble's ability to support the risk level (1/51 resolution, event count in the hindcast, tributary correlation), and convexity/speed/explainability. Each one drives a design choice (hourly step for 48 h, conditional tributary scenarios, tail post-processing if the risk level is ≤2%). It is very complete.
- **TC:** The arithmetic checks out. 120 km at 1.5 to 3 m/s gives 11 to 22 h. The binomial SE √(0.05·0.95/60) ≈ 0.028 is correct. 2σ²(1+ρ) at ρ = 0.8 gives 3.6σ², a ratio of about 1.34 in SD. 1,000 m³/s × 24 h = 86.4 × 10⁶ m³. Chords of a concave function lie below it, so the capacity constraint is conservative. Checking capacity at the lowest level in the block is a correct, subtle point. The size estimate (~80 steps × 16 branches × 10 variables ≈ 10⁴) is consistent. One small weakness: "a margin built on independence would be about 25% too thin" is loose (1/1.34 ≈ 0.75, so the margin is about 25% short, which is acceptable). The claim that a "similar setup elsewhere took several hours" has no source.
- **ME:** The pipeline is complete and ordered: the representation table, tributary estimation steps, lexicographic objectives, a time budget per step, tree-size sweep, surrogate-fidelity loop, closed-loop replay with five benchmarks, leave-one-year-out tuning, stress cases, and reporting of statistical resolution. A terminal storage condition beyond day 10 appears only as an "end-of-event target", not as a formal terminal value or constraint.
- **PR:** Very specific: gate steps and hold times; a one-gate-out default; the "no man-made flood" rule; the tool constrained to the manual, with "would require approval" flags; acceptance criteria agreed with the dam-safety authority before the trial; shadow mode, then decision support, then manual revision; reversion triggers with a named decision owner; a deterministic, reproducible solver; downstream notification; after-event review.
- **CA:** Assumptions are labelled (wave speed, events per year, gauged station, concavity). It states what would change the choice, including adopting the simpler rule if the replay cannot resolve a gain. It does not claim benefits. The minor issue is the unsourced "several hours elsewhere" remark.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- **Done well:** It quantifies the travel time (11 to 22 h) and turns it into the point of no return, the time-step design and the committed first block. It states the 1/51 ensemble resolution limit and the event-count binomial SE for the replay. It shows the tributary correlation variance inflation and conditions tributary scenarios on reservoir-inflow members. Its capacity checks are conservative, including capacity at the falling level. It uses lexicographic objectives including "no man-made flood", mass-balance plausibility of the promised benefits, a design-flood check when holding above the flood-limited level, and a per-step time budget with a p99/max criterion. It has strong regulatory and adoption provisions and reversion triggers with a named owner.
- **Missing or weak:** There is no explicit terminal storage condition or value-of-storage beyond day 10. CVaR is not offered as a risk measure option, and there is no check that the tool's own stated probabilities match observed frequencies. The unsourced "several hours elsewhere" claim should be removed. It does not discuss the case where tributary-driven floods leave the reservoir little ability to reduce downstream risk, beyond gauging. The forecast data latency (issue time against availability) inside the 6 h cycle is not addressed.

### Answer 2
- **Done well:** It has an explicit terminal condition and explains why one is needed. Forecast post-processing is detailed and focused on tail dispersion. Scenario reduction is done on the joint reservoir-plus-tributary series. It offers CVaR or chance constraints. It checks the tool's risk statements against outcomes. Realistic data latency is built into the replay. It gives regime-dependent surrogate kernels and a clear alternatives table that includes full nonlinear MPC as the verification layer. Its operator interface has a what-if tool and a change log. It honestly states that tributary-dominated risk may limit what the reservoir can do.
- **Missing or wrong:** It does no travel-time calculation, so there is no point-of-no-return or committed-block reasoning, and it does not justify a 6 h decision step against an 11 to 22 h lag. It misses the 1/51 ensemble probability floor, even though it promises "P(exceed) = x%" outputs. The illustrative 3% vs 18% figures should be flagged as hypothetical. It has no per-step time budget and no p99 run-time criterion tied to flood periods. There is no "no man-made flood" constraint and no capacity check at the falling level within a block. It does not check that the hindcast forecast version matches the operational one. Reversion triggers and decision authority are less concrete.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- The location of each tributary confluence and its own travel time to the station, compared with the dam-release travel time, to establish which part of the station hydrograph is controllable at each lead. Also the surrogate's peak-timing error expressed in hours against that window.
- How the existing manual's emergency or surcharge provisions (for example, release equal to inflow or gates fully open above a trigger level) take precedence over the optimizer. The tool should detect and hand over to that regime explicitly.
- Gate actuation time and staffing constraints (how quickly a commanded change is physically achieved, night operations), and tailwater or downstream stage effects on the outlet rating at high flows.
- Uncertainty in the level–storage curve itself (sedimentation, survey date) and its effect on the flood-control volume available, handled as a margin.
- The reforecast configuration of the hindcasts (often fewer members or a lower issue frequency than the operational 51-member, 6-hourly feed) and how that limits the reliability statistics that can be transferred to operation.
