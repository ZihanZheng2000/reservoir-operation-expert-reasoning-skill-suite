# Judgment U1-j1

## What an excellent answer must contain (my own reference)

- Deciding questions: (1) is there a real, stable within-season difference in flood hazard (peaks AND multi-day volumes), with a physical cause, beyond the sampling noise of 45 years; (2) does the staged rule keep annual risk to the dam and to the town at or below the current rule and the design standard, combined over stages and transitions; (3) is the benefit real, i.e. does the current fixed FLWL actually cause failure to refill / spill, or is performance limited by demand or release capacity.
- Risk combination: n stages each designed at annual frequency p gives annual risk 1 − (1 − p)^n; for p = 1 %, n = 3: 1 − 0.99³ = 2.97 %, about 3 times the standard. Either allocate risk (Π(1 − p_i) ≥ 1 − p) or simulate whole seasons and compare annual risk directly.
- Daily data vs. peaks: daily means flatten peaks; need sub-daily hydrographs or peak/mean ratios. Travel time over 40 km at 1–3 m/s is roughly 4–11 h, i.e. inside one daily step; town flow = routed release + intermediate local inflow.
- Sample-size reasoning: 45 years cannot check 1 % risks directly (0.45 expected exceedances); stochastic generation with Monte Carlo sizes ~ (1 − p)/(p e²) (≈ 9,900 yr for p = 1 %, e = 10 %), common random numbers for rule comparison.
- Forecasts: 8 monsoons are too short for tail error; pre-release feasibility ΔV = A·ΔH ≤ (Q_safe − Q_local − inflow during drawdown) × usable lead time, minus decision/gate time; forecast is a margin, not the safety basis.
- Dam safety: inflow design flood (often PMF/SPF per CWC / IS 11223 in India) routed from each stage FLWL; caution on a "seasonal PMF".
- Benefit: end-of-monsoon storage/refill probability, irrigation reliability, energy and firm power, spill; benefit concentrated in non-refill years; mass-balance bound.
- Sufficiency: all of seasonality real, dam safety holds, no increase in annual risk at the upper confidence bound, material robust benefit, operable rule with fallback, safety data actually obtained; graded claims if data missing.
- Practicality: owner (state WRD/irrigation dept or power utility), Dam Safety Act 2021 (SDSO/state committee, NDSA), CWC guidance, revision of the operation manual/rule curve, shadow operation, reversion triggers, downstream district/disaster authority warning.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had
- Name the governing Indian design-flood criteria (IS 11223 / CWC guidelines: PMF for large dams, SPF or 100-yr for smaller) and the fact that the current FLWL's basis must be recovered from the project's design/operation manual before staging is argued.
- Check whether the dam's spillway capacity and MWL were designed assuming the reservoir at FRL or at FLWL at flood onset; if designed at FRL, the FLWL mainly protects the town, which shifts the binding constraint entirely downstream.
- Treat the downstream channel capacity as possibly time-varying (encroachment, sedimentation in the 40 km reach) and seek the district flood-danger level and historical flood damage records.
- Link stage transition timing to the rabi irrigation start date and the hydropower firm-power commitments, and value the energy/irrigation gain economically to compare with the cost of the rule change.
- Explicit plan for multi-reservoir or upstream-dam interactions in the basin, if any, which affect both inflow stationarity and the town's coincident flows.
