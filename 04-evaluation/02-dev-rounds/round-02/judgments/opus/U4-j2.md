# Judgment U4-j2

## What an excellent answer must contain (judge's own outline)

- Clear verdict: no direct replacement next season; current approved rule stays the legal basis; shadow/advisory operation, then staged adoption subject to the authority that approved the rule.
- Deciding questions: (a) information leakage / hindsight (observed inflows vs forecasts as issued, 2023 used for tuning); (b) baseline fairness (actual operator decisions vs the current rule simulated under identical inputs and constraints); (c) closed-loop physical consistency (mass balance, level-storage-discharge, gate capacity at head, ramp limits); (d) what the peak cut cost: extra storage, max level, freeboard, end-of-event storage, downstream control-point stages (timing with tributaries); (e) generalization: one event, 13-year record, extrapolation to design/check floods; (f) the imitation paradox (a model learned from operators beating them by 30%).
- Calculation: mass balance of the peak reduction, delta V of roughly delta Q x duration x shape factor, compared with remaining flood-control storage above the flood-limit level and with the pre-release volume achievable within reliable forecast lead time and safe downstream discharge; probability arguments for record length (1 - 0.99^13 of about 12%) and small-sample failure-rate bounds (rule of three); check 2023 peak inflow vs training-period maximum.
- Method: reproduce, validate the simulator against the actual 2023 releases, closed-loop rerun with forecasts as issued against multiple comparators (actual, rule, simple forecast-informed rule, perfect-foresight bound), leave-one-event-out, design-flood and stress tests, failure modes, pre-agreed acceptance criteria.
- Practicality: approval instrument and authority, hard safety envelope and override, fallback/reversion triggers and who decides, operator training, downstream warning chain, version control, after-action review of 2023 applicable to the rule itself.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1
- Done well: ranked deciding questions; mass-balance check of the 30% claim with explicit physical-ceiling logic (storage vs pre-release); record-length probability and rule-of-three sample-size bound; simulator validation by replaying actual 2023 releases; four comparators including perfect-foresight upper bound; mechanism attribution leading to the option of a transparent forecast-based rule revision; concrete reversion triggers.
- Missing/wrong: pre-release time should be bounded by safe downstream discharge and ramp/drawdown-rate limits, not outlet capacity alone; no explicit downstream routing/hydraulic model; no mention of regime changes (rule revisions, new works) in the training period; does not ask to compare the 2023 peak inflow with the training-set maximum; other purposes (refill, supply, hydropower) only touched; no mention of forecast ensemble/probabilistic information.

### Answer 2
- Done well: comprehensive metric set including minimum freeboard, surcharge encroachment, duration above flood thresholds, end storage for a following storm; regime-change and tuning-on-test concerns; MPC / optimization benchmarks to separate ML value from simpler improvements; safety wrapper plus human-in-the-loop; after-action review of 2023 as a faster route to rule improvement; accountability and stakeholder points.
- Missing/wrong: no quantitative reasoning anywhere (mass balance of the 30% cut against available flood-control storage, pre-release feasibility within forecast lead time, sample-size / record-length argument); no simulator validation step; "probably hindsight-informed" asserted without evidence; ten unranked questions dilute which ones decide the case; reversion triggers and approval instrument not concretely specified.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- Express the 30% relative to peak inflow: compute the actual and modelled attenuation (peak outflow / peak inflow) and check whether the actual operation was already bound by a rule constraint (e.g., outflow not to exceed inflow, or a mandated control-point limit), which would make the operators' release non-discretionary.
- Check the starting level against the flood-limit water level in both runs and the remaining flood-control storage up to the design flood level, and require the delta V check to use the reservoir's actual storage-level curve.
- Compare the 2023 peak inflow and volume with the 2010-2022 maximum to state directly whether 2023 was in- or out-of-sample in magnitude.
- Require uncertainty bands on the claimed reduction (e.g., ensemble-forecast or bootstrap over forecast error) rather than a single deterministic number.
- Consider multi-reservoir / basin coordination: the model was trained on single-reservoir behaviour, but releases in the basin are dispatched jointly, so adoption affects other reservoirs and control points.
- Name the legal liability shift if operators follow a model-recommended release that contributes to damage, and require the model's role to be written into the approved operating manual / emergency action plan before any non-shadow use.
