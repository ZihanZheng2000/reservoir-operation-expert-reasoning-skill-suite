# Judgment U3-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- **What "95% confidence" means.** It measures Monte Carlo spread under the firm's own error model. With n = 5,000 the sampling SE of a proportion near 0.95 is √(0.95·0.05/5000) ≈ 0.003, which is negligible. Error-model uncertainty, hydrograph uncertainty, initial-level uncertainty and design-flood uncertainty are all left out. The answer should also note that it is unclear whether the confidence is about the sign of the effect or its size.
- **Error-model flaws and the direction of their bias:**
  - Pooling across flow size understates flood-time σ.
  - Pooling across lead time understates day-3 error, and day 3 is what drives prerelease.
  - Pooling across season dilutes flood-season errors.
  - A zero-mean normal hides peak underforecast bias and has thin tails. Additive errors can also give negative flows.
  - Sampling each day independently understates 3-day volume error. Var(sum of 3) = 3σ²(1+2ρ) for equal pairwise correlation, so the SD is up to √3 too small at ρ→1. It also ignores correlation between successive forecast issues and timing errors.
  - The net effect is that every flaw flatters the forecast-based rule.
- **Extrapolation to the 100-year flood.** The chance that 12 years contain at least one 100-year event is 1−0.99¹² ≈ 11%, so the error fit almost certainly contains no event of that size.
- **Scenario scope:**
  - Only one hydrograph and one initial level were tested.
  - Frequent floods, dam-safety design and check floods, false alarms, missed events and back-to-back floods are missing.
  - The answer should note that a design-flood study is conditioned on the flood occurring, so it cannot reveal the cost of false alarms.
- **Metric scope.** The review should ask for:
  - the maximum water level and freeboard, and the tail probability of exceeding safety levels;
  - downstream flows caused by prerelease;
  - refill probability;
  - the share of runs in which the new rule does worse.
- **Physical plausibility.** A mass balance should test the 18% claim: the storage freed by prerelease within the effective lead time, at the downstream safe discharge, against the volume the 18% cut requires. The answer should also note that the relevant volume is the flood-control storage, not the 600 million m³ total.
- **Fair comparison.** Both rules should be run on common random numbers with the same simulator. The current rule should be encoded as it is actually operated, and the new rule should not be tuned on the evaluation data.
- **Required work:**
  - a stratified, heteroscedastic, correlated error model, validated against the record;
  - a hindcast with archived real forecasts;
  - a broader scenario set;
  - bracketing runs with no forecast and with a perfect forecast;
  - a stress test with inflated error, with a break-even error level;
  - bootstrapping of error-model parameters;
  - simulator verification.
- **Adoption path.** The new rule needs approval by the competent authority, with acceptance criteria agreed in advance. The path should include shadow or trial operation, reversion triggers, downstream warning protocols, and handling of liability and compensation for flooding caused by prerelease.

## 2. Notes for revision

**Answer 1: strengths**
- It quantifies everything that can be checked: the Monte Carlo SE, the 11% chance of a 100-year event in 12 years, and the correlated volume-error factor.
- Its mass-balance plausibility test spells out the reporting list.
- It brackets the result with no-forecast and perfect-forecast runs and a break-even stress test.
- It points out that a design-flood study cannot see the cost of false alarms.

**Answer 1: missing or weak**

- It does not check the new rule's tuning on held-out data.
- It does not ask about nonstationarity of forecast skill within the 12 years, beyond testing segments if the system changed.
- It does not propagate the uncertainty of the design flood estimate itself.
- It does not weight across return periods by expected annual damage.
- It does not suggest using ensemble forecasts directly.

**Answer 2: strengths**
- It covers timing and peak-shift errors and nonstationarity of the forecast system.
- It flags in-sample tuning and requires split-sample evaluation.
- It propagates design-flood uncertainty and weights by expected annual damage.
- It suggests using ensemble forecasts directly.
- It treats missed events (unforecast floods) as a separate case.
- It asks for downstream routing that includes tributary inflows.

**Answer 2: missing or wrong**
- It has no mass-balance or feasibility check. It never tests whether the prerelease volume available in the lead time at the safe downstream discharge can support an 18% cut. It never separates the flood-control storage from the 600 million m³.
- Its variance-inflation formula is garbled.
- It gives no numerical checks: no Monte Carlo SE and no exceedance probability for the 12-year record.
- It does not bracket the result with no-forecast and perfect-forecast runs.
- It names no regulatory approval body, does not keep the current rule as the legal basis until approval, and sets no acceptance criteria in advance.
- It gives no specific reversion triggers and no plan for shadow operation without gate control.

**Expert points neither answer had**
- **Effective lead time.** It is less than 3 days after accounting for forecast issue time, decision latency and gate travel time. It also depends on the rule's trigger threshold. The analysis should test sensitivity to the trigger parameters, and the false-alarm and miss rates at that threshold.
- **Whether the 18% matters downstream.** If the current rule's peak outflow is already at or below the downstream safe discharge, an 18% cut may have no damage value. If it is above, what matters is the reduction in peak stage at the control points. Neither answer ties the 18% to a stage–damage relation.
- **Liability for prerelease flooding.** A rule that deliberately releases water before the flood creates legal exposure for the owner if that release causes harm. Indemnity or compensation arrangements and documentation of the decision basis should be required before adoption.
- **Interaction with the flood-season limit level.** The answer should ask whether the rule implicitly raises the flood-season limit level, or the conservation pool it relies on refilling. That would be a separate regulatory change requiring its own approval.
- **Effective sample size of the error fit.** Daily errors within an event are autocorrelated, so the 12-year pooled sample holds far fewer independent pieces of information than the count of days suggests. The parameter uncertainty should be bootstrapped by event block, not by day. Answer 1 touches on this but does not state it as the reason.
