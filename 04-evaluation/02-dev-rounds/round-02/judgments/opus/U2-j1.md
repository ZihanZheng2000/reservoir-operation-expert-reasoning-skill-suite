# Judgment U2-j1

## Reference: what an excellent answer must contain

- Binding release constraint is downstream, not the gates: Q_rel,max = 5,000 - 1,200 = 3,800 m³/s (7,500 m³/s capacity is not binding). State the coincidence assumption for the intervening flow.
- Net drawdown rate = 3,800 - 1,500 = 2,300 m³/s = 8.28 million m³/h.
- Effective time = 36 - 8 = 28 h; volume = 2,300 x 28 x 3,600 = 231.8 million m³ > 180 million m³. Time to empty 152-154 m = 21.7 h, so detection needed at least ~29.7 h before arrival.
- With a 6 h update cycle the guaranteed detection lead is ~30 h, leaving 22 h and 182.2 million m³: essentially zero margin at 154 m.
- Convert volume to level: linear extrapolation at 90 million m³/m gives ~154.6 m theoretical; flag that storage above 154 m is unknown (wider surface means a lower true level).
- Judgement: 154 m is the break-even/edge case, not an operating limit with margin; rising inflow on the rising limb, gate ramping, forecast timing/volume error, latency overruns, travel time and timing of intervening flow, storage-curve error each consume the margin and are correlated within one storm.
- Controlling check: route design and check floods (and back-to-back floods) from the raised start level with a missed forecast; dam must stay within design/check flood levels and the town's protection standard must not be lowered.
- Verifications: forecast hindcast skill (detection, false alarm, timing/volume error), closed-loop back-test, downstream routing, N-1 gate, risk comparison with current rule, seasonal staging.
- Missing info: stage-storage above 154 m, discharge curves, intervening-catchment basis and timing, travel time, forecast skill, design hydrographs and characteristic levels, decision-chain statistics, drawdown-rate limits, approval route.
- Practical provisions: trigger/abort/reversion rules, warning for man-made releases, approval by the competent flood-control authority, 152 m remains legal basis until approval, trial operation.

## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1

- **Done well:** It reaches the correct core calculation and recognises the 6 h update loss. It frames the issue as asymmetric dam-safety and downstream-protection risk. The margins table is good, and it includes N-1 gates, sedimentation and seasonal staging. Its list of missing information is good.
- **Missing or weak:**
  - There is no operating rule: no trigger based on remaining slack, no abort or reversion conditions, and no warning protocol for man-made pre-releases.
  - Approval is mentioned only in one generic bullet.
  - It does not treat the confirmation/false-alarm dilemma at 154 m, the cost of false-alarm drawdowns, or refill.
  - It does not mention a limit on drawdown rate for the upstream slope and banks.
  - It gives no acceptance criterion, such as "risk no higher than the current 152 m rule" or "design/check levels and protection standard unchanged".
  - It does not note that the historical sample size limits back-testing.
  - The headline theoretical maximum mixes two scenarios instead of reporting the nominal 154.6 m (linear) and treating 154.0 m as a worst-case sensitivity.

### Answer 2

- **Done well:**
  - It gives clear deciding questions and a full sensitivity table, all arithmetic verified.
  - It treats the risks as correlated within one storm.
  - It identifies design and check flood routing from a raised start level with a missed forecast as the controlling dam-safety check.
  - It uses joint Monte Carlo sampling and states a sample-size caveat.
  - It covers the drawdown-rate limit and whether 5,000 m³/s includes freeboard.
  - It gives concrete operating, abort, reversion and approval provisions and recommends shadow operation.
- **Missing or weak:**
  - The 153.5 m figure still relies on linear storage above 152 m. It should say that the level-storage curve could shift it.
  - It does not quantify the release path, such as whether turbines can pass part of the 3,800 m³/s.
  - It does not check minimum gate opening or capacity at intermediate levels during drawdown beyond the N-1 remark.
  - It does not discuss how much protection the town loses, expressed as a return period, if drawdown is incomplete.
  - "Unannounced-looking rise" is imprecise wording.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither answer had

- Cite the Chinese technical basis for dynamic control of flood-limited water levels: the SL-series guidance on flood-season water-level dynamic control and the reservoir flood-regulation design code. Its standard acceptance principles are that the dam's design and check flood levels are not raised, the downstream protection standard is not lowered, and the range is approved within the flood-control operation plan.
- Treat the allowable upper level as a function of the current forecast, not a fixed number: a forecast-conditioned dynamic rule curve with a level-versus-forecast-inflow table updated every 6 h.
- Account for release during the 8 h latency. Some gate opening may begin once the decision is made and warning starts, if permitted. Also check whether the intervening flow is actually active early in the drawdown, which affects how conservative the 1,200 m³/s coincidence assumption is.
- Quantify the residual flood-control storage shortfall if drawdown stops partway, for example at 152.4 m, as a reduced protection return period for the town. This makes the consequence side of the risk comparison explicit.
- Account for sustained high downstream flow for about 22 h (bank stability, navigation, people in the channel) as a constraint separate from the 5,000 m³/s peak limit.
