## 2. Notes for revision

### Answer 1: done well
- Clean tree-MPC design with non-anticipative first stage, CVaR, terminal storage condition, inner piecewise-linear capacity.
- Strong forecast post-processing plan (quantile mapping, EMOS/BMA, rank histograms, reliability diagrams, CRPS/Brier by lead), and the idea that skill horizon sets branching and terminal weight.
- Reliability diagram for the tool's own risk statements; perfect-forecast regret; tree-without-post-processing ablation.
- Good alternatives table and good "what would change it" provisions (forecast-informed rule, MPC used offline to design a codified rule).

### Answer 1: missing or wrong
- No travel-time estimate for the 120 km reach and no point-of-no-return reasoning relative to tributary arrival; the time step choice is therefore not justified by the system's lag.
- No quantitative check of ensemble resolution (1/51) against the regulator's risk level, nor of how many independent flood events 20 years gives.
- No pre-release/flood-volume plausibility calculation (ΔQ·T against free flood storage; capacity falling with level during drawdown).
- No man-made-flood (release must not exceed natural flow) constraint; no design-flood check when plans hold storage above the flood-limited level; no gate-out case in constraints (only in stress tests).
- No explicit reversion triggers, downstream warning provisions, or statement that the existing manual remains legally binding; no check that hindcasts match the operational forecast version.
- "Only class of method" and solve-time claims stated without the caveat to measure.

### Answer 2: done well
- Settles the deciding questions with checked arithmetic: 11–22 h travel time, point of no return, 2% member resolution, binomial SE of replay, correlation inflation of tributary variance.
- Separates optimizer representation from verification representation per component.
- Lexicographic objectives with man-made flood avoidance; tree vs two-stage vs single-sequence argument.
- Detailed, measurable time budget, worst-case timing criterion, hard time limit with automatic fallback.
- Very specific regulatory path: manual compliance, pre-agreed acceptance criteria, shadow trial, reversion triggers with named decider, notifications, deterministic reproducibility.

### Answer 2: missing or wrong
- Replay resolution statement ("~5 pp") is loose; should distinguish paired per-event comparison from independent rates.
- Unsourced claim about an evolutionary setup elsewhere taking hours per event.
- Forecast post-processing is mentioned (under-dispersion, tail post-processor) but less specified than in Answer 1 (no named method, no lead-time-dependent skill horizon feeding branching beyond "where spread resolves").
- No explicit terminal value/storage condition at day 10 beyond the refill objective; no explicit risk measure (CVaR vs chance constraint) until the regulator sets a level.
- Wave celerity range 1.5–3 m/s could be high for a large lowland reach; a lower bound (~1 m/s, ~33 h) should be included as a test case.

### Points an expert answer must contain that neither had
- A worked pre-release feasibility calculation using the level-dependent capacity: achievable drawdown volume over the forecast lead = ∫(Q_cap(h(t)) − I(t))dt with h falling, compared with the forecast flood volume above Q_safe, to establish whether forecast-based pre-release can matter at all for this reservoir.
- Explicit comparison of the forecast skill horizon (from hindcasts) with the release travel time plus gate-operation latency, to fix the minimum useful lead and the length of the committed first stage.
- Attenuation in the 120 km reach: a release reduction is attenuated and delayed, so the station benefit per unit of release cut is less than one; the surrogate's gain should be quantified and reported to operators.
- Treatment of the station rating (and possible tributary backwater/hysteresis at the station) because the tributary residual and the safe threshold both depend on it; residual uncertainty from rating error should be propagated.
- Data latency and quality of real-time station and reservoir-level telemetry as part of the 30-minute budget and the residual estimate.
- Handling of the transition from gated control to uncontrolled spillway overflow at high levels, where releases stop being decisions and the optimizer must treat them as a state-dependent outflow.
